## Scope

The roadmap owns the Nash blowup and the normalised Nash blowup of a reduced scheme of finite type over a field, as intrinsic objects: their construction through the Grassmannian of locally free quotients of the sheaf of differentials, their universal properties, their locality, their description by the Gauss map for an embedded variety, the Nobile-type smoothness criteria, and the formal theory of the two iterations with the "never resolves" principle. It owns nothing about toric varieties, nothing about surfaces, and no computation.

**Setting (general form).** `k` is an arbitrary field. `X` is a reduced separated `k`-scheme of finite type, of pure dimension `d`, whose smooth locus `X_sm` (Mathlib's `Scheme.Hom.smoothLocus` of the structure morphism) is dense. The roadmap proves that this is the same as `X` reduced, separated, of finite type, pure of dimension `d` and geometrically reduced (stacksVarieties, Lemma 25.7, tag 056V), and that on `X_sm` the sheaf of differentials `Ω_{X/k}` is locally free of rank `d`. No algebraic closedness and no characteristic hypothesis enter the constructions; they enter only the smoothness criteria, and the generality of each criterion is stated below.

**Owned objects.**
- The Nash blowup `ν : X* → X`: the closure, with reduced structure, of the section of `Grass_d(Ω_{X/k}) → X` over `X_sm` given by the identity quotient, together with its universal rank-`d` locally free quotient `ν^*Ω_{X/k} → L` (the Nash cotangent bundle). The iterates `X^{(0)} = X`, `X^{(i+1)} = (X^{(i)})*`.
- The normalised Nash blowup `ν̄ : X̄* → X`: the normalisation `η : X̄* → X*` composed with `ν`. The iterates `Y_0 = X`, `Y_{i+1} = normalisation of (Y_i)*`.
- The predicates "the Nash iteration resolves `X`" (some `X^{(i)}` is smooth over `k`) and "the normalised Nash iteration resolves `X`" (some `Y_i` is smooth over `k`).

**Imported, never re-planned.**
- The Grassmannian of locally free rank-`r` quotients of a finite type quasi-coherent module on an arbitrary base, its representability by a scheme projective over the base, the universal quotient, base change, and the Plücker closed immersion into the projective bundle of the `r`-th exterior power: ModularCurves Layer 0G (finite locally free case, charts, Plücker) and AlgebraicModuliForArithmeticGeometry §1.3 (coherent case by restriction along a presentation). The roadmap may add the one interface lemma it needs, that a surjection of modules induces a closed immersion of Grassmannians compatible with the universal quotients, if the supplier's API lacks it.
- The quasi-coherent sheaf of relative differentials `Ω_{X/S}` with its base change and the relative singular subscheme: StableReduction Layer 1. Smooth morphisms, the open smooth locus, formally smooth stalks: Mathlib.
- The normalisation of a scheme in a morphism and its universal property: Mathlib `Scheme.Hom.normalization` with `normalizationDesc` and `normalization.hom_ext`; its functoriality: Tau Ceti `normalizationMap`. The normalisation of a reduced scheme with finitely many irreducible components is Mathlib's relative normalisation of the inclusion of the generic points (stacksNormalization, Definition 55.1, tag 035N, and Lemma 55.3, tag 035P).
- Normal schemes, dimension theory of finite type `k`-schemes, modifications and strict transforms, Chow's lemma, regular and smooth loci: SchemeAndStackFoundations §0.30, §0.7, §4.11, §4.12, §4.13. Projective morphisms and relative Proj: StableReduction Layer 2 and SchemeAndStackFoundations §0.3.

**Boundaries.** The combinatorial description of Nash blowups of toric varieties by logarithmic Jacobian ideals, the Annals theorem that for `d ≥ 4` a normal affine toric variety is never resolved by the iteration, the surface results of Hironaka and Spivakovsky, higher-order Nash blowups (principal parts of order `n`), and Nash blowups over a base other than a field are not planned here. The first two belong to a toric consumer; the roadmap's deliverable toward them is that its definitions and its "never resolves" principle make the Annals theorem statable in Lean once the toric variety and its charts exist.

## Final targets

Locators refer to the manifest keys of `packages/NashBlowups/sources.toml`; "line N" is a line of the worker's extraction of that text.

1. **Setting lemma.** For `X` reduced, separated, of finite type over `k` and pure of dimension `d`: `X_sm` is dense iff `X` is geometrically reduced (stacksVarieties, Lemma 25.7, line 2112); `X_sm` is open; on `X_sm` the sheaf `Ω_{X/k}` is locally free of rank `d`; `X_sm` is dense in every irreducible component and contains every generic point. Check: over a perfect field every reduced `X` qualifies; over `k = F_p(t)` the point `Spec k[y]/(y^p - t)` is reduced but not geometrically reduced, its smooth locus is empty, and it is excluded by the lemma.

2. **Existence and basic properties of the Nash blowup** (gonzalezSprinberg1982, Section 1, lines 85-99; nashModificationNotes2024, Section 2, lines 153-162; duarteNunezBetancourt2020, Definition 2.1, lines 124-129). With `G = Grass_d(Ω_{X/k}) → X` and `σ : X_sm → G` the section classifying the identity quotient of `Ω_{X/k}|_{X_sm}`: `X*` is the scheme-theoretic closure of `σ(X_sm)` in `G` with its reduced structure, `ν : X* → X` the restriction of the structure map. Then (a) `ν` is projective, so proper, and is a modification in the sense of SchemeAndStackFoundations §4.11 with witness `X_sm`: it is an isomorphism over `X_sm`, `ν^{-1}(X_sm)` is dense in `X*`, and `ν` is birational on every irreducible component; (b) `X*` is reduced, separated, of finite type and pure of dimension `d`, with dense smooth locus, so it satisfies the Setting and the construction iterates; (c) the universal quotient of `G` restricts to a locally free rank-`d` quotient `ν^*Ω_{X/k} → L` extending the identity over `X_sm`; (d) if `X` is smooth over `k` then `ν` is an isomorphism; (e) formation of `X*` commutes with base change along a field extension `k'/k` (the base change is again reduced with dense smooth locus by target 1, and the Grassmannian and the closure commute with the flat base change).

3. **Universal property** (nashModificationNotes2024, Section 2, lines 153-162, Teissier's formulation). Let `h : Z → X` be a morphism from a reduced `k`-scheme `Z` such that `h^{-1}(X_sm)` is dense in `Z` and `h^*Ω_{X/k}` admits a locally free quotient `h^*Ω_{X/k} → M` of rank `d`. Then `h` factors uniquely through `ν`, by a morphism `q : Z → X*` with `q^*L = M` as quotients of `h^*Ω_{X/k}`; the quotient `M` is unique, being determined on the dense open `h^{-1}(X_sm)` where it is the identity. Consequence: `(X*, ν, L)` is unique up to unique isomorphism, and any projective birational `π : X_1 → X` from a reduced `X_1` with a locally free rank-`d` quotient of `π^*Ω_{X/k}` whose restriction over `X_sm` is the identity is the Nash blowup.

4. **Locality and the embedded description** (nobile1975, Introduction, lines 29-44; castilloDuarteLeytonLiendo2026, Introduction, lines 13-21). (a) For an open `U ⊆ X`, `U*` is `ν^{-1}(U)` with `ν` restricted; hence Nash blowups glue and are computed affine-locally. (b) For `X` a closed subscheme of an open of `A^n_k`, let `Φ : X_sm → Grass(d, n)` be the Gauss map `x ↦ T_x X`, the tangent space as a `d`-dimensional subspace of `k^n` read on the Grassmannian of rank-`d` quotients of the dual, so that `Φ` is the classifying map of the quotient `Ω_{A^n/k}|_{X_sm} → Ω_{X/k}|_{X_sm}`. Then `X*` is isomorphic over `X` to the closure, with reduced structure, of the graph of `Φ` in `X ×_k Grass(d, n)`, and the isomorphism is compatible with the universal quotients; the result is independent of the embedding. (c) `X*` is locally the blowup of an ideal (nobile1975, Theorem 1, line 105): on an affine chart it is the blowup of the ideal generated by the `d × d` minors of the Jacobian matrix, or of a Fitting ideal of `Ω_{X/k}`; the design decides whether to prove this via the Plücker immersion and the strict transform of SchemeAndStackFoundations §4.11 or to record it as a Check.

5. **Nobile's theorem, characteristic zero** (nobile1975, Theorem 2, lines 143-146; nashModificationNotes2024, Theorem 2.1 and Proposition 2.3, lines 143-145 and 179-199). Let `char k = 0`. Then `ν : X* → X` is an isomorphism iff `X` is smooth over `k`. The algebraic route is Teissier's: if `ν` is an isomorphism then `Ω_{X/k}` itself has a locally free quotient of rank `d`, and in characteristic zero a reduced `k`-scheme of pure dimension `d` whose sheaf of differentials has a locally free quotient of rank `d` is smooth; the proof of the latter is by induction on `d` at the completed local rings, using Zariski's lemma that a derivation `D` of a complete local `k`-algebra with `D(x) = 1` splits off a power series variable. The source states the theorem over an algebraically closed field; the general statement follows by target 2(e), smoothness being stable under and detected by base change to the algebraic closure. Checks: the cusp `y^2 = x^3` has `ν` not an isomorphism in characteristic zero but an isomorphism in characteristic 2 and 3 (nobile1975, lines 60-61 and 141; duarteNunezBetancourt2020, lines 139-141), so the characteristic hypothesis is necessary.

6. **The normal case in every characteristic** (duarteNunezBetancourt2020, Theorem 3.10, lines 256-257; nashModificationNotes2024, Theorem 5.1, lines 362-364). Let `k` be algebraically closed of characteristic `p > 0` and `X` normal. Then `ν` is an isomorphism iff `X` is smooth. The proof uses Kunz's theorem (a Noetherian local domain of characteristic `p` whose ring of `p`-th roots is finite free is regular). The design states the theorem over an algebraically closed field as the source does, and decides whether to extend it to a perfect field by base change (normality is preserved under algebraic separable base change) as a target or to leave the extension as a Check with the obstacle named.

7. **The normalised Nash blowup** (castilloDuarteLeytonLiendo2026, lines 20-21; gonzalezSprinberg1982, Section 1, lines 60-69; stacksNormalization, Definition 55.1 and Lemmas 55.3, 55.8, 55.11). With `η : X̄* → X*` the normalisation of `X*`: (a) `η` is finite (Nagata), birational and an isomorphism over the normal locus of `X*`, in particular over `ν^{-1}(X_sm)`; (b) `X̄*` is normal, separated, of finite type, pure of dimension `d`, with dense smooth locus, so it satisfies the Setting; (c) `ν̄ = ν ∘ η` is projective, birational and an isomorphism over `X_sm`, with `ν̄^{-1}(X_sm)` dense; (d) universal property: a morphism `h : Z → X` from a normal `Z` with `h^{-1}(X_sm)` dense and a locally free rank-`d` quotient of `h^*Ω_{X/k}` factors uniquely through `ν̄`; (e) locality over opens of `X` and compatibility with field base change in the form the normalisation allows (the design states the exact hypothesis, separable or perfect, since normalisation does not commute with inseparable base change).

8. **Isomorphism criteria for the normalised Nash blowup.** For normal `X`: `ν̄` is an isomorphism iff `ν` is an isomorphism iff `X` is smooth, the last equivalence in characteristic zero by target 5 and in positive characteristic over an algebraically closed field by target 6. The step "`ν̄` iso implies `ν` iso" is: `η` is finite and birational with `ν ∘ η` an isomorphism, so `η` is a surjective closed immersion of reduced schemes, hence an isomorphism; the converse is stacksNormalization Lemma 55.8 (tag 0AB1) applied to `η` when `X* ≅ X` is normal.

9. **Iteration and the "never resolves" principle** (castilloDuarteLeytonLiendo2026, Theorem of the Introduction, lines 58-67). Define the iterates `X^{(i)}` and `Y_i` as in Scope. Prove: (a) if `X` is smooth then every `X^{(i)}` and `Y_i` is `X`; (b) if `Y_m` contains an open subscheme `U` isomorphic to `X` for some `m ≥ 1`, then `Y_{jm}` contains an open subscheme isomorphic to `X` for every `j ≥ 1`, by locality of the normalised Nash blowup applied to `U`; the same for the un-normalised iterates `X^{(i)}`; (c) if `X` is singular and some `Y_m`, `m ≥ 1`, contains an open subscheme isomorphic to `X`, then no `Y_i` is smooth, since a smooth `Y_i` would be normal and all later iterates would equal it by (a) while `Y_{jm}` for `jm ≥ i` contains a singular open; the same for the `X^{(i)}`. Statement (c) is the exact form in which the Annals theorem is applied, and the roadmap records in `Suggested.lean` the implication "a normal singular `X` whose first or second normalised Nash blowup contains an open subscheme isomorphic to `X` is never resolved by the normalised Nash iteration", with the toric existence statement left to a consumer.

## Risks

- **Sheaf of differentials.** `Ω_{X/k}` as a quasi-coherent sheaf on a scheme is StableReduction Layer 1, a roadmap on main, not a library declaration; Mathlib has only the module `Ω[S⁄R]` and the formally-unramified criterion. Targets 1 and 2 depend on that layer's base change and locally-free-on-the-smooth-locus statements being available in the shape the Grassmannian needs.
- **Coherent Grassmannian.** The coherent Grassmannian of a non-locally-free module is AlgebraicModuliForArithmeticGeometry §1.3, a package under review (PR #853), built on ModularCurves Layer 0G at the head of PR #1101; Mathlib has only the affine functor. The design must import both by section and may not re-plan charts or the Plücker map; if the supplier's interface lacks the closed immersion induced by a surjection of modules, that one lemma is planned here.
- **Finiteness of normalisation.** Mathlib's relative normalisation is integral, not finite; finiteness for finite type `k`-schemes (Nagata, stacksNormalization Lemma 55.11, tag 035S) is not in the pinned libraries and SchemeAndStackFoundations §4.13 assumes it as a hypothesis. Target 7(a) needs it; the design must locate an owner or plan the commutative algebra (E. Noether's finiteness of the integral closure of a finitely generated domain over a field in a finite extension of its fraction field).
- **Characteristic-zero criterion.** Teissier's proof of target 5 runs at completed local rings and uses Zariski's derivation lemma `A = A_1[[x]]` for a complete local `k`-algebra in characteristic zero; nothing like it is in the pinned libraries, and Nobile's original proof is analytic over `C` (nobile1975, lines 147-150), which is not a formalisation route. The design should expect a substantial commutative-algebra layer or an alternative argument via the Jacobian criterion and the generic rank of `Ω`.
- **Positive-characteristic normal case.** Target 6 needs Kunz's theorem (regularity from flatness of Frobenius) and the structure of principal parts over a normal variety; the pinned Mathlib has no Kunz theorem. If this cannot be supplied, target 6 and the positive-characteristic half of target 8 should be stated in `Suggested.lean` with the algebraically closed hypothesis and planned in a Part II rather than dropped.
- **Base change.** The source statements are over algebraically closed fields; the general-field targets rest on commutation of the construction with field base change (target 2(e), 7(e)). For the normalised blowup this holds only for separable extensions; the design must keep the hypotheses honest rather than claim every statement over every field.
- **Sources.** Semple, Hironaka, Spivakovsky, Teissier and EGA I are paywalled and cited by locator only; no final target rests on them. The Annals paper's journal numbering was not checked against the arXiv v3 read; locators refer to the arXiv text.
