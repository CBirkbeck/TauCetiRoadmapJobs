## R02 (P2) — Keep Taylor–Wiles level away from conjugate protected places

**Location:** `packages/EllipticCurveModularityImaginaryQuadraticPartII/design/README.md:894`: “every Taylor–Wiles datum `Q` of §4.2”; line 906: “The complex conjugate places `P^c` are hyperspecial”. Also §5.2 at line 868 and `design/plan.json:793`, target 5.3 at line 851.

**Claim:** Every allowed auxiliary level keeps all of `P^c` hyperspecial, allowing the stated unramifiedness argument. But §4.2 only avoids `S ∪ B`, with arbitrary finite `B`; `P^c ∩ S = ∅`. Nothing here requires taking `B` to contain `P^c`.

**Witness:** CaptainSude26 line 302 excludes `S ∪ S^c` from `Q`. GlobalGaloisDeformations, pull request #920, `TauCetiRoadmap/GlobalGaloisDeformations/README.md:1082`, requires only `Q ∩ S = ∅`; it does not supply the missing exclusion.

**Exact repair:** Preserve the general selection theorem §4.2. For the protected Hecke/patching application, specify `Q ∩ (S ∪ S^c) = ∅` in §5.2 and §5.3, and choose the sequence in §5.4 using §4.2 with `B = S^c` (enlarged by any other fixed forbidden places). Carry this into the corresponding plan hypotheses/prerequisites and add the negative-control Check below. The generic deformation construction may remain available for arbitrary `Q`; the Hecke comparison must identify the more restricted class it uses.

**Test of repair:** If `w = v^c ∈ Q` for `v ∈ P`, with `q_w = 11`, level one and residual Frobenius `diag(2,3)` over `𝔽₅`, the Taylor–Wiles conditions hold locally: `11 ≡ 1 mod 5`, determinant `1`, distinct eigenvalues. The upper-triangular level has index `12` in the hyperspecial group, and its diamond-kernel level has index `60`; neither is hyperspecial. Over `𝔽₅[ε]/(ε²)`, inertia `diag(1+ε,1−ε)` commutes with that Frobenius and equals its own eleventh power, but is nontrivial. This is an allowed ramified tangent direction at `Q`. With the repaired exclusion, `w ∉ Q`, the level at every member of `P^c` remains hyperspecial, exactly as required by the cited proof. The new Check should compute these indices or this tangent example. This is an overclaim in the allowed auxiliary-level interface, not a counterexample to the final modularity theorem.
