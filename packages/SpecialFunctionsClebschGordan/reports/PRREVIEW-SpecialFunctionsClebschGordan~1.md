# Review of SpecialFunctionsClebschGordan (pull request #2930 at 9d5091f)

**Verdict:** needs changes

**Summary.** Needs changes, for one reason: the Regge-symmetry target of §2.1 says the 3-j symbol is invariant under a group that contains the odd column permutations and the reversal, which the same section correctly says change its sign. Every one of the 51 statements of `Suggested.lean` was recomputed on small parameters, junk values included, and on the pinned unitary model, and none failed. There are four small corrections and three format notes, none blocking. Two things are left to the human reviewers: the stated reuse of the author's own Apache-2.0 formalization, and that the four books could not be read, so wording and arrangement were compared with the DLMF only.

## R01 (P2) — The Regge-symmetry target of §2.1 asserts invariance of the 3-j symbol under a group whose odd elements change its sign

In §2.1 (README lines 314–317) the target reads: "The 3-j symbol is invariant under the 72-element group generated by the permutations, the reversal and the Regge symmetries". An implementer who takes this as the statement to prove is asked for something false, and it contradicts `threeJ_swap` and `threeJ_reflect` four lines above it, which carry the factor `(-1)^{j₁+j₂+j₃}`.

Witness, computed from the definition in `Suggested.lean`: `(1 1 1; 1 -1 0) = threeJ 2 2 2 2 0 1 = 1/√6`, while exchanging the first two columns, or reversing all `m`, gives `(1 1 1; -1 1 0) = threeJ 2 2 2 0 2 1 = -1/√6`. The DLMF states only 34.3.11 and 34.3.12 as equalities without sign (I checked 34.3.11 against the Lean definition on 681 instances, no failure) and says further symmetries come from combining them with 34.3.8–34.3.10, which carry the sign (DLMF34-3 lines 281–283 and 410–412).

Smallest change: "is invariant up to sign under the 72-element group …: an element acts on the Regge square by a row permutation, a column permutation and possibly a transposition, and multiplies the symbol by `(-1)^{j₁+j₂+j₃}` exactly when the two permutations have opposite parity."

## C01 — Four theorems of `Suggested.lean` hold by unfolding a definition, so as targets they ask an implementer for nothing

On a copy of the file, `isTriad_comm` closes with `grind [IsTriad]`; `clebschGordan_eq_zero_of_not_isTriad` and `clebschGordan_eq_zero_of_ne` each close with `simp [clebschGordan, h]` (the README itself gives their route as "the definition"); and `threeJ_spin_zero` closes with `simp [threeJ, clebschGordan_spin_zero h]`, so it is `clebschGordan_spin_zero` again.

If they are kept as sanity checks of the definitions, say so in their docstrings; otherwise they can live in the README as Checks, where the selection rules and the value `threeJ 1 1 0 1 0 0 = 1/√2` already are.

## C02 — Hahn orthogonality is stated for `α, β > -1` only, narrower than the DLMF row it cites and than its own proof route

`sum_hahn_mul_hahn` (§4.2, README line 488; Lean line 480) assumes `-1 < α` and `-1 < β`. Table 18.19.1 of the DLMF gives the Hahn row for `α, β > -1` or `α, β < -N`, and the route of §4.2 (a polynomial identity in `(α, β)` after clearing `(α+1)_n`) proves more. In exact rational arithmetic the Lean identity as written holds at all 275 instances I tried with `N ≤ 4` and `(α, β)` among `(-15/2, -63/10)`, `(-5/2, 7/3)`, `(-3/2, -9/4)`, `(-10, -12)`, `(1/3, -17/5)`, whenever `(α+1)_n (α+1)_m ≠ 0`.

The Racah orthogonality of §6.2 already takes the right form of hypothesis. The same here would be: replace `hα`, `hβ` by the non-vanishing of `(α+1)_n` and `(α+1)_m`, and keep `α, β > -1` as the case where the weights are positive.

## C03 — The build guide's list of what each layer uses omits two edges that the layers themselves state

"How to read the build" says "Layer 2 uses §§1.2, 1.4 and 1.5" and "Layer 4 uses §§1.2, 1.4 and 1.5" (README lines 50–52). But §2.4 derives the recursions in `j` from `threeJ_mul_sixJ` of §5.2 (line 376), and §4.1 takes the hypergeometric form of the coefficient from §§2.1 and 2.4 (line 475). There is no cycle, since §5.2 does not use §2.4, but a reader planning the build from the guide would start §2.4 before Layer 5.

Either add the two edges to the guide ("§2.4 also uses §5.2; §4.1 also uses §§2.1 and 2.4"), or move the recursions in `j` to Layer 5.

## C04 — Six prose targets are given by a locator alone, with no statement anywhere in the folder

The recursions of the 3-j symbol and its values at `j₃ = 1` (§2.4), the parameters of the `₃F₂` form (§2.4) and of the `₄F₃` form (§6.1), the Regge symmetries of the 6-j symbol (§5.3) and the dual Hahn orthogonality (§4.2) have neither a formula in the README nor a declaration in `Suggested.lean`. An implementer has to reconstruct each statement, with its range of validity, from the DLMF or from Edmonds.

One displayed formula each would do, as §3.1 does for the inverse Clebsch–Gordan series. (The targets that do have a Lean declaration are covered by its docstring.)

## N01 — The count in the last Check of §6.1 is over the instances with a nonzero 6-j symbol

With `e₁, e₂, e₃, e ≤ 6` the hypotheses of `sixJ_eq_racahPolynomial` admit 7296 instances; the printed constant fails on 4650 of them, and 5214 is the number on which the 6-j symbol is nonzero. Say "of the 5214 instances with a nonzero symbol".

## N02 — "Prior formalization" reports what another repository proves without naming a commit

The section's statements ("proved there without `sorry`", "stated there with `sorry`") are true of one revision only. Naming the commit in the README makes them a fixed fact.

## N03 — Three hypotheses are not needed for the truth of their statements

`sum_clebschGordan_mul_clebschGordan` holds without `h'` and `hi'` (both sides are `0`; 4400 instances), and `threeJ_swap` without `Even (d₁ + d₂ + d₃)` (both sides are `0` on an odd sum; 8194 instances). They do no harm; drop them or keep them as documentation.
