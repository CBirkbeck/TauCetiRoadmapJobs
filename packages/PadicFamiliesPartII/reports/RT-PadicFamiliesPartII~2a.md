1. **F01** (P1) Partial Frobenius kills the flags it proposes to transport

   **Location:** packages/PadicFamiliesPartII/README.md:932, “the subgroups `π(H_v)` for `v ≠ w`”.

   **Claim:** These subgroups equip the partial-primary quotient with its required mixed Klingen/Iwahori level.

   **Witness:** Take a real quadratic F, split p=3, and v≠w. The kernel contains A[v], hence π(H_v)=0, of order 1 rather than 3. BCGP21 line 6131 prints the same erroneous transport.

   **Exact repair text:** “For v≠w, let j_v : A[v^∞]/A[v] ≅ A[v^∞] be the canonical isomorphism induced by multiplication by p. Under Ã[v^∞] ≅ A[v^∞]/A[v], transport the distinguished subgroup and every member of the Iwahori flag by j_v⁻¹. Thus H̃_v = j_v⁻¹(H_v), equivalently π([p]⁻¹H_v), where the inverse image is taken inside A[v^∞]. At w retain H_w^(p).” Add this correction to Source issues and add the real-quadratic example as a Check.

   **Retest:** For H_v=μ₃ in an ordinary height-four v-divisible group, [3]⁻¹H_v has order 3⁵; quotienting by A[v] of order 3⁴ leaves order 3. The direct image π(H_v) has order 1. A rank-9 flag member is transported to rank 9 as well.

2. **F02** (P1) The slope bounds do not exhaust character space in central directions

   **Location:** packages/PadicFamiliesPartII/README.md:1126, “with `lim_μ N^{≤μ} = N^fs`”.

   **Claim:** The single-weight slope cones recover finite-slope localization for every solid positive-torus representation.

   **Witness:** For T=G_m, T⁺=T, the inequalities at p and p⁻¹ force slope=μ. Distinct μ have no transition. On the connected annulus |p|≤|X|≤1, independent constants on its rational-radius circles do not glue to an analytic function.

   **Exact repair text:** “Finite-slope localization is recovered by the derived inverse limit over the increasing affinoid exhaustion 𝒵_n specified above. Single-weight cones define the indicated slope restrictions; they are not an exhaustion in directions on which both t and t⁻¹ belong to T⁺. No inverse-limit identity over μ is asserted without fixing the central slope and giving an admissible exhaustion of that fixed-slope locus.” Delete the displayed inverse-limit identity over μ and record the qualification to BCGP25 §4.6.46, p. 95 (line 4257).

   **Retest:** Use E⟨X,pX⁻¹⟩ with p acting by X and ℤ_p^× trivially. It is a smooth torus representation and hence λ-smooth for λ=0. Its finite-slope analytic realization is the annulus. The discrete μ-limit admits the family 0 on the radius-1 circle and 1 on every other rational-radius circle; no analytic annulus section restricts to this family. The affinoid-exhaustion definition retains the gluing and works for this example. The same example embeds in the central direction of GL₂, so removing pure torus Shimura data would not fix it.

3. **F03** (P2) The repaired curve argument places every cusp in the wrong tube

   **Location:** packages/PadicFamiliesPartII/README.md:468, “the cusps lying in the multiplicative tube”.

   **Claim:** The dimension-one continuation automatically includes all boundary points because their selected Iwahori subgroup is multiplicative.

   **Witness:** On the compactified modular curve with Γ₀(3) level, the Tate cusp with selected subgroup μ₃ has degree 1; the opposite cusp has selected constant component subgroup ℤ/3, of degree 0. BPS lines 1353–1356 assert neither identification.

   **Exact repair text:** “Otherwise perform the continuation of §§2.2–2.7 on the smooth compactified modular curve, including both types of Tate boundary chart. The chart with H=μ_p is in the multiplicative region; the chart of Tate(t^p) with H generated by t has quotient Tate(t) and lies at degree zero. Prove the good/bad correspondence estimates and bounded gluing on these charts as well as on the open curve, with pullback of the invariant differential computed from the Tate isogenies. This boundary extension is part of the present target; proper GAGA applies only after it is proved.” Add a Check comparing these two cusps. Keep BPS Remark 5.3.2 as the locator for the proposed boundary extension, not as a proof that every cusp is multiplicative.

   **Retest:** For p=3 the two chart degrees are 1 and 0, respectively. The quotient maps Tate(q)→Tate(q³) and Tate(t³)→Tate(t) pull back dz/z by factors 3 and 1. The repaired route retains both charts and their different normalizations. The compact curve case remains unaffected. This is a new defect in the replacement proof route for earlier pass b/F06, not a recurrence of its forward dependency.

4. **F04** (P3) Two filtration Checks merely repeat its definition

   **Location:** packages/PadicFamiliesPartII/README.md:405–406, “If no complement is bad then V₁ = ∅”; “exactly one bad complement lies in V₁ ∖ V₂”.

   **Witness:** Both bullets are immediate substitutions in V_k={N≥k}; they hold for any counting function and cannot detect counting the wrong complements or reversing the degree inequality. Only the topology and dimension bullets remain discriminating.

   **Exact repair text:** Replace those two bullets with: “At p=3, over a valued extension containing the necessary torsion, a supersingular elliptic lift over W(F̄₃) has four order-three subgroups, each of degree 1/4. For any selected H, exactly three are complements. At β=3/4 all three are bad, so N=3; counting H too would give 4. At β=1/2 none is bad, so N=0; reversing the degree inequality would give 3.    **Retest:** The degree comparisons are 1/4≥1/4, 1/4<1/2, and respectively 1≥1/2 and 0<1/2. The resulting counts are 3,0,1. These distinguish the threshold, inequality direction and selected-subgroup exclusion.

5. **F05** (P2) Fixed central character omits the arithmetic normalization

   **Location:** packages/PadicFamiliesPartII/README.md:1465, “on which the centre acts by |·|²”.

   **Claim:** The displayed central-character submodule is the one in BCGP25 Lemma 7.3.8.

   **Witness:** On a central p-adic unit z=4 at p=3, the adelic norm squared is 1, but the source's integral action is 4⁻²=1/16. BCGP25 lines 7637–7649 explicitly distinguish these characters.

   **Exact repair text:** “For a module M with the central action of Z(Q)\Z(GSp₄(A_f)), write M^{|·|²} for the submodule on which a representative (z_ℓ)_ℓ in ∏_ℓ Z_ℓ^× acts as z_p⁻². This notation records the algebraic normalization corresponding to automorphic central character |·|²; it is not the literal norm character on ∏_ℓ Z_ℓ^×. After the displayed base change, the p-component already acts by z_p⁻², so this submodule is obtained by taking invariants for ∏_{ℓ≠p} Z_ℓ^×. Here ZK^p=∏_{ℓ≠p} Z(Z_ℓ)K_ℓ.” Cite BCGP25 §7.3, pp.166–167, including Remark 7.3.7, and add the unit calculation below as a Check.

   **Retest:** At p=3,z_p=4 and all other components 1, the prescribed action is 1/16 rather than 1; at p=2,z_p=3 it is 1/9 rather than 1. Both are integral units. The diagonal −1 acts by (−1)⁻²=1, so the prescription descends through the indicated quotient. A torsion-free nonzero module with the source's p-component action would have zero literal norm-character submodule, since (1/16−1)m=0 would force m=0; the repaired definition retains the intended module.

Construct C^la as the horizontal-nilradical invariants of analytic function germs on G at the identity tensored with O_FL.    **Retest:** For G=1, both sheaves are E and all Lie actions are zero. For G=G_m,P=G, FL is a point but C^la is the infinite-dimensional space of analytic germs on G_m, not the constant line E.
