## Corrections and support boundaries

- D7.1–D7.3 retain the explicit residue-preserving hypothesis. Finite characteristic-zero universal homeomorphisms are a corollary, not a proof of the unrestricted pre-adic residue-field assertion. SW20 line 3113 gives the assertion without the missing general argument; SW20 lines 3131–3141 explains exactly where equality of completed residue fields enters.
- D7.5 includes nonemptiness and proves existence by finite intersection of the closed inverse images of prime upper intervals. For an empty source the proposed image point does not exist. This proof avoids an unnecessary finite-rank valuation approximation.
- D7.9 corrects the title as well as the statement to open ideals, not open prime ideals. For F_p×F_p the whole locus is V(0) and zero is not prime. The empty locus is V(1). This evaluates the defect in GLE line 1088.
- D8.6 kills A·A°° in the ambient ring and takes the integral closure of the image of A⁺ there. It does not use a nonunital product as a plus ring. A Tate unit makes the quotient zero. This corrects GLE line 1271 in the sense prescribed by the accepted review.
- D8.1 separates perfect site objects from sheaves represented after perfection, and gives the cutoff-extension functor the direction from smaller to larger cutoff. D8.7 explicitly imports the one-point quotient theorem from DiamondEtaleCohomology C8.24; it is not silently re-proved or used without an owner.
- D9.7 proves formal separatedness using the closed counit and the diagonal criterion, without declaring all formal schemes formally p-adic. Spf O[[T]] has characteristic-p analytic points and is the negative control.
- D9.8 and D11.5 retain the underlined topological-space sheaves and the condition on the entire test-space image. Tubes are interiors of fibres. A rank-two closed-point fibre has one point while its tube has zero points. These are the strict inclusions of AGLR lines 895–904.
- D9.12 uses completed formally étale lifting. R⁺ and R⁺×R⁺[1/ϖ] are different ordinary étale algebras with the same special fibre, but their completions coincide. GLE lines 1623–1638 is read with this corrected uniqueness category.
- D10 uses continuous FS morphisms, allowing (π,q)-adic topologies. D10.2 compares the relative generic fibre, not the entire analytic locus. D10.10 explicitly proves ideal-of-definition continuity. The local connectedness and local bounded-functions targets supporting de Jong's theorem live in D10.4, and D11.15 applies that local result; the component theorem is not used to prove its own local input.
- The alternate pseudorigid Hebbarkeitssatz route to D10.4 is not prescribed: it would introduce an independent general extension theorem absent from these targets. The retained route has the local comparisons as explicit targets, followed by completions, faithful flatness and gluing. Lourenço's theorem is still cited for its supported integral-recovery statements.
- D10.11 keeps the nonseparated generality by localizing on separated affine target charts. D10.12 and D10.13 use formal GAGA for morphisms between existing proper schemes; they do not assume arbitrary proper formal schemes algebraize. Absolute weak normality uses the exact prime-indexed equations of AGLR lines 671–689, not arbitrary root closure.
- D10.14–D10.15 supply both closure comparison and closedness in the same ambient Grassmannian, as required by the omitted ambient comparison in KPZ lines 1329–1335. The GL minuscule comparison is a hypothesis, and no local-model existence theorem is claimed. Flatness with reduced special fibre alone is not used to infer normality: the cusp over O is a counterexample to the sentence at SW20 line 7494; the smooth generic fibre in the cited application provides the missing normality input.
- D11.6 distinguishes local spectrality from global quasicompactness. The open disc gives a smelted pair whose inverse image of the whole affine reduction is not quasicompact. AGLR line 870 defines the chart convention; the kimberlite axiom separately supplies quasicompact specialization.
- D11.8's perfected-disc computation explicitly uses characteristic-p C. D11.10's defining facets are over characteristic-p C; a smooth untilt is a separately proved example. D11.11 includes separatedness in the formal-scheme richness example.
- D11.14 retains the closed-specialization condition and proves the componentwise connectedness step after quotient descent, not only descent of clopens. GLE lines 1881–1888 does not itself justify the unrestricted closed-point inference. No unrestricted theorem is promised. The applications use Jacobson finite-type models, which do supply closed specializations. The unproved claim about arbitrary locally noetherian scheme models is not needed or used.
- D11.15's degree-f closed-point tube is an open disc over the corresponding finite unramified extension; it is not generally an open E-disc. D11.17 excludes the zero ring from the nonflat perfect-algebra example; the empty kimberlite is flat.
- D11.20 keeps algebraically closed residue field. If B=O_L for a nontrivial unramified L/Q_p and A=Z_p+pB, then B/A has F_p-dimension [L:Q_p]−1 while the only generic tube Spa(L,O_L) is connected. Thus the perfect-field generality printed at AGLR lines 1044–1064 cannot be supported by connectedness alone. The proof here uses weak normality directly after proving that the finite normalization map is a universal homeomorphism; it does not apply absolute-weak-normality full faithfulness to a merely weakly normal ring.

The two mathematical gaps already in the accepted design remain in its top-level gaps list. The README states the supported theorems with their exact extra hypotheses. It does not promise an unrestricted analytic residue-field theorem or an unrestricted component criterion. Five supplier gaps were added, and only the top-level gaps value of `design/plan.json` changed. Every added gap has matching text in the supplier's README subsection.
