## R01 (P3) — §7.4's paraphrase of the general conormal theorem leaves out the source's "reasonable" hypothesis

**Location.** `TauCetiRoadmap/FormalConnectionsOpers/README.md:2492–2497`: "(for a coisotropic subscheme of a formally smooth Poisson ind-scheme, itself formally smooth, whose normal bundle is locally projective, the conormal algebroid, its action and the diagram (4.9) are Frenkel–Gaitsgory, §4.4, p. 40)". It continues: "supplying the hypothesis used in the general conormal theorem."

**Claim.** The new parenthetical gives the hypotheses of the general theorem that §7.4 applies, but Frenkel–Gaitsgory also require the subscheme to be *reasonable*. That is a finiteness condition, and Frenkel–Gaitsgory use it to make the cotangent and conormal objects Tate bundles. §7.4 proves only local projectivity and never checks reasonableness for `Op(D) ⊂ Op(D×)`. As written, the README attributes to the source a theorem the source does not state.

**Witness.** FrenkelGaitsgory2006 line 1869 (p. 40) takes `S₁ ⊂ S` to be a reasonable subscheme. Line 5460 (§19.2, p. 112) defines this: the ideal of `S₁ ∩ Yᵢ` in each stage `Yᵢ` is locally finitely generated. Line 1788 uses reasonableness to get Tate tangents.

**Repair.**
- At 2492, write "(for a reasonable coisotropic subscheme, that is, one whose ideal is locally finitely generated in every stage of the ind-presentation (Frenkel–Gaitsgory, §19.2, p. 112), of a formally smooth Poisson ind-scheme, itself formally smooth, …".
- At 2496–2497, replace "supplying the hypothesis used in the general conormal theorem" with "supplying the hypotheses used in the general conormal theorem; `Op(D)` is reasonable in `Op(D×)`, since in the stage `Op_(≤N)` of §5.2 its ideal is generated by the `ℓN` coordinates `q_(i,j)`, `−N ≤ j < 0`".
- At 2845–2846, write "the conormal algebroid (4.9) of a reasonable coisotropic subscheme, p. 40".

**Test.** In rank one with `N = 3`, `ker(A₃ → A₀) = (q₋₁, q₋₂, q₋₃)` has three generators, so `Op(D)` is reasonable. Now take the wrong model that §5.2's second Check rules out: the scheme `Spec k[q_j : j ∈ ℤ]`. It is formally smooth, and its ideal `(q_j : j < 0) = (L_n : n ≥ −1)` is coisotropic for the Virasoro bracket, because the central term `½(m³ − m)` vanishes for `m ∈ {−1, 0, 1}`. But that ideal needs infinitely many generators. Reasonableness is the hypothesis that separates the ind-scheme from this scheme.
