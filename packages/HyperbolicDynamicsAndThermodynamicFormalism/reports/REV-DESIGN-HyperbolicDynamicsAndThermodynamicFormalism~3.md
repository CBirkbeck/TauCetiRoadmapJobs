## R01 (P1) — Signed-time transitivity makes the uniqueness route false

**Location:** D:25, D:695–697, D:762–766, D:790, D:1097–1101; P:1647,1817,1898,2731. Quote: “for a homeomorphism or flow ... the ℤ- or ℝ-flow”.

**Claim:** This transitivity plus hyperbolic local product structure implies dense periodic points, weak specification and unique Hölder equilibrium states. Consequently the basic-set definition suffices for §§10.2–10.4.

**Witness:** X={x∈{0,1}^ℤ : x_i≤x_(i+1)} has a dense ℤ-orbit and hyperbolic local product structure. Only 0^ℤ and 1^ℤ are periodic; h_top=0; both Dirac probabilities are equilibrium states of 0.

**Exact repair:** Use forward transitivity: `AddAction.IsTopologicallyTransitive ℕ X` via `Flow.fromIter` for homeomorphisms as well as maps, and the nonnegative-time action for flows. Spell out ∃n≥0 / ∃t≥0 in the conventions, supplier interpretation, affected statements and P. Keep the general signed action predicate available, but do not identify it with this stronger dynamical hypothesis. Update the basic-set definition and all consumers, and add this system and its roof-one suspension as negative controls. The repaired hypothesis excludes them: for U={x:x₀=1} and V={x:x₀=0}, T^n(U)∩V=∅ for all n≥0. The full shift and finite cyclic orbits still pass.

**Verification detail:** Every nonconstant sequence in X has exactly one transition from 0 to 1. These transition sequences form a single dense full orbit. With d=2^(−min{|i|:x_i≠y_i}), the stable and unstable contractions have c=1, λ=1/2; the bracket splices the past of z with the future of x, and is admissible whenever their central symbols agree. Thus A2/A3 really hold. A cylinder specifying 01 at adjacent coordinates is an isolated transition point and contains no periodic point. There are n+1 admissible words of length n, hence entropy 0. Invariant probabilities give each transition point the same mass, necessarily 0, so M(T) is exactly the segment between the two endpoint Dirac measures. Its roof-one suspension has two periodic-orbit invariant measures and the same obstruction. The forbidden-word condition also realizes X as a locally maximal subsystem of a horseshoe, so the defect propagates to the geometric basic-set statements, not just the abstract layer.

At the pin, `Mathlib/Dynamics/Transitive.lean`:31–35 quantifies over an arbitrary acting monoid element; selecting ℤ really permits negative iterates. BowenPeriodicPoints1971 lines 74–83 assumes periodic density before its spectral theorem. BowenRuelle1975 lines 25–29 likewise retains periodic density independently of its dense-orbit convention. The source does not justify dropping that axiom under the roadmap's signed-time convention.

## R02 (P2) — Compactness of invariant measures needs a continuous map

**Location:** D:373–378; P:766–804, target 3.1. Quote: “On a compact metric space ... M(T) ... is compact”.

**Claim:** The second API bullet only has the standing hypothesis that T is measurable. The plan's first API also claims nonemptiness on compact Hausdorff spaces without continuity or nonemptiness of X.

**Witness:** On X={0}∪{1/n:n≥1}, set T(0)=1 and T(1/n)=1/n. Every δ_(1/n) is invariant; δ_(1/n)→δ₀, but T_*δ₀=δ₁≠δ₀. Thus M(T) is not closed or compact.

**Exact repair:** Keep the measurable-space definition and convexity general. Require continuous T and the Borel structure for the compactness clause. Put continuous T and nonempty compact Hausdorff X explicitly in P's existence clause, matching D:377 and native Krylov–Bogolyubov. Add the displayed discontinuous-map negative control and the empty-space existence control, and carry the definition's existing plan locator, BowenRuelle1975 lines 157–159, into the README.

**Retest:** If T(0)=0 instead, T=id, M(T)=all probability measures and is compact. In the original example the continuous observable u(x)=x has integrals tending from 1/n to 0, while its integral after pushing δ₀ forward is 1: testing invariance against continuous observables does not work without continuity of u∘T. For the existence clause, T(0)=1 and T(1/n)=1/(n+1) has no invariant probability: invariance successively forces the mass of every point to be 0. On ∅ there is also no probability measure. The corrected hypotheses exclude both.

## R03 (P2) — The SRB headline omits transitivity and the basin restriction

**Location:** D:3, summary. Quote: “SRB measure of a C² Anosov flow (or hyperbolic attractor) ... whose generic points have full Lebesgue measure”.

**Claim:** The summary promises one full-volume physical measure for an arbitrary C² Anosov flow, and does not restrict full measure to an attractor's basin.

**Witness:** Take the disjoint union of two identical cat-map suspensions, each of normalized volume 1/2. Each component has its own SRB probability and basin of volume 1/2; no probability has a full-volume generic set.

**Exact repair:** Match §11.5: say “a transitive Anosov flow generated by a C² vector field, or a basic hyperbolic attractor with generic points of full volume in its basin”. Retain the smooth-metric and regularity conventions. This preserves the general theorem and does not incorrectly impose transitivity on the definitions of Anosov or hyperbolic sets.

**Retest:** On either component alone its SRB generic set has relative volume 1. On the union the observable equal to 0 on the first component and 1 on the second has orbit averages 0 and 1, respectively. A single measure cannot have both integrals. D:1225 and BowenRuelle1975 Theorem 5.1, lines 421–469, already give the correctly restricted statement.

## R04 (P2) — The volume-pressure proof uses the wrong covering radius

**Location:** D:1213, §11.4, against the separation convention D:23. Quote: “B_Λ(ε, T) ⊆ ∪_{x∈E} B_x(δ + ε, T)”.

**Claim:** A maximal set with pairwise disjoint open δ-Bowen balls supplies a δ-spanning set. This is the classical pairwise-distance separation implication, not the native disjoint-ball implication used here.

**Witness:** Near an attracting periodic circle, take equally spaced centres with gap 3.9δ and ε=1.01δ. Their δ-balls form a maximal disjoint family. A midpoint displaced normally by ε is 2.196042δ from every nearest centre, exceeding δ+ε=2.01δ.

**Exact repair:** For the native separation convention use the 2δ-spanning conclusion and replace the right-hand radius by 2δ+ε (or explicitly introduce and compare a classical distance-separated set). Carry the factor through the volume constants and choose scales sufficiently small. The exponential growth limit is unchanged. Add this as a convention Check and inspect other maximal-separated-set-to-cover arguments for the same factor.

**Retest and concrete model:** On the flat two-torus with coordinates (θ,r) modulo 1, use the smooth vector field θ′=1, r′=−sin(2πr)/(2π). Λ={r=0} is a hyperbolic attracting periodic orbit with stable multiplier e^(−t). For any sufficiently large integer m, put δ=1/(3.9m), ε=1.01δ, and E={(j/m,0):0≤j<m}. Restricted to Λ, d_T is the circle distance. The δ-balls are disjoint, and every possible extra centre is at distance at most 1.95δ<2δ from an existing centre, so the family is maximal. At y=(1/(2m),ε), radial distance decreases, hence y∈B_Λ(ε,T) for every T>0. At time 0 its nearest-centre distance is √(1.95²+1.01²)δ=2.19604189δ, refuting the displayed inclusion. The corrected radius 3.01δ contains it. Maximality generally supplies an intersection of two δ-balls and therefore a distance <2δ. BowenRuelle1975 lines 386–390 uses the other separation convention; this is a roadmap convention mismatch, not an error in the source.

## R05 (P3) — The zero-iterate Check is false on nonprecompact uniform spaces

**Location:** D:161, §1.3, whose theorem allows a uniformly continuous map of any uniform space. Quote: “For k = 0 the identity has entropy 0 ... on a nonempty space”.

**Claim:** The native whole-space entropy of the identity is 0 whenever the space is nonempty.

**Witness:** For id on ℤ with its discrete uniformity, the diagonal entourage has coverMincard(n)=∞ for every n≥1. Thus coverEntropy(id,univ)=∞, whereas 0·coverEntropy(id,univ)=0 in EReal.

**Exact repair:** Restrict this Check to nonempty compact spaces, or more generally nonempty totally bounded uniform spaces, and add the discrete ℤ non-example. Keep the main power theorem's k≥1 and its full uniform-space scope.

**Retest:** On an m-point discrete space coverMincard(n)=m for n≥1, giving lim log(m)/n=0. On infinite discrete ℤ the cardinal stays ∞ and the growth rate stays ∞. The definitions are `Mathlib/Dynamics/TopologicalEntropy/CoverEntropy.lean`:212,338,434 and `Mathlib/Analysis/Asymptotics/ExpGrowth.lean`:41. The convention 0·∞=0 is correct; its asserted identification with h(id) is not general.
