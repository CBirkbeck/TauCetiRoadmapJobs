## C01 — "It is not a complete intersection at `x`" (4.9) is contradicted, as a statement about the scheme, by the toy datum

In the paragraph after the statement of 4.9 (lines 248–250) the README says of `Spec(A/I ⊗ E)`: "it is not a complete intersection at `x`, where the negative-zero locus … has excess dimension". In the toy datum of the first Check, `πⁿ − X` is a unit at `x`, `I` is generated by `b` there, and the local ring is that of the smooth plane `b = 0` (the second Check says so itself, lines 310–311). A regular local ring is a complete intersection, so the sentence is false as a property of the scheme and true only of the presentation: two equations in three coordinates cut out a surface. A reader comparing with the Wiles–Lenstra setting will take the first reading.

The smallest change: "the equations do not form a regular sequence at `x`, where the negative-zero locus … has dimension larger than the expected one".

## C02 — "Point derivation of weight `j`" in 4.8 (3) is never defined, and the bound is false for the weaker reading

The derivation bound (line 183) asks that the values of `D` be reductions of "`O`-valued point derivations of weight `j` at `x`". The term is not defined in 4.8 or in the Conventions. The Route needs it to mean that the derivation vanishes on `A_i` for `i ≠ j`: only then is `D(m)` determined by its restriction to `A_j`, so that `M` embeds in `Hom_O(cot_j(x)/span F, O/c)`. Without that clause the statement fails: `A = O[b]` with `b` of weight `1`, `x(b) = 0`, `j = 2`, `F = ∅`, `M = O/c`, `D(m) = m·(∂/∂b at 0) mod c` gives `length M = v(c) > 0 = cotLen_2(∅)`, since `A_2 = O·b² ⊆ (ker x)²`.

One clause at first use repairs it: "an `O`-linear map `d : A → O` with `d(fg) = x(f)d(g) + x(g)d(f)` that vanishes on `A_i` for `i ≠ j`".

## C03 — The free case of 3.17 (1) is already in the pinned Tau Ceti and is not cited

The Route of 3.17 says "for `M` free over `O` the first equality is Smith normal form" (line 126) and lists Mathlib's Smith normal form among its needs (lines 131, 748). The pinned Tau Ceti proves exactly this step: `Matrix.length_quotient_range_mulVecLin_eq_ord_det` (`TauCeti/LinearAlgebra/Matrix/SmithNormalForm/Length.lean`, line 58) states, for a square matrix with nonzero determinant over a principal ideal domain, that the length of the cokernel is `Ring.ord` of the determinant. The target stays: it allows torsion in `M`, subtracts the kernel and takes the determinant on `M ⊗ E`, so the library statement is a special case and does not close it. An implementer should be pointed at it.

Name the declaration in the *Needs* line of 3.17 and under a `### From Tau Ceti` entry of the supplier contracts.

## C05 — Topological finite generation of `Ph` in 6.13 (1) is used nowhere

The limit-group clause (line 528) assumes `Ph` topologically finitely generated. The Route uses only that each `Ph/V` is finite (finitely many subgroups, so one occurs for `𝒰`-almost all `N`) and that `Ph` is compact (the intersection argument, and the existence of `lim_𝒰`); the commutation argument uses the `ℓ`-part of `|Ph/V|` and nothing more. So existence, uniqueness and commutation hold for every profinite `Ph`. The Conventions allow restrictions carried over from the formal proof, but only the commutation clause is marked as its form. Either mark the hypothesis as belonging to that form, or state beside it that the Route gives the clause for any profinite `Ph`.

## N01 — "Limit group" is a standard term with another meaning

In geometric group theory, and in FreeGroupsAndLimitGroups on main, a limit group is a finitely generated fully residually free group. Here the heading of 6.13 and its clause (1) use the term for `G_∞ = Ph/K_∞` without defining it. A defining clause at first use ("the limit group `G_∞` of the surjections `π_N` along `𝒰`") would prevent the confusion.
