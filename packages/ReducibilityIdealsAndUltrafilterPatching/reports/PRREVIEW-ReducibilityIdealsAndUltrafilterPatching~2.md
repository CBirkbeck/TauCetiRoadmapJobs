## R01 (P2) — Patching up to an element (6.13 (2)) is statable at the pins, but `Suggested.lean` calls it unstatable and leaves it out

The acceptance test leaves out one of the four headlines it could carry, so the roadmap's patching theorem has no formal target. The module docstring (`Suggested.lean`, lines 24–33) says Mathlib lacks quasi-regular sequences and that weakly regular sequences "are not the notion stated". In 6.13 (2) they coincide, however. 𝔹 is Noetherian and complete for the ideal generated by the σ, so that ideal lies in the Jacobson radical of 𝔹, and 𝕄 is finite over 𝔹. The proof of Stacks Lemma 10.69.6 (tag 061S) then gives quasi-regular ⇒ regular. The local Krull intersection theorem it uses is replaced by Stacks Lemma 10.51.5 (2) (tag 00IQ), which covers an ideal in the Jacobson radical. For 𝕄 ≠ 0, Nakayama makes `𝕄/(σ)𝕄 ≠ 0`, so weakly regular is regular there, and Lemma 10.69.2 (tag 00LN) gives the converse. When 𝕄 = 0 both conditions hold trivially. So `RingTheory.Sequence.IsWeaklyRegular` (Mathlib `RingTheory/Regular/RegularSequence.lean:136`) states exactly what the README states. Every other carrier exists at the pin, including the ring `AdicCompletion` (`RingTheory/AdicCompletion/Algebra.lean:97`, with its algebra structure at line 103). The statement below, after `open IsLocalRing`, elaborates against the pinned Mathlib with `sorry` as its only warning.

The smallest fix: add the statement to `Suggested.lean` under a name of your choice, name that declaration in 6.13, and add one clause to 6.13 (2) saying that there quasi-regularity is equivalent to `σ₁, …, σ_s` being a weakly regular sequence on 𝕄. Then remove the second bullet from the docstring's list of headlines that cannot be stated.

## C01 — The free profinite group on a finite set is ProfiniteProPGroups Layer 4, not Layer 3

A reader who follows the citation in 6.13 (1) to ProfiniteProPGroups Layer 3 finds pro-`p` groups and Frattini theory instead. `freeProfiniteGroup X` and its universal property are in Layer 4 (`Completed/ProfiniteProPGroups/README.md`, lines 706, 743 and 792–795), and the pinned Tau Ceti realises them as `TauCeti.freeProfiniteGroup` and `TauCeti.freeProfiniteGroup.lift` (`TauCeti/Topology/Algebra/Group/Profinite/Free/Basic.lean:124`). The contract could also name the two Tau Ceti declarations.

## C02 — The remark that the derivation bound fails for additive `D` "for infinite `k`" is too general

The remark is in the route of 4.8 (3), line 177. Its witness, that `k²` embeds additively in `k`, works exactly when `k` has infinite dimension over its prime field, for example every infinite `k` of characteristic `p`. It does not work for every infinite `k`. Take `O = ℚ[[π]]`, which 4.8 allows since `E` has characteristic `0`, so that `k = ℚ`. Additive maps between `O`-modules are then `ℚ`-linear, and an `O`-module of finite length has length equal to its `ℚ`-dimension. So an additive injection still gives `length_O M ≤ cotLen_j(F)`. Writing "for infinite `k` of characteristic `p`" (or "of infinite dimension over its prime field") would make the remark true.

## N01 — Mathlib already has Fulton's order function as `Ring.ord`

Mathlib defines `Ring.ord R x = Module.length R (R ⧸ Ideal.span {x})` (`RingTheory/OrderOfVanishing/Basic.lean:37`) and proves `Ring.ord_eq_addVal` for a DVR (`OrderOfVanishing/Noetherian.lean:89`). The `v(d) = length_O(O/dO)` of 3.17 and the last term of `length_quot_eq_length_ker_add_valuation_det` are `Ring.ord O d`. Naming it in the Mathlib contract, and in the Lean statement if you wish, would tie θ to the library's notion.
