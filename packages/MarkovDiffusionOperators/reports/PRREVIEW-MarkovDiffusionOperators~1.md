**Summary.** Changes are needed, but none of them is a false statement: every identity, Check and worked example I recomputed is correct, and `Suggested.lean` elaborates with `sorry` as its only warning. The four findings are a headline theorem (Brascamp–Lieb) that is statable at the pins and missing from `Suggested.lean`, two sets of objects that another roadmap already owns and the Scope section does not name (the semigroup of `Δ − ∇V·∇` in OptimalTransport §11.9 on main; the Bakry–Émery inequalities and the Ornstein–Uhlenbeck formulas in two open pull requests), and a scope subsection that plans future roadmaps by the chapters of the main source and lists its sections in order. ## R01 (P2) — The Brascamp–Lieb inequality is a headline theorem, is statable at the pins, and is not in `Suggested.lean`

The Summary lists six headline theorems; the sixth is `Var_{μ_V}(f) ≤ ∫ ⟨(∇²V)⁻¹ ∇f, ∇f⟩ dμ_V` for smooth `V` with positive-definite Hessian and `e^{−V}` integrable (README line 11, Layer 11 line 249). The Lean file states nothing of Layer 11, and its module docstring (lines 10–21) does not say which headline theorems are not statable or what each lacks; it only says that the local Bobkov inequalities need the semigroup. An implementer therefore has no acceptance test for Layer 11 and cannot tell an omission from an obstruction.

It is statable with what the file already has. On a copy of the file I added the statement for smooth `f` with `MemLp f 2 (gibbsMeasure V)` and `MemLp (fun x => ‖fderiv ℝ f x‖) 2 (gibbsMeasure V)`, hypothesis `∀ x v, v ≠ 0 → 0 < fderiv ℝ (fderiv ℝ V) x v v`, left side `ENNReal.ofReal (variance f (gibbsMeasure V))`, right side the lower integral `∫⁻` of `ENNReal.ofReal` of `fderiv ℝ f x` applied to the inverse Hessian operator (`InnerProductSpace.toDual … .symm` composed with `fderiv ℝ (fderiv ℝ V) x`, then `ContinuousLinearMap.inverse`) of the gradient. It elaborates against the pinned Mathlib with `sorry` as the only warning.

The smallest change: add that theorem, and extend the docstring with one clause per headline that is not stated (the gradient bounds, mass conservation, the extension of `Γ₂` to `𝒟(L)`, and the Poincaré and logarithmic Sobolev inequalities of a full triple all need the semigroup and the full-triple structure, which are constructions of Layers 2–4). The Gibbs case of the fourth headline, `P(1/ρ)` for `∇²V ≥ ρ > 0` on compactly supported smooth `f`, is statable in the same way and would give the Poincaré headline a test too.

## R02 (P2) — The Markov semigroup generated by `Δ − ∇V·∇` already has an owner on main, which Scope does not name

Layer 9 (lines 212–218) builds, for smooth `V` with `e^{−V}` integrable, the semigroup `P_t` of `L = Δ − ∇V·∇` on `L²(μ_V)` and proves that it is a full triple. OptimalTransport §11.9 on main (`TauCetiRoadmap/OptimalTransport/README.md`, lines 3394–3407) already has the target "Construct here the Markov semigroup `(P_t)` on functions generated by `L = Δ − ∇V·∇`", for `V ∈ C²` with `∇²V ≥ λI`, as the dual of the Fokker–Planck flow, with the invariant law `Z⁻¹e^{−V} dx` and the Ornstein–Uhlenbeck case. The Scope section here leaves "Fokker–Planck flows to OptimalTransport" (line 289) but does not mention that semigroup, so for smooth `V` with a Hessian bounded below and `Z < ∞` the library would get two constructions of one object and no statement that they agree. Both READMEs even carry the same Check, `V = (x² − 1)²` with growth `e^{4t}`.

The two are not the same construction and both are needed: this one is the symmetric `L²(μ_V)` realisation, with no Hessian bound; that one is the dual of the Wasserstein flow, with no integrability of `e^{−V}`. The smallest change is one sentence in Scope and one in the Gibbs item of Layer 9: name OptimalTransport §11.9 as the owner of the semigroup built from the Fokker–Planck flow, say in a clause how the semigroup here differs, and say who proves that the two agree on the common class (a target here, since OptimalTransport is earlier, or an explicit statement that the identification is not claimed).

## R03 (P2) — Three results of Layer 9 are owned by open roadmaps that Scope does not name

Layer 9's last Gibbs item (line 218) proves that for `∇²V ≥ ρ > 0` the measure `μ_V` satisfies the Poincaré and logarithmic Sobolev inequalities with constant `1/ρ`. LogConcaveFunctionalInequalities (pull request #2778) owns exactly this: its §2.3 target `logSobolevInequality_gibbsMeasure` and §2.4 (its `README.md`, line 288 and the ownership paragraph at line 518), proved by optimal transport; it puts "the `Γ₂` calculus" and the Brascamp–Lieb inequality outside its scope (line 581). Layer 9's Ornstein–Uhlenbeck item (line 210) states the Mehler formula and the Hermite eigenfunctions; OrnsteinUhlenbeckSemigroup (pull request #2775) owns both as headline theorems (its §§1.2 and 2.4). Neither roadmap is mentioned here, and neither mentions this one, so each result has two owners.

The README already handles this situation correctly for RandomMatrices §2.2 and GaussianIsoperimetryAndClusters Layer 8 ("recovered, not consumed", line 270). The smallest change is to do the same for these two: in Scope, name each roadmap with its sections, say that the Gibbs inequalities come out here as an instance of Layers 7–8 by a second proof (and on which class of test functions each roadmap states them: #2778 uses bounded `C¹` functions with bounded gradient, this one Tau Ceti's classes), and for the Ornstein–Uhlenbeck model keep as a target only the identification of the triple's `P_t` with the kernel semigroup, citing the Mehler formula and the Hermite eigenfunctions. Which roadmap is the owner is the authors' decision; neither is in the merge order yet.

## R04 (P2) — The subsection "Roadmap for a roadmap" arranges future roadmaps by the chapters of [BGL] and lists the book's sections in order

The project's rule is that a source's arrangement is never the plan and that a source is not summarised section by section, because the selection and arrangement of a book belong to its authors. Lines 300–319 do both, in the README's own words: "Every section of [BGL] is in this roadmap, in one row of the table, or owned by an existing roadmap"; the rows are keyed to "Chapters 4–5", "Chapter 6; §§7.1–7.4", "Chapter 9", "Chapters 1–2; Appendix B"; and six bullets introduced by "The sections of each row, in order" enumerate the topics of the sections with their numbers (for example eleven topics for §§6.1–6.11). The subsection also opens with "This subsection describes future roadmaps. Contributors should not work from it", which is material for later that no layer contains.

What the Scope section needs is narrower and is already there in part: which results in this roadmap's own subject are left out, and which existing roadmap owns each neighbouring object. The smallest change keeps those statements (the "It leaves to named owners" list, and the exclusions of line 297–298 said by mathematical object, such as tensorization, defective logarithmic Sobolev inequalities, the intrinsic distance, the case `n = 1`), and removes the six bullets and the columns that map future roadmaps to chapters. An extension of this roadmap is a Part II roadmap with its own README, organised by what its targets need.

I could not read [BGL] or its table of contents, so this finding rests on what the README says about its own table, not on a comparison with the book.

## C02 — `gibbs_isoperimetric` and `gibbs_enlargement` assume `A` closed, and hold for every set

For an arbitrary `A` with closure `Ā`, `Metric.thickening r A = Metric.thickening r Ā` and `μ_V(A) ≤ μ_V(Ā)`. If the two measures are equal, both statements for `A` are the statements for `Ā`. If `μ_V(A) < μ_V(Ā)`, then `μ_V(A_r) − μ_V(A) ≥ μ_V(Ā) − μ_V(A) > 0` for every `r > 0`, which gives the first statement, and the second follows from the closed case since `Φ(a) ≤ μ_V(A) ≤ μ_V(Ā)`. So `hA : IsClosed A` (lines 189, 201) is used by the route of Layer 10 but not needed by the statements, and GaussianIsoperimetryAndClusters states its Gaussian forms `gaussian_minkowskiContent_ge` and `borell_sudakov_tsirelson` for every set. The hypothesis could be dropped, with the closed case kept as the route; the README's "for closed sets" (line 15) would then change with it.
