## R01 (P3) — §2.5 step 2 asserts an integer `a` prime to `|G|` with two congruences, which the two congruences alone do not supply

**Location.** `TauCetiRoadmap/GlaubermanZStar/README.md` lines 656–657: "for `v ∈ O(C)` and `a` prime to `|G|` with `a ≡ 1 (mod 4)` and `a ≡ −1 (mod |O(C)|)`".

**Claim.** Such an `a` exists, and `ψᵢ(g^a) = ψᵢ(g)` for it by step 1.

**Witness.** `|G|` may have odd prime divisors `q ∤ |O(C)|` (in `SL(2, 3)` with `C = ⟨i⟩`, `O(C) = 1` and `q = 3`): the two congruences fix `a` only modulo `4 |O(C)|`, so `a = 1 + 4|O(C)|·j` may be divisible by `q` (`O(C) = 1`: `a = 9`, `q = 3`), and then `a` is not prime to `|G|` and step 1 does not apply to it. The step is correct once a third congruence `a ≡ 1 (mod q)` for each such `q` is added (the moduli are coprime, so CRT gives `a`), or once step 1 is stated as "`ψᵢ(g^a) = ψᵢ(g)` for every `a` prime to the order of `g`", which is what an integer-valued character satisfies and what `(u v)^a` needs (`orderOf (u v)` divides `4 |O(C)|`).

**Repair.** In step 2 write "`a ≡ 1 (mod 4)`, `a ≡ −1 (mod |O(C)|)` and `a ≡ 1` modulo every other prime dividing `|G|`, which exists by the Chinese remainder theorem" (Mathlib `Nat.chineseRemainder`), or restate the conclusion of step 1 as "`ψᵢ(g^a) = ψᵢ(g)` for every `a` prime to `orderOf g`" (lift `a` to `a'` prime to `|G|` with `a' ≡ a (mod orderOf g)` by the same CRT) and use `a` prime to `4 |O(C)|` in step 2. Tested on `SL(2, 3)`: with `q = 3` added, `a ≡ 1 (mod 12)` works.
