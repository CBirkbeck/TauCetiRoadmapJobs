## R01 (P2) — The one-dimensional form of Brauer's theorem is attributed to `TauCeti.ClassFunction.character_mem_indCharacterSpanInt_isElementary`, which gives only induction from irreducible characters of elementary subgroups; the monomial step that bridges them has no owner and no locator

**Location.** `TauCetiRoadmap/ColemanIntegration/README.md` line 96 ("Brauer's induction theorem (every character is a `ℤ`-combination of characters induced from one-dimensional characters of elementary subgroups) is Tau Ceti's `…character_mem_indCharacterSpanInt_isElementary` …; elementary groups are nilpotent, hence monomial, so each inducing character is itself induced from a one-dimensional character of a subgroup"); line 44 (the *From Tau Ceti* entry); L3.Fd.2(d) ("write `χ = Σ_i a_i Ind_(H_i)^G(χ_i)` with `H_i ⊆ G`, each `χ_i` one-dimensional") and `padicArtinL_brauer_indep` (line 2801, "with one-dimensional characters of subgroups of that finite group").

**Claim.** The pinned declaration supplies the decomposition of every character of `Gal(L/k)` into integer combinations of characters induced from one-dimensional characters, which L3.Fd.2(d) needs because the p-adic L-functions of (a)–(c) exist only for one-dimensional characters.

**Witness.** At the Tau Ceti pin `a91d3aa`, `TauCeti/RepresentationTheory/Induction/Brauer/Induction.lean:127` states `V.character ∈ indCharacterSpanInt k G (fun E ↦ IsElementary E)` for `[Field k] [Group G] [Finite G] [CharZero k] [IsAlgClosed k]`, and `TauCeti/RepresentationTheory/Induction/Spanning.lean:299` defines `indCharacterSpanInt` as the additive closure of characters induced from *irreducible* characters of the subgroups satisfying the predicate. Instance: `G = Q₈` is 2-elementary (`C₁ × Q₈`), so the theorem applied to its two-dimensional irreducible `ψ` returns the decomposition `ψ = Ind_{Q₈}^{Q₈} ψ`, whose inducing character has degree 2. The decomposition L3.Fd.2(d) uses, `ψ = Ind_{C₄}^{Q₈} λ` with `λ` a faithful linear character of `C₄`, is the monomial property of `Q₈`, which the pinned library does not state (no match for `monomial`, `supersolvable` or a nilpotent-group induction theorem under `TauCeti/RepresentationTheory`, and none in Mathlib's `RepresentationTheory` at `6b7abb3c`). RepresentationTheory/InductionRestriction Layer 6 on main states Brauer's theorem in the elementary-subgroup form only ("Every character is a `ℤ`-linear combination of characters induced from **elementary** subgroups") and has no monomial statement either. So the clause "elementary groups are nilpotent, hence monomial" is a theorem used by a proof route that no layer, supplier section or pinned declaration provides, and it carries no locator.

**Repair.** (1) At lines 44 and 96 credit the declaration with what it states: every character of a finite group over an algebraically closed field of characteristic zero is an integer combination of characters induced from irreducible characters of elementary subgroups. (2) In L3.Fd.2(d) add the bridging target, in prose, as this roadmap's own (no other roadmap owns it): "Prove that every irreducible character of an elementary group `E` is induced from a one-dimensional character of a subgroup of `E`: `E = C × P` is nilpotent, hence supersolvable, and supersolvable groups are monomial (Serre, *Linear Representations of Finite Groups*, Part II, §8.5, Theorem 16). With transitivity of induction this turns the supplied decomposition into one by one-dimensional characters of subgroups of `G`, the form `padicArtinL_brauer_indep` quantifies over." Add it to the *Needs:* line of (d) in place of the bare declaration name, and a Check: for `Q₈` the two-dimensional irreducible is `Ind_{C₄}^{Q₈} λ`, while the supplied decomposition alone leaves it as a degree-two inducing character. Tested on the instance: with the added target, `ψ = Ind_{C₄}^{Q₈} λ` and `Ind_{Q₈}^{G} Ind_{C₄}^{Q₈} = Ind_{C₄}^{G}` give a one-dimensional decomposition for every character of a group with a `Q₈` elementary subgroup, and the totally-real fixed-field argument of line 96 is unchanged (`C₄ ⊆ Q₈ ⊆ Gal(L/k)` has fixed field inside `L`).
