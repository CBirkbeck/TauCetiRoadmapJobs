The base roadmap lives at `TauCetiRoadmap/RepresentationTheory/LieGroups`. Title of the roadmap: **LieGroups, Part II: parabolic subgroups, Langlands decompositions and the
Bruhat decomposition of real reductive groups.** Folder `TauCetiRoadmap/LieGroupsPartII` (the
Part II of a `RepresentationTheory/` roadmap lives at top level, as `ClassicalGroupsPartII` does).

## Scope

The base roadmap, `TauCetiRoadmap/RepresentationTheory/LieGroups`, builds in its Layer 9 the
structure theory of a real reductive Lie group `G` up to the Cartan, Iwasawa and KAK decompositions:
the Cartan involution datum (`CartanInvolution`, `IsRealReductive`), the Iwasawa datum
(`IwasawaData`: `K = G^θ`, a maximal abelian `𝔞 ⊆ 𝔭`, `A = lieExp 𝔞`, a closed connected
exponential complement `N`, the closed positive chamber), `iwasawaDecomposition`,
`kakDecomposition`, `cartan_polar_decomposition`, and (in its README, pinned as fields of
`IwasawaData` at the head of pull request #1051) the possibly non-reduced restricted root system
`Σ = Σ(𝔤, 𝔞)`, the root-generated `N₊`, the restricted Weyl group `W = N_K(𝔞)/Z_K(𝔞)`, the
restricted reflections, `Ad(K)𝔞 = 𝔭` and the chambers. Layer 9 stops there on purpose ("structure
is retained; spherical/Harish-Chandra harmonic analysis is downstream work") and its README names
`RealReductiveGroups` (Layer 9) as a natural fracture. This Part II starts where Layer 9 stops and
owns the next level of the same structure theory:

1. **Conjugacy of the Iwasawa data.** Any two Cartan involution data of `G` are conjugate under
   `Ad(G)`; for a fixed `θ`, any two maximal abelian `𝔞 ⊆ 𝔭` are `Ad(K)`-conjugate and any two
   positive systems `Σ⁺` of `Σ(𝔤, 𝔞)` are conjugate under `W`; hence any two triples
   `(θ, 𝔞, Σ⁺)` are conjugate under `Ad(G)`. Where Layer 9 (at the head that lands) already pins a
   piece of this, Part II imports it by name and states only the composite.
2. **The minimal parabolic subgroup and its Langlands decomposition.** `M = Z_K(𝔞)`,
   `P = MAN` with `N = N₊ = lieExp 𝔫`, `𝔫 = ⨁_{β ∈ Σ⁺} 𝔤^β`: `P` is a closed subgroup with Lie
   algebra `𝔮_∅ = 𝔪 ⊕ 𝔞 ⊕ 𝔫`, `𝔪 = Z_𝔨(𝔞)`; `M` is compact, possibly disconnected, with Lie
   algebra `𝔪`; `M` centralises `A`, `MA = Z_G(𝔞)`, `MA` normalises `N`; `M × A × N → P` is a
   diffeomorphism; `P = N_G(P) = N_G(𝔫)`, `P ∩ θ(P) = MA`, `G = KP` and `G/P ≅ K/M` is compact.
   Minimal parabolic subgroups are the conjugates of `P` and form one conjugacy class.
   **Ownership note.** `AutomorphicFormsOnReductiveGroups` §2.3a (pull request #1037, a draft)
   currently plans exactly this item, including the Borel–Tits comparison of item 6, as its own
   target. That roadmap is a consumer of the structure theory (it induces from `P = MAN` in §2.3
   and from general parabolics `P = MAN` it never defines), and it sits at line 63 of the merge
   order, after every structural supplier. A Part II that imported `P` from §2.3a while supplying
   the general parabolics §2.3 induces from would close a cycle; the single-direction-of-supply
   principle resolves it by moving ownership to the supplier side. Part II owns items 2 and 6;
   `AutomorphicFormsOnReductiveGroups` §2.3a becomes an import of them (a note for that roadmap's
   author; the design must cite §2.3a's statements as the consumer contract it has to satisfy,
   including the `SL₂(ℝ)`, `GL_n(ℝ)`, `SO₀(n,1)`, compact and `ℝ^×` checks recorded there).
3. **The Bruhat decomposition of `G`.** `(G, P, N_G(𝔞), S)` is a Tits system with Weyl group
   `N_G(𝔞)/Z_G(𝔞) ≅ N_K(𝔞)/Z_K(𝔞) = W` and `S` the simple restricted reflections, so
   `G = ⨆_{w ∈ W} P w P`; the abstract Bruhat existence and uniqueness is Tau Ceti's
   `TauCeti.TitsSystem` with `TauCeti.TitsSystem.existsUnique_mem_bruhatCell` and
   `TauCeti.TitsSystem.disjoint_bruhatCell_iff` and is consumed, not re-proved. Part II owns the
   verification of the Tits axioms for `(G, P, N_G(𝔞))` (the rank-one computation in the
   centraliser of a root hyperplane) and the identification of the cells with the big-cell
   coordinates `N^{w} × M A × N`. This is the real-group analogue of the algebraic Bruhat theorem
   that `ReductiveGroups` Layer 7 owns for `𝐇(k)`; it is not that theorem and is not imported from
   it, because the real group `G` of Layer 9 is not assumed to be the real points of an algebraic
   group.
4. **Standard parabolic subalgebras and subgroups.** For `Π' ⊆ Π`, `Σ_{Π'} = Σ ∩ Span(Π')`,
   `𝔮_{Π'} = 𝔪 ⊕ 𝔞 ⊕ ⨁_{β ∈ Σ⁺ ∪ Σ_{Π'}} 𝔤^β`, `Q_{Π'} = N_G(𝔮_{Π'})`. Theorems: `Π' ↦ 𝔮_{Π'}`
   is an inclusion-preserving bijection onto the subalgebras containing `𝔮_∅`; `Q_{Π'}` is closed
   with Lie algebra `𝔮_{Π'}`, `Q_{Π'} = ⨆_{w ∈ W_{Π'}} P w P` with `W_{Π'}` the subgroup
   generated by the simple reflections in `Π'`; every subgroup of `G` containing `P` is one of the
   `Q_{Π'}` (hence closed, and `Π' ↦ Q_{Π'}` is an inclusion-preserving bijection onto the subgroups
   containing `P`); `Q_{Π'} = N_G(Q_{Π'})`; the double cosets `Q_{Π'} \ G / Q_{Π''}` are in
   bijection with `W_{Π'} \ W / W_{Π''}`. The Tits-system part of this (standard parabolic
   subgroups `P_J = B W_J B` of a BN-pair and the classification of subgroups containing `B`) is not
   in the pinned Tau Ceti nor in any roadmap: Part II owns it as an abstract supplement to
   `TauCeti.TitsSystem`, stated for an arbitrary Tits system so that `ReductiveGroups` and the
   nonarchimedean roadmaps can reuse it.
5. **Langlands decompositions of the standard parabolics.** `𝔞_{Π'} = ⋂_{α ∈ Π'} ker α`,
   `A_{Π'} = lieExp 𝔞_{Π'}`, `𝔫_{Π'} = ⨁_{β ∈ Σ⁺ ∖ Σ_{Π'}} 𝔤^β`, `N_{Π'} = lieExp 𝔫_{Π'}`,
   `L_{Π'} = Z_G(𝔞_{Π'})`, `M_{Π'}` the subgroup of `L_{Π'}` on which the characters trivial on
   `𝔞_{Π'}` vanish (equivalently `M_{Π'} = ⁰L_{Π'}`, the kernel of the Harish-Chandra map of
   `L_{Π'}`). Theorems: `L_{Π'} = M_{Π'} × A_{Π'}` with `M_{Π'}` a real reductive group of the same
   class (its Cartan involution `θ|_{M_{Π'}}`, maximal compact `M_{Π'} ∩ K`), `L_{Π'} = Q_{Π'} ∩
   θ(Q_{Π'})`, `N_{Π'}` is the nilradical of `Q_{Π'}` (closed, simply connected, nilpotent,
   normal in `Q_{Π'}`), `M_{Π'} × A_{Π'} × N_{Π'} → Q_{Π'}` is a diffeomorphism, and
   `Q_{Π'} = L_{Π'} ⋉ N_{Π'}`. The Iwasawa data of `M_{Π'}` are `(𝔞 ∩ 𝔪_{Π'}, Σ_{Π'}⁺)`, and its
   minimal parabolic is `P ∩ M_{Π'}`.
6. **Parabolic subgroups and subalgebras in general, and the comparison with algebraic groups.**
   A parabolic subgroup is a subgroup containing a minimal parabolic subgroup; equivalently a
   conjugate of some `Q_{Π'}`, with `Π'` uniquely determined (the type); parabolic subalgebras are
   the `Ad(G)`-conjugates of the `𝔮_{Π'}`, equivalently the real subalgebras whose
   complexification contains a Borel subalgebra of `𝔤_ℂ`; `Q ↦ Lie(Q)` and `𝔮 ↦ N_G(𝔮)` are inverse
   bijections between parabolic subgroups and parabolic subalgebras. The dynamical description:
   for `X ∈ 𝔞` with `α(X) ≥ 0` for all `α ∈ Σ⁺` and `Π' = {α ∈ Π : α(X) = 0}`,
   `Q_{Π'} = {g ∈ G : t ↦ exp(−tX) g exp(tX) is bounded as t → +∞}` and `𝔮_{Π'}` is the sum of
   the non-negative eigenspaces of `ad X`. The comparison: for `𝐇` a connected reductive group
   over `ℝ`, realised as a transpose-stable subgroup of `GL_n` with `θ(g) = (gᵀ)⁻¹` (such a
   realisation exists: `ArithmeticLocallySymmetricSpaces` ALS.0.1–0.2 at pull request #871), and
   `G = 𝐇(ℝ)` or `𝐇(ℝ)⁰`, the maximal `ℝ`-split torus `𝐒` is the Zariski closure of `A`, `𝔞 =
   Lie(𝐒)(ℝ)`, `Σ = Φ(𝐇, 𝐒)` (differentials of the relative roots), `W` is the relative Weyl
   group, the minimal parabolic is `P = G ∩ 𝐏(ℝ)` for a minimal `ℝ`-parabolic `𝐏`, `N = R_u(𝐏)(ℝ)`,
   `MA = G ∩ Z_𝐇(𝐒)(ℝ)`, and the standard `Q_{Π'}` are `G ∩ 𝐏_𝐇(λ)(ℝ)` for cocharacters `λ` of
   `𝐒` with `dλ(1) = X` as above, with Levi `G ∩ Z_𝐇(λ)(ℝ)` and unipotent radical
   `U_𝐇(λ)(ℝ)`; the algebraic side is `ReductiveGroups` Layer 7 (parabolic `k`-subgroups,
   `P = P_𝐇(λ)`, `U_𝐇(λ)`, `Z_𝐇(λ)`, the relative root system and Bruhat decomposition over `k`)
   and Tau Ceti's `TauCeti.Cocharacter.parabolic`; the bridge is `borel-tits1965` §14.7
   (lines 3775–3821, pp. 147–148), stated there for semisimple `𝐇` and extended to reductive `𝐇`
   by the argument `AutomorphicFormsOnReductiveGroups` §2.3a records (the `𝔭`-part of the centre
   lies in `𝔞`).

**Generality.** The filed wording is "connected semisimple with finite centre, extended to the
reductive case". The library's carrier is Layer 9's real reductive datum: a Lie group `G`, possibly
disconnected, with a smooth involution `θ` whose fixed group is compact and whose Lie algebra is
reductive. Part II is stated on that carrier. Knapp's "reductive Lie group" class adds the axioms
that `G` has finitely many components, that `Ad(G)` lies in `Int(𝔤_ℂ)` (so that the `M`'s behave)
and that the Cartan involution is the restriction of a global one; the design must decide which of
these it needs beyond Layer 9's datum (item 4's "every subgroup containing `P` is closed" and the
`M_{Π'}` of item 5 are where `Ad(G) ⊆ Int(𝔤_ℂ)` is used) and state them as hypotheses of the
affected theorems, never by shrinking to the connected semisimple case. The connected semisimple
finite-centre case, `GL_n(ℝ)` (disconnected, `M` the diagonal sign matrices meeting every
component), `SL₂(ℝ)`, `SO₀(n,1)`, `Sp_{2n}(ℝ)`, compact `G` (`P = G`) and `ℝ^×` are Checks, as is
the non-example of the Euclidean motion group (Cartan-type involution with compact fixed group but
a non-reductive Lie algebra).

**Boundaries.** Part II does not own: harmonic analysis or representation theory on `G`
(principal series, normalised induction, Casselman embedding: `AutomorphicFormsOnReductiveGroups`
§2.3 and later); `θ`-stable parabolic subalgebras `𝔮 = 𝔩 ⊕ 𝔲` of `𝔤_ℂ` and cohomological
induction (`AutomorphicFormsOnReductiveGroups` §3, Vogan–Zuckerman); Cartan subgroups and the
classification of Cartan subalgebras up to conjugacy; the algebraic parabolics over an arbitrary
field (`ReductiveGroups` Layer 7, main) and the rational parabolics, horospherical decompositions
and Borel–Serre compactification (`ArithmeticLocallySymmetricSpaces` ALS.2.1–2.2, pull request
#871, with `AdelicAlgebraicGroups` §3.1 at pull request #792); the nonarchimedean Iwasawa,
Cartan and Iwahori–Bruhat decompositions (`ReductiveGroupsPartII` RG2.4, main); the complex
Bruhat decomposition and Schubert cells of `G_ℂ/B` (`LieGroups` Layer 8 and `ReductiveGroups`
Layer 7); and the Cartan, Iwasawa and KAK decompositions themselves with the restricted root
system and `W` (`LieGroups` Layer 9, imported).

## Final targets

Throughout, `G` is a real reductive Lie group in the sense of `LieGroups` Layer 9 (a
`CartanInvolution` datum `θ`, `K = G^θ`, `𝔤 = 𝔨 ⊕ 𝔭`), with an Iwasawa datum (`𝔞 ⊆ 𝔭` maximal
abelian, `Σ = Σ(𝔤, 𝔞)`, a positive system `Σ⁺` with simple roots `Π`, `𝔫`, `A`, `N`, `M = Z_K(𝔞)`,
`W = N_K(𝔞)/Z_K(𝔞)`), plus whichever of Knapp's reductive-group axioms the design records as needed
(see Generality). Locators into paywalled books are section-level and `uncertified`.

1. **Conjugacy of Iwasawa data.** Any two Cartan involution data of `G` are `Ad(G)`-conjugate; for
   fixed `θ`, any two maximal abelian subspaces of `𝔭` are `Ad(K)`-conjugate and `W` acts simply
   transitively on the positive systems of `Σ`; so any two triples `(θ, 𝔞, Σ⁺)` are
   `Ad(G)`-conjugate, and the minimal parabolic subgroups of `G` form a single conjugacy class.
   (`knapp2002` Ch. VI §§2, 4–5. Comparison target: `IsCartanInvolution.conj` of
   `ArithmeticLocallySymmetricSpaces` ALS.0.1 for `G = 𝐇(ℝ)`.)
2. **Minimal parabolic subgroup and Langlands decomposition.** `P = MAN` is a closed subgroup with
   Lie algebra `𝔪 ⊕ 𝔞 ⊕ 𝔫`; `M` is compact with Lie algebra `𝔪 = Z_𝔨(𝔞)`; `MA = Z_G(𝔞) ≅ M × A`;
   `MA` normalises `N`; `M × A × N → P` is a diffeomorphism; `P = N_G(P) = N_G(𝔫)`;
   `P ∩ θ(P) = MA`; `G = KP` and `G/P ≅ K/M` is compact. (`knapp2002` Ch. VII §§2, 5 and the
   Langlands decomposition in §7; `warner1972` Ch. 1 §1.2; consumer contract:
   `AutomorphicFormsOnReductiveGroups` §2.3a.)
3. **Tits system and Bruhat decomposition.** `(G, P, N_G(𝔞), S)` is a `TauCeti.TitsSystem` with
   Weyl group `N_G(𝔞)/Z_G(𝔞) ≅ W` and `S` the simple restricted reflections; consequently
   `G = ⨆_{w ∈ W} P w P` (existence and uniqueness of the cell from
   `TauCeti.TitsSystem.existsUnique_mem_bruhatCell`), and the big cell `P w₀ P` is open and dense
   with `N^{−} × M × A × N → P w₀ P` a diffeomorphism. (`knapp2002` Ch. VII §4; `warner1972`
   Ch. 1 §1.2; abstract Tits-system theory `bourbaki-lie4-6` Ch. IV §2.)
4. **Standard parabolic subalgebras.** `Π' ↦ 𝔮_{Π'}` is an inclusion-preserving bijection from
   the subsets of `Π` onto the Lie subalgebras of `𝔤` containing `𝔪 ⊕ 𝔞 ⊕ 𝔫`; `𝔮_{Π'}` is the sum
   of the non-negative `ad X`-eigenspaces for any `X ∈ 𝔞` with `α(X) ≥ 0` on `Σ⁺` and
   `Π' = {α ∈ Π : α(X) = 0}`. (`knapp2002` Ch. VII §7, the proposition the two 2022 Annals
   papers cite as Proposition 7.76: `brown-fisher-hurtado2022` lines 511–521,
   `brown-rodriguezhertz-wang2022` lines 132–144.)
5. **Standard parabolic subgroups.** For a Tits system `(G, B, N, S)` and `J ⊆ S`, `P_J = B W_J B`
   is a subgroup, the `P_J` are exactly the subgroups containing `B`, `J ↦ P_J` is an
   inclusion-preserving bijection, `P_J` is self-normalising, and `P_J \ G / P_{J'} ≅ W_J \ W / W_{J'}`
   (stated for an arbitrary `TauCeti.TitsSystem`: `bourbaki-lie4-6` Ch. IV §2 nos. 5–6; the
   algebraic instance is `borel-tits1965` 5.17–5.20, lines 1924–1955, pp. 101–102). Applied to
   item 3: `Q_{Π'} := N_G(𝔮_{Π'})` equals `⨆_{w ∈ W_{Π'}} P w P`, is closed with Lie algebra
   `𝔮_{Π'}`, every subgroup containing `P` is some `Q_{Π'}`, and `Π' ↦ Q_{Π'}` is an
   inclusion-preserving bijection onto the subgroups containing `P`. (`knapp2002` Ch. VII §7;
   conventions `brown-rodriguezhertz-wang2022` lines 152–157.)
6. **Langlands decomposition of `Q_{Π'}`.** With `𝔞_{Π'} = ⋂_{α ∈ Π'} ker α`, `A_{Π'} = lieExp
   𝔞_{Π'}`, `N_{Π'} = lieExp 𝔫_{Π'}`, `L_{Π'} = Z_G(𝔞_{Π'}) = Q_{Π'} ∩ θ(Q_{Π'})` and `M_{Π'} = ⁰L_{Π'}`:
   `L_{Π'} = M_{Π'} × A_{Π'}`; `M_{Π'}` is real reductive of the same class with Cartan
   involution `θ|_{M_{Π'}}`, maximal compact `M_{Π'} ∩ K`, Iwasawa data `(𝔞 ∩ 𝔪_{Π'}, Σ_{Π'}⁺)` and
   minimal parabolic `P ∩ M_{Π'}`; `N_{Π'}` is closed, simply connected, nilpotent and normal in
   `Q_{Π'}`; `M_{Π'} × A_{Π'} × N_{Π'} → Q_{Π'}` is a diffeomorphism and `Q_{Π'} = L_{Π'} ⋉ N_{Π'}`.
   (`knapp2002` Ch. VII §7; `wallach1988` Ch. 2 §2.2; `warner1972` Ch. 1 §1.2.)
7. **Parabolic subgroups and subalgebras.** A subgroup containing a minimal parabolic subgroup is
   conjugate to exactly one `Q_{Π'}`; parabolic subalgebras (conjugates of the `𝔮_{Π'}`) are
   exactly the real subalgebras `𝔮` with `𝔮_ℂ` containing a Borel subalgebra of `𝔤_ℂ`;
   `Q ↦ Lie(Q)` and `𝔮 ↦ N_G(𝔮)` are inverse bijections between parabolic subgroups and parabolic
   subalgebras; for `X ∈ 𝔞` dominant with `Π' = {α(X) = 0}`,
   `Q_{Π'} = {g : exp(−tX) g exp(tX) bounded as t → +∞}`. (`knapp2002` Ch. VII §7; the
   boundedness description is the Lie-group form of `borel-tits1965` 4.15, line 1488, pp. 90–91,
   and of the dynamic parabolics `TauCeti.Cocharacter.parabolic`; the design must locate or prove
   the Lie-group statement, see Risks.)
8. **Comparison with `ℝ`-parabolic subgroups.** For `𝐇` connected reductive over `ℝ`,
   transpose-stable in `GL_n`, `θ(g) = (gᵀ)⁻¹`, and `G = 𝐇(ℝ)` or `𝐇(ℝ)⁰`: `𝐒 :=` Zariski closure
   of `A` is a maximal `ℝ`-split torus with `Lie(𝐒)(ℝ) = 𝔞`; `Σ = Φ(𝐇, 𝐒)`; `W` is the relative
   Weyl group; `P = G ∩ 𝐏(ℝ)` for a minimal `ℝ`-parabolic `𝐏 = 𝐏_𝐇(λ)` with `λ` a cocharacter of
   `𝐒` positive on `Σ⁺`, `N = R_u(𝐏)(ℝ)`, `A = 𝐒(ℝ)⁰`, `MA = G ∩ Z_𝐇(𝐒)(ℝ)`; and
   `Q_{Π'} = G ∩ 𝐏_𝐇(λ)(ℝ)`, `L_{Π'} = G ∩ Z_𝐇(λ)(ℝ)`, `N_{Π'} = U_𝐇(λ)(ℝ)` for `λ` with `dλ(1)` as
   in item 7. (`borel-tits1965` Théorème 4.15, line 1488, pp. 90–91; Corollaire 5.9, line 1802,
   p. 98; Théorème 5.15, line 1868, pp. 100–101; Corollaires 5.18–5.20, lines 1930–1955,
   pp. 101–102; §14.7, lines 3775–3821, pp. 147–148; algebraic side `ReductiveGroups` Layer 7.)

Checks the design should record: `SL₂(ℝ)` (`P` upper triangular, `W = C₂`, Bruhat `G = P ⊔ P w P`,
the only parabolics are `P`, `G` and conjugates); `GL_n(ℝ)` (`M` the `2^n` sign matrices, standard
parabolics the block upper triangular groups indexed by compositions of `n`, Levi the block
diagonal, `QR` as `G = KP`); `SL₃(ℝ)` (four standard parabolics, `W = S₃`, six Bruhat cells);
`SO₀(n,1)` (rank one, `Σ = {±λ}`, `N ≅ ℝ^{n−1}`, `G/P ≅ S^{n−1}`); `Sp₄(ℝ)` (`Σ` of type `C₂`,
Siegel and Klingen parabolics); `SU(n,1)` (non-reduced `Σ = BC₁`, `𝔫 = 𝔤_λ ⊕ 𝔤_{2λ}` Heisenberg);
`SL₂(ℂ)` as a real group (`M = U(1)`, showing `M` can be positive-dimensional); compact `G`
(`P = G`, `Σ = ∅`); `ℝ^×` (`P = G`, `𝔭 ≠ 0`); the Euclidean motion group as a non-example of the
datum.

## Risks

- **Pin drift in the base.** The main copy of `LieGroups` pins `IwasawaData` with a bare chamber
  and leaves the restricted root system README-only; the head of pull request #1051 adds
  `restrictedRoots` and `positiveRestrictedRoots` fields and the `N₊`/chamber inequalities. Part
  II's statements depend on which lands. The design must write against main's names and record
  the #1051 refinements as the expected interface, and its `Suggested.lean` (single `import
  Mathlib`, so it cannot import the base's `Suggested.lean`) must restate only the simple
  definitions it needs: a Cartan-involution datum as a smooth involution with compact fixed set, an
  Iwasawa datum as subgroups with the stated properties, and parabolics as subgroups containing
  `P`. The headline theorems to pin are targets 2, 3, 5 and 6; target 8 needs algebraic-group
  carriers that are themselves `sorry`-targets of `ReductiveGroups` and may have to be stated on
  `GL_n` matrix groups only.
- **Ownership move.** `AutomorphicFormsOnReductiveGroups` §2.3a plans targets 1, 2 and 8. The
  verdict moves that ownership to this Part II (the supplier side). This needs the worker or a
  human to tell that pull request's author; until then two documents claim the same theorems.
  The design must match §2.3a's statements and Checks exactly so that the later edit of #1037 is a
  replacement of text by citations.
- **Generality decision on the carrier.** Layer 9's datum (compact fixed group plus reductive Lie
  algebra) is weaker than Knapp's reductive-group axioms. The theorems "every subgroup containing
  `P` is closed", "`Q_{Π'} = N_G(𝔮_{Π'})`" and the structure of `M_{Π'}` use that `Ad(G)` acts on
  `𝔤_ℂ` by inner automorphisms, or at least that `G/G⁰` is finite and realised in `K`. Layer 9's
  datum forces `K` compact and `K` meets every component, so `G/G⁰` is finite; whether
  `Ad(G) ⊆ Int(𝔤_ℂ)` can be dropped from each theorem (for `GL_n(ℝ)` it holds, for a group
  containing an outer automorphism of `𝔤` as a component it fails for the structure of `M`) is
  for the design to settle theorem by theorem, and it must not retreat to connected semisimple `G`.
- **Paywalled main sources.** Knapp, Warner, Wallach and Bourbaki are cited by section only; only
  Borel–Tits and the three Annals preprints can be read. The Lie-group form of the dynamical
  description (target 7) and the proof route for target 5's "subgroups containing `P` are closed"
  come from these books; the design must supply its own proofs or state the route in its own words
  with section-level locators. If a human can name an open text (lecture notes by the author of
  one of the books, or a locally held copy), the citations sharpen.
- **Foundations far above the library.** Everything from `lieExp` upward is a `sorry`-target of
  the base; nothing in the pinned Mathlib or Tau Ceti is a real Lie-group structure theorem. Part
  II is therefore a statement-level roadmap for a long time; its value is the fixed interface the
  automorphic roadmaps already consume by name.
- **Non-reduced root systems.** `Σ` can be of type `BC_n`; Mathlib's `RootPairing` admits this,
  but the Weyl-group and base theory of `RootSystems` must be used in its non-reduced form, and
  the standard-parabolic bijection must be stated with `Span(Π')` intersected with `Σ`, not with
  "positive combinations of `Π'`", to catch `2α`.
