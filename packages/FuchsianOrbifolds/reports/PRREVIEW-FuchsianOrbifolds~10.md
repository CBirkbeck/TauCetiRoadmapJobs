## R02 (P3) — Layer 2 item 6 calls the triangle group "the reflection group"

- **Location.** README 547–549: "a hyperbolic triangle with the corresponding angles whose reflection group is cofinite and isomorphic to the abstract triangle group (`TauCeti.UpperHalfPlane.ConvexPolygon.triangleGroupEquiv`)".
- **Claim.** As written this is false.
  - The group generated by the reflections in the sides contains orientation-reversing maps, so it is not a subgroup of `PSL(2,ℝ)`, and `Subgroup.IsCofinite` cannot apply to it.
  - It is also not isomorphic to `Δ(p, q, r)`, which has index two in it.
  - The pinned theorem is about the orientation-preserving subgroup. No target depends on the sentence; the defect is a misdescription of a pinned declaration.
- **Witness.**
  - `TauCeti.UpperHalfPlane.ConvexPolygon.triangleGroup` (`UpperHalfPlane/Polygon/Convex/Triangle/Group.lean` 105–108) is the closure of products of two side reflections, the orientation-preserving subgroup.
  - Computed on `(2, 3, 7)`: the triangle has area `π − π(1/2 + 1/3 + 1/7) = π/42`, the coarea of the reflection group. `Δ(2,3,7)` has covolume `π/21` (Layer 5 item 7's Check).
  - Martin2013 lines 118–121 describe the same index-two relation.
- **Repair.** Replace the quoted clause with: "a hyperbolic triangle with the corresponding angles whose triangle group `TauCeti.UpperHalfPlane.ConvexPolygon.triangleGroup`, the orientation-preserving index-two subgroup of the group generated by the reflections in its sides, is cofinite and isomorphic to the abstract triangle group `Δ(p, q, r)`".
