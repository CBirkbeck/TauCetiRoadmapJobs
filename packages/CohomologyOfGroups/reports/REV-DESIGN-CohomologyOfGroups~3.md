1. **F01** (P2) The planned p-group classification needs primality

   **Location:** `packages/CohomologyOfGroups/design/plan.json:2008–2014`, target 8.2, particularly “`IsPGroup p P`”; compare `packages/CohomologyOfGroups/design/README.md:1560–1566`.

   **Claim:** The listed hypotheses imply that the group is cyclic when `p` is odd. The explicit list does not require `p.Prime`. Mathematical “p-group” terminology does not repair a Lean predicate which admits composite parameters.

   **Witness:** At `Mathlib/GroupTheory/PGroup.lean:45`, `IsPGroup p G` requires only elementwise annihilation by powers of `p`. For `p=21`, the nonabelian `C₇ ⋊ C₃` satisfies it and has only cyclic abelian subgroups.

   Here the generator of `C₃` acts on `C₇` by multiplication by 2. On pairs, multiplication is `(a,b)(c,d)=(a+2^b c,b+d)`, modulo 7 and 3. Enumeration gives element-order counts `{1:1, 3:14, 7:6}`; every element has 21st power 1. The subgroup-size counts are `{1:1, 3:7, 7:1, 21:1}`. Precisely the proper subgroups are abelian, and all are cyclic. But `(1,0)(0,1)=(1,1)` and `(0,1)(1,0)=(2,1)`, so the whole group is not cyclic. The source explicitly requires an odd prime: Loh19 line 2416, Corollary 1.6.19, printed p. 53.

   **Exact repair:** Add `[Fact p.Prime]` (or the explicit proposition `p.Prime`) to target 8.2's hypothesis list, and begin its README statement “For a prime `p`, …”. Add the order-21 example as a negative-control Check explaining why `IsPGroup p P` alone is insufficient. The counterexample is then excluded because 21 is composite, while the stated `Q₈`, elementary-abelian and odd-prime Heisenberg controls retain their intended meanings. Do not narrow the theorem to selected primes.
