## N02 — A Check of 1.2 on a group other than `S_3` would test the coset condition where the normal subgroup is not the obvious one

On `Q_8` with `Σ = {i, j}`: `Σ` generates `Q_8`, lies in no coset of `{±1}`, `⟨i⟩` or `⟨j⟩`, but lies in the coset `i⟨k⟩ = {±i, ±j}` of the normal subgroup `⟨k⟩`, so the walk has period 2 and does not converge. A criterion that tested only the centre, or only the subgroups generated by single elements of `Σ`, would miss it. Offered, not required.

## Questions for the author

- In 4.2 the route ends with "What remains is Diaconis and Shahshahani's estimate of this sum", and the only locator for the theorem is Saloff-Coste, Theorem 9.2, p. 327. Does that text prove the estimate of `Σ_{λ ≠ (n)} d_λ² r_λ^(2k)`, or only state it? If it only states it, the proof an implementer must follow lives in the two works the References list "without a locator" (Diaconis–Shahshahani 1981; Diaconis 1988, Chapter 3), and a locator into one of them would be needed for the hardest step of the roadmap. I could not check: no legal copy of Saloff-Coste's survey, of the 1981 paper or of the lecture notes was readable here.
- Same theorem: is it stated in the survey for all `n` and all `c > 0` with `k` an integer, as in 4.2, or for `n` beyond a threshold? The statement of 4.2 is true in either case (for each fixed `n`, `e^c` times the `ℓ²` distance is at most `√(n!)/n · e^(−c)`, so finitely many `n` only change `A`), but the README's sentence "Saloff-Coste, Theorem 9.2, p. 327, where the two bounds are …" should then say which form is the source's.
