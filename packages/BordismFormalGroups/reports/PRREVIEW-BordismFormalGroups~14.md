## R02 (P3) — `succ_le_order_sub_of_lt_height` is cited for the defect of an arbitrary approximate homomorphism; it states the case of the identity series only

Location: `TauCetiRoadmap/BordismFormalGroups/README.md` lines 282–290 ("when an approximate homomorphism f from F to G has homomorphism defect vanishing below degree n, the degree-n part of the defect is c·C_n …, vanishing automatically in degree p^k for k<h (`succ_le_order_sub_of_lt_height`, module `LeadingDifference`)").

Claim: for an approximate homomorphism f whose defect vanishes below degree p^k, the degree-p^k part of the defect vanishes automatically when k is below the heights, and this is the pinned `succ_le_order_sub_of_lt_height`.

Witness: at the pin (`FormalGroup/LeadingDifference.lean` lines 112–119) the statement is about the two laws themselves: if `↑k < F.height p`, `↑k < G.height p` and `↑(p ^ k) ≤ (F.toPowerSeries - G.toPowerSeries).order`, then `↑(p ^ k + 1) ≤ (F.toPowerSeries - G.toPowerSeries).order`. No series f occurs; it is the case f = X of the sentence. The general statement is true, and follows at the pin from `homogeneousComponent_homDefect_eq_smul_lazardCocycle` (`Obstruction.lean` line 400): the degree-p^k part is the coefficient of X^(p^k) in [p]_G(f(X)) − f([p]_F(X)) times C_(p^k), and both terms have order at least p^(k+1) when f has order 1 and the heights exceed k (`coeff_nsmulSeries_eq_zero_of_lt_height`, `Height/Basic.lean` line 251, gives the p-series order). But the name cited is not the statement made.

Repair: say "vanishing automatically in degree p^k for k below both heights, since by `homogeneousComponent_homDefect_eq_smul_lazardCocycle` the degree-p^k obstruction is a coefficient of [p]_G(f(X)) − f([p]_F(X)), which has order above p^k by `coeff_nsmulSeries_eq_zero_of_lt_height`; for f the identity series this is `succ_le_order_sub_of_lt_height` (module `LeadingDifference`)". Tested on the instance that refuted the original: F = G a commutative law of height 2 over 𝔽₂ (so [2]_F has order 4) and f = X + X², which is not the identity series, so the pinned lemma says nothing about it; the degree-2 part of f(F(X,Y)) − F(f(X), f(Y)) is (X+Y)² − X² − Y² = 2XY = 0, and the coefficient of X² in [2]_F(f(X)) − f([2]_F(X)) is 0 because both series have order at least 4, as the repaired sentence predicts.
