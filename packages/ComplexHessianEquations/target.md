## Scope

The roadmap builds the general theory of fully nonlinear elliptic equations of complex Hessian type on a compact complex manifold with a Hermitian metric, in the form in which the modern a priori estimates are proved: an operator `f(λ)` of the eigenvalues of a real (1,1)-form relative to the background metric, on an admissible cone `Γ`. The complex Monge–Ampère equation is the model case, not the only case. Nothing in it is planned elsewhere: no roadmap on main, no open roadmap pull request and no board package owns the eigenvalue framework, the admissible pairs, the equation, Yau's theorem, its Hermitian extension or the Guo–Phong–Tong bounds (see the report for the search). Two open pull requests plan special cases as single sections for their own use, K3Surfaces §2.1 (the Ricci-flat form on a K3 surface) and ShimuraVarieties §7.0g (a negative Kähler–Einstein metric, which is a different equation, see Consumers); neither is a library owner, and both are later consumers of this roadmap.

The roadmap owns four layers.

1. **Eigenvalues relative to a Hermitian metric, and spectral functions.** On a complex manifold `X` of dimension `n` with Hermitian metric `g` and fundamental form `ω_X`, a real (1,1)-form `α` defines a `g`-self-adjoint endomorphism `h[α]` of `T^{1,0}X` and an unordered real eigenvalue vector `λ[α] ∈ ℝⁿ/S_n`, the roots of `det(α − λ g) = 0`. For a `C²` real function `φ` and a smooth real (1,1)-form `χ`, `χ_φ = χ + i∂∂̄φ` and `λ[χ_φ]`, always relative to `ω_X` even when `χ ≠ ω_X`. The pointwise layer is linear algebra on a finite-dimensional inner product space over `𝕜 = ℝ` or `ℂ`: a symmetric function `f` of the eigenvalues of a self-adjoint endomorphism defines `F(h) = f(λ[h])`; the roadmap proves that `F` is well defined, that it is `C¹` (and `C^k` when `f` is `C^k`) on the open set of endomorphisms with eigenvalues in an open symmetric set, with the derivative in an eigenbasis given by the partial derivatives of `f`, that ellipticity (`∂f/∂λ_j > 0`) makes `∂F/∂h` positive definite, that concavity of `f` gives concavity of `F`, and that `det(∂F/∂h) ≥ γ` is equivalent to `∏_j ∂f/∂λ_j ≥ γ` on `Γ` (`guo-phong2024` line 83–90; `szekelyhidi2018` line 17–31). The real symmetric case `𝕜 = ℝ` is the Caffarelli–Nirenberg–Spruck setting (`cns1985` §1) and comes for free from the `RCLike` statement; it is stated so that real Hessian consumers (OptimalTransport Layer 6) need not rebuild it.
2. **Admissible cones and admissible pairs.** The cones `Γ_k = {λ : σ_j(λ) > 0, 1 ≤ j ≤ k}`, `Γ_n ⊆ Γ ⊆ Γ_1`, open symmetric convex cones with vertex `0` containing the positive orthant (`guo-phong2024` line 76–80; `szekelyhidi2018` line 29–30, 183–186). An admissible pair `(f, Γ)`: such a `Γ` and a `C¹` symmetric `f : Γ → ℝ`, elliptic and positively homogeneous of degree one; the structural condition `∏_j ∂f/∂λ_j ≥ γ > 0` on `Γ`. The examples, each proved to be an admissible pair with an explicit structural constant where it holds: complex Monge–Ampère `(Γ_n, (λ_1⋯λ_n)^{1/n})`, the complex `k`-Hessian `(Γ_k, σ_k^{1/k})`, the Hessian quotients `(Γ_k, (σ_k/σ_l)^{1/(k−l)})`, `l < k`, and positive combinations of these (`gpt2023` line 90–93; `guo-phong2024` line 91–104; `dinew-kolodziej2014` line 19–35 for `σ_m` and the form identity). Convexity of `Γ_k` and the Gårding inequalities needed for these examples are owned here.
3. **The equation.** On compact `X`: `(χ, Γ)`-admissible functions (`λ[χ_φ] ∈ Γ` everywhere) and `C²` solutions of `f(λ[χ_φ]) = c e^F`, `sup_X φ = 0`, with the normalisation `∫_X e^{nF} ω_Xⁿ = ∫_X ω_Xⁿ = V` and the constant `c` read off from `cⁿ = V⁻¹ ∫_X f(λ[χ_φ])ⁿ ω_Xⁿ` (`gpt2023` line 62–75; `guo-phong2024` line 108–113). The model case: `χ_φⁿ = cⁿ e^{nF} ω_Xⁿ` with `χ_φ > 0` is exactly the Monge–Ampère instance (`tosatti-weinkove2010` line 21–31 for the Hermitian equation in its usual form). The degenerate-background family `ω_t = χ + t ω_X`, `t ∈ (0,1]`, with `χ` closed and nonnegative on a compact Kähler manifold, its volumes `V_t`, the constants `c_t` and the energy `E_t(φ) = V_t⁻¹ ∫_X (−φ) f(λ[ω_{t,φ}])ⁿ ω_Xⁿ` (`gpt2023` line 101–119). The entropy `Ent_p(F) = V⁻¹ ∫_X e^{nF} |F|^p ω_Xⁿ` (`gpt2023` line 82–83) and its Hermitian variant `‖e^{nF}‖_{L¹(log L)^p}` (`guo-phong2024` line 120–126).
4. **The main theorems**, stated in Final targets: uniqueness by the comparison principle; existence for the complex Monge–Ampère equation on compact Hermitian manifolds (Tosatti–Weinkove), with Yau's theorem as the Kähler case where the constant is cohomological; the sharp `L^∞` estimates of Guo–Phong–Tong on compact Kähler manifolds for every admissible pair satisfying the structural condition, with fixed and degenerating backgrounds, and the Guo–Phong extension to compact Hermitian manifolds.

Boundaries.

- Pluripotential theory (Bedford–Taylor capacities, weak solutions in `L^q`, Kołodziej's method) is not built: the `L^∞` bounds are the PDE statements of Guo–Phong–Tong, which recover Kołodziej's bound for the Monge–Ampère equation and the Dinew–Kołodziej bound for Hessian equations as consequences (`gpt2023` line 93–96). Weak and viscosity solutions (`szekelyhidi2018` line 1067–1071) are not owned.
- Subsolution theory and the general `C^{2,α}` estimate of Székelyhidi for a `C`-subsolution (`szekelyhidi2018` line 41–53), Hessian-equation existence under curvature hypotheses (`dinew-kolodziej2014` line 594–598), `(n−1)`-form and almost-complex equations (`guo-phong2024` line 240–260), Kähler–Einstein metrics (the Aubin–Yau equation with a `φ`-dependent right-hand side) and the Kähler–Ricci flow are natural Part II roadmaps; they are not in scope.
- Sections `3` and `4` are stated for a Hermitian background wherever the sources prove them there (uniqueness, the equation, Tosatti–Weinkove, Guo–Phong). Kähler is a hypothesis only where the proof uses `dω_X = 0`: the cohomological value of the constant, and the degenerating-background estimate.

## Final targets

Throughout `X` is a compact complex manifold of dimension `n ≥ 1` without boundary, `ω_X` a smooth Hermitian metric, eigenvalues are relative to `ω_X`, and solutions are at least `C²`. Each target is a theorem the finished roadmap proves.

**(T1) The spectral function.** For an admissible pair `(f, Γ)`, the map `F(h) = f(λ[h])` on `g`-self-adjoint endomorphisms with eigenvalues in `Γ` is well defined and `C¹`; in a unitary eigenbasis of `h` its derivative is `diag(∂f/∂λ_j(λ[h]))`; `∂F/∂h` is positive definite; `det(∂F/∂h) ≥ γ` for all such `h` if and only if `∏_j ∂f/∂λ_j ≥ γ` on `Γ`; and `F` is concave if `f` is. Locators: `guo-phong2024` line 83–90 (conditions (2)–(4)), `szekelyhidi2018` line 17–31 (the operator `F(A)`), `cns1985` §1 (the real case, uncertified). Checks: for the Monge–Ampère pair the structural constant is `γ = n^{−n}`; for `σ_1` it is `1` and the equation is linear; the `k`-Hessian and quotient pairs satisfy the structural condition on `Γ_k` (`gpt2023` line 90–93).

**(T2) Uniqueness.** Let `(f, Γ)` be an admissible pair, `χ` a smooth real (1,1)-form and `F` continuous. If `(c, φ)` and `(c', ψ)` are two pairs of a positive constant and a `C²` `(χ, Γ)`-admissible function with `f(λ[χ_φ]) = c e^F`, `f(λ[χ_ψ]) = c' e^F` and `sup φ = sup ψ = 0`, then `c = c'` and `φ = ψ`. The proof is the comparison principle: at a maximum of `ψ − φ` the eigenvalues of `χ_ψ` are dominated by those of `χ_φ` and ellipticity forces `c' ≤ c`; with `c = c'` the difference solves a linear elliptic equation without zeroth-order term and is constant by the strong maximum principle. Locators: `tosatti-weinkove2010` line 38–47 (uniqueness of the constant and the function in the Monge–Ampère case on Hermitian manifolds); the filed Kähler Monge–Ampère case is the Check.

**(T3) Existence for the complex Monge–Ampère equation on compact Hermitian manifolds.** For every smooth real `F` on `X` there exist a unique constant `b` and a unique smooth real `φ` with `(ω_X + i∂∂̄φ)ⁿ = e^{F+b} ω_Xⁿ`, `ω_X + i∂∂̄φ > 0`, `sup_X φ = 0`, together with uniform `C^∞` a priori estimates on `φ` depending only on `(X, ω_X)` and `F` (`tosatti-weinkove2010` line 33–47, Main Theorem and Corollary 1). In the admissible-pair notation this is the Monge–Ampère pair with `χ = ω_X` and `c = e^{b/n}`.
- *Yau's theorem (the Kähler case).* If `dω_X = 0` then `b = log(∫ω_Xⁿ / ∫e^F ω_Xⁿ)`, so with the normalisation `∫e^{nF}ω_Xⁿ = V` the equation `χ_φⁿ = e^{nF}ω_Xⁿ` has a unique smooth admissible solution with `c = 1` (`tosatti-weinkove2010` line 52–59; `yau1978`, Theorem 1 of the introduction, uncertified; `szekelyhidi2014` Ch. 3, uncertified). The proof is the continuity method with the `C⁰` estimate by Moser iteration, the `C²` estimate by the maximum principle applied to `log tr_{ω_X} χ_φ − Aφ`, the complex Evans–Krylov `C^{2,α}` estimate and Schauder bootstrapping; the open account `pss2012` §§2–3 (Phong–Song–Sturm) is the readable locator for these steps.
- Check: `χ` any Kähler form in place of `ω_X` with eigenvalues still relative to `ω_X`: `χ_φⁿ = cⁿe^{nF}ω_Xⁿ` is solved by applying the theorem with background `χ`, since `cⁿ e^{nF} ω_Xⁿ/χⁿ` is a smooth positive function; `c` is then `(∫χⁿ/V)^{1/n}`.
- Check: when `F = 0`, `φ = 0` and `b = 0`.

**(T4) Sharp `L^∞` bounds on compact Kähler manifolds (Guo–Phong–Tong).** Let `dω_X = 0`, `(f, Γ)` an admissible pair with structural constant `γ`, `p > n`.
- *Fixed background* (`gpt2023` line 84–89, Theorem 1): if `φ ∈ C²(X)` solves `f(λ[ω_{X,φ}]) = e^F`, `sup φ = 0`, `λ ∈ Γ`, with `∫e^{nF}ω_Xⁿ = V`, then `sup_X |φ| ≤ C(n, p, γ, ω_X, Ent_p(F))`.
- *Degenerating background* (`gpt2023` line 101–130, Theorem 2): for `χ` closed and nonnegative, `ω_t = χ + tω_X`, and `C²` solutions `φ_t` of `f(λ[ω_{t,φ_t}]) = c_t e^{F_t}`, `sup φ_t = 0`, `λ ∈ Γ`, `∫e^{nF_t}ω_Xⁿ = V`, one has `sup_X |φ_t| ≤ C` with `C` depending only on `ω_X, χ, p, n, γ` and upper bounds for `c_tⁿ/V_t`, `E_t(φ_t)` and `Ent_p(F_t)`.
- Checks: for the Monge–Ampère pair the relative volume is `1/V` and the energy is bounded by Jensen, so Theorem 2 gives the Kołodziej–Eyssidieux–Guedj–Zeriahi–Demailly–Pali bound (`gpt2023` line 136–143, 847–850, Theorem 4); for `Γ = Γ_k` and `‖e^{nF_t}‖_{L^q}` bounded with `q > n/k`, the energy is bounded by a multiple of `c_tⁿ/V_t` (`gpt2023` line 948–952, Theorem 5).
- The proof compares with an auxiliary complex Monge–Ampère equation solved by (T3) in the Kähler case, so (T3) precedes (T4) in the roadmap.

**(T5) `L^∞` bounds on compact Hermitian manifolds (Guo–Phong).** For `(X, ω_X)` compact Hermitian, `(f, Γ)` admissible with structural constant `γ`, `p > n`, and a `C²` solution of `f(λ[ω_{X,φ}]) = e^F`, `λ ∈ Γ`, `sup φ = 0`: `sup_X |φ| ≤ C(X, ω_X, n, p, γ, ‖e^{nF}‖_{L¹(log L)^p})` (`guo-phong2024` line 116–126, Theorem 1). This is the general statement of (T4)'s fixed-background bound; (T4) is kept separately because its degenerating-background form has no Hermitian analogue and its proof is different. The Hermitian proof compares with a local Dirichlet problem on a coordinate ball (`guo-phong2024` line 46–56, 179–192, Theorem 2), whose solvability is the Caffarelli–Kohn–Nirenberg–Spruck theorem (`ckns1985`, uncertified); see Risks for the cost. If the design finds that cost too high, (T5) is the first section of a Part II and the design says so explicitly; it is not silently dropped.

## Suppliers

All on main unless a pull request is named. Sections are those of the supplier's README at main.

- **ComplexKahlerGeometry** (main; revisions open as #1234 and #1398 keep the K-numbering): K1 for complex structures, type decomposition, real (1,1)-forms and the metric convention `g(u,v) = ω(u, Jv)`; K2 for `∂`, `∂̄`, `i∂∂̄` on functions and the local `∂∂̄`-lemma; K4 for the Chern connection and curvature of a Hermitian metric (needed for the Hermitian `C²` estimate, with its torsion, see Risks); K5a for bundle Sobolev scales, Laplace-type elliptic regularity, Rellich compactness and the Fredholm/spectral realisation of the Laplacian on a compact manifold (the linearised operator and its inverse on mean-zero functions in the continuity method); K6.0 for Kähler normal coordinates, the Kähler identities and the comparison `Δ_d = 2Δ_∂̄` with the sign conventions.

## Risks

- **Complex Evans–Krylov.** The `C^{2,α}` step of (T3) is a fully nonlinear interior estimate for concave uniformly elliptic equations in complex form. PDE plans Schauder and De Giorgi–Nash–Moser but explicitly not Krylov–Safonov or Evans–Krylov (PDE README line 127, 339), and no other roadmap owns them. The roadmap must own the complex Evans–Krylov estimate (`twwy2015`) or the real one (`gilbarg-trudinger2001` Theorem 17.14) with a complex adapter. This is the largest piece of analysis in the design and has no supplier.
- **Spectral functions of eigenvalues.** Mathlib provides eigenvalues of Hermitian matrices but no theorem that a symmetric `C^k` function of the eigenvalues is a `C^k` function of the matrix. (T1) needs it (at least `C¹` with the derivative formula, `C²` for the estimates). This is a self-contained real-analysis theorem the roadmap must own.
- **Torsion on Hermitian manifolds.** The Tosatti–Weinkove `C²` estimate and the Guo–Phong comparison use the Chern connection's torsion and the failure of `∫Δφ ω_Xⁿ = 0` when `dω_X ≠ 0`. CKG K4 owns the Chern connection; its torsion tensor and the Hermitian integration-by-parts identities with torsion terms must be built here.
- **(T5) needs the Dirichlet problem.** The Hermitian `L^∞` bound rests on the Caffarelli–Kohn–Nirenberg–Spruck solution of the Dirichlet problem for the complex Monge–Ampère equation on a ball (`ckns1985`, paywalled, uncertified; no open account read). If the design cannot plan that input at acceptable cost, (T5) becomes the first section of a Part II and the design records that decision.
- **Size.** (T3) alone (continuity method, `C⁰` by Moser iteration, `C²`, `C^{2,α}`, bootstrapping, both Kähler and Hermitian) is a roadmap-sized object, and (T4) builds on it. The design should order the layers so that the framework (T1), the examples, uniqueness (T2) and the Kähler existence theorem land before the Hermitian extension and the `L^∞` theory, and may propose a split; it must not plan (T3) route-specifically (for example only in dimension two or only for `F` small).
- **Sign and normalisation conventions.** CKG pins `g(u,v) = ω(u,Jv)`, a nonnegative Hodge Laplacian, and `ω = (i/2) Σ h_T dz ∧ dz̄` in coordinates. The target's `ω_X = i g_{k̄j} dz^j ∧ dz̄^k` differs by the factor `1/2` and `tr_{ω_X} i∂∂̄φ = −(1/2)Δ_d φ` on Kähler manifolds. The design must fix one dictionary and prove it once; a mismatch silently changes the constants `c`, `V` and `γ`.
- **Sources that could not be read.** `yau1978`, `szekelyhidi2014`, `gilbarg-trudinger2001` and `ckns1985` are paywalled and cited by locator only. `cns1985` and `kolodziej1998` are open on Project Euclid but the sources service was blocked; they are in `needs-references.toml`. Every final target is covered by an open text that was read (`gpt2023`, `guo-phong2024`, `tosatti-weinkove2010`, `szekelyhidi2018`, `dinew-kolodziej2014`) or by the open accounts `pss2012` and `twwy2015`, so the design can proceed without the blocked texts.
- **Special-case planning upstream.** K3Surfaces §2.1 plans a dimension-two instance of (T3). If it merges first, the design should still state (T3) in full and show that §2.1 is an instance, not treat §2.1 as a supplier.
