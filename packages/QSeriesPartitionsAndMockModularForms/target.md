## Scope

Own the general q-series calculus, partition statistics and congruences, Appell–Lerch sums, and classical mock modular theory described below. Supply arithmetic consumers with identities, convergence, completions, shadows and transformation laws on actual mathematical objects. The endpoints are a triage brief, not implementation stages.

Finite product identities belong over arbitrary commutative coefficient rings where their expressions make sense; formal identities use native power series and Laurent-polynomial coefficients, with units or localizations specified for divisions. Analytic statements use complex parameters on their full convergence domains. Half-integral weights, nontrivial multipliers, vector-valued forms, every allowed level and every cusp are essential. Restrictions on poles, growth, weights and characteristics belong to individual theorems. No restriction to positive real q, odd-order roots of unity, one example of a mock theta function, or one Habiro consumer is imposed across the library.

Existing work remains owned upstream. Import Mathlib's partition carrier, weighted generating-function theorem, Glaisher/Euler partition theorems and pentagonal identities; ClassicalArithmeticCompletion §§4.20, 4.40, 4.60–4.61; and the analytic triple product assigned to GeometryOfNumbersAndQuadraticArithmetic §4.3. New q-Pochhammer notation must agree with those results. This is neither a duplicate of those proper subtheories nor a Part II of the integral-weight ModularForms or positive-definite ThetaSeries roadmaps. Their remaining targets keep their owners.

Habiro completion, evaluation at roots of unity, q-Witt vectors, geometric q-cohomology, and Nahm-specific modularity criteria remain outside this producer. A general Nahm sum is not automatically modular or mock modular. Zwegers' signature-(r−1,1) error-function theta construction belongs here as part of the completion theory; the Siegel–Narain theta kernel, theta lifts and Siegel–Weil identities reserved to IndefiniteThetaAndSiegelWeil remain distinct. Import generic metaplectic/Weil infrastructure from its earlier owner; the missing section contract is a design risk, not permission to bypass it.

This triage writes no Lean. A subsequent Suggested.lean must have a single `import Mathlib`, easy headline statements on existing carriers, proved examples testing its own definitions, no sorry'd data and no `set_option`. Numerical algorithms for coefficients or transformation matrices are outside scope.

## Final targets

Here `(a;q)_n = product_(j=0..n−1)(1−a*q^j)`, and a list in its first argument denotes a product of such factors. Analytic sums and products below include convergence assertions. Locators refer to the versions recorded in `packages/QSeriesPartitionsAndMockModularForms/sources.toml`.

1. **q-Pochhammer and basic hypergeometric calculus.** Supply finite shift, concatenation and inversion identities, the infinite-product limit, negative integer indices wherever the reciprocal factors exist, and the normalized unilateral `r-phi-s` and bilateral `r-psi-s` series for arbitrary finite parameter lists. Denominator factors must be nonzero; a terminating sum is stated on its actual finite range rather than evaluated through a later 0/0. Prove the source's convergence domains and changes of base. In particular, for complex a, `|q|<1` and `|z|<1`,

   `sum_(n>=0) (a;q)_n/(q;q)_n * z^n = (a*z;q)_infinity/(z;q)_infinity`.

   Its two Euler forms are `sum z^n/(q;q)_n = 1/(z;q)_infinity` for `|z|<1`, and `sum q^(n(n−1)/2)*z^n/(q;q)_n = (−z;q)_infinity` for every complex z. For `|q|>1`, the q-binomial identity instead has right side `(z/q;q^−1)_infinity/(a*z/q;q^−1)_infinity` on `|a*z/q|<1`. Finite inversion assumes the inverted parameters are units. There is no unsupported infinite product at `|q|=1`.

   Include Ramanujan's bilateral summation: for `0<|q|<1`, `a != 0`, `|b/a|<|z|<1`, and all denominator factors nonzero,

   `sum_(j in Z) (a;q)_j/(b;q)_j*z^j = (q,b/a,a*z,q/(a*z);q)_infinity / (b,q/a,z,b/(a*z);q)_infinity`.

   Locators: Gasper1995 §1.2, equations (1.2.3), (1.2.7)–(1.2.8), pp. 2–3 (Gasper1995 line 81, Gasper1995 line 127, Gasper1995 line 135); §1.3, (1.3.6)–(1.3.11), pp. 5–6 (Gasper1995 line 286); §2.1, (2.1.3), (2.1.9), pp. 8–9 (Gasper1995 line 435, Gasper1995 line 497); §3.1, (3.1.6), (3.1.11)–(3.1.12), pp. 13–15 (Gasper1995 line 720, Gasper1995 line 761, Gasper1995 line 805). Formal versions retain the appropriate coefficient rings and coefficientwise local finiteness; an analytic bilateral identity is not automatically a formal Laurent series.

2. **Product identities and partition generating functions.** Import both triple products from their existing owners. For `0<|q|<1` and nonzero complex z prove the quintuple identity

   `sum_(n in Z) (−1)^n*q^(n(3n−1)/2)*z^(3n)*(1+z*q^n) = (q,−z,−q/z;q)_infinity*(q*z^2,q/z^2;q^2)_infinity`.

   For `|q|<1`, prove both Rogers–Ramanujan identities: the sums with numerators `q^(n^2)` and `q^(n(n+1))`, respectively, and denominator `(q;q)_n` equal the reciprocal products on positive exponents congruent to ±1 and ±2 modulo 5. Also supply their formal identities over arbitrary commutative rings, obtained from integral coefficients, and their counting statements for every n≥0: partitions with successive differences at least two correspond in number to parts congruent to ±1 modulo 5; imposing smallest part at least two gives ±2 modulo 5. The q=0 limits are included for the unilateral identities.

   Reuse `Nat.Partition.genFun` and `Nat.Partition.hasProd_genFun` for arbitrary multiplicative weights on part size and multiplicity; add the ordinary partition-series adapter `sum p(n)*X^n = product_(m>=1)(1−X^m)^−1`, with `p(0)=1`, and its convergent complex realization for `|q|<1`. Existing Glaisher, odd/distinct and pentagonal theorems are checks of the new adapters, not new targets to prove again.

   Locators: Gasper1995 §2.2, (2.2.10), p. 11 (Gasper1995 line 605), and §4.2, (4.2.11)–(4.2.12), p. 29 (Gasper1995 line 1672); Pak2002 §2.2.1, p. 10 (Pak2002 line 442), §§7.1.1–7.1.3, pp. 38–39 (Pak2002 line 3703, Pak2002 line 3745). The quintuple product is explicitly stated in Gasper; its proof is a genuine mathematical obligation, not a claim that those notes provide the proof.

3. **Partition congruences, rank and crank.** On the existing partition carrier, define rank as largest part minus number of parts, with empty partition rank zero. Define crank by largest part when there are no ones, and otherwise by the number of parts strictly larger than the number of ones, minus the number of ones. Set the empty crank to zero. Supply the bivariate generating functions, reduction into every positive modulus, conjugation/symmetry identities where valid, and the following exact congruences and equidistributions.

   For every n≥0, prove `p(5n+4)=0 mod 5`, `p(7n+5)=0 mod 7`, and `p(11n+6)=0 mod 11`. Rank classes are equally numerous modulo 5 at size 5n+4 and modulo 7 at size 7n+5. Crank classes are equally numerous modulo 5, 7 and 11 at those three sizes. For every r≥1 let `0<=delta_(ell,r)<ell^r` satisfy `24*delta_(ell,r)=1 mod ell^r`. For every n≥0 prove divisibility of `p(ell^r*n+delta_(ell,r))` by `ell^r` when ell=5 or 11, and by `7^(floor(r/2)+1)` when ell=7. Do not replace the last exponent by r.

   The usual crank product `(q;q)_infinity/((z*q;q)_infinity*(q/z;q)_infinity)` counts weighted vector partitions at every size and ordinary crank partitions for sizes greater than one. Its coefficient of q is `z+z^−1−1`; ordinary size-one crank contributes `z^−1`. State the correction at size one and the empty convention explicitly.

   Locators: AndrewsGarvan1988 §1, equations (1.1)–(1.15), crank definition and Theorem 1, pp. 167–169 (AndrewsGarvan1988 line 10, AndrewsGarvan1988 line 62, AndrewsGarvan1988 line 69, AndrewsGarvan1988 line 80, AndrewsGarvan1988 line 87), and §2, p. 170 (AndrewsGarvan1988 line 124); Zagier2009 §7, pp. 986–16–17 (Zagier2009 line 898, Zagier2009 line 912); BevilacquaChandranChoi2019 introduction, p. 1, unnumbered prime-power formulas (BevilacquaChandranChoi2019 line 36). The size-one correction is our coefficient calculation from the displayed product and definition.

4. **Appell–Lerch sums and their completion.** For every tau in the upper half-plane and u,v outside `Z*tau+Z`, construct Zwegers' normalized mu by its convergent bilateral series, its meromorphic extension, simple poles and residues, its elliptic laws and its modular defect expressed by the Mordell integral. Construct the error-function series R for every complex argument and the completion `muHat(u,v;tau)=mu(u,v;tau)+(i/2)*R(u−v;tau)` in the thesis normalization. For all integers a,b,c,d with ad−bc=1 prove

   `muHat(u/(c*tau+d),v/(c*tau+d);(a*tau+b)/(c*tau+d)) = nu_eta(gamma)^−3*(c*tau+d)^(1/2)*exp(−pi*i*c*(u−v)^2/(c*tau+d))*muHat(u,v;tau)`.

   Here `nu_eta` and the square-root branch are those of the eta transformation law; retain them as actual factors. Include the joint elliptic law for arbitrary integral shifts in both variables, symmetry, and the unary-theta period-integral expression for R. The period formula has `−1/2<a<1/2`, b real in Theorem 1.16(1), and both a,b in that open interval in part (2); extend through the proved shift laws, not by discarding boundary terms. Torsion specializations must avoid poles or use a proved removable-limit formula.

   Locators: Zwegers2002 Propositions 1.4–1.5, pp. 8–11 (Zwegers2002 line 362, Zwegers2002 line 466), Lemma 1.8 and Propositions 1.9–1.10; Theorem 1.11, pp. 15–16 (Zwegers2002 line 734); Definition 1.14, Proposition 1.15 and Theorem 1.16, pp. 17–18 (Zwegers2002 line 818, Zwegers2002 line 835). Zagier2009 Theorem 2.1, p. 986–07 (Zagier2009 line 327), uses a different normalization of mu and the correction; supply the comparison rather than mixing their formulas.

5. **The general Zwegers constructions underlying mock theta functions.** For every nonsingular integral symmetric r-by-r matrix A of type `(r−1,1)`, choose a negative-cone component. Allow each cone datum to be any negative vector in that component or a primitive integral isotropic boundary vector, as in Definition 2.1. For characteristics a,b use the domains `R(c)=R^r` for negative c and `R(c)={a | B(c,a) is not an integer}` for boundary c. Construct the error-function/sign-kernel theta series on the resulting domain, prove absolute convergence, and its full characteristic shift, T and S laws, including the sum over `A^−1 Z^r/Z^r` and determinant factor. The S law requires both characteristics to lie in each R(c). Do not restrict to binary forms or to negative cone data only.

   Also supply the meromorphic Jacobi decomposition of Theorem 3.9 for every positive integral index m and integral weight k. A meromorphic function of the elliptic variable satisfying `phi(z+lambda*tau+mu)=exp(−2*pi*i*m*(lambda^2*tau+2*lambda*z))*phi(z)` decomposes into its completed theta-coefficient part and the residue terms at every pole modulo the elliptic lattice, with arbitrary pole orders. The coefficient definition includes the R corrections and a fundamental parallelogram whose boundary avoids poles; the resulting coefficients are independent of that auxiliary choice. Under the stated modular law for phi, they transform with weight k−1/2 and the source's theta representation. Do not replace them by uncorrected Fourier integrals or assume simple poles.

   Locators: Zwegers2002 §2.2, Definition 2.1 and Proposition 2.4, pp. 25–27 (Zwegers2002 line 1275, Zwegers2002 line 1304, Zwegers2002 line 1311, Zwegers2002 line 1351); Corollary 2.9, p. 42 (Zwegers2002 line 2308); §3.1, conditions (E) and (M), p. 49 (Zwegers2002 line 2660), Theorem 3.9, pp. 58–59 (Zwegers2002 line 3101). Positive-definite lattice theta theory and the Siegel–Narain construction retain their separate owners.

6. **Harmonic weak Maass forms, holomorphic parts and shadows.** Define the real-analytic modular-function spaces for every compatible scalar multiplier and finite-dimensional representation covered by the sources, with level, weight, cusp growth and slash action explicit. In the Weil-representation setting of Bruinier–Funke §3, retain every compatible `k in (1/2)Z`, k≠1, every allowed even lattice and every finite-index subgroup of `Mp_2(Z)`. A harmonic weak Maass form is C², has at most exponential growth at every cusp, transforms by the specified representation, and is killed by

   `Delta_k = −y^2*(partial_x^2+partial_y^2)+i*k*y*(partial_x+i*partial_y)`.

   Supply its holomorphic/nonholomorphic Fourier decomposition with the constant y^(1−k) term, raising and lowering operators, and the antilinear map `xi_k(F)=2*i*y^k*conj(partial_bar_tau F)` into weight 2−k with dual representation. Its kernel is precisely the weakly holomorphic forms. On the full exponential-growth space the target is weakly holomorphic, not necessarily cuspidal; on the inverse image of cusp forms it is cuspidal. Prove the two surjective exact sequences of Corollary 3.8 in their full stated Weil-representation setting. Those surjectivity assertions are not asserted for an arbitrary representation merely because a transformation-law definition exists for it.

   A mock modular form is a holomorphic part with the specified nonholomorphic completion and shadow; prove the normalization comparison with the Eichler-integral formulation and zero shadow iff weak holomorphy. Keep constants, conjugation and cusp-growth conventions explicit. The general definition need not exclude weight one, but the quoted Bruinier–Funke Fourier/surjectivity theorem does: its k≠1 assumption must stay visible; a weight-one logarithmic Fourier treatment needs its own justification.

   Locators: BruinierFunke2003 §3, (3.1)–(3.3), pp. 8–10 (BruinierFunke2003 line 366, BruinierFunke2003 line 395, BruinierFunke2003 line 423, BruinierFunke2003 line 443), Proposition 3.2 and definition of the cusp-shadow subspace, pp. 10–11 (BruinierFunke2003 line 478, BruinierFunke2003 line 487), Theorem 3.7 and Corollary 3.8, pp. 13–15 (BruinierFunke2003 line 626, BruinierFunke2003 line 697). Zagier2009 §5, pp. 986–12–14 (Zagier2009 line 640, Zagier2009 line 662, Zagier2009 line 705), supplies the scalar/vector-valued mock-form interpretation. Its convenient integral formula assumes a vanishing constant term at infinity; do not transfer that assumption to the whole library.

7. **Mock theta transformations and partition-rank modularity.** Apply the general completion theory to the third-, fifth- and seventh-order mock theta families treated in the certified sources. Export the exact q-shifts, completed vectors, T and S matrices, unary-theta shadows and rational-cusp behavior, rather than only an existential assertion that a completion exists. For every nontrivial root of unity xi, the function `q^(−1/24)*R_rank(xi;q)` is the holomorphic part of the weight-1/2 completion in Zagier's Theorem 7.1. Include every order of xi, with the character, level and shadow derived from its rational elliptic characteristics and the completed Appell–Lerch laws. If an intermediate formula has poles, establish the limiting cancellation before specialization. For xi=1 the ordinary partition series has its eta description instead. For every positive t and residue r modulo t, supply the finite root-of-unity filtering relation for the rank-counting series, retaining the t=1 case.

   Locators: Zagier2009 §2, third-order example, pp. 986–07–08 (Zagier2009 line 348), and §7, Theorem 7.1 with proof and following corollary, pp. 986–17–18 (Zagier2009 line 912, Zagier2009 line 929, Zagier2009 line 937, Zagier2009 line 952); Zwegers2002 Chapter 4, especially Propositions 4.5, 4.10, 4.13–4.14 (Zwegers2002 line 3781, Zwegers2002 line 4415, Zwegers2002 line 4857, Zwegers2002 line 5029). Bringmann–Ono's odd-order restriction reported in Zagier's remark is not a restriction on Zagier's stated Theorem 7.1.
