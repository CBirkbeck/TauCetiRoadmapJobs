### Principles, scope, and package shape

I read all 1,402 README lines, all 672 Lean lines, `metadata.toml`, all 18 manifest entries, the declared gaps in `design/plan.json`, and the available repair report. I compared the shape with `TauCetiRoadmap/ThetaSeries/README.md`, `TauCetiRoadmap/ComplexTori/README.md`, and `TauCetiRoadmap/ModularForms/README.md` on main.

The package contains 11 ordered layers, 69 numbered subsections, 69 local `*Needs:*` lines, and 69 Checks blocks. Each defining subsection supplies an API and at least three checks; theorem-only subsections sometimes have two. Each layer has Examples and Dependencies. The summary, scope, conventions, exact supplier contracts, reading guide, consumers, and references are present. The statements use mathematical prose and locators rather than a target-number catalogue. I found no copied source prose, computational algorithm presented as the deliverable, process narrative affecting scope, or attempt to replace an existing roadmap.

The generality is substantive: finite algebra includes the zero ring and q=1; coefficient extraction works over commutative semirings; bounded inverse-of-24 offsets include every positive coprime modulus; negative indices carry unit domains; parabolic Fourier theory includes nonunitary and nonsemisimple monodromy; negative-cone theta includes isotropic boundary vectors and odd lattices; Jacobi residues include arbitrary pole orders. The arithmetic congruences remain the particular theorems supported by their sources. The package does not infer boundary evaluation from disc convergence, harmonicity from an arbitrary indefinite theta product, or a universal completion criterion for arbitrary q-series.

The three extensions in existing suppliers' directions are stated as Part II contracts with their base stopping points. Generic metaplectic, positive theta, integral-weight modular, formal triple-product, and analytic q-product theory retain their existing owners. The new finite/formal adapters and exponential-growth harmonic spaces explain their differences from those objects.
