## C01 — The displayed formula for `h` in the Conventions is `0·∞` at `ε = −1/n²`, `n > ℓ`, `r < 0`, points that the Wronskian and the series of `h` admit.

For `ε < 0`, `r < 0` the Conventions put `κ = −(−ε)^{−1/2}` and `S = 0`, so the second term of the display (lines 99–100) is `S · Γ(ℓ + 1 + κ) · M`, and at `κ = −n` with `n > ℓ` the factor `Γ(ℓ + 1 − n)` is at a pole. The Conventions name junk values for `r > 0` only (lines 104–105), and the Checks of 6.2 and 6.4 use `(−1/4, 0, −1.4)` and `(−1/9, 1, −1.4)`. The Lean definition is right there, because Mathlib's `Γ` is `0` at its poles and the product is `0`: it gives `h = 0.0247609509590849` and `−0.0751540107103261`, `𝒲{h, f}·π/2 = 1`, and the series of 6.4 returns the same two values. A reader of the prose formula meets an undefined product. One clause in the Conventions would do: for `ε < 0`, `r < 0` the term with `M_{κ,ℓ+½}` is absent (`S = 0`), also where `Γ(ℓ + 1 + κ)` has a pole.
