## R05 (P3) — §6.2 (seeded sample) attributes local finiteness to `[V, 30.5.5]` "for R a DVR"; the statement needs a complete DVR with finite residue field

**Location.** `README.md:1612`: "the local statement, for `R` a DVR, is `[V, 30.5.5]`."

**Claim.** Sections 30.5–30.6 of Voight assume throughout that `R` is a complete DVR with finite residue field. Without the finite residue field, the local statement is false.

**Witness.** The hypothesis is at `voight lines 23309–23310`. Counterexample:
- Take `R = ℂ[[t]]`, `O = O_0(t²)`, `K = ℂ((t^{1/2}))` and `S = R[t√t]`.
- For each `s ∈ R`, put `g_s = [[ts, 1],[t²(t−s²), −ts]]`. Then `g_s ∈ O`, `g_s² = t³`, and `γ ↦ g_s` is optimal: the `(1,2)` entry forces `y ∈ R`.
- The diagonal map `O_0(t²) → R/t² × R/t²` is a ring map onto a commutative ring, so conjugation by `O^×` fixes `ts mod t²`.
- Hence there are infinitely many `O^×`-classes, one for each `s mod t ∈ ℂ`. The normalizer only swaps the two diagonal entries.

**Repair.** "the local statement, for `R` a complete DVR with finite residue field, is `[V, 30.5.5]` (p. 541)".
