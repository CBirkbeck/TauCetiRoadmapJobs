## R01 (P3) — A false Check in §4.9: the displayed matrix is not metric-Hermitian

Location: README.md line 1141 ("with `η=diag(1,−1)` on `ℂ²` the matrix `[[0,1],[1,0]]` is metric-Hermitian but not Hilbert-Hermitian times `η`").
Claim: the matrix `A = [[0,1],[1,0]]` is Hermitian for the pairing `[ξ,ζ] = ⟨ηξ,ζ⟩`, `η = diag(1,−1)`, and not for the Hilbert pairing.
Witness: metric-Hermitian means `ηA = (ηA)*`. Here `ηA = [[0,1],[−1,0]]`, whose adjoint is `[[0,−1],[1,0]] = −ηA`: `A` is metric-anti-Hermitian, and it is Hilbert-Hermitian (`A* = A`). The Check has the two notions the wrong way round.
Repair: use `A = [[0,1],[−1,0]]`: then `ηA = [[0,1],[1,0]]` is Hermitian, so `A` is metric-Hermitian, while `A* = −A` is not Hilbert-Hermitian. Delete the phrase "times `η`".

## R02 (P3) — A false Check in §6.23: the regulated tadpole has no poles at the negative integers

Location: README.md line 3153 ("equals `π²m^(2−2z)κ^(2z)Γ(z−1)/Γ(1+z)`, meromorphic with poles at `z=1,0,−1,…`, all simple").
Claim: the family `∫d⁴k (k²+m²)^(−1)((k²+m²)/κ²)^(−z)` has simple poles at every integer `≤ 1`.
Witness: `Γ(1+z) = z(z−1)Γ(z−1)`, so the value is `π² m^(2−2z) κ^(2z) / (z(z−1))` exactly: simple poles at `z = 1` and `z = 0` only. At `z = −1` it equals `π² m⁴ κ^(−2)/2`, finite. (The closed form itself is right: `∫d⁴k (k²+m²)^(−s) = π² Γ(s−2) m^(4−2s)/Γ(s)` at `s = 1+z`, convergent for `Re z > 1`.)
Repair: "meromorphic with exactly two simple poles, at `z=1` and `z=0`, since `Γ(z−1)/Γ(1+z) = 1/(z(z−1))`". §6.26 line 3458 ("simple pole at `z=0`") is consistent and needs no change.
