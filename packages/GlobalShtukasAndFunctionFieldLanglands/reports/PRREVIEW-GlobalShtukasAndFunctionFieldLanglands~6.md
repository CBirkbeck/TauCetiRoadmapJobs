### The changed passages, checked

**The new Check sentence.** On P¹ over F₃ the Picard scheme is the constant group Z and Frobenius acts trivially on it, so the Lang map Frob − id is the zero map: its kernel is all of Z, with one component per integer degree, and its fibre over 0 is infinite, so the Lang map on the whole Picard scheme is not finite, as the Check says. A central lattice Ξ whose degrees are dZ acts by translating the degree by multiples of d, so Pic/Ξ = Z/dZ has d components; d = 1 gives one and d = 2 gives two, the two values the Check states. The negative control d = 0 would leave infinitely many components and is excluded both by the Check's “d ≥ 1” and by §0.10's requirement that the central-degree image of Ξ have finite index. The count agrees with the §0.10 Check (remaining degree classes Z/(nd)Z, here n = 1) and with the §2.4 Check (zero-leg GL₁ shtukas on P¹/F₃ form ⊔_{d∈Z} B(F₃×), and the quotient by Pic(F₃) = Z leaves B(C₂): one component of mass 1/2, the d = 1 case).

**The dependents.** §2.4 *Fractional-infinity shtukas* and §3.3 *Horocycle compact direct images* are the two targets whose `*Needs:*` lines cite §0.15. §3.3 maps line/quotient ratios to `Pic^{Ni}` and uses a finite étale translation group on each fixed-degree component; that is the Pic⁰(F_q)-torsor description §0.15 keeps (the finite étale Lang isogeny on Pic⁰), not the infinite whole-Picard kernel, so the clarified Check leaves §3.3's statement and its shift `[#I(κ)](#I(κ)/2)` untouched. §2.4's Check was recomputed above and agrees.

**The new Source line.** LL line 9634–9641 (page 168, the remark following Proposition VI.15(iii)) states that for r = 1 there is no truncation and no boundary, that Cht¹_N/aZ is a finite étale cover of (X − N) × (X − N) obtained by base change from the Lang-isogeny cover of the Picard variety, hence abelian, with partial Frobenii Frob∞ and Frob₀. This is exactly the two-leg rank-one cover after the central quotient as a pullback of the Picard Lang isogeny. LL line 112 fixes a as an idele of degree 1, so LL's quotient is the d = 1 case of the README's §0.10 quotient; the README's Source sentence does not claim LL for the general nonzero-degree a or for arbitrary finite leg data, which it assigns to the supplied Picard stack and the connected Lang theorem. The retained reciprocity locators: the §1 subsection headings d) *La correspondance de Langlands globale* (LL line 9051) and e) *Hypothèse de récurrence et réductions* (line 9132); the remark after Theorem VI.9 at line 9106 is on page 158 (page marker at line 9077) and identifies rank one with class field theory; the proof of Proposition VI.11 at line 9149 is on page 159 (page marker at line 9117) and identifies finite-order characters of F×\A× with rank-one finite-order ℓ-adic sheaves, respecting local L and ε factors.
