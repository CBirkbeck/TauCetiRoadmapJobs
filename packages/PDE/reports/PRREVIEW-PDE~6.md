## R01 (P3) — §5.2 cites Meyers, Theorem 1 for a `C¹` domain; his hypothesis is the class `𝒞_q`

**Location:** `TauCetiRoadmap/PDE/README.md:1886–1887`: "State the lower range `2 − ε < p < 2` separately as `W^{1,p}` well-posedness of the Dirichlet problem with `‖∇u‖_p ≤ C‖F‖_p` on a bounded `C¹` domain (Meyers, Theorem 1, p. 198)".

**Claim:** Meyers's Theorem 1 assumes that `Ω` is of class `𝒞_q`, which he defines as unique `W^{1,q}_0` solvability of the Dirichlet problem for the Laplacian with the gradient estimate, and he remarks only that the condition "will hold for any value of `q` if the boundary is sufficiently smooth" and that every ball is of class `𝒞_q` for every `q`. The text contains no `C¹` hypothesis and no theorem that `C¹` domains are of class `𝒞_q`; that is the `C¹` Dirichlet theory of the Laplacian (Fabes–Jodeit–Rivière, or Jerison–Kenig's Lipschitz theory in its `p`-range), which the README does not cite.

**Witness:** `meyers-1963` lines 164–169 (the definition of the class and the smoothness remark, printed p. 194), 266–267 (Theorem 1's hypothesis "of class `𝒞_q` for some `q`", printed p. 198), 322–323 (balls are of class `𝒞_q` for every `q`, printed p. 201).

**Repair:** State the lower-range target with Meyers's hypothesis, "on a bounded open set on which the Dirichlet problem for `−Δ` is uniquely `W^{1,q}_0`-solvable with `‖∇u‖_q ≤ C‖F‖_q` for the relevant `q`, in particular on balls", or keep "`C¹`" and add the source that supplies the implication, with its own locator.

## R02 (P3) — §5.4's `n = 1, 2` route: the pinned iteration gives `R^{−1/(1−2/q)}`, not `R^{−n/2}`

**Location:** `TauCetiRoadmap/PDE/README.md:2012–2016` ("the pinned energy recursion applies there verbatim once a Sobolev inequality with some exponent `q > 2` is supplied on `W^{1,2}_0` of the ball, which is the finite-`q` inequality for `n = 2` and the `L^∞` bound of §1.1 for `n = 1`") and `:2020–2024` ("the targets are the same bound for `n = 1, 2`", that is `|u| ≤ D R^{−n/2}‖u‖_{L²(B(x₀,R))}`); `:1973` ("`D = D(λ, Λ, n)`").

**Claim:** The recursion `TauCeti.PDE.UniformlyEllipticOn.setIntegral_sq_mul_max_sub_sq_le` does apply verbatim (it takes any `q ≥ 2` and a Sobolev constant `S` on `W^{1,2}_0(Ω)`), but the iterated theorem it feeds, `exists_ae_value_le_add_mul_rpow_mul_sqrt_setIntegral` (`Analysis/PDE/Regularity/LocalBoundedness.lean:503`), concludes `u ≤ k + D R^{−1/(1−2/q)}‖(u − k)⁺‖_{L²(B(x₀,R))}` with `D = D(q, S, λ, Λ, n)`. For `n = 2` and a finite `q` the exponent `−q/(q − 2)` is not `−n/2 = −1`; the two agree only after the `R`-dependence of the ball's Sobolev constant, `S(B_R) ≍ R^{n/q − n/2 + 1}` (`= R^{2/q}` for `n = 2`), is inserted into `D`. The route sentence does not say this, and a reader would expect the pinned theorem to deliver the `R^{−n/2}` bound directly. The pinned `D` also depends on the Haar normalization (`mu` is a section variable of the theorem and `D` is quantified after it), which the contracts bullet at `:459` records and `:1973` omits.

**Witness:** `LocalBoundedness.lean:503–518` at `358314d`: `R ^ (-(1 - 2 / q.toReal)⁻¹)` with `S` fixed before `Omega`; dilation `u ↦ u(R·)` on `W^{1,2}_0(B_R)` in `ℝ²` gives `‖v‖_q ≤ C_q R^{2/q}‖∇v‖_2`.

**Repair:** After "applies there verbatim", add "with the Sobolev constant of the ball `B(x₀, R)`, which scales as `R^{n/q − n/2 + 1}`, so that the pinned exponent `−1/(1 − 2/q)` becomes `−n/2` once that scaling is carried through `D`"; write `D = D(λ, Λ, n)` for Lebesgue measure, or `D(λ, Λ, n, μ)`, consistently in `:1973` and `:459`.

## R03 (P3) — §6.2's second energy estimate is written for symmetric `a`

**Location:** `TauCetiRoadmap/PDE/README.md:2268–2275`: "by the second Galerkin energy estimate, testing against `∂ₜu_m`, in which `∂ₜa` or its absence is what controls `d/dt ∫ a∇u_m · ∇u_m`".

**Claim:** For the roadmap's not-necessarily-symmetric `a`, testing `B(u_m, ·)` against `∂ₜu_m` produces `∫ ∇∂ₜu_m · a∇u_m`, which equals `(1/2) d/dt ∫ ∇u_m · a_s ∇u_m − (1/2)∫ ∇u_m · ∂ₜa_s ∇u_m + ∫ ∇∂ₜu_m · a_a ∇u_m` with `a_s, a_a` the symmetric and antisymmetric parts; the last term is not a time derivative and is not controlled by `∂ₜa`. It is handled by the bullet's own Lipschitz hypothesis: with `a_a` antisymmetric and Lipschitz in `x`, `∫ ∇∂ₜu_m · a_a ∇u_m = −∫ ∂ₜu_m (∂ⱼa_a^{ij}) ∂ᵢu_m` (the second-order term cancels by antisymmetry), a first-order term absorbed into `‖∂ₜu_m‖²_{L²}`. The conclusion stands; the route omits the step that uses the hypothesis it carries.

**Witness:** `a = I + x₁J` of §4.1 (`:1664–1666`), for which `B` is not symmetric although `ξ · a(x)ξ = ‖ξ‖²`: `∫ ∇v · a_a ∇u = −∫ u ∂₂v` is not a time derivative when `v = ∂ₜu`.

**Repair:** Add to the route "for the antisymmetric part of `a`, the term `∫ ∇∂ₜu_m · a_a ∇u_m` is rewritten as `−∫ ∂ₜu_m ∂ⱼ(a_a^{ij}) ∂ᵢu_m` by the Lipschitz hypothesis and absorbed".
