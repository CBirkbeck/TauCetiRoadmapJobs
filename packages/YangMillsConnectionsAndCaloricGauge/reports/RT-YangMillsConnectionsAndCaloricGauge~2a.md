1. **F01** (P1) Curvature perturbation bound omits the cutoff size

   **Location:** `packages/YangMillsConnectionsAndCaloricGauge/README.md:1203`: “1 + ‖B‖ … + ‖O_(∞)‖ … + ‖b‖”; §5.1 at line 1019 permits any smooth cutoff vanishing near zero and equal to one outside a compact set.

   **Claim:** The displayed curvature-difference bound is uniform over all good representatives, without dependence on their cutoff.

   **Witness:** Take SU(2), O=exp(i x₁/|x|), B=0, η_M=M near 3e₂, and b=jβ dx₂ supported there. The k-component of the curvature difference is −2Mβ∂₁(x₁/|x|)k. Its L² norm grows with M; the claimed bound does not.

   **Exact repair text:** “For a good representative A = −η′ O_(∞);x + B, the curvature difference is bounded by C‖b‖_{Ẇ^{1,d/2}}(1 + ‖B‖_{Ẇ^{1,d/2}} + ‖η′‖_∞‖O_(∞)‖_{C¹(S^{d−1})} + ‖b‖_{Ẇ^{1,d/2}}).”

   Here β is fixed, smooth and nonzero, and η_M is extended smoothly to zero near the origin and one outside a compact set. Each A is admissible and has finite critical curvature. At 3e₂, ∂₁(x₁/|x|)=1/3; on a sufficiently small fixed ball it is bounded below by a positive constant. The original right side is independent of M; the repaired one grows as M, as required by Hardy's inequality. This does not restrict the permitted good representatives.


2. **F02** (P2) Quantitative patching needs a uniform covering margin

   **Location:** `packages/YangMillsConnectionsAndCaloricGauge/README.md:823`: “centres are pairwise at distance at least c(d) > 0”; line 825 gives a bound depending only on R,M.

   **Claim:** Centre separation supplies the uniform geometry needed for the cited quantitative patching argument.

   **Witness:** Eight balls B₁(±(1−ε)e_j) cover B₁⊂ℝ⁴. Their centre separation is √2(1−ε), but their covering margin at 0 is ε. The cited construction depends on that margin (OhTataru-TopClass line 1279).

   **Exact repair text:** “For the quantitative assertion fix ℓ>0 and additionally require that every x∈B_R has B_ℓ(x)∩B_R contained in a cover member. Then the global-potential bound is C(d,G,R,M,ℓ). In applications choose the cover so that the half-radius balls cover B_R while the local data are available on the full-radius balls; this supplies a fixed ℓ. The qualitative global-gauge statement has no uniform-cover requirement.” Add this cover choice to §4.4's proof route.

   Verification of the witness: put a=1−ε, with 0<ε<1/2. For x with r=‖x‖<1, choose a coordinate with |x_j|≥r/2 and its matching sign. Its squared distance to the corresponding centre is at most r²+a²−ar. This convex function of r is bounded above on [0,1] by max(a²,1+a²−a)<1. Thus these eight balls really cover B₁. Their minimum separation is √2a. At 0 their covering margin is exactly 1−a=ε, despite fixed dimension, R=1, multiplicity≤8 and uniformly separated centres. The repaired bound is allowed to depend on ε; for a half-radius cover one can instead take ℓ=1/2.

   The source explicitly uses a refinement parameter depending on the Lebesgue number (OhTataru-TopClass lines 1265–1281), then incorrectly infers a uniform Lebesgue number from separation. The witness refutes that inference. This finding concerns the unsupported uniform quantitative assertion and its proof input; the qualitative patching statement is unaffected. The original 1b/F06 wording repair was applied faithfully, but did not repair this additional geometric issue.
