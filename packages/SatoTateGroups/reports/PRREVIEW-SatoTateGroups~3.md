## R01 (P1) — Bogomolov's openness theorem is cited to a section that consumes it and owns it nowhere

Location: `TauCetiRoadmap/SatoTateGroups/README.md:277-281` (Exact supplier contracts), `:2138-2144` (9.2), `:104-105`. Quote: "Section 1.20 supplies its Bogomolov Hodge–Tate openness input independently of the density-one maximality statement: the arithmetic Hodge–Tate image is open in the rational points of its algebraic monodromy group."

Claim: PotentialModularityAndCompatibleSystems §1.20 provides, for an abelian variety over a number field, that `ρ_{A,ℓ}(G_k)` is open in `G_ℓ^{Zar}(ℚ_ℓ)`; 9.2 derives `G_ℓ^{1,Zar} = ker(G_ℓ^{Zar} → 𝔾_m)` from it, 9.5 derives the unconditional surjection `ST(A)/ST(A)⁰ → Gal(K/k)` and `L ⊇ K` from it.

Witness: at its open head, PotentialModularityAndCompatibleSystems §1.20 "Good integral monodromy primes" is a density-one integral-model statement (Larsen–Pink 8.9, Larsen 3.17, Bruhat–Tits II 5.1.40). Its text says "Bogomolov's Hodge–Tate openness theorem supplies openness of `Γ_l` in `G_l(ℚ_l)` … these are distinct inputs": it names Bogomolov 1980 as an input it uses, lists no supplier for it, and states no openness target. A README-only search of every roadmap on main and every open head for "Bogomolov" finds the theorem stated nowhere; searches for "open in its/the Zariski closure", "Galois image is open" and "open subgroup of the ℚ_ℓ-points" find no owner either (ArithmeticGaloisRepresentations §7.6 defines monodromy groups and the predicates `IsPGaloisGeneric`/`IsGaloisGeneric`, it does not prove openness). This is the unrepaired half of the previous R11; the maximal-compact half is repaired (ALS.F.1 states existence, containment and conjugacy for reductive real linear algebraic groups).

Repair: state Bogomolov's theorem as a target of 9.2 with its hypotheses (`A` over a number field `k`, every prime `ℓ`): the image `ρ_{A,ℓ}(G_k)` is open in `G_ℓ^{Zar}(ℚ_ℓ)`, equivalently `Lie(image) = Lie(G_ℓ^{Zar})`. Then delete the "From PotentialModularityAndCompatibleSystems" contract or reduce it to what §1.20 states.
