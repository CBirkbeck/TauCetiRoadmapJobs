## Scope

Own the shared geometric K3 predicate on native schemes over an arbitrary field, its field-extension and complex-analytic comparisons, the geometric realization of the Picard lattice, and the boundedness and automorphism statements below. Smoothness is relative to the field, including imperfect fields. Projectivity is a theorem, not part of the predicate. Neither an embedding, a polarization nor a chosen canonical-bundle trivialization belongs to an unpolarized K3 surface.

Import general coherent cohomology, duality, intersection theory, numerical equivalence, Hilbert schemes and stack theory from their existing owners. Import abstract integral lattices, discriminant forms and their classification from IntegralLattices. This roadmap supplies their K3 instances and comparison maps. It does not replace the existing curve, abelian-variety or analytic-space carriers.

Boundedness has explicit parameters: Picard rank; degree of a field extension making the geometric Picard rank visible; order of a finite automorphism subgroup; or fixed polarization degree in a finite-type moduli problem. There is no assertion that all Picard lattices form a finite set, that all unpolarized K3 surfaces form a bounded family over every field, or that the full symplectic automorphism group is finite. Period maps, Global Torelli, Kuga–Satake, Bloch-group identities, special values and arithmetic Tate/Shafarevich theorems remain outside the endpoints. Any K3 lifting or cohomological calculation needed for the endpoints is genuine prerequisite mathematics to be discharged, not an assumed certificate.

## Final targets

The labels below identify endpoints for the design brief; they are not a proposed implementation sequence. All source numbering refers to the versions in `packages/K3SurfacesAndSymplecticBoundedness/sources.toml`.

1. **K3 predicate and descent.** For a field `k` and a native morphism `X → Spec k`, the predicate means: smooth, proper, geometrically integral, of relative dimension two, with `∧² Ω¹_{X/k} ≅ O_X` and `H¹(X,O_X)=0`. Prove invariance under isomorphism and, for every field extension `K/k`, equivalence with the predicate on `X_K`. On this carrier prove projectivity over `k`. Any auxiliary projectivity theorem used here must retain its general smooth proper surface statement over arbitrary fields. Locator: Huybrechts author draft, Chapter 1, Definition 1.1 and Remark 1.2, p. 7 (`huybrechts line 133`, `huybrechts line 147`); Chapter 17, §2.1, p. 365, for coherent field base change (`huybrechts line 13964`). Faithfully flat descent and the equivalence of geometric and original canonical triviality are applications of the imported generic theory, not a new definition.

2. **Cohomology and complex comparison.** For every such `X/k`, identify `ω_{X/k}` with the exterior-square canonical sheaf and prove `h⁰(O_X)=h²(O_X)=1`, `χ(O_X)=2`, and `χ(L)=2+(L·L)/2` for every invertible sheaf `L`. Prove `H⁰(X,T_X)=0`, the Hodge numbers `h^{p,q}=dim_k H^q(X,Ω^p)` given by `h^{0,0}=h^{2,0}=h^{0,2}=h^{2,2}=1`, `h^{1,1}=20` and all other entries zero, and that geometric integral étale cohomology has ranks `(1,0,22,0,1)` for every prime `ℓ` invertible in `k`. These are characteristic-independent K3 results, including characteristic two. Over `ℂ`, compare coherent cohomology and canonical bundles through the supplied proper GAGA functor; analytification gives a compact complex K3 surface, and the algebraizable complex K3 surfaces are exactly the projective ones. Locators: Chapter 1, §2.3, equation (2.5), pp. 11–12 (`huybrechts line 305`, `huybrechts line 316`), §2.4, p. 13 (`huybrechts line 367`), §3.1 and Proposition 3.2, p. 14 (`huybrechts line 405`); Rizov v2, Proposition 2.2.1 and Remark 2.2.2, p. 6 (`rizov line 218`), and §2.4, p. 7 (`rizov line 273`). General comparison theorems are imported; no new GAGA or general étale-cohomology roadmap is planned here.

3. **Picard lattice and rank boundedness.** For every `X/k` as above, realize `Pic(X)` using the existing line-bundle class group and its intersection pairing; prove it is free of finite rank, even and nondegenerate, with signature `(1,ρ(X)−1)`. Compare to the supplied numerical divisor quotient; over an algebraically closed field identify the algebraic Néron–Severi quotient as well. For every extension `K/k`, pullback on Picard groups is injective and preserves the pairing; it is bijective if `k` is algebraically closed. With `ρ_geom(X)=rank Pic(X_kbar)`, prove `1 ≤ ρ(X) ≤ ρ_geom(X) ≤ 22`, and replace the last bound by `20` in characteristic zero. Prove that some finite extension realizes the whole geometric Picard group. Also prove that there exists a universal integer `B` such that a finite extension of degree at most `B` realizes the geometric Picard **rank**; the same bounded-degree conclusion for the entire Picard group is required only when `k` is finite, or when `char k=0` and `X(k)` is nonempty. Locators: Chapter 1, Proposition 2.4 and Remark 2.5, p. 12 (`huybrechts line 335`); Chapter 17, §2, pp. 364–367, Lemmas 2.2 and 2.6 and Remark 2.4 (`huybrechts line 13945`, `huybrechts line 13971`, `huybrechts line 13994`, `huybrechts line 14001`, `huybrechts line 14038`). Do not replace the arithmetic Picard group by the Galois invariants of the geometric one without the descent obstruction hypotheses.

4. **Symplectic predicate and polarized finiteness.** For any field `k`, define a `k`-automorphism to be symplectic when its pullback acts identically on `H⁰(X,ω_{X/k})`; this is independent of a generator and defines a subgroup, compatible with field extension. For every ample line bundle `L`, the group of automorphisms satisfying `f*L ≅ L` is finite; its representing group scheme over `k` is finite étale. The same finiteness applies to its symplectic subgroup. A chosen isomorphism `f*L ≅ L` is not extra automorphism data: retaining it would introduce scalar automorphisms. Locators: Chapter 15, Definition 1.1, p. 307 (`huybrechts line 11765`); Chapter 5, Proposition 3.3 and Remark 3.4, pp. 91–92 (`huybrechts line 3372`, `huybrechts line 3400`); Rizov v2, Proposition 3.3.3, pp. 14–15 (`rizov line 615`).

5. **Finite-subgroup boundedness over every field.** For every K3 surface `X/k` and every finite subgroup `G ≤ Aut_k(X)`, prove a universal order bound, hence finitely many possible abstract finite groups. A precise, deliberately coarse endpoint is
   `|G| ≤ max(|GL₂₂(F₃)|, |GL₂₂(F₅)|)`.
   This is a derived target: faithful action on geometric rank-22 integral `ℓ`-adic cohomology, with `ℓ=3` unless `char k=3`, and `ℓ=5` in that case, reduces the assertion to the general torsion-freeness of the principal congruence kernel for odd `ℓ`. The latter lemma must be supplied at its general linear-group statement. This covers wild finite groups and characteristic two without a classification assumption. Locators for the geometric inputs: Keum v9, Theorem 1.4, p. 3 (`keum line 120`), Rizov v2, Lemma 3.4.1, p. 15, for characteristic zero (`rizov line 659`), and §2.4 for the integral rank (`rizov line 273`). The displayed numerical bound is our consequence, not a theorem quoted verbatim from these sources.

   Retain the sharper symplectic conclusions with their actual hypotheses. Over characteristic zero a finite symplectic subgroup has order at most `960`. Over algebraically closed characteristic `p>0`, a tame finite symplectic subgroup embeds in `M₂₃`; if either `X` or the minimal resolution of `X/G` is not supersingular of Artin invariant one, it belongs to Mukai's list and has order at most `960`. In the remaining tame case retain the `M₂₃` bound, rather than asserting the five-orbit condition. A nonidentity tame symplectic element has `(order, number of geometric fixed points)` in `{(2,8),(3,6),(4,4),(5,4),(6,2),(7,3),(8,2)}`. A wild finite subgroup can occur only when `p ≤ 11`; it still satisfies the coarse bound above. Statements over nonclosed fields are obtained by geometric base change. Locators: Chapter 15, Theorem 3.1 and the maximal orders, pp. 319–320 (`huybrechts line 12268`); Dolgachev–Keum v5, Theorems 2.1, 3.3 and 4.7, pp. 4, 13 and 19–20 (`dolgachev-keum line 138`, `dolgachev-keum line 507`, `dolgachev-keum line 785`), Theorem 5.2 and Proposition 5.14, pp. 21–22 and 37 (`dolgachev-keum line 862`, `dolgachev-keum line 1566`). These are bounds and necessary conditions, not an assertion that every exceptional candidate is realized.

6. **Fixed-degree moduli boundedness in all characteristics.** For every integer `d>0`, use smooth proper families with geometric K3 fibres and a relatively ample section of the relative Picard sheaf of square `2d`; geometric primitivity is an additional condition. The polarized and primitively polarized moduli stacks are separated Deligne–Mumford stacks of finite type over `Spec ℤ`, the primitive locus is open, and they have separated coarse algebraic spaces. Allow the algebraic-space families used by the source, with their geometric fibres identified with the native scheme predicate. A polarization need only be represented by a line bundle locally on the base; do not assume a universal global line bundle or a fine moduli scheme.

   For an actual ample line bundle `L` of square `2d` on a field-valued K3 surface, prove `P_L(t)=dt²+2`, higher-cohomology vanishing for positive powers, and very ampleness of `L³`, giving an embedding into `P^(9d+1)` with Hilbert polynomial `9dt²+2`. Supply a finite-type atlas with families covering all geometric objects, not merely a set of numerical invariants. Finite-type existence includes primes dividing `2d`. Smoothness of relative dimension `19` is a separate conclusion over `ℤ[1/(2d)]`; no smoothness claim at the excluded primes is requested. Locators: Rizov v2, Theorem 1.2.2, p. 4 (`rizov line 142`), Definition 3.2.3, p. 13 (`rizov line 555`), Theorem 4.3.3, p. 20, and Corollary 4.3.10, p. 25 (`rizov line 898`, `rizov line 1099`); Huybrechts Chapter 2, Theorem 2.7 and its characteristic-two qualification, pp. 27–28 (`huybrechts line 913`, `huybrechts line 943`), Chapter 5, §2.1, pp. 84–86 (`huybrechts line 3102`) and Remark 4.11, p. 96 (`huybrechts line 3577`).

Checks should include a smooth quartic over an arbitrary field, with the Fermat quartic restricted to characteristic different from two; `χ(O_X)=2` and the quartic Hilbert polynomial; identity and composition for the actual symplectic predicate; and a complex example with an infinite symplectic subgroup, preventing an incorrect full-group finiteness endpoint. Over each fixed finite field, fixed-degree polarized isomorphism classes should be finite, retaining the forms/twists rather than identifying them with coarse rational points. Locators: Chapter 1, Example 1.3(i), p. 8 (`huybrechts line 160`); Chapter 15, §2.5(i), p. 318 (`huybrechts line 12221`); Chapter 17, §3 preceding Proposition 3.8, p. 379 (`huybrechts line 14545`).

## Risks

- Much of the general foundation is planned rather than implemented at the pins. Surface duality, relative Picard representability for K3 families, Hilbert/stack infrastructure, proper GAGA and integral étale realizations must be available at their stated generality. The curve-only pieces do not fill the surface gaps.
- No-global-vector-fields, lifting/cohomological rank calculations and faithfulness in characteristic two are deep proof floors. They cannot become hypotheses silently added to the K3 predicate. If crystalline or lifting suppliers are needed, verify their actual earlier contracts; their names alone are insufficient.
- The displayed universal automorphism bound is an elementary consequence of sourced geometric results, not a sourced sharp bound. Its general congruence-kernel lemma needs a real proof or an identified supplier. Sharp complex bounds cannot simply be transported to wild or supersingular positive-characteristic actions.
- Rank boundedness does not bound lattice discriminants or imply finitely many Picard lattices. Arithmetic and geometric Picard groups may differ. Bounded extension degree for rank must not be strengthened to integral Picard descent without the stated extra hypotheses.
- Finite-type polarized moduli over all of `Spec ℤ` must not be replaced by a construction over `ℤ[1/(2d)]`. Conversely, all-prime smoothness, quasi-projectivity of every coarse space, and separatedness for smooth quasi-polarized families are not licensed by these endpoints.
- The missing named supplier/consumer artifacts prevent a complete cross-roadmap cycle audit. Resolve their section contracts during design while preserving the supply direction; this is an inventory limitation, not evidence that their work may be replanned.
- A later `Suggested.lean` must contain only easy headline statements on existing carriers, one `import Mathlib`, and proved checks of this roadmap's own definitions. No sorry'd geometric data, `set_option`, algorithms or assumed boundedness certificates.
