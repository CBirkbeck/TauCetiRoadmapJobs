**The Oh attribution (F2.4, the one new source claim).** The manifest records `oh1996` as paywalled and uncertified. EuDML's record of the paper links a digitized copy at the Göttingen digitization centre (GDZ), an open repository in the brief's list; the per-article download of Math. Z. 222 (logical unit LOG_0043) was fetched through the sources service into a scratch manifest and read (image-only scan, recognised by OCR; file page = printed page − 503). What it says, by printed page:

- p. 506, lines 63–69: the paper's approach perturbs the boundary condition with the complex structure fixed, in three forms (Hamiltonian isotopies, Lagrangian isotopies, totally real isotopies, lines 77–79).
- p. 507, Theorem III (line 136) and p. 519, Theorem 4.2 (line 592): for a dense set of totally real submanifolds isotopic to a given one, every `J`-holomorphic disc satisfying condition (3.5) is Fredholm regular; integrability of `J` can be dropped (line 596, Remark 3.6(i) at line 516).
- p. 512, condition (3.5) (line 344, and the definition of the universal moduli space at lines 326–327): there is a boundary point `z` with `w⁻¹(w(z)) ∩ ∂D² = {z}` and `Dw(z) ≠ 0`. This is the "boundary injective point" the README names.
- p. 516, lines 479–488: the transversality proof. A cokernel element `(η, α)` has normal boundary component `α^⊥ = 0` on an open boundary arc `A` where (3.5) holds, because the boundary variations span the normal bundle at the image of an injective boundary point; then `α = 0` and `η|_A = 0`, and unique continuation gives `η = 0`.
- p. 512, Theorem 3.2 (lines 340–355, quoted from Oh's structure paper): a disc without a boundary injective point factors through a Blaschke product of degree at least 2, under the hypothesis that `(M, J)` contains no nontrivial closed `J`-holomorphic curve; Remark 3.3 (lines 358–375) gives the counterexamples without that hypothesis.

So both README sentences are accurate: Oh's regularity theorem for totally real boundary goes "through boundary injective points", and what F2.4 borrows is "the boundary injective-point argument" (the vanishing of the cokernel's boundary component on an injective arc, then unique continuation). The README's own route perturbs `J` rather than the boundary; the adversarial row below checks that the borrowed step is used where it is valid. The exact locators are now available to the author (Theorem 4.2, p. 519; condition (3.5), p. 512; the argument, p. 516): the README's "by locator" citation and the manifest's `uncertified` status are the settled convention for this key and are not a finding, but the worker may wish to record the GDZ copy.
