## R01 (P2) — Slicing-good does not imply the pointwise gradient formula

**Location:** `packages/GaussianIsoperimetryAndClusters/design/README.md:481`, “at every good `z`”; also `packages/GaussianIsoperimetryAndClusters/design/plan.json:1236`, “at good z”. Good is defined by the slicing conclusions in §6.2, line 472.

**Claim:** Those geometric slicing conclusions suffice to identify the gradient of the section mass at every such point. The source only gives this identification almost everywhere: CianchiFuscoMaggiPratelli2011 line 618, Lemma 4.4, equation (4.8), printed p.12.

**Witness:** Let h(z)=max(−¼,min(z,¼)). Set U_z=(Q(−h),0) for h<0, U_0=(−∞,0), and U_z=(−∞,0)∪(Q(1−h),∞) for h>0. Then v=½+h, v′(0)=1; zero is good, but its boundary sum is 0.

Here Q is the interior Gaussian quantile; no endpoint quantile is evaluated. The detailed verification and a finite-perimeter bound are recorded with the adversarial table below. In particular the witness has an ordinary derivative at zero: merely adding differentiability would not fix the assertion. A Radon–Nikodym density can be changed at zero, but that does not identify it there with the displayed gradient of v.

**Exact repair:** Replace the pointwise assertion in both files by “for volume-almost every good z”, equivalently equality of the absolutely continuous density almost everywhere. Keep §6.2's geometric notion of good, or explicitly refine it by deleting the additional null exceptional set. Add this escaping-tail example as a negative-control Check. In §§6.4 and 6.6 use the density identity under the integral; their statements need no strengthening or weakening. On the witness the repaired formula holds off zero and the two clipping points, with both sides 1 for 0<|z|<1/4 and both sides 0 for |z|>1/4. Thus the repair is tested on the failing instance and preserves every integral consumer.

## R02 (P3) — Adding a dense null exterior does not destroy the ball's interior

**Location:** `packages/GaussianIsoperimetryAndClusters/design/README.md:172`, “respectively becomes all of `ℝ²`”.

The plan's null-modification Check states only the correct perimeter invariance, so it needs no mathematical change.
