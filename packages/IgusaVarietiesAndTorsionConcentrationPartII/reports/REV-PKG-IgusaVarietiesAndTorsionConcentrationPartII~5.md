1. **F01** (P2) Reciprocal roots require the Hecke prime to be invertible

   **Location:** `packages/IgusaVarietiesAndTorsionConcentrationPartII/README.md:297`, §8.5 API: “The roots of `P_v` modulo `𝔪^∨` are the images of the roots modulo `𝔪` under `λ ↦ q_v^{n−1}λ⁻¹`”. See also lines 283, 286 and 290.

   **Claim:** This API is stated for a maximal ideal with finite residue field. Neither that hypothesis nor the involution guarantees that `q_v` is nonzero in the residue field. The integral Hecke algebra admits ideals of residue characteristic different from the fixed cohomological prime `ℓ`. The Conventions expressly allow such ideals for torsion localization. The reciprocal-root assertion therefore needs an additional hypothesis; the general ideal-involution assertions do not.

   **Witness:** At `n=2,q_v=5,ℓ=3`, the degree character modulo 5 has `T_1=1,T_2=1` and is invariant under inversion. Thus both polynomials are `X²−X`, with roots `{0,1}`; reciprocal scaling is undefined, or gives `{0,0}` with total inversion.

   **Exact repair:** Keep the ring involution, maximality and residue-field isomorphism for every finite-residue maximal ideal. State the root formula over an algebraic closure of `κ(𝔪)`, transported along that residue-field isomorphism, under `q_v∉𝔪`. For its stated `𝔽̄_ℓ` specialization, explicitly require `ℓ∈𝔪` and a residue-field embedding into `𝔽̄_ℓ`; `q≠ℓ` then supplies invertibility. Explain that the constant term is nonzero because both `q_v` and the central Hecke operator `T_{n,v}` are units. Carry this hypothesis into the root API and the coefficient calculation at line 290. Add the characteristic-5 example as a negative-control Check. Do not narrow the involution itself or the roadmap to special primes.

   **Repair tested:** The displayed witness is excluded precisely because `q_v=0` in `κ(𝔪)`. For the same degree character modulo 3, `P_v=X²+2` has roots `{1,2}` and the corrected formula sends these to `{2,1}`. The existing `q_v=7,ℓ=11` Check remains valid, with roots `{1,2}` going to `{7,9}`. The residual Galois and concentration theorems already concern characteristic-`ℓ` eigensystems and need no restriction beyond their existing hypotheses.

   The degree character used here is an actual spherical Hecke character: a double coset acts on the trivial representation by its degree. For `GL₂(ℚ₅)`, the first generator has degree 6 and the central generator has degree 1; inverse double cosets have the same degree. Tensor these characters over the spherical factors and reduce modulo 5 to obtain a maximal ideal of the abstract integral Hecke algebra. A compatible Case 1 datum is obtained from the definite quaternion algebra over `ℚ` ramified at 13 and infinity, extended to `ℚ(i)`; 5 is split and good, while 13 and 2 can be included in `S`. Thus this is not a character of an unrelated algebra. CS17 line 4470 concerns eigenvalues modulo `ℓ`, and CS17 lines 4475–4478 apply duality to that characteristic-`ℓ` situation; they do not support the broader API.
