5. **F05** (P3) §4.1 overstates the defect of the source's κ

   **Location.** README line 732: "the source's κ is a positive multiple of the K-Casimir, which does not satisfy `dim τ ≤ κ_τ^{1/2}` with a fixed constant".

   **Witness.** nv21 §A.1, p. 136, offers κ = −c Σ x² for large c. For K = SO(3), κ_τ = c·l(l + 1) and (2l + 1)² ≤ c(l² + l) fails for every c at l = 0 (left side 1, right side 0): the real defect is that the Casimir vanishes on the trivial type, so π(κ) is not invertible and `dim τ ≤ κ_τ^{1/2}` fails at τ trivial, not that the constant cannot be fixed. The "fixed constant" statement is true only for K with at least two positive roots (K = SU(3): dim τ = (a + 1)(b + 1)(a + b + 2)/2 grows like |λ|³ against c^{1/2}|λ|), and summability Σ κ_τ^{−1} < ∞ fails for the Casimir when rank K ≥ 2 (a rank-r torus needs 2L > r, line 742).

   **Repair.** Line 732: "the source's κ = −c Σ x² vanishes on the trivial K-type, so π(κ) is not invertible, and for K of rank ≥ 2 or with two or more positive roots its square root does not dominate dim τ nor is Σ κ_τ^{−1} finite; a high power of 1 − Σ x² satisfies all three properties".
