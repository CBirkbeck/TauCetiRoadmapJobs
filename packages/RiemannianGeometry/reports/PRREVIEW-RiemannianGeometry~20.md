## R02 (P3) — The new Morse/Serre locator at R4.8 cites a hypothesis that fails on part of the headline's range

**Location.** `README.md:1495–1500`: "(a special case of Morse's theorem, … Theorem 13.3, p. 239, infinitely many geodesics between two points once the based loop space has nonzero homology in infinitely many degrees, which Serre's theorem gives on a compact manifold, both as stated in Caponio–Javaloyes … §1, p. 1)"; the same sentence in the references, `:2839–2843`.

**Claim.** `two_distinct_geodesics` (`Suggested.lean:231`) quantifies over every connected compact manifold of dimension at least one. The parenthetical justifies it by Morse's theorem under the hypothesis that `H_*(ΩM; K)` is nonzero in infinitely many degrees, and says Serre's theorem gives that on a compact manifold. It does not: for an aspherical compact manifold the based loop space is homotopy equivalent to the discrete group `π₁(M)`, so its homology lives in degree 0 only. The circle and the flat torus, both in the headline's range, are such manifolds. The sentence is copied from Caponio–Javaloyes's introduction, where it is already an overstatement (see Source issues); Serre's theorem needs a simply connected (or finite-`π₁`, via the universal cover) non-contractible compact manifold. The headline is still true on `S¹` and `T²` (distinct path-homotopy classes from `p` to `q` each contain a length-minimizing geodesic), but the route cited for it does not cover them.

**Witness.** `caponio-javaloyes-morse` line 14–20 (Morse: complete connected Riemannian, homology of `ΩM` nontrivial in infinitely many dimensions; "by a theorem of Serre" this holds if some `H_i(M, K)`, `i > 0`, is nonzero, e.g. `M` compact). Counterexample: `M = S¹`, `H_1(S¹; ℚ) = ℚ ≠ 0`, `ΩS¹ ≃ ℤ`, `H_i(ΩS¹; ℚ) = 0` for every `i > 0`; `M = T²` likewise with `ΩT² ≃ ℤ²`. For contrast `H_k(ΩS²; ℤ) ≅ ℤ` for every `k ≥ 0`, so the hypothesis holds on `S²`.

**Repair.** Split the parenthetical by the fundamental group: "for finite `π₁(M)`, a special case of Morse's theorem (Morse, *The Calculus of Variations in the Large*, Theorem 13.3, p. 239, as recalled in Caponio–Javaloyes §1, p. 1), whose hypothesis, homology of the based loop space in infinitely many degrees, Serre's theorem supplies on the compact simply connected universal cover of positive dimension; for infinite `π₁(M)`, two distinct path-homotopy classes from `p` to `q`, each containing a length-minimizing geodesic by completeness (HopfRinow)". Make the references entry (`:2839–2843`) say the same.
