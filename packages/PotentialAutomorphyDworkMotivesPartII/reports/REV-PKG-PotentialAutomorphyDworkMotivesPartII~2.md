## R01 (P1) — The repaired Levelt overview still compares the wrong spectra

**Location:** README.md:138: “the characteristic polynomials of `h₀` and `h∞` when these have no common root; the triple is then absolutely irreducible.”

**Claim:** Disjoint spectra of h₀ and h∞ suffice.

**Witness:** Over ℚ take h₀=diag(1/2,1/3), h∞=diag(2,4), h₁=diag(1,3/4). Their product is I; rank(h₁−I)=1; the specified spectra are disjoint, but both coordinate lines are invariant.

**Repair:** Replace h₀ by h₀⁻¹ in the overview, consistently with §0.3 and the supplier contract. Add this negative control. The repaired test compares {2,3} with {2,4}, whose intersection is {2}, and correctly excludes the example. This is a newly found remaining occurrence of the inverse-convention defect, not a request to undo the repaired triple translation.

**Witness:** Over ℚ, w(2)=[[0,2],[−1/2,0]], w(−1)=[[0,−1],[1,0]], and w(2)w(−1)=diag(2,1/2). Each w is the three-transvection product in README.md:150.

**Repair:** Delete the denial. If describing the output of the Mathlib factorization, say that it leaves a determinant-one diagonal factor. Cite the existing complete generation theorem identified in R03. The six factors above multiply to the stated diagonal matrix.

## R04 (P1) — The interval-intersection formula can be negative

**Location:** README.md:435: “`#(C ∩ (C + d)) = (L − |d|) + max(0, L + |d| − N)`”.

**Claim:** This integer expression computes the intersection in the self-dual stabilizer proof.

**Witness:** N=11, n=8, c=1, L=3, d=5 satisfy the displayed hypotheses. C={10,0,1} and C+5={4,5,6}; the intersection has size 0, while the expression is −2.

**Repair:** Use `max(0,L−|d|)+max(0,L+|d|−N)`, with integer subtraction, then give the short argument that the value L−1 forces |d|=1. Add the disjoint-interval Check. The corrected expression is 0+0=0 in the witness; at d=1 it is 2=L−1. Natural truncated subtraction cannot silently repair a formula whose convention expressly excludes such truncation.

## R05 (P2) — Fermat eigenlines need the full cyclotomic coefficient field

**Location:** README.md:568: “The same splitting holds … for the self-dual real forms”.

**Claim:** The individual character lines also split over the real coefficient ring.

**Witness:** N=5, n=2, l=3 gives real residue field 𝔽₉. Its multiplicative group has order 8, so it contains no primitive fifth root. The two monodromy eigenvalues ζ₅², ζ₅³ cannot act on 𝔽₉-lines.

**Repair:** State the individual eigenline splitting after extending the real form to O, O_λ or the full cyclotomic residue field. Over the real coefficients retain the descended rank-two blocks pairing conjugate indices. BLGHT11 line 1114 explicitly extends coefficients before the eigenvalue argument. In the example the splitting occurs over 𝔽₈₁, whereas the rank-two object over 𝔽₉ has no eigenline for this operator.

## R06 (P1) — The purported symplectic torsor can have empty geometric fibres

**Location:** README.md:987: the symplectic clause requires “a perfect alternating pairing with multiplier … matching” while the functor also imposes “`∧ⁿφᵢ = ηᵢ`”.

**Claim:** Arbitrary determinant isomorphisms together with matching alternating pairings give a symplectic torsor.

**Witness:** Take n=2, N=5 and primes 11,19. At the first prime identify U with the Fermat fibre and its form, but choose η=2 on its determinant. Every form-preserving matrix has determinant 1; the fibre is empty.

**Repair:** For the symplectic case use pairing-preserving isomorphisms alone, with the determinant map induced by the symplectic volume, or explicitly require η to be that map. Do not retain an independent arbitrary η. This volume is defined by the Pfaffian, without dividing by (n/2)!. Over 𝔽₁₁ the corrected fibre has 11(11²−1)=1320 elements; η=2 has 0. Use the canonical choice at the second prime. The data can be taken over F=ℚ(ζ₅), with U the fibre at t=0 and the global determinant identification supplied by §5.9.

Propagate this condition into §5.12 and the switch in §9.4. Merely matching multipliers does not impose it. BLGGT14 lines 1963–1966 require symplectic isomorphisms, and do not authorize a second incompatible determinant constraint.

## R09 (P2) — The slope bound lacks its essential normalization

**Location:** README.md:1326: “Fix a `p`-adic valuation … slopes … normalised by `deg x`”; then consecutive differences are at most 1.

**Claim:** The bound holds for the valuation and degree as stated, without fixing either scale.

**Witness:** A nontrivial tame Kummer extension on 𝔾ₘ has inertia [[1,1],[0,1]] and geometric Frobenius diag(1,p). It is indecomposable. With v(p)=2 its degree-one slopes are 2,0, whose gap is 2>1.

**Repair:** Specify v(p)=1 and `deg x=[κ(x):𝔽_p]`, not the degree over an arbitrary finite ground field. DK17 lines 194–195 and 204–213 specify both. Add a base-field-extension Check: over 𝔽_{p²}, eigenvalues 1,p² give slopes 0,1 after division by the absolute degree 2; division by the relative degree 1 incorrectly gives 0,2. With the repaired normalization the Kummer example has gap 1.

## R10 (P2) — The untwisted compatible system omits semisimplification

**Location:** README.md:1312: “The untwisted system `{V_{t,λ}}` has purity weight …; for the self-dual data it is a system over `M⁺`”.

**Claim:** The actual untwisted fibres, rather than their semisimplifications, form the imported weakly compatible system.

**Witness:** BCGNT25 lines 2086–2090 explicitly uses semisimplified members. TauCetiRoadmap/PotentialModularityAndCompatibleSystems/README.md:73 requires continuous semisimple members. No target proves arithmetic semisimplicity of every Dwork fibre.

**Repair:** Write `{V^{ss}_{t,λ}}` in both untwisted and self-dual assertions, and distinguish compatibility from a separate semisimplicity theorem. Semisimplification preserves the characteristic polynomials, purity and Hodge multisets used here. A pure unipotent Frobenius matrix [[1,1],[0,1]] has polynomial (X−1)² but is not semisimple; polynomial/purity checks do not fill this gap.

Also correct this passage's locator to BCGNT25 Proposition 4.2.5(3), arXiv v3 p. 42, not p. 43: its statement is between the page marker at line 2059 and lines 2086–2090.

## R11 (P3) — The bad-characteristic Check is false for composite N

**Location:** README.md:819: “If `λ | N` then `ζ_N ≡ 1 (mod λ)`”.

**Claim:** Every prime dividing N kills the entire root of unity.

**Witness:** N=15 at characteristic 3: Φ₁₅ reduces to Φ₅². The image of ζ₁₅ has order 5, not 1. For the rank-two self-dual indices 7,8 its eigenvalues still have order 5.

**Repair:** State that reduction kills only the l-primary part of the order. Use N an l-power for a Check in which every root becomes 1, and keep N=15,l=3 as a contrary Check. Such excluded-prime examples are taken over ℤ[ζ_N], not over the already localized coefficient ring. For N=9,l=3 the reduced order is 1; for N=15,l=3 it is 5.

## R12 (P3) — The Gauss-sum Check confuses residue characteristic with residue cardinality

**Location:** README.md:1072: “`n = 2`, `l = 3`, `N = 301`, `p = 5` … `g(b)g(−b) = 5`”.

Thus the repaired Check still tests the intended descent argument.

## R13 (P3) — Cyclotomic determinant nontriviality needs a condition on the base field

**Location:** README.md:968: “is nontrivial for `n > 1` and `l > n(n−1)/2 + 1`”.

**Witness:** Set n=3, N=401, l=7 and F=ℚ(ζ₄₀₁,ζ₇). The inequality is 7>4, but ε̄₇ restricted to G_F is 1, and its negative third power is also 1.

**Repair:** Add `ord(ε̄_l(G_F)) ∤ n(n−1)/2`, or present a specific base field where the cyclotomic image is full. For F=ℚ(ζ₄₀₁) the image at 7 has order 6 and the negative third power has order 2; adjoining ζ₇ changes that order to 1. Include both computations.

## R14 (P3) — The ramified-base-change Check identifies the wrong obstruction

**Location:** README.md:1401: “`US² = Z₁⋯Z_r` … (the special fibre is nonreduced)”.

**Claim:** This base change makes the special fibre nonreduced.

**Witness:** With U=1,r=2 the special fibre is k[x,y]/(xy), which is reduced. The total space xy=S² is singular at the origin. With r=1, x=S² is smooth over k[[S]].

**Repair:** For r≥2 at the crossing, explain failure of regularity of the total space, not nonreducedness of the fibre. State the smooth r=1 case separately. The reducedness test gives radical ideal (xy); the total-space local ring has dimension 2 and embedding dimension 3. Subdivision resolves the latter defect.

## R15 (P3) — The pullback irreducibility Check supplies no counterexample

**Claim:** These examples demonstrate that an irreducible sheaf can become reducible after finite pullback.

**Witness:** A rank-one trivial sheaf remains irreducible. A direct sum of two characters is reducible before pullback. Neither example has the claimed before/after behavior.

Realize this through an S₃-cover if phrasing the Check geometrically. The dimensions of irreducible constituents change from [2] to [1,1].
