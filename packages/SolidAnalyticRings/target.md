## Scope

Develop the general light solid spectral coefficient library and its analytic module structures. The new work comprises countable product tensor comparisons, split finite free towers, balanced scalar change, countable-limit interchange, spectral null families and bounded-below realization comparisons. Coefficients retain the sphere and its higher homotopy groups. Neither a single arithmetic completion nor an Eilenberg–Mac Lane replacement defines the scope.

There is existing work to preserve. The board package `packages/RefinedTraceMethods/README.md`, §6.2, already plans **Light condensed and solid spectra**, including the discrete embedding, null-sequence solidity, reflection, compact generator and bounded-below p-complete comparison. Transfer ownership of that foundational block to this earlier supplier, retaining its existing construction and statements; the later §6.2 imports it. This is a relocation, not a second design of those foundations. Trace-class maps, nuclear modules, perfect-even sites and trace filtrations remain in RefinedTraceMethods §§6.2–6.4. The relocation is necessary because RefinedTraceMethods consumes HabiroRings, which needs this supplier first.

Import `TauCetiRoadmap/VStackSheavesAndLisseCategories/README.md`, VS2.1, for abelian solidity and its enhanced derived category, and VS2.2 for analytic measures and the already planned integer-linear analytic structures. Own their comparison with the light spectral setting and the additional spectral coefficient structures. Do not rebuild Breen–Deligne resolutions, integer solidification, analytic spectra or geometric gluing. An animated ring, an Hℤ-algebra and an arbitrary spectral algebra are distinct inputs; identify them only through proved comparison functors.

Habiro completion, its factorial ideals, cyclotomic descent, scalar idempotence and the arithmetic ring remain in `TauCetiRoadmap/HabiroRings/README.md`, §§2.6–2.12, and its named suppliers. They consume the general comparisons here. Banach/Smith functional analysis, profinite group representation theory, analytic stacks and six operations remain separate subjects. Existing references to prospective sections SA.1–SA.4 are requests, not existing section ownership.

## Final targets

These are endpoints for the design brief, not a layer or API plan. Statements explicitly described below as spectral extensions require proofs; their locators identify the source model or argument rather than certify an unwritten generalization.

1. **Light spectral setting, preserving the existing plan.** Fix universes and use the site of second-countable profinite sets with finite jointly surjective covers. Construct its hypercomplete spectrum-valued sheaves, Cond(Sp), as a presentable stable closed symmetric monoidal category. For every spectrum X, the discrete functor δ satisfies δ ⊣ evaluation at the point, the adjunction unit is an equivalence and δ is symmetric monoidal. It therefore preserves colimits; preservation of arbitrary inverse limits is not a target. Reuse the ambient construction of RefinedTraceMethods §6.2. Locators: W §2, paragraph 2.1, p.11, W line 437; MW §4.1, paragraph 4.1, p.41, MW line 1748; the hypersheaf definition is also RC Definition 4.0.1, pp.27–28, RC line 1392.

2. **Solidity and its generator, preserving the existing plan.** Let N be the cofiber of the free condensed sphere on the point at infinity into the free sphere on the convergent sequence ℕ∪{∞}; let σ be induced by n↦n+1. An object M is solid precisely when 1−σ* on the internal Hom from N to M is invertible. Discrete spectra are solid. The inclusion Sp■→Cond(Sp) creates small limits and colimits and has an accessible symmetric monoidal reflector L■, with M⊗■N=L■(M⊗N). The object G=L■N is the countable product of spheres and is a compact generator. For every E₁-algebra A in Sp■, A⊗■G is a compact generator of its left module category. Retain the existing p-complete comparison for every prime, including its boundedness restriction and its completed tensor unit. Locators: RefinedTraceMethods §6.2; W paragraphs 2.1–2.3, pp.11–12, W line 443, W line 452 and W line 472. The integer-linear comparison is imported from VS2.1; RC Theorem 3.2.4(5)–(13), pp.17–18, RC line 761, is its light model.

3. **Product, tower and balanced tensor comparisons.** For all countable sets I,J, including finite and empty sets, prove the natural equivalence (∏I 𝕊)⊗■(∏J 𝕊)≃∏(I×J) 𝕊. If P and Q are sequential towers of finite free sphere modules with specified coherent sphere-linear splittings of their transition surjections, prove

   (lim δP)⊗■(lim δQ) ≃ lim(m,n) δ(P_m⊗Q_n).

   For a commutative algebra A in Sp■ and A-modules X,Y, identify the balanced tensor with (X⊗■Y)⊗■_(A⊗■A)A, using multiplication A⊗■A→A. Supply the coherent bar-realization and scalar-change comparisons needed to apply this identity after the independent-tower comparison. A splitting over the sphere does not become A-linear. Scalar extension through an arbitrary inverse limit requires its own hypothesis or proof. Locators: Q, proof of Lemma B.8, printed pp.79–80, Q line 3672 and Q line 3685; the product model is RC Theorem 3.2.4(13), RC line 777. This extends that argument to a reusable spectral theorem; the particular arithmetic balanced multiplication remains the consumer's theorem.

4. **Countable-limit interchange.** For every small category K whose nerve has countably many simplices, every small ω₁-filtered category J and every diagram F:J×K→Cond(Sp), prove that colim_j lim_k F(j,k)→lim_k colim_j F(j,k) is an equivalence. Establish the same assertion in Sp■ and in modules over a fixed solid spectral algebra. Ordinary filteredness is insufficient. Locators: Q, proof of B.8, p.80, Q line 3688; RC Remark 4.0.3, p.28, RC line 1411. These motivate the spectral/site proof obligation, not a general interchange law in every presentable category. Import any abstract cardinal-filtered theorem from EnhancedDerivedSheaves E0.8/E5:presentability rather than replanning it.

5. **Spectral null families for complete filtered coefficients.** Require a general theorem for the following data: a connective commutative solid spectral algebra A, elements a_r of π₀A(*) with a₀=1 and coherent divisibility a_r∣a_s for r≤s, A≃lim_r A/a_r, and a quotient tower whose underlying spectra are discrete finite free sphere modules with coherent sphere-linear split surjections. Quotients are derived cofibers. Write J_r=fib(A→A/a_r) and Λ_a(M)=lim_r(M⊗■_A A/a_r). For countable sets I_j, prove the natural comparison

   Λ_a(⊕j ∏(i∈I_j) A) ≃ colim_f ∏j ∏(i∈I_j) J_(f(j)),

   where j∈ℕ, f:ℕ→ℕ tends to infinity, and f→g means f≥g pointwise. Establish it as an equivalence of spectra, and identify every homotopy sheaf π_k, k∈ℤ, with the corresponding filtered colimit of product homotopy sheaves. Include naturality for maps factoring each input block through finitely many output blocks. Locators: the spectral model is Q, proof of B.8, p.80, Q line 3688; the abelian p-adic model is Bosco Lemma A.4, p.93, Bosco line 3488. The stated general filtered spectral theorem is a proof obligation beyond those texts. Its hypotheses must be checked in the design. It must cover general split finite free quotient towers, not just the Habiro coefficients. The proper-profile poset is filtered, but not ω₁-filtered, so endpoint 4 cannot be applied to this profile colimit.

6. **Bounded-below resolutions and realizations.** Equip Sp■, and modules over connective solid spectral algebras, with the compatible homotopy t-structure and the comparison with integer-linear solid homology. Bounded-below objects admit uniformly bounded-below simplicial resolutions by coproducts of shifted free solid generators. For the derived quotient completion in endpoint 5, and a simplicial module M• with one lower homotopy bound valid in every simplicial degree, prove |Λ_a(M•)|≃Λ_a(|M•|), naturally and compatibly with the balanced tensor comparisons. State the uniform connectivity estimates that justify this equivalence. The p-adic specialization must recover the existing bounded-below comparison rather than weaken its hypotheses. Locators: Q Lemma B.8 and its proof, pp.79–80, Q line 3666 and Q line 3688; Bosco Proposition A.3 and its resolution argument, pp.92–93, Bosco line 3459; W paragraph 2.2, W line 452. General spectral resolutions and realization compatibility remain explicit proof obligations.

7. **Spectral solid analytic coefficients.** For a connective commutative algebra A in Sp■, develop uncompleted analytic coefficient structures as reflective stable full subcategories D of Mod_A(Sp■), closed under small limits, colimits and internal Hom from arbitrary A-modules, whose reflector preserves connective objects. A completed analytic structure additionally requires A∈D. Prove the resulting closed symmetric monoidal complete tensor L_D(M⊗■_A N), retaining its unit L_D(A). For morphisms A→B whose restriction carries complete B-modules to complete A-modules, prove that completed scalar extension is left adjoint to restriction, with identity, composition and base-change coherences. Construct the spectral summability localizations tested by 1−fσ* on Hom_A(A⊗N,M), for specified families f∈π₀A(*); retain the completed coefficient algebra if completion changes A. Compare with the already owned integer-linear measure formalism, rather than assert an equivalence for all condensed universes. Locators: AG Definition 12.1, Proposition 12.4 and Remark 12.5, pp.79–80, AG line 3014 and AG line 3041; RC Definition 4.1.1 and Proposition 4.1.6, pp.28–30, RC line 1431 and RC line 1489; the f-solidity model is MW paragraph 4.2, p.41, MW line 1764. These sources formulate the analytic theory with animated/integer-linear coefficients; its sphere-linear extension and comparison are additional obligations here.

Checks include finite and empty index sets, Hℤ coefficients, every prime in the p-adic comparison, and the independent-variable versus balanced tensor distinction. The Habiro quotient tower is a consumer Check of the general results. No restriction to good primes, a fixed field or static homotopy is permitted.

## Risks

- The board already owns part of the target. Failing to relocate that exact RefinedTraceMethods block both duplicates planning and introduces a supply cycle. Its remaining trace and nuclearity theory must stay downstream.
- The pins contain light condensed modules and ordinary solidity predicates, not the required presentable category of spectral hypersheaves. The enhanced foundations and concrete-spectrum adapters are substantial prerequisites, not declaration aliases.
- W, MW and Q recall or sketch spectral foundations. Bosco proves an integer-linear p-adic theorem. Endpoints 3–7 require genuine spectral proofs; in particular the general filtered hypotheses in endpoint 5 need mathematical validation. Access certification of a PDF does not certify these extensions.
- Countable products, filtered profile colimits, completed coproducts, point evaluation and ordinary spectral products must keep their distinct comparison maps. No tensor–arbitrary-limit law or ω₁-filteredness of the profile index follows from the cited material. Boundedness must be uniform through simplicial degree.
- Analytic rings in the read sources are animated/integer-linear. Extending them to sphere-linear coefficients must preserve the analytic axioms and verify the Hℤ comparison. Light and unrestricted condensed categories are not interchangeable; no claim covers all size cutoffs automatically.
- The public transcript contains automated transcription errors. A legal alternative was found for its initially unavailable repository URL, so there is no outstanding missing-text request. The proof gaps above are mathematical obligations, not requests for unavailable books.
- The later `Suggested.lean` must contain only headline theorem acceptance statements with `sorry`, necessary simple definitions, one `import Mathlib`, no sorry'd data and no `set_option`. Missing supplier carriers cannot be hidden in axioms or abstract replacement structures. This triage writes no Lean.
