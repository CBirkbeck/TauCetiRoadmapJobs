The checks below combine direct mathematical reasoning with exact integer/rational computations. Computations are finite adversarial tests, not proofs of the universal signatures. For the realization-uniqueness claim (README 145 and 151), I also checked the argument beyond the small examples: the group of linear automorphisms permuting a finite spanning root set embeds into its finite permutation group. Averaging a positive form over that group makes every candidate reflection orthogonal. Its prescribed minus-one line is the root line, so the orthogonal reflection and normalized coroot functional are unique. Thus the chamber and cone theorems are legitimately quantified over any `OrbitSumRealization`; their conclusions are not fields of that structure.

The component-cycle argument supplies actual restriction and diagonal inverse maps, not a dimension-count substitute. Positive-root descent keeps each root within one connected component. The maximal-root proof in §1.2 uses only the supplied negative-pairing root-sum fact, nonoppositeness and off-diagonal signs. The fixed-Weyl proof uses right inversion descent and a strictly dominant fixed vector, without importing its own conclusion. The ambient argument uses the nonsingular simple-coroot evaluation matrix to split root span and common kernel. In the torsion argument, the exact monoid difference lies in the root subgroup, so injectivity there both separates realized endpoints and lifts the residual budget. No injectivity on all of A is asserted.

Fields of positive characteristic, nonreduced systems, infinite root sets and affine root systems are outside the explicit real, finite, reduced hypotheses. In particular, the positive-definite step in Stembridge Claim 4 is never used for affine input. Empty and nonspanning cases are distinguished: a rootless *spanning* realization has zero ambient space, while §4.4 allows arbitrary central directions. All orbit-size, length and cycle denominators are positive where used; no period-zero or natural-subtraction junk formula appears.
