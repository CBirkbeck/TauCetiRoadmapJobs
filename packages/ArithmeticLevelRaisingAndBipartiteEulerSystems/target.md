## Scope

**SelmerIwasawaCohomology, Part II: arithmetic level raising and bipartite Euler systems.** Continue the filed Selmer producer with unitary Shimura arithmetic level raising, its diagonal Abel–Jacobi classes, the two explicit reciprocity laws, and the resulting Rankin–Selberg Selmer rank theorems. The requested job artifacts remain in `packages/ArithmeticLevelRaisingAndBipartiteEulerSystems/`; the eventual roadmap folder is `TauCetiRoadmap/SelmerIwasawaCohomologyPartII/`. The Part II naming rule takes precedence over retaining the descriptive suggested folder name.

Develop reusable level-raising and reciprocity interfaces for every CM extension, rank, coefficient field, lattice, level and congruence exponent covered by the sources. Import general continuous Galois cohomology, Bloch–Kato local conditions, Selmer groups, duality, Shimura varieties, automorphic Galois representations, Chow/cycle-class theory and deformation/patching theory from their existing owners. Own their level-raising constructions and compatibility here. Any abstract bipartite-system formalism already owned by EulerSystemsAndKolyvaginSystems is imported; the new obligation is this arithmetic realization and the deductions not already owned. Ordinary norm-compatible Euler systems must not be substituted for the source's level-raising system.

Do not impose a global good-reduction, rational-base-field, fixed-rank or fixed-model restriction. Conversely, keep the source's actual admissibility and special-inert-place hypotheses on the theorems that need them. A general library does not turn a source's restricted theorem into an unsupported all-prime theorem. Hilbert modular supersingular geometry and Liu–Tian's precursor are background/import checks against HilbertModularVarietiesAndShimuraCurves, not a second independent Hilbert modular roadmap. Heights, arithmetic GGP derivative identities and a proof of the full Beilinson–Bloch–Kato conjecture are outside this continuation.

## Final targets

These are endpoints, not implementation stages. Locators refer to the preprint versions in Sources. Let F/F⁺ be any CM quadratic extension of a totally real number field, n≥2, and n₀,n₁ the even and odd members of {n,n+1}. Put rα=⌊nα/2⌋. Take irreducible cuspidal automorphic representations Πα of GL(nα,A_F) with Πα composed with complex conjugation isomorphic to Πα∨, and archimedean principal-series characters with exponents 1−nα,3−nα,…,nα−1 on z/|z|. Let E⊂C be a common strong coefficient number field: it contains both rationality fields and realizes each associated λ-adic Galois representation over Eλ with the prescribed scalar-extension compatibility. Write

`Vλ = ρΠ₀,λ ⊗Eλ ρΠ₁,λ(n)`.

Here the final `(n)` is the Tate twist of the tensor product. Locators: Definition 1.1.3, p. 3 (ltxzz line 124), Definition 3.2.5, p. 27 (ltxzz line 1222), and §7.1, p. 120 (ltxzz line 5822).

1. **Exact admissibility and auxiliary-prime interfaces.** Define admissibility of a coefficient prime λ|ℓ relative to this pair using all seven conditions of Definition 8.1.1, pp. 131–132 (ltxzz line 6368). They require: ℓ>4n and unramified in F; no ℓ-adic places in the union Σ⁺min of the two ramification sets; absolute irreducibility of the tensor representation; residual absolute irreducibility of each factor; the polarized-lattice and residual-image conditions (L5-1),(L5-2); rigidity of the even factor together with absolute irreducibility after restriction to F(ζℓ); and cohomological genericity of both residual Hecke characters. These are separate obligations, not a single unspecified big-image assumption.

   In (L5), retain lattices Rα in ρΠα,λ(rα), their (1−α)-polarizations and independence of bases/polarizations. For R=R₀⊗R₁, (L5-1) permits either a nonidentity scalar in the residual image, or the semisimplicity, dimension bound `dim R̄ ≤ min((ℓ+1)/2,ℓ−3)` and vanishing `HomΓF(End R̄,R̄)=0` of Lemma 2.3.5 (ltxzz line 678). Use (L5-2)'s exact residual image condition over the real reflexive closure; Remark 8.1.2 spells it out by an element (γ₀,γ₁,ξ) with ξ²≠1, conjugate-coset components of order prime to ℓ, simple eigenvalue 1 in hγ₀, hγ₁ and their tensor, and exclusion of −1 and −ξ in the respective factors (ltxzz line 6390). Rigidity means minimal ramification at Σ⁺min, the specified eigenvalue pair at added raising places, regular Fontaine–Laffaille crystallinity above ℓ, and unramifiedness elsewhere (Definition 6.3.4, ltxzz line 5623). Cohomological genericity quantifies over all standard indefinite spaces, permitted levels and enlarged exceptional sets, with localized cohomology vanishing outside degree N−1 (Definition D.1.1, p. 171, ltxzz line 9474).

   Keep coefficient admissibility distinct from auxiliary special/very-special inert places (Definition 3.3.4, p. 28, ltxzz line 1293), Satake genericity (Definition 3.1.5, p. 23, ltxzz line 1044), and mod-λᵐ level-raising conditions. For a reciprocal monic Satake polynomial P over a ring in which q is invertible, Tate genericity in odd rank requires P′(1) invertible; level-raising specialness in even rank requires P(q)=0 and P′(q) invertible. Intertwining genericity requires P(−q) invertible in odd rank and P(−1) invertible in even rank. Apply these over the stated residual or congruence ring. A special inert place lies over an odd rational prime unramified in F and has F⁺-completion Q_p. Preserve the reflexive-closure refinement for very-special places. Supply the source's auxiliary-place existence/detection statements using imported Chebotarev and cohomology. All-but-finitely-many admissibility is a theorem for the families of Lemmas 8.1.3–8.1.4 (ltxzz line 6400, ltxzz line 6418), not an unconditional property of every pair.

2. **Arithmetic level raising on native unitary Shimura cohomology.** For every positive m and the definite/indefinite unitary data in §6.1, including its lattice, level, exceptional-set, transferability, CM-type and (P1)–(P6) conditions, export the odd-rank Tate-cycle comparison and the even-rank integral arithmetic level-raising theorem. In the even case N=2r, retain residual absolute irreducibility, localized nearby-cycle cohomology concentrated in degree N−1 and free there, Hypothesis 3.2.10, ℓ>2(N+1) unramified in F, rigidity, irreducibility over F(ζℓ), residual cohomological genericity and nonzero localized definite forms. The theorem gives integral freeness and the three-term E₂ concentration, its Frobenius restrictions, the singular-local-cohomology comparison with definite Shimura functions modulo the Hecke congruence ideal, and the Galois-module realization in copies of the congruence lattice. This is stronger than existence of an automorphic congruence.

   In odd rank N=2r+1, the map from the middle E₂ term to localized definite Shimura functions is surjective; with Assumptions 6.1.4, 6.1.6 and Hypothesis 3.2.10 it induces an isomorphism on Frobenius coinvariants, and the middle term is weakly semisimple with the stated residual Frobenius eigenvalues. Locators: §6.1, pp. 106–107 (ltxzz line 5136, ltxzz line 5164, ltxzz line 5204); Theorem 6.2.3, p. 109 (ltxzz line 5304); Theorem 6.3.5(1)–(5), pp. 115–116 (ltxzz line 5634). Preserve the hypotheses separately for the odd and even theorems; do not impose even-rank rigidity on the whole library. Reuse generic weight spectral sequences and patching rather than making their conclusions certificates supplied by the user.

3. **Diagonal cycle and Selmer-valued Abel–Jacobi class.** For every standard indefinite pair V_n⊂V_(n+1), self-dual lattices away from Σ⁺min and compatible levels as in Theorem 8.3.2, let X be the product of the two Shimura varieties over F. Construct its codimension-n graph cycle Δ and the Hecke-projected Hochschild–Serre Abel–Jacobi class

   `AJλ(Δ) ∈ H¹(F, H^(2n−1)_ét(X_F̄,Eλ(n))/(ker φΠ₀,ker φΠ₁))`.

   Prove its finite local conditions under the source hypotheses and relate its coefficient representation to a finite multiplicity of Vλ through the imported cohomological realization. Preserve multiplicities and complex-conjugation/duality conventions; Δλ is not canonically a chosen vector in one copy of H¹_f(F,Vλ). Functoriality in compatible level maps and coefficients must use the imported cycle and cohomology operations. Locators: §8.3 preceding Theorem 8.3.2, equation (8.10), p. 139 (ltxzz line 6764, ltxzz line 6771, ltxzz line 6773), and the Selmer-membership argument, pp. 139–140 (ltxzz line 6796); purity input Lemma 8.1.6, p. 133 (ltxzz line 6484).

The assumptions include residual absolute irreducibility of both factors, rigidity and cyclotomic-restriction irreducibility of the even factor, integral middle-degree concentration/freeness, and even-factor cohomological genericity. The place conditions include ℓ>2(n₀+1), unramifiedness in F, ℓ∤p(p²−1), the CM-type condition, individual and tensor Satake level-raising conditions, intertwining genericity, and the specified nonzero localization comparison.

   Under this comparison the singular localization of the diagonal Abel–Jacobi class pairs with each permitted test function f as

   `(p+1) φΠ₀(I°_(n₀,p)) φΠ₁(T°_(n₁,p)) Σ_s f(s,sh°↑(s))`.

   Keep the Hecke ideals, test-function modules and pairing of Theorem 7.2.8; the operators and factor p+1 cannot be suppressed as unnamed units. Include the consequent equality of annihilator exponents between the singular localized class and the pushed-forward diagonal characteristic function. Locators: §7.2, pp. 120–121 (ltxzz line 5835, ltxzz line 5875, ltxzz line 5918); Proposition 7.2.7 and Theorem 7.2.8(1)–(3), p. 124 (ltxzz line 6024, ltxzz line 6037); Corollary 7.2.9, p. 126 (ltxzz line 6158).
