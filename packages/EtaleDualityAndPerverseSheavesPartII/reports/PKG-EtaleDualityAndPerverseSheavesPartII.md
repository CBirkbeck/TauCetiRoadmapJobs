## Corrections and source limits

The accepted corrections retained in the package are: the md adjunction and its proper codomain; the degree-zero hypercover condition; the higher-graded approximation comparison; geometric irreducibility in equivariant curve alteration; bounded-below torsion abelian descent; the correct residue-field direction in dimension transport; codimension-zero/one derived transitions and only group-level higher transitions; the joint top-class induction using regular pinned complexes; componentwise coefficient shifts for composite n; and use of supported completion before the excellent local-duality argument.

The package also makes the following corrections, with explicit numerical witnesses in the tables below. Formal open exchange is justified by the read CS24 proof and its earlier supplier contract; no unread SGA 5 citation is used. PerfectoidQuotients references identify §§0.4–0.6 separately from the numbered results of CS24. The Godement t-structure route does not consume duality or its open-exchange contract:

- **Prime-to-ℓ coverings:** generic degrees alone do not upgrade an arbitrary weak cover. The connected étale cubic over the excellent trait 𝔽₃[t]_(t), after deleting its degree-one special point, has generic degree 3 and only a degree-two special lift. It is not an alt_2′ cover. The Check now uses the proper/Nisnevich standard form and records the counterexample.

- **Nonreduced bases:** a reduced regular source cannot be birationally isomorphic on a dense open of a generically nonreduced base. The standard modification is taken over X_red, with the reduction map retained.

- **Ordinary versus sheafified purity:** the claim that ordinary support cohomology vanishes in every degree other than 2d is false without a separably closed residue field. A DVR with finite residue field has H³_s(μ_n)=H¹(k,ℤ/n), which can be ℤ/n. Only the sheafified purity complex is concentrated in degree 2d; ordinary low-degree vanishing and the correctly twisted examples remain.

- **Duality coefficients:** Layer EQ.6 now states n≥2, as ILO XVII does (ILO14 line 12199). At n=1 every object is zero, so the doubled-object non-example and the geometric finite-dimension criterion fail to distinguish anything.

- **Ideal algebraization:** the geometric reformulation retains closed subschemes and their ideals. Equal supports do not distinguish (x) and (x²), whose quotient lengths are 1 and 2.

- **Characteristic-p identity Check:** an arbitrary henselian ring of characteristic p need not equal its perfectoid completion or its completed tilt. The identity Check is for complete perfect characteristic-p rings; the general comparison theorem still has the full henselian-pair hypotheses.

- **Rank-zero valuation factors:** for an algebraically closed field and ϖ=0, the generic open is empty and has H⁰=0. The singleton fraction-field Check now requires a rank-one valuation ring and nonzero ϖ in its maximal ideal.

- **Dimension-function example:** a strictly henselian trait has δ(η)=1 and δ(s)=0; this does not prove it excellent. The unsupported excellence assertion is absent.

- **Relative cohomological dimension:** the finite-dimensional-base corollary explicitly retains that ℓ is invertible on the base. The d=0 counterexample uses integer 2d−1=−1, not truncated natural subtraction.

- **Oriented proper descent:** the ordinary proper Čech-descent Check now uses a separably closed point as base. A strict trait has three underlying self-oriented point pairs, whereas its ordinary self-fibre product has two; the latter cannot replace the former.

- **Trait duality normalization:** the supplier’s unshifted dualizing Λ has closed support in degree 2; the normalized Λ(1)[2] has closed support in degree 0. The Check now states the precise invertible twist and shift relating them.

- **Standard-form and truncation Checks:** the finite-flat identity standard form uses integral base and source; the truncation inequality N≥q+1 is a sufficient general bound, not a necessary bound for a constant hypercover.

- **Perfect-field support Check:** full support over an algebraically closed characteristic-p field gives G in degree 0. Perfectness alone is insufficient: H¹(𝔽_p,ℤ/ℓ)=ℤ/ℓ. The example now includes that negative control. In the audit, a one-parameter root tower has generic tame H¹=ℤ/ℓ(−1), unlike an algebraically closed perfectoid field, whose generic H¹ is 0.

- **Proof locators and proof claims:** ILO XVII §5.2, rather than Definition 5.2.1, is cited for the pointwise perverse conditions. SGA4half [Th. finitude] 4.3 is restricted to its finite-Tor-dimension domain; the all-constructible extension is imported separately. Epp and Illusie are cited as uncertified original texts. Their complete proofs are not claimed to have been audited, and guessed semistable proof prerequisites were removed from the trait route.

All statements that depend on the original Epp or Illusie theorem are supported by the read ILO statements, with original locators kept as uncertified citations. The two original proof-audit gaps remain in the accepted plan. The non-abelian oriented-descent companion is explicitly conditional on continuity for the strict-local inverse systems and proper direct images of ind-finite stacks; none of the abelian or tilting proofs consumes that assertion. No unresolved legal-source request remains and no settled uncertified citation is put into a missing-reference request.

### Conventions

| Convention | Instances and computed values |
| --- | --- |
| Nonzero local rings and universes | A field has maximal ideal 0 and dimension 0; the zero ring has 0=1 and is excluded by IsLocalRing. A product indexed by the empty set is the zero ring, allowed only in the perfectoid examples. |
| Finite coefficients and duality n≥2 | For n=1, ZMod 1 has one element and every sheaf module is 0; for n=2, Λ has 2 elements while Λ⁴ has 16. The latter distinguishes the doubled nondualizing candidate. |
| Cohomological shift and Tate sign | Λ[2] has H^(−2)=Λ and H²=0. On a strict trait H²_s(Λ(1))=Λ and therefore RΓ_s(Λ(1)[2])=Λ in degree 0; on a strict regular surface the corresponding degree is 4 and twist 2. |
| Specialization and dimension sign | η⇝s on a trait has codimension 1, δη=1,δs=0 and pη=−2,ps=0; reversing either sign gives the wrong generic degree for Λ(1)[2]. |
| Extended support dimensions | δ(0)=−∞, δ(skyscraper at s)=0 and δ(lisse nonzero sheaf on a trait)=1; subtracting any q from −∞ leaves −∞. |
| Alteration degree conventions | Allowed degrees include 2 for ell=1 and ell=3, exclude 2 for ell=2, and only permit 1 for ell=∞. The empty divisor has support ∅ and pulls back to ∅. |
| Generic fibres and residue equations | For a power-series identity parametrization the residue map is constant coefficient and generic degree is 1. A ramified π^ℓ=p extension can have residue degree 1 but generic degree ℓ, so the two degrees cannot be substituted. |
| Henselian perfectoid pair and quotient exponent | For A=𝒪_C and ϖ=p^(1/p), the powered quotient is A^♭/(ϖ^♭)^p≅A/p, while the unpowered quotient is A^♭/ϖ^♭≅A/p^(1/p). These are related by Frobenius, not by silently deleting p. |
| Total-function and cardinality boundaries | For d=0 the local-Lefschetz threshold is the integer −1, not natural 0; the mixed Cohen bundle has a field 2≤d and cannot occur for d=0,1, so d−1 is only used in its intended range. Empty ideal families still require dim B<dim A. |
| Tilting identity domain and rank-zero factors | For complete perfect A of characteristic p, A^♭=A. For a rank-zero field with ϖ=0, D(ϖ)=∅ and H⁰=0, whereas the full spectrum has H⁰=G. For 𝒪_C the generic fibre is C, and the localized tilt is C^♭, of characteristic p rather than 0. |
