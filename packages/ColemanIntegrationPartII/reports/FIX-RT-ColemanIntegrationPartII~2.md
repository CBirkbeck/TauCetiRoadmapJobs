## Mathematical evidence and ownership

The trace-torus construction uses a finite characteristic-zero extension L/k and a basis only to present the genuine principal open of its norm polynomial. It does not require a general Weil-restriction supplier. Its four API targets identify points, closed invariant forms, scalar pullback and the pullback of traced rational logarithmic forms. The finite-extension topology and uniqueness theorem then specialize k to each closed K⊆ℂ_p.

For the uniqueness step, let δ be the difference between any axiomatic integral of η_c and `Tr(c log_univ z)`. Naturality for multiplication and projections makes δ a homomorphism; equal differentials and local analyticity make its kernel open. Restriction to scalar points gives δ(K×)=0. A unit has finite-order residue, and principal-unit p-power powers approach 1 by the displayed binomial estimate. Thus δ kills units. Completeness makes all conjugate absolute values equal, so `z^n/N(z)` is a unit and `n δ(z)=δ(N(z))=0`. Characteristic zero finishes the proof. This works at p=2 and p=3 and over nondiscrete closed fields.

For the quadratic Check, conjugate summation gives η_c=`(x dy-y dx)/(x^2-2y^2)` when α²=2 and c=1/(2α). Substitution `(x,y)=(t,-1)` gives `dt/(t^2-2)`. Swapping the basis exchanges coordinates and preserves the form. The norm-nonzero condition excludes the zero element. The other new Check uses the closed field generated by a compatible tower of p-power roots of p: scaled principal units form a separated sequence in each identity neighborhood, precluding compact neighborhoods while preserving the torsion argument.
