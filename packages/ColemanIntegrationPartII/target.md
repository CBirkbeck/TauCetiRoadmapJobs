## Final targets

1. **Retain the general integration theorem in full as an imported headline.** Let p be any prime, K/Q_p finite, U/K a smooth geometrically connected algebraic variety, and ω a regular closed algebraic one-form on U. There is a canonical single-integration theory, with universal values in K + K·T inside K[T], where T represents log(p). Specializing T to λ in K gives I_λ(a,b;ω) for a,b in U(K). It is K-linear in ω, locally analytic in the endpoints with differential ω in the second endpoint, satisfies I(a,a)=0 and I(a,c)=I(a,b)+I(b,c), and commutes with algebraic pullback. It integrates df to f(b)−f(a) and df/f to log_λ(f(b)/f(a)) wherever defined. Uniqueness means uniqueness of the natural theory with these properties across smooth varieties, not uniqueness from the differential equation alone. It agrees with Coleman single integration in the good-reduction setting and is compatible with finite coefficient extensions. For a smooth proper geometrically connected curve X/K and any rational one-form ω, apply this on X minus its poles. This includes all meromorphic forms on curves, not only holomorphic or second-kind forms, and imposes no graph or model hypothesis. Endpoints over finite extensions are included by base change.

   Locators: colmez1998 line 2957, Definitions II.1.2–II.1.3 and Theorem II.1.4, pp. 73–74 (colmez1998 line 2982); good-reduction comparison, Introduction Remark 2(ii), p. 2 (colmez1998 line 229). Vologodsky's identification with Colmez single integration is Proposition 52, §17.2, p. 42 of the **2001 v1 preprint** (vologodsky2001 line 1800); finite extension compatibility is Theorem B(4), p. 5 (vologodsky2001 line 187). The preprint has a standing p>2 convention (vologodsky2001 line 51); use Colmez for the all-prime single-integration statement. Do not infer all-prime iterated transport from that preprint. This headline preserves the requested ColemanIntegration §7.7 import; it is not a second construction of its theory.

2. **Harmonic realization on semistable curves.** Let X/K be smooth, proper and geometrically connected with a split regular semistable model, and let ω be meromorphic. After separating its poles as necessary, choose Coleman primitives F_v on the component wide opens away from poles. For an oriented connecting annulus e from v to w, put c_ω(e)=F_w|e−F_v|e. Constants can be chosen so that c_ω is harmonic, meaning antisymmetry under edge reversal and zero sum over edges leaving each vertex. Such choices are unique modulo one common constant. The resulting values on the residue domains of smooth points equal a Vologodsky primitive modulo one common constant. On a connecting annulus with equation xy=π, orientation and coordinate x as in the source, the extension to algebraic points has the additional term c_ω(e)·v_π(x), with v_π(π)=1. State the coordinate/orientation convention together with this sign. Do not replace harmonicity by c_ω=0 or glue the component functions as equal analytic functions across overlaps.

   Locators: besser-zerbes2017 line 68, Theorem 1.1, pp. 2–3; Corollary 1.2, p. 3 (besser-zerbes2017 line 108); removal of the component-intersection simplification and finite-extension argument, §2, p. 3 (besser-zerbes2017 line 130). Graph decomposition is Besser Theorem 4.4, p. 12 (besser2017 line 528 and besser2017 line 553). These are unrestricted finite graph statements; a tree is only a Check.

3. **Annular double index and the corrected global pairing.** On an oriented K-annulus e, put A_log,1(e)=O(e)+K·log(z), with the formal punctured-disc version K((z))+K·log(z). The double index B_e is the unique alternating K-bilinear pairing that gives Res_e(F dG) when F is analytic/meromorphic without a logarithmic term. In particular B_e(C,G)=C Res_e(dG), and reversing orientation negates the pairing. The construction is invariant under orientation-preserving parameter change. On a Coleman wide open, the sum over oriented ends depends only on the de Rham classes of the differentials and is compatible with shrinking.

   For meromorphic ω,η on a proper semistable X, regular off a finite set Z, let c_ω,c_η be the jumps of harmonic component primitives. With ends oriented as ends of the tail component, the global double pairing is the sum of the component wide-open pairings plus
   `Σ_{e modulo reversal} (c_ω(e) Res_e η − c_η(e) Res_e ω)`.
   It also equals the sum of point double indices of the Vologodsky primitives at Z. It descends to H¹_dR(X−Z/K), restricts on H¹_dR(X/K) to cup product with the source's order of factors, and is invariant under model blowups. For nonsplit Z use residue-field traces and descent, rather than a rational-point hypothesis in the exported theorem.

   Locators: Besser Definitions 3.1–3.2 and Lemma 3.3, p. 9 (besser2017 line 402); Definitions 3.4, 3.8, Propositions 3.5, 3.9 and Remark 3.10, pp. 9–11 (besser2017 line 435 and besser2017 line 492). The corrected expression is Theorem 4.10, Definition 4.11 and Proposition 4.12, pp. 14–16 (besser2017 line 700 and besser2017 line 738); its Vologodsky identification is Corollary 5.3, p. 16 (besser2017 line 807). The nonsplit trace formulation is a descent consequence of target 5, not an additional quoted theorem in §3's split-point notation.

4. **Local and global triple indices with their actual choice laws.** Over a characteristic-zero coefficient field, for F,G,H in A_log,1 and auxiliary primitives I_{R dS} satisfying I_{R dS}+I_{S dR}=RS, construct T_e(F,G;H). It is trilinear in compatible data, symmetric in F,G, satisfies
   `T_e(F,G;H)+T_e(F,H;G)+T_e(G,H;F)=0`,
   and reduces to B_e(F,I_{G dH}) when G has no logarithmic term. Increasing I_{G dH} by C, and decreasing I_{H dG} by C, changes T_e by −C Res_e(dF); interchange F,G for the other change law. It is independent of I_{F dG}. Orientation reversal negates it; orientation-preserving annular pullback of degree n multiplies it by n, with all auxiliary data pulled back.

   For a connected Coleman wide open U and Coleman F,G,H whose differentials are analytic one-forms, choose those auxiliary primitives globally as Coleman integrals. The end-sum T_U(F,G;H) is independent of these auxiliary choices, is compatible with restriction to smaller end annuli, and takes values in the coefficient field when all data descend there. It still depends on the functions F,G,H, including constants: T_U(F,C;H)=C·B_U(F,H), whereas T_U(F,G;C)=0. Include the resulting bilinear pairing with Coleman one-forms, characterized by pairing F with G dH as T_U(F,G;H). In particular, no assertion that the triple index factors through the three de Rham classes is permitted. Frobenius vanishing has its actual hypothesis: [dF] and [dG] are q-eigenvectors for the chosen q-Frobenius.

   Locators: Besser–de Jeu Definition 7.1, Proposition 7.3 and Lemma 7.4, published pp. 355–357 (besser-dejeu2012 line 2227 and besser-dejeu2012 line 2277); parameter independence and degree law, §8 and Lemma 8.1, pp. 360–361 (besser-dejeu2012 line 2519); Lemmas 8.2–8.3 and Proposition 8.4, pp. 361–362 (besser-dejeu2012 line 2546); Proposition 9.2, p. 364 (besser-dejeu2012 line 2776). Shrinking and coefficient-field descent are described in the introduction, p. 310 (besser-dejeu2012 line 250); retain their compatible global-data hypotheses. Orientation and trace formulations are consequences of the residue characterization, not separate quoted claims of Lemma 8.1.

5. **Branch, trace and finite semistable-extension descent.** Keep the universal logarithm parameter through every comparison. If universal single integration is A+B·T, then I_{λ'}−I_λ=(λ'−λ)B; on K× this is log_{λ'}(u)−log_λ(u)=(λ'−λ)v_p(u), normalized by v_p(p)=1. Holomorphic integration on proper curves is independent of the branch. Double and triple indices use a common branch and transported auxiliary data; changing branch means transporting those data and applying the residue/change-of-constant identities, not declaring all indices branch independent.

   For any finite L/K with compatible logarithm branches, base change preserves integration of K-data. For ω on U_L and a,b in U(K), coefficient trace satisfies
   `Tr_{L/K}(I_L(a,b;ω)) = I_K(a,b;Tr_{L/K} ω)`.
   Use trace at a nonrational point for its local index contribution; traces compose in towers. For a degree-zero divisor over K away from the poles, a splitting extension and a basepoint over that extension give a sum of endpoint integrals independent of the basepoint; Galois descent gives a K-valued result independent of the splitting extension. No K-rational basepoint is required in the exported divisor interface. Similarly, whenever a double or triple index expression and its auxiliary data descend, its value descends; do not apply trace multiplicatively to arbitrary products.

   After any finite extension giving a split semistable realization and separated poles/support, the harmonic and index descriptions agree with the general integration theorem and with each other after further extension, subdivision and model refinement. Thus they descend to the original arbitrary-reduction curve. Field-trace formulas follow from linearity, Galois equivariance and scalar extension; they are consequences to establish, not quotations of an unlocated trace theorem.

   Locators: Colmez Definition II.1.3 and Theorem II.1.4, p. 74 (colmez1998 line 2986); universal logarithm and norm/trace normalization, §II.2.5, p. 97 (colmez1998 line 3950); Galois descent, Introduction Remark 2(iv), pp. 2–3 (colmez1998 line 246). Vologodsky Theorem B(4) and Proposition 52 are at vologodsky2001 line 195 and vologodsky2001 line 1806, subject to that preprint's standing convention. Besser–Zerbes Corollary 1.2 and §2 begin at besser-zerbes2017 line 95 and besser-zerbes2017 line 130. Proper holomorphic branch independence is explained at besser-zerbes2017 line 117 and checked on the Tate curve in §3, pp. 5–6 (besser-zerbes2017 line 245); Colmez's abelian logarithm characterization is Lemma II.1.13 and Remark II.1.18, pp. 78–79 (colmez1998 line 3145 and colmez1998 line 3213).
