**Verification.** The finding is right. LefschetzPencilsAndVanishingCycles §3.5 ("Theorem (descent of pencils to a finite field)", read at its open head) puts a good axis over some `𝔽_{q^r}` and says that the open set need not have an `𝔽_q`-point. Its §3.4 works over an algebraically closed field. The base field of a fibration defined over `𝔽_{q^r}` with `r > 1` contains `𝔽_{q^r}`, while the constant field of `K` is `𝔽_q`. For `K = 𝔽₂(s,t)`, `X² + X + 1` takes the value 1 at both 0 and 1, so it has no root in `K`, while `𝔽₄(s)` contains one.

A finite constant extension does not rescue the route either. The degree `r` depends on `K` and `v`, but 5.7 needs one formula for all `K` with `δ(K) = 2`, so §0.4's interpretations of finite extensions of bounded degree cannot absorb it. The mathematical statement of §5.4 is true over the original field: Dittmann–Pop assert it (`dittmann_pop line 681`), and a proof is recorded as the gap's route.

**The gap.** The `needs` sentence asks for:
- a smooth projective surface `X′` with a birational `k`-morphism `X′ → X`;
- a smooth projective geometrically integral `k`-curve `B`;
- a dominant `X′ → B` whose generic fibre is smooth and geometrically integral, and onto which the strict transform of `C` maps dominantly;
- all of this over `k` itself.

The README line gives a route. Poonen 2004, Theorem 1.2, p. 2 (`poonen_bertini line 90`), with the Taylor condition `f(P) ≠ 0` at a closed point `P ∈ C`, gives a smooth section over `k` that misses `C`. Poonen 2004, Remark 1 after Theorem 3.3, p. 10 (`poonen_bertini line 522`), makes it geometrically connected. Then come a second form `g` that is not a multiple of `f` on `C`, the pencil `f − t g`, generic smoothness from the smooth member at `t = 0`, and resolution of the base points.

I checked each step of the route:
- The two forms share no component on `X`, otherwise `g/f ∈ H⁰(X, O_X) = k`.
- `g` exists because the restrictions of degree-`d` forms to the curve `C` span at least two dimensions (`x_i^d` and `x_i^{d−1}x_j`).

**Changes in §5.4.**
- **Model.** The route now builds the model on which `O_v` is the local ring of a curve. Take a normal affine model generated by elements of `O_v`, one of them with transcendental residue. Then `𝔪_v ∩ A′` is a nonzero prime (otherwise `K ⊆ O_v`), it is not maximal, so it has height one, and `A′_𝔭` is a discrete valuation ring dominated by `O_v`, hence equal to it.
