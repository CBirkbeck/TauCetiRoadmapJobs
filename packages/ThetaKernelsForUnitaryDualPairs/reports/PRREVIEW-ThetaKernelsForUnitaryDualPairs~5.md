## C01 — The numerical Check of 3.47b, repeated in the Examples of Layer 3, is not an instance of 3.47b as stated

A reader takes the case `a = 3`, `a′ = 1`, `(P, Q) = (2, 1)`, `(P′, Q′) = (1, 0)`, `m_β = 1`, `m_α = −5` (Checks of 3.47b, line 856; Examples of Layer 3, line 920) as an instance of the theorem, and the Examples say of it that "the generating type is `La`". The displayed hypothesis of 3.47b is `−κ > a + a′` and `2(Q + P′) + 1 < min((a − a′)/2, a′)`. Here `κ = −6`, so the first holds (`6 > 4`), but `2(Q + P′) + 1 = 5` and `min((a − a′)/2, a′) = min(1, 1) = 1`, so the second fails. The case lies only in the wider range `−κ > |P − Q| + |P′ − Q′|` of the Remark (`6 > 2`), about which the Remark says that the statement "is not claimed here". The following Check (*The general range*, line 861) then contrasts `κ = −4` with this case as if only `−κ > a + a′` separated them. The arithmetic of the Check itself is right (I recomputed `La = (−3, −3) ⊗ (1, −2)`, `λ + 2ρ(𝔲 ∩ 𝔭) = La`, `λ + ρ = (½, −3/2, −5/2, −7/2)`, `Ω = 26` and `38`, difference `12 = N + 8`).

The smallest change: say in the Check and in the Examples that the case is in the range of the Remark and outside the displayed range (the displayed range with `Q + P′ ≥ 1` needs `a′ ≥ 4` and `a − a′ ≥ 8`), and that it tests the formulas for `La`, `λ` and the Casimir values; or replace it by a case in the displayed range.

## C03 — In the Check of 4.4a on the role of (5), the step "an element of `E^1` equal to `1` at a real place is `1`" needs a real place not split in `E`, which 4.4a does not assume

4.4a is stated for every quadratic extension `E/F` of number fields (line 932). The Check (line 1008) shows that an element `x ≠ 1` of `E_v^1`, `v` inert, is not in the group `H` generated by `E^1` and the `E_w^1`, `w` split, by looking at a real place: if `x = e·s` with `e ∈ E^1` and `s` supported at split places, then `e = 1` at a real place, hence `e = 1`. For `F` totally complex there is no real place, and at a real place of `F` that splits in `E` the factor `s` may be nontrivial, so the step as written does not apply; for a CM extension it does. The conclusion holds in general: take any place `u ≠ v` not split in `E` (there are infinitely many inert ones); there `x_u = 1` and `s_u = 1`, so `e = 1` in `E_u`, hence `e = 1`, and then `x = s` forces `x = 1`.

The smallest change: replace "at a real place" by "at a place other than `v` that is not split in `E`".
