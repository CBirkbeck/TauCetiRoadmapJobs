1. **F01** (P2) §5.3 trace clause lacks the hypothesis "elementary"

   **Location.** `packages/FourierDeligneTransform/README.md` line 531, last clause of the theorem sentence: "over `𝔽_q` the trace function of `NFT_ψ(ℱ)` is `−𝔉_ψ(t_ℱ)`".

   **Claim.** As written the clause is quantified over every constructible `ℱ`, since the preceding clauses are scoped ("for elementary `ℱ`, …; for Fourier `ℱ`, …") and this one is not.

   **Witness.** `ℱ = Λ_{𝔸¹}` over `𝔽_q`. By §3.4, `Rpr'_!ℒ_ψ(x x') ≅ σ'_*Λ(−1)[−2]`, so `NFT_ψ(Λ) = R¹pr'_!ℒ_ψ(x x') = 0` and its trace function is `0`; but `−𝔉_ψ(1)(x') = −Σ_x ψ(x x')` is `−q` at `x' = 0'`. The source states the identity for elementary `ℱ` only (ESDE Theorem 7.3.8 (4), ESDE lines 7307–7310), where `R⁰pr'_!` and `R²pr'_!` vanish by Elem(1) and Elem(2), and then `FT(ℱ[1]) ≅ NFT_ψ(ℱ)[1]` and §3.8 give the sign `(−1)^1`.

   **Repair.** Replace the clause by "over `𝔽_q`, for elementary `ℱ`, the trace function of `NFT_ψ(ℱ)` is `−𝔉_ψ(t_ℱ)`, since `R⁰pr'_!` and `R²pr'_!` of `pr^*ℱ ⊗ ℒ_ψ(x x')` vanish and §3.8 applies to `FT(ℱ[1]) ≅ NFT_ψ(ℱ)[1]`". Add the negative control as a Check: "`ℱ = Λ` is not elementary; `NFT_ψ(Λ) = 0` has trace `0`, while `−𝔉_ψ(1)` is `−q` at `0'`: the trace identity for `NFT_ψ` needs elementarity, although `t_{FT(Λ[1])} = −𝔉_ψ(1)` holds by §3.8." Tested on the instances: with the hypothesis added, `Λ` is excluded; `j_!ℒ_χ` (elementary by §5.3's third Check) has `NFT_ψ(j_!ℒ_χ) = j'_!ℒ_{χ^{−1}} ⊗ G(χ, ψ)` with trace `χ^{−1}(x')·(−g(χ, ψ))` for `x' ≠ 0'`, and `−𝔉_ψ(t_{j_!ℒ_χ})(x') = −Σ_{x ≠ 0} χ(x) ψ(x x') = −χ^{−1}(x') g(χ, ψ)`; both sides agree, and both vanish at `0'`.

2. **F02** (P3) §5.6 cites the ℓ-adic monodromy theorem for finite-field coefficients

   **Location.** `packages/FourierDeligneTransform/README.md` line 584, the sentence "When `k` is finite, every tamely ramified `V` is quasi-unipotent (LefschetzPencilsAndVanishingCycles §1.1), so after finite extensions of `k` and of `Λ` … it is a successive extension of unramified twists of Kummer modules and of the trivial module".

   **Claim.** Layer 5 admits `Λ` a finite field of characteristic `ℓ ≠ p` (Layer 5 introduction). LefschetzPencilsAndVanishingCycles §1.1 (read at its head, its lines 934–950) defines quasi-unipotence and states Grothendieck's theorem for ℓ-adic representations only; for `𝔽_ℓ`-coefficients the notion is not defined there.

   **Witness.** LefschetzPencilsAndVanishingCycles §1.1: "An ℓ-adic representation `ρ : G → GL(V)` is quasi-unipotent if …; Theorem … every ℓ-adic representation of `G` is quasi-unipotent."

   **Repair.** For `Λ` a finite field the conclusion holds without the theorem: a continuous representation of the procyclic `I/P ≅ ∏_{ℓ' ≠ p} ℤ_{ℓ'}(1)` on a finite-dimensional `Λ`-space has finite image, and after extending `Λ` to contain the roots of unity of that order it is a successive extension of characters of `I/P`, each a Kummer module twisted by an unramified character. Reword: "When `k` is finite, every tamely ramified `V` is, after finite extensions of `k` and of `Λ`, a successive extension of unramified twists of Kummer modules and of the trivial module: for `E` and `ℚ̄_ℓ` by Grothendieck's monodromy theorem (LefschetzPencilsAndVanishingCycles §1.1), for a finite `Λ` because the image of inertia is finite; with the compatibility (2.5.2) …". Tested on the instance: the nontrivial quadratic Kummer module over `𝔽_3` with `k = 𝔽_5` has image of order `2` and is itself a Kummer module, as the reworded sentence says.
