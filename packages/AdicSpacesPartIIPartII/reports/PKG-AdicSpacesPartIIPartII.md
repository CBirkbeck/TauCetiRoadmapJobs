## Corrections and source boundaries

- The dimension codomain includes an infinite global dimension. The disjoint union of polydiscs Dⁿ for all n has unbounded global dimension, while every point has finite local dimension.
- The nowhere-density proof no longer claims a pointwise equivalence between containing a minimal prime and local dimension equality. A line component meeting a plane component has dimensions 1<2 at the intersection but equality 1=1 at its other points. The global criterion uses every component and every point.
- For nonreduced X, the normal open in an irreducible component is normal with its reduced structure, and open in X_red. A nilpotent thickening of a disc has no normal ambient open, although its reduced component is a normal disc.
- The finite normalization stalk product is stated at classical points, the scope of CONRAD99 Lemma 2.1.1. No unverified all-adic-stalk product generalization remains.
- Smooth equals regular and the resulting finite smooth stratification are stated over all perfect complete K, as supported by CONRAD99 line 1008; characteristic zero is a specialization. Inseparable residue fields remain an explicit tangent/cotangent negative control.
- Affinoid regular restriction precedes normalization and singular loci. Completed scalar-extension regularity in characteristic zero is a separate explicit target; neither follows from flatness alone.
- Coherent radical restriction uses reduced affinoid localization. Normalization is over the reduction, and source-side nowhere density excludes extra vertical components.
- Irreducible global components are not stalk branches. The nodal cubic has one global component, two analytic stalk branches and two normalization points above the node (t=±1). The local formulation is CONRAD10 line 189, Lemma 2.1.3.
- The Shilov valuation ring is a completed localization of A° before reduction. In Ã the pseudo-uniformizer is zero, so it cannot be the valuation-ring uniformizer. Compare BH22 lines 238–240 and 265–281. The zero-algebra spectral supremum is zero, without taking a maximum over its empty Shilov set.
- Blow-up unit and zero ideals, marked Cartier stages, countably ordered hypersequences and the nonreduced resolution distinction are retained. The local cycle between blow-up and sequence construction is broken by constructing rigid blow-ups first.
- Analytic chart uniqueness means compatible maximal atlases. Hausdorffness is imposed for separated spaces, and finite scalar-restriction dimensions only for finite extensions. The analytic finite-extension chart argument works for inseparable finite extensions too; algebraic Weil restriction is not asserted.
- Orbit submersions require a represented quotient and smooth stabilizer. Smoothness descends after a field extension with a lift of the quotient point; a lift over F itself and surjectivity on F-points are not assumed.
- The subfield identity theorem uses Banach-valued coefficient recovery over the smaller field; a connected-set identity theorem over C is insufficient.

Six cited works were fetched and read through the sources service. SCHNEIDER11 is settled as uncertified/paywalled, with exact locators and no claim of reading its proof. Every cited work has one manifest key and provenance; there is no outstanding missing-text request. HK2, BH22 and TEMKIN12 use the exact arXiv v2 numbering read, not guessed published numbering.

The target-level bounded extension statement is supported by CONRAD10 lines 154–161; Bartenwerfer's original proof was not independently decomposed. The Banach Shilov-boundary comparison is supported by BH22 Proposition 2.2, including its proof, while the cited earlier Berkovich proof was not independently read. Temkin's scheme proof decomposition is consumed from ResolutionOfSingularitiesCharacteristicZero §§7.1–7.8. These limitations concern proof-route depth, not weakened target statements. The certified statements and the unsettled supplier extensions are distinguished explicitly.

The accepted source assessments remain: the incorrect BH definition number is corrected to Definition 2.5; the nodal local/global overstatement and pre-reduction localization typo are excluded. Infinitude rather than algebraic closedness of the residue field is sufficient for Gauss closure; this is not described as a paper error. Connectedness of resolution and resolving a fibre product's reduction are justified standard steps, not allegations of errors in HK2.

## Supply direction and target coverage

The point-class construction has been moved down from the diamond application to a definition on finite-extension points and arbitrary field-valued functors, independent of diamonds. The rigid bijection to classical points is proved here. Diamondification, seminormal full faithfulness and the v-surjection criterion remain downstream interpretations, not suppliers. Their full statements and Checks remain under Downstream consumers, including the Grassmannian and nonreduced fibre-product examples. This breaks the design's upward dependence without rebuilding the v-site or using a free predicate as a stand-in. The minimal regular scalar-extension assertion needed by a later field-compatibility comparison is stated here in §0.5.

The main-branch formal/coherent contracts are existing work permitted by the brief; no unmerged later extension is consumed. The formal localization needed beyond those exact main statements is routed through the earlier AdicSpacesPartII bridge, not silently asserted as part of Raynaud equivalence. The three top-level gaps are formal-affinoid-localization, generically-reduced-functorial-resolution and represented-smooth-homogeneous-quotient; their README text matches each needs sentence verbatim.
