The small-rank and linear tests are supplemented by the proof-route checks: the Weyl product puts all weights in the root lattice; surjectivity makes every nontrivial Fourier character nontrivial on a positive root; its geometric series has bound 2/|1−χ(α)| with a nonzero denominator. The other N−1 factors are bounded by 4m+1, giving O(1/m) after division by (4m+1)^N. The constant is chosen before m and M. For the local theorem, the maximum is over finitely many torus types. In the rank-zero case the only possible surjective quotient is trivial and a highest-weight-generated module has dimension 1.

For compact representatives, fix a strongly regular t∈T(E). Evaluation of geometrically conjugate E-embeddings at t identifies their rational-conjugacy classes with the rational classes of that stable element: its centralizer is exactly T. Thus ETUTC §1.14(b)'s finite class set supplies the embedding finiteness used in §3.1. This is a direct consequence of the cited section, not an additional assertion about compact conjugacy orbits.

For the bidual repair, compactness alone would not suffice: compact induction from O_E× in E× has O_E×-invariants ℂ[ℤ], of infinite dimension. Its smooth dual is ℂ^ℤ. Bidual evaluation is injective but not surjective; a functional vanishing on finite-support sequences and taking the constant sequence to 1 is not a finite coordinate combination. The package uses only injection and the independently established finite length of the dual output.
