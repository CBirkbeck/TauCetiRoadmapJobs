For the new theorem, the proof route is a real consequence of the sources: the Weyl product has N root-string factors, each of length 4m+1. For a nontrivial character χ of A, surjectivity gives a root α with ζ=χφ(α)≠1. Its factor has norm at most 2/|1−ζ|, while the others have norm at most 4m+1. After division by (4m+1)^N, Fourier inversion bounds the total error by the finite sum of these nontrivial Fourier bounds, hence C/m. The argument works for every finite quotient and every specified cyclic highest-weight module, independently of an embedding K→ℂ. In rank zero surjectivity forces A=0 and generation by a nonzero vector gives dimension 1.
