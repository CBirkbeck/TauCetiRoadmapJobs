**The mathematics of the changed route, recomputed.** BPCZ (A.0.3.1), bpcz lines 6514–6516 (p. 328; page marker at line 6499): for quasi-complete `V`, `ϕ : M → V` is holomorphic iff it is continuous and `s ↦ ⟨ϕ(s), λ⟩` is holomorphic for every `λ` in some *total* subspace `H ⊆ V′`. A subspace of `V′` is total for the duality `⟨V, V′⟩` exactly when its orthogonal in `V` is zero, i.e. when it separates the points of `V`; so the new wording ("separate the points") is the old one ("total set") made explicit, and the criterion is applied correctly. The route's four steps hold with the stated hypotheses: (i) `V = E ⊗̂π F` is complete, hence quasi-complete; (ii) the canonical bilinear map `E × F → E ⊗̂π F` is continuous, so `λ ↦ e(λ) ⊗ f(λ)` is continuous; (iii) `ℓ ⊗ m` extends to `V′` and pairs to `ℓ(e(λ)) m(f(λ))`, a product of two holomorphic scalar functions; (iv) with `E` nuclear, `E ⊗̂π F ≅ E ⊗̂ε F` (§5.2), and with `E, F` complete the element `u` of `E ⊗̂ε F` is the bilinear form `(ℓ, m) ↦ ⟨u, ℓ ⊗ m⟩` on the weak duals (§5.1), so vanishing of all pairings gives `u = 0`. Only `E` needs to be nuclear; the statement's "complete nuclear Fréchet spaces E and F" is stronger and true. A Fréchet space is complete, so "complete" is redundant but harmless. Instances: `E = ℂ` gives `V = F` and `ℓ ⊗ m = c·m`, separation by Hahn–Banach; `F = 0` gives `V = 0`; `E = F = ℓ²` (not nuclear) still separates, since `ℓ² ⊗̂π ℓ²` is the trace class and `ℓ²` has the approximation property, so nuclearity is sufficient, not necessary, as the statement claims; for a Banach `E` without the approximation property the map `E ⊗̂π F → E ⊗̂ε F` is not injective for a suitable `F`, so some hypothesis of this kind is required and nuclearity is the one the §2.15 spaces have (bpcz line 1402, (2.5.12.10), p. 215). The three Checks of §2.15 (lines 981–985, untouched) still agree: `λ·λ² = λ³` holomorphic, `λ·conj(λ) = |λ|²` not; the Gaussian family `exp(−π(x²+y²) + λ(x+y))` has value `1` at `(λ, x, y) = (0, 0, 0)`.
