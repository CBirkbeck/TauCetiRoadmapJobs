# Roadmap: tautological rings and motivic structures in the cohomology of moduli of stable curves

**Summary.** This roadmap develops the rational cohomology of the moduli stacks `M̄_{g,n}` of stable pointed curves through the operations that build it from smaller moduli spaces. From the stacks, their boundary strata, clutching and forgetful morphisms, cotangent lines and Hodge bundle, it builds the tautological rings, the semi-tautological extensions (STEs) of Canning–Larson–Payne, the boundary-induction machinery of Arbarello–Cornalba, the Chow–Künneth generation property, the genus-four point-count sieve of Bergström–Faber–Payne and the stable-graph assembly of Getzler–Kapranov. Its end theorems are: odd cohomology of every `M̄_{g,n}` vanishes in degrees at most nine (BFP, Theorem 1.1); `M̄_{4,n}` has polynomial point count with pure Tate cohomology for `n ≤ 3` (BFP, Theorem 1.4); every degree-`k` cohomology group is contained in an STE generated by finitely many explicit groups, so only finitely many simple Hodge structures or Galois representations occur in each degree (CLP, Theorem 1.3 and Corollary 1.4); `H⁴(M̄_{g,n})` is tautological for all `g, n`, `H⁶` for `g ≥ 10`, and even homology in degrees at most fourteen is tautological (CLP, Theorem 1.5); homology in degrees thirteen and fifteen is generated from the genus-one cusp classes (CLP, Theorems 1.6–1.7); `M_{7,n}` has the Chow–Künneth generation property and tautological Chow ring for `n ≤ 3` (CLP, Theorem 1.10); and the classification `H¹³(M̄_{g,n})^ss ≅ ⊕ L S₁₂`, `H¹⁴(M̄_{g,n})^ss ≅ ⊕ L⁷`, and for `g ≥ 2` `H¹⁵(M̄_{g,n})^ss ≅ ⊕ L² S₁₂`, as Hodge structures and as `ℓ`-adic Galois representations (CLP, Theorem 1.1).

## Conventions

- **Stable pairs.** A stable pair is `(g, n) ∈ ℕ²` with `2g − 2 + n > 0`; `d = d_{g,n} = 3g − 3 + n` is the dimension of `M̄_{g,n}` and of `M_{g,n}`. Statements over all `g, n` range over stable pairs only. There is no forgetful morphism to an unstable target; in particular there is none from `M_{1,1}` to `M_{1,0}`.
- **Stacks.** `M̄_{g,n}` is the smooth proper Deligne–Mumford stack over `ℤ` of stable `n`-pointed curves of genus `g`, with ordered distinct markings; `M_{g,n}` is the open substack of smooth curves, generally not proper. Normalized boundary strata have compact factors `M̄_{g_v,n_v}`; strata of the open stratification have factors `M_{g_v,n_v}`. `M_{0,4}` is `ℙ¹` minus three points and `M̄_{0,4} ≅ ℙ¹`.
- **Cohomology.** `H^*(X)` is rational Betti cohomology of the associated complex analytic stack with its mixed Hodge structure, or `ℚ_ℓ`-étale cohomology of `X_ℚ̄` with its continuous `G_ℚ`-action; a statement made "in both realizations" holds for each, compatibly with the Artin comparison. `H_c^*` is compactly supported cohomology. `W_k H^k(U)` is the lowest-weight part of the weight filtration of a smooth `U`; `Gr^W_k H_c^k(U)` is its dual top piece. Superscript `ss` is semisimplification; Hodge structures of smooth proper DM stacks are semisimple, so `ss` matters only for Galois representations. No statement about a semisimplification splits a Galois extension.
- **Tate twist and Frobenius.** `L = ℚ(−1) = H²(ℙ¹)`; geometric Frobenius acts on `L` by `q`. A codimension-`c` cycle class lies in `H^{2c}` with Hodge type `(c, c)`. Witness: on `ℙ¹` over `𝔽_q`, `H²` has geometric Frobenius eigenvalue `q` and the cycle class of a point spans it; replacing `L` by `ℚ(1)` would give eigenvalue `q⁻¹`.
- **Homology.** For a smooth proper `X` of pure dimension `d`, `H_k(X) = H^{2d−k}(X)(d)`. For smooth `U`, Borel–Moore homology is `H_k^{BM}(U) = H^{2d−k}(U)(d)`, dual to `H_c^k(U)`. Following CLP, for an STE `S` the space `S_k(M̄_{g,n})` means the cohomological subspace `S^{2d−k}(M̄_{g,n})`; an STE is never redefined as a homology ring. Witness: for `M̄_{0,4} = ℙ¹`, `H₀ = H²(1)` is one-dimensional of weight zero.
- **Cusp realizations.** `S₁₂ = H¹¹(M̄_{1,11})` and `S₁₆ = W₁₅H¹⁵(M_{1,15})`, each of rank two, of Hodge types `(11,0),(0,11)` and `(15,0),(0,15)`. More generally `S_{k+1}` denotes the parabolic realization of level-one cusp forms of weight `k + 1`, of rank `2 dim S_{k+1}(SL₂(ℤ))`.
- **Point counts.** A finite-field count of a stack is groupoid mass, the sum of `1/#Aut` over isomorphism classes of rational points (WeilConjectures, WC.1, finite groupoid mass). Polynomial point count means `HasPolynomialPointCount` in the sense of WeilConjectures, WC.5: equality at every prime power.
- **Symmetric groups.** `T_λ` is the irreducible rational representation (Specht module) of `S_n` attached to a partition `λ ⊢ n`; `s_λ` its Schur function; `T_{(n)}` is trivial. `V_λ` denotes a symplectic local system (§3.1), a different object.
- **Coefficients and characteristic.** Chow groups have rational coefficients, `A^*(X) = CH^*(X) ⊗ ℚ`. Algebraic-cycle constructions of Layers 4–6 are over fields of characteristic zero. The point counts of Layers 7–9 range over all finite fields, including characteristic two.

## How to read the build

Layer 0 sets up the tautological rings, kappa classes, the stable-pair order, the boundary exact sequences and the cusp realizations. Layer 1 builds the equivariant counting machinery on curve moduli (marking twists, the weight-graded equivariant Euler characteristic, the stable-graph transform and the Getzler–Kapranov formula) and the low-genus polynomial counts. Layer 2 proves the dimension bounds and odd vanishing through degree seven and, for genus at most three, through degree nine. Layer 3 treats genus one, symplectic local systems and the stable range. Layer 4 constructs semi-tautological extensions and proves finite generation. Layer 5 develops the Chow–Künneth generation property, which Layers 6 and 7 establish for `M_{7,n}`, `n ≤ 3`, through its gonality strata. Layers 8 and 9 compute the hyperelliptic and genus-four point counts and prove odd vanishing through degree nine in all genera; they are independent of Layers 4–7. Layer 10 treats even cohomology and homology, Layer 11 the exceptional genus-one and genus-two cusp bases, and Layer 12 proves the generation and classification theorems in degrees thirteen to fifteen. Each target's *Needs* line lists the inputs of its statement and of the route given; another route may need less.

## Layer 0: Tautological operations and cusp realizations

This layer adds to the imported moduli stacks the ring operations and realization conventions specific to curve cohomology. The test of every pushforward is its codimension and its Tate twist.

### 0.1 Tautological Chow and cohomology rings

Define the tautological system as the least collection of unital graded `ℚ`-subalgebras `R^*(M̄_{g,n}) ⊂ A^*(M̄_{g,n})`, one for each stable pair, that is closed under pushforward along forgetful and clutching morphisms, under pullback along forgetful and clutching morphisms, and under relabelling of markings. The full Chow system satisfies these conditions, and an intersection of such systems satisfies them, so the least system exists. Put `R^*(M_{g,n})` for the image of `R^*(M̄_{g,n})` under restriction, and `RH^*` for the image of `R^*` under the cycle class map; cohomological degree is twice Chow codimension. Prove that `R^*(M̄_{g,n})` is spanned by pushforwards from boundary strata of monomials in marking psi classes and vertex kappa classes (the decorated stable-graph description), by the projection formula, the excess intersection formula for clutching pullbacks and the forgetful pullback formulas for psi and kappa classes (CLP, Introduction pp. 1–3 and §2.1 p. 4; the graph description is recalled in CLP §5, pp. 13–22).

*Needs:* StableReductionPartII, MC.2 and MC.3 (forgetful and clutching morphisms, cotangent lines); SchemeAndStackFoundations, gap:stack-rational-chow (proper pushforward, pullback and intersection products on smooth DM stacks); MotivesAndAlgebraicCycles, gap:dm-stack-cycle-class (the cycle class map for `RH^*`).

API:

- Every collection of unital graded subalgebras closed under the tautological operations contains the tautological system.
- Restriction maps `R^*(M̄_{g,n})` onto `R^*(M_{g,n})`, by definition.
- The cycle class map sends `R^c` into `RH^{2c}` with Hodge type `(c, c)` and commutes with the tautological operations.
- `R^*(M̄_{g,n})` is spanned by decorated stable-graph pushforwards of psi and kappa monomials.
- Two operation-stable systems with equal subalgebras at every stable pair are equal.

**Checks.**

- `R^*(M̄_{0,3}) = ℚ` in degree zero only, since `M̄_{0,3}` is a point; a definition that also adjoined a nonzero degree-one class would be wrong here.
- On `M̄_{0,4} ≅ ℙ¹` a boundary point is a nonzero tautological class in `R¹` and in `RH²`; the three boundary points have equal classes.
- A nonzero class in `H¹¹(M̄_{1,11})` is not in `RH^*`: tautological classes are algebraic and have even degree. A definition of `RH^*` as "all cohomology" would contain it.

### 0.2 Kappa classes of pointed curves

For a stable pair `(g, n)` and the forgetful morphism `π : M̄_{g,n+1} → M̄_{g,n}` define `κ_a = π_*(ψ_{n+1}^{a+1}) ∈ R^a(M̄_{g,n})` for `a ≥ 0`. The marking line and its psi class are StableReductionPartII, MC.3; `λ_j = c_j(E)` for the Hodge bundle `E` of StableReductionPartII, MC.5; no new Hodge bundle is constructed. Prove the comparison `κ_a = π^*κ_a + ψ_{n+1}^a` on `M̄_{g,n+1}` (CLP, §2.1 p. 4; §§3 and 5, pp. 9–10 and 21–22).

*Needs:* §0.1; StableReductionPartII, MC.3 (cotangent lines and their forgetful correction) and MC.5 (Hodge bundle); SchemeAndStackFoundations, gap:stack-rational-chow (proper pushforward and Chern classes on DM stacks).

API:

- `κ_a` has Chow codimension `a`.
- On `M̄_{g,n+1}`, `κ_a = π^*κ_a + ψ_{n+1}^a`, where the left side is the kappa class of `(g, n+1)` and `π^*κ_a` the pullback of that of `(g, n)`.
- `κ₀ = (2g − 2 + n)·1`.
- The lambda classes are the Chern classes of the imported finite locally free Hodge bundle and satisfy its determinant and exterior-power identities.
- For every `a ≥ 0`, `π_*(ψ_{n+1}^{a+1}) = κ_a`, pushforward lowering Chow degree by one.

**Checks.**

- `κ₀ = 1` on `M̄_{0,3}` (`2·0 − 2 + 3 = 1`).
- `κ₀ = 1` on `M̄_{1,1}` (`2 − 2 + 1 = 1`).
- `κ₀` increases by one when a marking is added; the unpointed value `2g − 2` would be wrong for `n > 0`. On `M̄_7`, `κ₀ = 12`, the value used in §7.7.

### 0.3 The stable-pair order

For pairs of natural numbers define `(g′, n′) ≺ (g, n)` if and only if `g′ ≤ g`, `2g′ + n′ ≤ 2g + n` and the pairs are different. Prove that `≺` is a well-founded strict partial order, by lexicographic descent of `(g, 2g + n)`, that every vertex pair `(g_v, n_v)` of a stable graph with at least one edge and total pair `(g, n)` satisfies `(g_v, n_v) ≺ (g, n)`, and that `(g, n − 1) ≺ (g, n)` (CLP, §2.1 p. 4; BFP, Notation 4.1, p. 1331).

*Needs:* StableReductionPartII, MC.3 (stable graphs and their genus formula).

API:

- No pair precedes itself.
- The relation is transitive and well founded.
- Every vertex of a stable graph with at least one edge precedes its total pair.
- `(g, n − 1)` precedes `(g, n)` when both are stable and `n > 0`.

**Checks.**

- `(0, 4) ≺ (1, 2)`, although both have `2g + n = 4`.
- `(1, 2)` does not precede `(0, 4)`, since its genus is larger.
- `(1, 1)` does not precede itself; the unstable pair `(0, 2)` precedes `(0, 3)` numerically but is excluded from every moduli statement.

### 0.4 Pure-weight boundary exact sequences

Let `(g, n)` be stable and let `ν : D̃ → M̄_{g,n}` be the normalization of the boundary, a disjoint union of quotients of products of compact moduli stacks by graph automorphisms. Prove the exact sequences

    H^{k−2}(D̃)(−1) → H^k(M̄_{g,n}) → W_k H^k(M_{g,n}) → 0,
    H_k(D̃) → H_k(M̄_{g,n}) → W_{−k} H_k^{BM}(M_{g,n}) → 0,

in both realizations, where the first map is the sum of the Gysin maps of the normalized one-edge strata, taken on graph-automorphism invariants, and `H_k^{BM}(M_{g,n}) = H^{2d−k}(M_{g,n})(d)` (CLP, §2.1 p. 4; §6.2, equation (6.1), p. 23). Route: the Gysin sequence of the pair `(M̄_{g,n}, ∂M̄_{g,n})`, strictness of the weight filtration and purity of the smooth proper strata identify the image of the boundary with the image of the normalized one-edge Gysin maps; dualize with the Tate twist.

*Needs:* StableReductionPartII, MC.2 and MC.3 (normal-crossings boundary and its normalization with graph automorphisms); DeligneWeightsAndPurity §8.24 (weight spectral sequence and purity, ℓ-adic); MixedHodgeModulesAndIrregularHodgeTheory, gap:dm-stack-mixed-hodge (the Hodge analogue); WeilConjectures, gap:open-dm-stack-duality (duality on `M_{g,n}`); §0.3.

**Checks.**

- For `M̄_{0,4}`, the Gysin map of a boundary point is `ℚ(−1) = H⁰(pt)(−1) → H²(ℙ¹)`, an isomorphism of weight two; omitting the twist `(−1)` would map a weight-zero space onto a weight-two space.
- `H_k` of a smooth proper `X` is `H^{2d−k}(X)(d)`, never `H^{2d−k}(X)` without the twist: for `ℙ¹`, `H₂ = H⁰(1)` has weight `−2`.

### 0.5 Cusp realizations on genus-one moduli

Prove that `H¹¹(M̄_{1,11})` and `W₁₅H¹⁵(M_{1,15})` are, in both realizations, the parabolic realizations of the level-one cusp forms of weights twelve and sixteen, each of rank two; that `S₁₂` has Hodge types `(11,0),(0,11)` and `S₁₆` has `(15,0),(0,15)`; and more generally that the sign-isotypic part of `W_k H^k(M_{1,k})` for the action of `S_k` on the markings is `S_{k+1}`, of rank `2 dim S_{k+1}(SL₂(ℤ))` (CLP, Introduction p. 1; Proposition 2.2, pp. 5–7; Lemma 8.3, p. 29; the genus-one classification is due to Getzler, recalled in CLP §2.2, p. 5). Route: the alternating part of the configuration space of `k − 1` points on the universal elliptic curve over `M_{1,1}` gives `Sym^{k−1}` of the standard local system, whose parabolic cohomology is the Eichler–Shimura realization.

*Needs:* StableReductionPartII, MC.0 and MC.2 (the stacks and universal curve); `CuspForm`, `CuspForm.rank_eq_one_of_weight_eq_twelve`, `ModularForm.dimension_level_one`; `TauCeti.finrank_periodPolynomials_eq_finrank_modularForm_add_finrank_cuspForm`; AutomorphicGaloisRepresentations §1.2 (parabolic realizations at level one); MixedHodgeModulesAndIrregularHodgeTheory, gap:dm-stack-mixed-hodge (Hodge structure of the stacks); the symmetric powers of the standard local system `R¹f_*ℚ` on `M_{1,1}`, which on this one-dimensional base need no input beyond the universal elliptic curve.

**Checks.**

- `S₁₄ = 0`, while `S₁₂` and `S₁₆` each have rational dimension two, from `dim S₁₄ = 0`, `dim S₁₂ = dim S₁₆ = 1`.
- `L S₁₂` has pure weight thirteen and Hodge types `(12,1),(1,12)`, not `(11,0),(0,11)`: the twist shifts both Hodge numbers by one.
- `S_r` has rank `2 dim S_r(SL₂(ℤ))`, not always two: for weight twenty-four the cusp space has dimension two and `S₂₄` has rank four.

### Examples

On `M̄_{0,4} = ℙ¹` the tautological ring is `ℚ[δ]/(δ²)` with `δ` the class of a boundary point; its cohomological realization is all of `H^*(ℙ¹)`. On `M̄_{1,1}`, `κ₀ = 1` and `ψ₁ = δ_irr/12` with the stack normalization of the boundary divisor. The first non-tautological cohomology of any `M̄_{g,n}` is `H¹¹(M̄_{1,11}) = S₁₂`.

## Layer 1: Equivariant counts and the stable-graph transform

Twisting the markings by a permutation extracts symmetric-group characters from point counts; summing over stable graphs assembles boundary counts from the open strata. Two different sign conventions occur and are kept apart: the ordinary assembly of open strata uses the Koszul signs of graph automorphisms only, while the weight spectral sequence of normalized closed strata carries the edge-orientation character `det ℚ^{E(Γ)}` (DeligneWeightsAndPurity §8.24).

### 1.1 Twists of moduli by permutations of the markings

For a stable pair `(g, n)`, a finite field `𝔽_q` and `σ ∈ S_n`, define the twist `M_{g,n}^σ` over `𝔽_q` as the descent of `M_{g,n} ⊗ 𝔽̄_q` along the twisted Frobenius `σF_q`: its rational points over `𝔽_q` are smooth curves over `𝔽_q` with `n` distinct geometric marked points permuted by Frobenius as `σ` permutes the labels. Define `M̄_{g,n}^σ`, `∂M̄_{g,n}^σ` and the hyperelliptic twist `H_{g,n}^σ` in the same way. Prove that the twist exists as a DM stack of finite type over `𝔽_q`, that its groupoid mass equals the alternating trace of `F_q σ^*` on `H_c^*(M_{g,n} ⊗ 𝔽̄_q, ℚ_ℓ)`, and that the twists are compatible with forgetting a union of Frobenius cycles (BFP, §9.1, pp. 1351–1352, and §11, pp. 1356–1357). The twist construction for schemes and its trace formula are WeilConjectures, WC.1 (twists and Frobenius); this target extends them to the curve moduli stacks, where descent must retain automorphism groups.

*Needs:* StableReductionPartII, MC.0 and MC.2 (the stacks and forgetful morphisms with the marking action); WeilConjectures, WC.1 (finite groupoid mass, stack compact supports and trace, twists and Frobenius); SchemeAndStackFoundations §§1.18–1.21 (effective descent for stacks).

API:

- Conjugate permutations give isomorphic twists with equal masses; conjugating `σ` by a marking permutation gives an isomorphism of twists compatible with compact-support traces.
- The mass of `M_{g,n}^σ(𝔽_q)` is `Σ_i (−1)^i Tr(F_q σ^* | H_c^i(M_{g,n} ⊗ 𝔽̄_q, ℚ_ℓ))`.
- The identity twist is the ordinary moduli stack with ordered rational markings.
- Forgetting a union of Frobenius cycles descends the corresponding forgetful morphism.

**Checks.**

- For `σ` of cycle type `(1,1)` the rational points have two distinct rational marks; for type `(2)` they have one Frobenius orbit of two distinct geometric points, and no rational marked point.
- A fixed mark together with a conjugate pair has cycle type `(2,1)`, not three independent rational marks. On `ℙ¹` over `𝔽_q`, the twisted configuration count for cycle type `(2)` is `q² − q` (one degree-two point with its two labellings), while for type `(1,1)` it is `(q + 1)q`; a definition that ignored the twist would return `(q + 1)q` in both cases.
- The groupoid of rational points is not replaced by its set of isomorphism classes: for `M_{1,1}` over `𝔽_q` the mass is `q`, while the number of isomorphism classes is `2q + O(1)` (twists of each `j`-invariant).

### 1.2 The weight-graded equivariant Euler characteristic

For a finite group `G`, a separated finite-type DM stack `X` over `ℂ` with a `G`-action, define `e_c^G(X) = Σ_{i,w} (−1)^i [Gr^W_w H_c^i(X)]` in the Grothendieck group of rational mixed Hodge structures with `G`-action; in the `ℓ`-adic realization over a finite field use the Frobenius weights. When every contributing weight piece is a sum of Tate structures, write `e_c^G(X) = Σ_j a_j L^j` with `a_j` in the rational representation ring of `G`; for `G = S_n` identify `a_j` with its Frobenius characteristic, a degree-`n` symmetric function (BFP, §9, pp. 1351–1354, and Remark 1.6, p. 1326). No positivity or purity of individual groups of an open stack is asserted.

*Needs:* `TauCeti.repRing`, `TauCeti.repRingCharacter`; SchurWeyl, Layer 7 (fixed-degree Frobenius characteristic); DeligneWeightsAndPurity §8.24 (weights, ℓ-adic); MixedHodgeModulesAndIrregularHodgeTheory, gap:dm-stack-mixed-hodge (weights, Hodge); WeilConjectures, WC.1 (Künneth and localization for compact supports).

API:

- For a closed `G`-stable substack `Z ⊂ X` with complement `U`, `e_c^G(X) = e_c^G(U) + e_c^G(Z)`.
- `e_c` of a product is the tensor product, with Koszul signs in cohomological degree.
- Evaluating characters at the identity recovers the nonequivariant weight Euler polynomial.
- Under the trace hypotheses of WeilConjectures, WC.1 and WC.5, replacing `L` by `q` and the Adams operation by `ψ_r(q) = q^r` gives the twisted point counts of §1.1.
- The coefficient of weight `w` is the alternating sum of the `Gr^W_w H_c^i` as virtual representations; it is not ordinary `H^i` for a nonproper stack.

**Checks.**

- `e_c(𝔸¹) = L`, since `H_c²(𝔸¹) = ℚ(−1)` is the only compactly supported group.
- `e_c(𝔾_m) = L − 1`: coefficients need not be nonnegative.
- For `S₃`, the standard representation coefficient evaluated at the identity contributes twice its multiplicity: `e_c^{S₃} = s_{(2,1)}` has nonequivariant value `2`.

### 1.3 The stable-graph transform

For a stable collection `V = (V(g, n))` of finite-dimensional graded rational `S_n`-representations, zero when `2g − 2 + n ≤ 0`, define `MV(g, n) = ⊕_Γ (⊗_v V(g_v, n_v))_{Aut Γ}`, the sum over isomorphism classes of connected stable graphs `Γ` of total genus `g` with `n` legs, where `Aut Γ` acts by permuting flags with Koszul signs of the cohomological grading and the sum is finite for each `(g, n)` (GK, §2, the free modular operad; recalled in GK §8 and in BFP, Proposition 9.6, p. 1354). There is no edge-orientation twist in this ordinary transform. In characteristic zero coinvariants equal invariants.

*Needs:* StableReductionPartII, MC.3 (stable graphs and their automorphisms); §0.3; SchurWeyl, Layer 7.

API:

- The graph with one vertex and no edge contributes `V(g, n)`.
- `MV − V` is exactly the sum over connected stable graphs with at least one edge.
- Relabelling the legs defines the `S_n`-action on `MV(g, n)`.
- The total genus of `Γ` is `Σ_v g_v + b₁(Γ)`.
- A morphism of stable collections from `V` to the underlying collection of a modular operad extends uniquely to a morphism of modular operads from `MV`, compatible with flag permutations and contractions.

**Checks.**

- A one-vertex graph with one loop raises the genus by one and uses two flags of the vertex: from `V(g − 1, n + 2)` it contributes to `MV(g, n)`.
- A separating one-edge graph has total genus `g₁ + g₂`.
- For a two-edge graph with an automorphism exchanging the edges, the ordinary transform takes plain invariants; inserting the sign of the edge permutation would give the term of the closed-strata weight complex instead (compare §12.6).

### 1.4 The Getzler–Kapranov formula

In the completed ring of SchurWeyl, gap:completed-symmetric-functions, put `Ch(V) = Σ_{g,n} h^{g−1} ch(V(g, n))` and `Δ = Σ_{r≥1} h^r ((r/2) ∂²/∂p_r² + ∂/∂p_{2r})`. Prove `Ch(MV) = Log(exp(Δ) Exp(Ch(V)))`, where `exp(Δ)` is the ordinary exponential of the operator and `Exp`, `Log` are the plethystic exponential and logarithm (GK, Theorem 8.13; BFP, §9, p. 1354, where the theorem is cited as GK Theorem 8.3 and must be read as Theorem 8.13). Route: `Exp` forms unordered collections of vertices, `exp(Δ)` joins pairs of flags (the factor `r/2` counts edges joining two flags in an `r`-cycle and the `p_{2r}` term edges whose endpoints are exchanged), and `Log` selects connected graphs; Euler's formula gives the exponent of `h`. For point counts specialize `ψ_r(q) = q^r`.

*Needs:* §1.3; §1.2; SchurWeyl, gap:completed-symmetric-functions; SchemeKTheoryOperations §§6.2.1–6.2.2; HabiroNahmSeries §5.1 with gap:complete-adams-series.

**Checks.**

- `Δ(p₁²) = h·(1/2)·2 = h` and `Δ(p₂) = h`: both terms of `Δ` are pinned by these two values.
- The positive-edge part `Ch(MV) − Ch(V)` vanishes in genus zero with three legs: `M̄_{0,3}` has no boundary.
- On the collection `V(0, 3) = ℚ` (trivial) and zero otherwise, `MV(0, 4)` is three-dimensional with the permutation representation of `S₄` on the three partitions of four labels into pairs, matching the three boundary points of `M̄_{0,4}`.

### 1.5 Polynomial counts of open moduli and of the boundary

Let `(g, n)` be stable and suppose `M̄_{g′,n′}` has polynomial point count for every `(g′, n′) ≼ (g, n)`. Prove that every Frobenius eigenvalue on `H_c^*(M_{g,n} ⊗ 𝔽̄_q, ℚ_ℓ)` is an integral power of `q`, that `M_{g,n}` and `∂M̄_{g,n}` have polynomial point counts, and that their weight-graded equivariant Euler characteristics determine all twisted counts (BFP, Proposition 4.2, pp. 1331–1332; Corollary 9.5, p. 1353). Route: the weight spectral sequence of normalized closed strata with orientation characters controls the eigenvalues; ordinary graph additivity of open strata (§§1.3–1.4) counts the boundary. Frobenius on `H_c^*` of the open stack is not asserted to be semisimple.

*Needs:* §§1.1–1.4; §0.3; DeligneWeightsAndPurity §8.24; WeilConjectures, WC.5 (polynomial counts over all of `Spec ℤ`, equivariant polynomial counts) and WC.6 (smooth proper DM purity).

**Checks.**

- The orientation character appears in the weight spectral sequence but not in the open-stratum sum: for `M̄_{1,2}`, the codimension-two stratum of two rational vertices joined by two edges, each carrying one leg, has an edge-exchanging automorphism acting by `−1` on `det ℚ^E`, so its twisted `H⁰` invariants vanish while its untwisted invariants do not.
- A class function given by arbitrary twisted counts need not lie in the integral representation ring: for `X = Spec 𝔽_{q³}` with `G = C₃`, the twisted counts are `(0, 0, 3)` up to ordering, which is not a rational character (BFP, Definition 9.1, p. 1351, read with this qualification; WeilConjectures, WC.1, the integral character lattice).

### 1.6 Polynomial point counts in low genus

Prove that `M̄_{g,n}` has polynomial point count over every finite field, with `S_n`-equivariant polynomial counts, for `g = 0` and all `n`, for `g = 1` and `n ≤ 10`, for `g = 2` and `n ≤ 7`, and for `g = 3` and `n ≤ 5` (BFP, §2, p. 1329, recalling Getzler for genus one, Bergström's equivariant hyperelliptic counts §11.2 for genus two, and Bergström's genus-three counts Theorems 2.1–2.4; BFP §9, p. 1353, for the equivariant ranges `g′ ≤ 3`, `(g′, n′) ≺ (4, 3)`). Route: count the open strata `M_{g,n}^σ` for every cycle type, through hyperelliptic equations in genus two and plane quartics together with hyperelliptic curves in genus three, and assemble the compact counts by §§1.4–1.5.

*Needs:* §§1.1–1.5; WeilConjectures, WC.1 (stack mass, quotients by connected groups via Lang) and WC.5 (exact and equivariant polynomial counts); StableReductionPartII, MC.0.

**Checks.**

- The genus-one range stops at ten markings: `H¹¹(M̄_{1,11}) = S₁₂` has dimension two and its Frobenius traces `τ(p)` are not polynomial in `p`.
- `#M̄_{0,4}(𝔽_q) = q + 1` and `#M̄_{0,5}(𝔽_q) = q² + 5q + 1`.
- `#M_{1,1}(𝔽_q) = q` as a mass, while `#M̄_{1,1}(𝔽_q) = q + 1`.

### Examples

`e_c(M_{0,4}) = L − 2`, since `M_{0,4}` is `ℙ¹` minus three points; with the boundary of three points this gives `e_c(M̄_{0,4}) = L + 1`. As `S₄`-representations, the boundary contributes the three-dimensional permutation representation on pairings, which is `s_{(4)} + s_{(2,2)}`.

### Dependencies

Layer 0; StableReductionPartII, MC.0, MC.2, MC.3; SchemeAndStackFoundations §§1.18–1.21; WeilConjectures, WC.1, WC.5, WC.6; DeligneWeightsAndPurity §8.24; MixedHodgeModulesAndIrregularHodgeTheory with gap:dm-stack-mixed-hodge; SchurWeyl, Layer 7 with gap:completed-symmetric-functions; SchemeKTheoryOperations §§6.2.1–6.2.2; HabiroNahmSeries §5.1 with gap:complete-adams-series; Tau Ceti representation rings.

## Layer 2: Dimension bounds and odd vanishing in low degree

Boundary induction turns dimension bounds into a finite list of base cases. The strict improvement for zero or one marking (§2.2) is essential: the virtual cohomological dimension alone leaves the base cases `H⁹(M̄₅)` and `H⁹(M̄_{5,1})` open (BFP, Remark 2.4, p. 1329).

### 2.1 The virtual cohomological dimension

Prove that `H^i(M_{g,n}, ℚ) = 0` for `i > 4g − 5` when `g ≥ 2` and `n = 0`, and for `i > 4g − 4 + n` when `g ≥ 1` and `n ≥ 1`. Route: by the Teichmüller uniformization `H^*(M_{g,n}, ℚ) = H^*(Mod_{g,n}, ℚ)`; a torsion-free finite-index subgroup of the mapping class group has the stated cohomological dimension by Harer's equivariant spine of Teichmüller space, and the rational cohomology of the group is the invariants of that of the subgroup (Harer, the virtual cohomological dimension theorem; recalled in BFP, §2, p. 1328). These are rational bounds; they do not assert that the stacks have trivial stabilizers.

*Needs:* MappingClassGroupsAndCanonicalRepresentations §C.0 (Teichmüller uniformization); StableReductionPartII, MC.0.

**Checks.**

- For `M_{1,1}` the bound is `4 − 4 + 1 = 1`. It allows a nonzero `H¹` and does not by itself give `H¹(M_{1,1}, ℚ) = 0`, which holds because `H¹(SL₂(ℤ), ℚ) = 0`.
- For `M₂` the bound is `3`; for `M_{g,1}` it is `4g − 3`, the two values used in §2.2.

### 2.2 Vanishing in the virtual cohomological dimension

Prove that `H^{4g−5}(M_g, ℚ) = 0` for `g ≥ 2` and `H^{4g−3}(M_{g,1}, ℚ) = 0` for `g ≥ 1` (CFP, Theorem 1, which treats the closed surface and the surface with one marked point; MSS, Theorem 1.4 and Theorem 6.2, for the closed surface; recalled in BFP, proof of Proposition 2.1, p. 1328). No analogous statement for `n ≥ 2` is made: the top-weight part of `H_c^{n+2}(M_{2,n})` is nonzero for `4 ≤ n ≤ 17` (BFP, Remark 2.3, p. 1328).

*Needs:* §2.1; MappingClassGroupsAndCanonicalRepresentations §C.0.

**Checks.**

- For `g = 1` the marked statement is `H¹(M_{1,1}, ℚ) = H¹(SL₂(ℤ), ℚ) = 0`.
- With §2.2 the degree-nine base region of §2.5 contains no genus-five pair. With the bounds of §2.1 alone, the compact-support vanishing of §2.3 for `n ≤ 1` would hold only for `k < 2g − 1`, and `(5, 0)`, `(5, 1)` would enter the base region since `2·5 − 1 = 9` (BFP, Remark 2.4, p. 1329).

### 2.3 Low compact-support and Borel–Moore vanishing

For `g ≥ 1` prove that `H_c^k(M_{g,n}) = 0` if `k < 2g` and `n ∈ {0, 1}`, and if `k < 2g − 2 + n` and `n ≥ 2`; equivalently `H_k^{BM}(M_{g,n}) = 0` in the same ranges (BFP, Proposition 2.1, p. 1328, where the bound for `n ≥ 2` is credited to Arbarello–Cornalba; CLP §6.2, p. 23). Route: Poincaré duality `H_c^k ≅ H^{2d−k}(d)^∨` turns §§2.1–2.2 into these bounds.

*Needs:* §§2.1–2.2; WeilConjectures, gap:open-dm-stack-duality; MixedHodgeModulesAndIrregularHodgeTheory, gap:dm-stack-mixed-hodge.

**Checks.**

- At `(g, n) = (4, 1)`, `H_c^k = 0` for `k < 8`; the vanishing does not extend to `k = 8`.
- At `(2, 12)` the threshold is `2·2 − 2 + 12 = 14`, which leaves the degree-fifteen calculation of §11.9.
- Genus zero is excluded: `M_{0,4}` is `ℙ¹` minus three points and has `H_c¹ ≅ ℚ²`.

### 2.4 The Arbarello–Cornalba boundary injection

Let `(g, n)` be stable with `H_c^k(M_{g,n}) = 0`. Prove that pullback to the normalization `D̃` of the boundary, the disjoint union of the normalized one-edge strata with their graph automorphisms, is injective on `H^k(M̄_{g,n})`, in both realizations (AC, Lemma (2.6); recalled in BFP, §2, p. 1328). Route: the exact sequence `H_c^k(M_{g,n}) → H^k(M̄_{g,n}) → H^k(∂M̄_{g,n})`, purity of `H^k(M̄_{g,n})`, and injectivity of the weight-`k` part of `H^k(∂M̄_{g,n})` into `H^k(D̃)`.

*Needs:* §0.4; DeligneWeightsAndPurity §8.24; MixedHodgeModulesAndIrregularHodgeTheory, gap:dm-stack-mixed-hodge.

**Checks.**

- The hypothesis cannot be dropped: `H¹¹(M̄_{1,11}) ≠ 0` restricts to zero on every boundary component, whose factors have no `H¹¹`, and `H_c^{11}(M_{1,11}) ≠ 0`.
- Cohomology of the singular boundary is not replaced by that of its normalization in all weights; only the weight-`k` part injects.

### 2.5 The Arbarello–Cornalba odd-degree induction

Let `k ≥ 1` be odd. Suppose `H^q(M̄_{g,n}) = 0` for every odd `q ≤ k` and every stable pair with `g ≥ 1` and `max(2g, 2g − 2 + n) ≤ q`. Then `H^q(M̄_{g,n}) = 0` for every odd `q ≤ k` and every stable pair (AC, Lemma (2.9); BFP, §2, pp. 1328–1329). Route: induct on `q` and on the stable-pair order; outside the base region §2.3 gives `H_c^q(M_{g,n}) = 0`, so §2.4 injects `H^q` into the cohomology of boundary products, in which Künneth produces an odd factor of smaller degree or smaller pair; genus zero has no odd cohomology by §2.6.

*Needs:* §§2.3, 2.4, 2.6; §0.3.

**Checks.**

- For `k = 9`, after the genus-at-most-three cases of §2.9, the base region leaves only `(4, n)` with `n ≤ 3`.
- The base region is finite for each `k`: `2g ≤ k` bounds the genus and `2g − 2 + n ≤ k` the markings.

### 2.6 Keel's theorem in genus zero

For `n ≥ 3` prove that the cycle class map `A^*(M̄_{0,n}) → H^{2*}(M̄_{0,n})` is a ring isomorphism, that the ring is generated by boundary divisors subject to the symmetry, four-point and disjointness relations, and that `H^{odd}(M̄_{0,n}) = 0` (Keel, Theorem 1 and its proof; BFP, §2, p. 1329; CLP §6.2, p. 23). Route: `M̄_{0,n+1}` is an iterated blow-up of `M̄_{0,n} × ℙ¹` along smooth centres; apply the blow-up formulas for Chow groups and cohomology. In particular `H^*(M̄_{0,n}) = RH^*(M̄_{0,n})`.

*Needs:* §0.1; SchemeAndStackFoundations Layer 5, §§5.1–5.6 (Chow groups of smooth projective schemes and blow-ups); MotivesAndAlgebraicCycles §2.6 (Betti cycle maps of smooth projective schemes).

**Checks.**

- `M̄_{0,3}` is a point; `M̄_{0,4} ≅ ℙ¹` has three equal boundary-point classes and `H² = ℚ`.
- `M̄_{0,5}` is the blow-up of `ℙ²` at four points: `b₂ = 5`, and its ten boundary divisors span a space of rank five.
- The open `M_{0,4}` has `H¹ ≠ 0`; the theorem concerns the compactification only.

### 2.7 Vanishing in degrees one, three and five

Prove that `H^k(M̄_{g,n}) = 0` for `k ∈ {1, 3, 5}` and every stable pair (AC, Theorem (2.1); BFP, proof of Theorem 1.1 for `k = 7`, p. 1329). Route: the base cases of §2.5 for `k ≤ 5` are the pairs with `g ≥ 1` and `max(2g, 2g − 2 + n) ≤ 5`, each with polynomial point count by §1.6; apply §2.5.

*Needs:* §§2.5, 2.6, 1.6; WeilConjectures, WC.5 (polynomial counts over all of `Spec ℤ`) and WC.6.

**Checks.**

- `H¹(M̄_{1,1}) = 0` although `M̄_{1,1}` has nontrivial stabilizers everywhere; rational coefficients kill stabilizer cohomology.
- The statement concerns compact moduli: `gr^W_4 H³(M_{1,n}) ≠ 0` for `n ≥ 4` (Petersen, Theorem 1.3), so `H³` of the open stack need not vanish.

### 2.8 Vanishing in degree seven

Prove that `H⁷(M̄_{g,n}) = 0` for every stable pair (BFP, Theorem 1.1 and its proof for `k = 7`, p. 1329). Route: the base cases with `g ≥ 1` and `max(2g, 2g − 2 + n) ≤ 7` lie in the ranges of §1.6, so their cohomology is even; apply §2.5 with §2.7.

*Needs:* §§2.5, 2.7, 1.6; WeilConjectures, WC.5 and WC.6.

**Checks.**

- Without §2.2 the base region for `k = 7` would include `M̄₄` and `M̄_{4,1}` (BFP, Remark 2.4, p. 1329).

### 2.9 Odd vanishing through nine in genus at most three

Prove that `H^q(M̄_{g,n}) = 0` for every odd `q ≤ 9` and every stable pair with `g ≤ 3` (BFP, §2, pp. 1328–1329). Route: run §2.5 restricted to `g ≤ 3`; its degree-nine base cases have `n ≤ 9, 7, 5` in genera one, two, three, which are the ranges of §1.6.

- At `(3, 5)`, `2g − 2 + n = 9` is the largest genus-three base case and lies in the polynomial range.

## Layer 3: Genus one, symplectic local systems and the stable range

The genus-one cusp formula supplies the first non-tautological seeds. Symplectic local systems keep the tensor weight separate from the Specht representation that records the permutation of markings.

### 3.1 Symplectic local systems on curve moduli

For `g ≥ 1` let `f : C → M_g` be the universal curve (for `g = 1`, over `M_{1,1}`) and `V = R¹f_*ℚ`, of rank `2g`, with the alternating cup-product pairing `V ⊗ V → ℚ(−1)`. For a partition `λ = (λ₁ ≥ … ≥ λ_g ≥ 0)` with at most `g` parts define `V_λ` as the summand of `V^{⊗|λ|}` cut out by the harmonic (contraction-free) tensors and the Young symmetrizer of `λ`, the irreducible local system of highest weight `λ` for the symplectic monodromy, of weight `|λ|` (CLP, §3, pp. 9–10; Petersen–Tavakol–Yin, §3.2; BFP, §11.4, pp. 1361–1362). Prove that the formation of `V_λ` commutes with pullback along morphisms of families of smooth curves, and that `V^{⊗n}` modulo the images of the `n(n−1)/2` insertions of the symplectic form is `⊕_{|λ|=n} V_λ ⊗ T_{λ^T}` as local systems with `S_n`-action (CLP, proof of Lemma 3.1, p. 10).

*Needs:* StableReductionPartII, MC.2 (the universal curve over `M_g`); SchurWeyl, Layers 7 and 9 (Young symmetrizers, Brauer contractions and harmonic tensors for the alternating form); DeligneWeightsAndPurity §8.24 and MixedHodgeModulesAndIrregularHodgeTheory, gap:dm-stack-mixed-hodge (cohomology of local systems on DM stacks).

API:

- `V_{(1)} = V` and `V_∅ = ℚ`.
- `V_λ` is pure of weight `|λ|`.
- The central element `−1` of the symplectic group acts on `V_λ` by `(−1)^{|λ|}`; on `M₂`, where every curve is hyperelliptic, the hyperelliptic involution acts on the fibres of `V_λ` by this sign.
- Formation of `V_λ` commutes with pullback along morphisms of smooth curve families.
- The contraction `V ⊗ V → ℚ(−1)` is the cup product; the primitive part of `Λ²V` is `V_{(1,1)}`, the kernel of the contraction.

**Checks.**

- For `λ = (1)`, `V_λ = V` has rank `2g`, which is `8` on `M₄`.
- For `g = 1`, `V_{(r)} = Sym^r V`; there are no partitions with two nonzero parts.
- `Λ²V = V_{(1,1)} ⊕ ℚ(−1)`; identifying `V_{(1,1)}` with all of `Λ²V` would retain a contraction summand of rank one. For `g = 2`, `rank V_{(1,1)} = 6 − 1 = 5`.

### 3.2 Pure cohomology of smooth genus-one moduli

For `k ≥ 1` and `n ≥ k` prove that `W_k H^k(M_{1,n}) ≅ S_{k+1} ⊗ T_{(n−k+1, 1^{k−1})}` as `S_n`-equivariant Hodge structures and Galois representations, with a basis of the multiplicity space given by the `binom(n−1, k−1)` pullbacks from `W_k H^k(M_{1,A})` along the forgetful maps, `A` running over the `k`-element subsets of the markings containing `1`; and `W_k H^k(M_{1,n}) = 0` for `n < k` (CLP, Proposition 2.2, pp. 5–7, after Getzler). Route: `M_{1,n}` is open in the `(n−1)`-fold fibre power `E^{n−1}` of the universal elliptic curve; the Leray decomposition and Künneth leave the summand `H¹(M_{1,1}, V^{⊗(k−1)})`, whose pure part is `S_{k+1}` by Eichler–Shimura (§0.5); straightening subsets containing a fixed marking identifies the hook Specht module.

*Needs:* §0.5; §3.1; SchurWeyl, Layer 7 (hook characters); StableReductionPartII, MC.2.

**Checks.**

- At `n = k = 11` the group is `S₁₂`, of rational dimension two; at `n = 12, k = 11` it is `S₁₂^{11}` of dimension twenty-two, the hook `(2, 1^{10})` having dimension eleven.
- For odd `k + 1` there are no cusp forms of level one, so `W_k H^k(M_{1,n}) = 0` for every even `k ≥ 2`; at `k = 13`, `S₁₄ = 0` for every `n`.
- The multiplicity `binom(n−1, k−1)` is the dimension of the hook `(n−k+1, 1^{k−1})`, not `binom(n, k)`: subsets not containing the fixed marking are linear combinations of those that do.

### 3.3 Even genus-one cohomology is tautological

For every `n ≥ 1` prove that `H^{2r}(M̄_{1,n})` is spanned by cycle classes of boundary strata, hence equals `RH^{2r}(M̄_{1,n})`, for every `r` (Petersen, Theorem 1.1 and Corollary 1.2; CLP, §2.2, pp. 5–7, and proof of Proposition 2.4, p. 7). Route: Petersen's vanishing of pure even cohomology of `M_{1,n}` in positive degree and the boundary exact sequence of §0.4, by induction on `n`. In particular every psi class on `M̄_{1,n}` is a rational combination of boundary classes.

*Needs:* §0.1; §0.4; §2.6; §3.2.

**Checks.**

- At `n = 1`, `ψ₁ = δ_irr/12` on `M̄_{1,1}`, with `δ_irr` the class of the boundary point in the stack normalization (degree `1/2`), so `deg ψ₁ = 1/24`.
- The statement is about cycle classes, not merely about the Tate type: an even class of type `(r, r)` that is not a boundary combination would violate it, and the even Tate type alone does not imply it.
- Odd cohomology is not covered: `H¹¹(M̄_{1,11}) ≠ 0`.

### 3.4 Homological stability and the stable cohomology

Prove: for every stable pair `(g, n)` with `g ≥ 2`, the natural map `ℚ[κ₁, κ₂, …] ⊗ ℚ[ψ₁, …, ψ_n] → H^*(M_{g,n}, ℚ)` is an isomorphism in degrees `k ≤ (2g − 2)/3` (Wahl, Theorem 1.1, for the stability range in Harer's theorem; Madsen–Weiss, Theorem 1.1.1, for the stable cohomology `ℚ[κ₁, κ₂, …]`; Looijenga, Proposition (2.1), for the marked points). Route: by §C.0 of MappingClassGroupsAndCanonicalRepresentations, `H^*(M_{g,n}, ℚ) = H^*(Mod_{g,n}, ℚ)`; Harer stability with Wahl's range identifies the cohomology with its stable limit, the Madsen–Weiss theorem computes the unmarked stable cohomology, and the Gysin sequences of the marked points add the polynomial generators `ψᵢ`. The kappa classes here are the Mumford–Morita–Miller classes, which agree with §0.2 up to the normalization of `κ_a` as `π_*(ψ^{a+1})`.

*Needs:* MappingClassGroupsAndCanonicalRepresentations §C.0; §0.2; §2.1.

**Checks.**

- In degree two with `g ≥ 4`, the stable cohomology is spanned by `κ₁, ψ₁, …, ψ_n`, of dimension `n + 1`: this is the stable part of the Arbarello–Cornalba description of `H²` (AC, Theorem (2.2)).
- The range is a condition on `g`: `k ≤ (2g − 2)/3` is equivalent to `g ≥ 3k/2 + 1`. For `k = 2` this needs `g ≥ 4`; no claim is made for `M_3` in degree two.
- In degree one the stable cohomology vanishes, matching `H¹(M_{g,n}, ℚ) = 0` for `g ≥ 1`.

### 3.5 The stable range is tautological

For `g ≥ 2` and every `n`, prove that `H^k(M_{g,n})` is spanned by restrictions of tautological classes for `k ≤ (2g − 2)/3`, and that `H^k(M_{g,n}) = 0` for `k > 4g − 4 + n` (CLP, proof of Theorem 1.3, p. 10). These deliberately non-sharp bounds are those used in §4.9.

*Needs:* §3.4; §2.1; §0.1.

**Checks.**

- `H⁶(M_{g,n})` is tautological for `g ≥ 10`, since `6 ≤ (2·10 − 2)/3 = 6`; for `g = 9` the bound gives only degrees at most `5`.
- The vanishing bound `4g − 4 + n` is weaker than §2.1 for `n = 0` by one; either suffices for §4.9.

### Examples

`W₁₁H¹¹(M_{1,12}) ≅ S₁₂ ⊗ T_{(2,1^{10})}`, of dimension `2 · 11 = 22`, spanned by pullbacks along the eleven forgetful maps that forget one of the markings `2, …, 12`. On `M₂` every local system of odd weight has vanishing cohomology because the hyperelliptic involution acts by `−1`.

### Dependencies

Layers 0 and 2; StableReductionPartII, MC.2; MappingClassGroupsAndCanonicalRepresentations §C.0; SchurWeyl, Layers 7 and 9; DeligneWeightsAndPurity §8.24; MixedHodgeModulesAndIrregularHodgeTheory with gap:dm-stack-mixed-hodge.

## Layer 4: Semi-tautological extensions and finite generation

STEs are the algebras in which uniform generation statements make sense. The filling criterion of §4.5 strengthens the printed hypotheses of CLP Lemma 2.1, so that forgetting a marking in the same degree is justified.

### 4.1 The marking subspaces Φ and Ψ

For a stable pair `(g, n)` and `k ≥ 0` define `Φ^k_{g,n} ⊂ W_k H^k(M_{g,n})` as the sum of the images of the pullbacks `π_i^* : W_k H^k(M_{g,n−1}) → W_k H^k(M_{g,n})` along the forgetful maps `π_i` whose target is stable, and `Ψ^k_{g,n} = Σ_i ψ_i · Φ^{k−2}_{g,n}`. A sum over an empty set of stable forgetful targets is zero, and negative degrees contribute zero (CLP, §2.1, p. 4, and §3, p. 9).

*Needs:* §0.3; StableReductionPartII, MC.2 and MC.3 (forgetful maps and psi classes); §0.4 (the weight filtration of `M_{g,n}`).

API:

- Each pullback along a forgetful map to a stable target lands in `Φ^k_{g,n}`.
- `Ψ^k_{g,n}` is spanned by products of degree-two psi classes with classes in `Φ^{k−2}_{g,n}`.
- Relabelling the markings carries the summands of `Φ` and `Ψ` to the permuted summands; both are `S_n`-stable.
- There is no forgetful map from `M_{1,1}`, so `Φ^k_{1,1} = 0`.
- A pure degree-`k` class lies in `Φ^k_{g,n}` exactly when it is a sum of pullbacks from the stable targets obtained by forgetting one marking.
- A class lies in `Ψ^k_{g,n}` exactly when it is a sum of products `ψ_i x_i` with `x_i ∈ Φ^{k−2}_{g,n}`.

**Checks.**

- `Φ^k_{1,1} = 0` for every `k`, including `k = 0`, under the stable-target convention.
- `Φ⁰_{g,n} = ℚ = W₀H⁰(M_{g,n})` when `n > 0` and `(g, n − 1)` is stable.
- `Ψ¹_{g,n} = 0`, since `Φ^{−1} = 0`.

### 4.2 The local-system quotient of the marking filtration

For `g ≥ 2` prove `W_k H^k(M_{g,n}) / (Φ^k_{g,n} + Ψ^k_{g,n}) ≅ ⊕_{|λ|=n} W_k H^{k−n}(M_g, V_λ) ⊗ T_{λ^T}`, the sum over partitions of `n` with at most `g` parts, and that `W_k H^k(M_{g,n}) = Φ^k_{g,n}` when `n > 0` and `n ≥ k` (CLP, Lemma 3.1, pp. 9–10, using Petersen–Tavakol–Yin, §§5.1 and 5.2.2). Route: `M_{g,n}` is open in the `n`-fold fibre power `C^n` of the universal curve; the Leray decomposition and Künneth write `H^k(C^n)` as a sum over `(i_1, …, i_n)`; summands with some `i_s = 0` give `Φ`, those with some `i_s = 2` give `Ψ`, diagonal classes die on `M_{g,n}`, and the remaining summand `H^{k−n}(M_g, V^{⊗n})` modulo symplectic insertions is the right side by §3.1, the transpose arising from the odd degree of `H¹`. For `n ≥ k` a summand with `i_s = 2` forces another `i_{s′} = 0`, and `H⁰(M_g, V_λ) = 0` for nontrivial `λ`.

*Needs:* §§3.1, 4.1; SchurWeyl, Layers 7 and 9; StableReductionPartII, MC.2; DeligneWeightsAndPurity §8.24 and MixedHodgeModulesAndIrregularHodgeTheory, gap:dm-stack-mixed-hodge (Leray degeneration and purity for the smooth proper family `C^n → M_g`).

**Checks.**

- The Specht index is transposed: for `n = 2` the local system `V_{(2)} = Sym²V` pairs with the sign representation `T_{(1,1)}`, and `V_{(1,1)}` with the trivial one.
- For `n = 1` the quotient is `W_k H^{k−1}(M_g, V)`.
- For `n = k = 0` the equality `W₀H⁰ = Φ⁰` is not asserted: the hypothesis `n > 0` excludes it, and `Φ⁰_{g,0} = 0 ≠ ℚ`.

### 4.3 Even cohomology of genus-two moduli

For `0 ≤ r ≤ 10` and every `n ≥ 0` prove `W_{2r} H^{2r}(M_{2,n}) = RH^{2r}(M_{2,n})` and consequently `H^{2r}(M̄_{2,n}) = RH^{2r}(M̄_{2,n})` (CLP, Proposition 2.6, p. 7, and Corollary 2.7, p. 8; Petersen, genus two, Theorem 3.8 for `n < 20`). Route: for `n < 20` use Petersen's theorem on compact type; for `n ≥ 20 ≥ 2r` the second part of §4.2 gives `W_{2r}H^{2r}(M_{2,n}) = Φ^{2r}_{2,n}`, which is tautological by induction on `n` because forgetful pullbacks preserve tautological classes; lift by the filling criterion §4.5 applied to the tautological STE, with §§2.6, 2.9 and 3.3 for the boundary factors.

*Needs:* §§4.2, 4.5; §0.4; §§2.6, 2.9, 3.3; §3.1.

**Checks.**

- The bound is cohomological degree `20`, that is `r ≤ 10`, not Chow codimension twenty.
- `H²²(M̄_{2,20})` contains a non-tautological algebraic class (Graber–Pandharipande, recalled in CLP, Introduction, p. 3), so the range cannot be extended to `r = 11`.

### 4.4 Semi-tautological extensions

An STE is a collection of unital graded `ℚ`-subalgebras `S^*(M̄_{g,n}) ⊂ H^*(M̄_{g,n})`, one for each stable pair, containing `RH^*(M̄_{g,n})` and closed under pullback along forgetful morphisms and relabellings and under pushforward along clutching morphisms, including clutching of exterior products of classes on the factors (CLP, Definition 1.2, p. 2). Closure under forgetful pushforward or clutching pullback is not part of the definition. The STE generated by a family of subspaces is the intersection of all STEs containing it; an STE is motivic if each `S^*` is a sub-Hodge structure and its `ℚ_ℓ`-span is `G_ℚ`-stable.

*Needs:* §0.1; StableReductionPartII, MC.2 and MC.3.

API:

- The STE generated by a family is contained in an STE `S` if and only if `S` contains the family.
- Enlarging the generating family enlarges the generated STE.
- The empty family generates `RH^*`.
- Every class of a generated STE is a finite sum of decorated-graph expressions: clutching pushforwards of exterior products of pullbacks of generators, multiplied by tautological classes.
- Two STEs with the same subalgebras at every stable pair are equal.
- An STE contains the unit and `RH^*` and is closed under relabelling, forgetful pullback and clutching pushforward of exterior products.

**Checks.**

- The full cohomology system `H^*` is an STE, and so is the system of subrings generated by algebraic cycle classes.
- The empty family generates `RH^*`, not the zero system: the unit is required.
- `H¹¹(M̄_{1,11})` is not in the STE generated by the empty family, since every class there has even degree.

### 4.5 The filling criterion

Let `S` be an STE, `(g, n)` a stable pair and `k ≥ 0`. Suppose that `S^{k′}(M̄_{g′,n′}) → W_{k′} H^{k′}(M_{g′,n′}) / (Φ^{k′}_{g′,n′} + RH^{k′}(M_{g′,n′}))` is surjective for `(g′, n′, k′) = (g, n, k)` and for every `(g′, n′, k′)` with `(g′, n′) ≺ (g, n)`, `2d_{g′,n′} − k′ ≤ 2d_{g,n} − k` and `0 ≤ k′ ≤ k`. Then `S^k(M̄_{g,n}) = H^k(M̄_{g,n})`. This corrects CLP, Lemma 2.1, pp. 4–5, whose hypothesis (2.2) requires only `k′ ≤ k − 2`; its proof also uses `S^k(M̄_{g,n−1}) = H^k(M̄_{g,n−1})`, a same-degree case that (2.2) does not provide. Route: induct on the stable-pair order; the enlarged hypothesis passes to every boundary Künneth factor (degree at most `k − 2`) and to every stable forgetful source in degree `k`; lift `Φ` from those sources, `RH` by definition, and the remaining quotient by assumption; §0.4 gives the equality.

*Needs:* §§4.1, 4.4; §0.3; §0.4.

**Checks.**

- The printed hypothesis is insufficient. Take `S = RH^*` and `(g, n, k) = (1, 12, 11)`. By §3.2 every class of `W₁₁H¹¹(M_{1,12})` is a pullback along a forgetful map, so the quotient at `(1, 12, 11)` vanishes; all triples with `k′ ≤ 9` in genus at most one have vanishing quotient, since `W_{k′}H^{k′}(M_{1,n′}) = 0` for `1 ≤ k′ ≤ 9`, and genus zero is tautological. Yet `RH¹¹ = 0` while `dim H¹¹(M̄_{1,12}) = 22`. The corrected hypothesis includes `(1, 11, 11)`, where the quotient is `S₁₂ ≠ 0`, and so excludes this case.
- The hypothesis at `(g, n, k)` itself cannot be dropped: for `S = RH^*` at `(1, 11, 11)` every smaller triple is fine but the conclusion fails.
- For `k = 0` the criterion reduces to the unit: `W₀H⁰(M_{g,n}) = ℚ = RH⁰`.

### 4.6 The odd-marking reduction in genus two

For odd `n ≥ 1` and `k ≥ 2` prove that the STE generated by `H^k(M̄_{2,n−1})`, `H^{k−2}(M̄_{2,n−1})` and `H^{k−2}(M̄_{1,m})` for `1 ≤ m ≤ n + 2` contains `H^k(M̄_{2,n})` (CLP, Lemma 3.2, p. 10). Route: by §4.2 the quotient by `Φ + Ψ` is pure cohomology of local systems of odd weight `n` on `M₂`, killed by the hyperelliptic involution (§3.1); `Φ` and `Ψ` come from `(2, n − 1)`; boundary classes come from the listed genus-one and genus-two groups.

*Needs:* §§3.1, 4.2, 4.4; §0.4; §2.6.

**Checks.**

- `n = 11, k = 13` and `n = 13, k = 15` give the reductions used in §11.10.
- For even `n` the argument fails: local systems of even weight on `M₂` can have pure cohomology, for instance `W₁₇H¹⁷(M_{2,14}) ≠ 0` (CLP, Introduction, p. 3).

### 4.7 The eleventh cohomology group in all genera

Prove that `H¹¹(M̄_{g,n}) ≠ 0` if and only if `g = 1` and `n ≥ 11`, that `H¹¹(M̄_{1,n}) ≅ S₁₂^{⊕ binom(n−1, 10)}` is spanned by pullbacks of `H¹¹(M̄_{1,11})` along forgetful maps, and that the pullback of `H¹¹(M̄_{1,n})` to any boundary divisor lies in the STE generated by `H¹¹(M̄_{1,11})` (CLP-H11, Theorem 1.1, Lemma 2.1 and Lemma 2.2). Route: the Arbarello–Cornalba induction of §§2.4–2.5 in degree eleven, whose base cases are `(1, n)` with `n ≤ 11` and are settled by §3.2 and §1.6, together with the boundary formula for the forms `ω_A`.

*Needs:* §§1.6, 2.4, 2.5, 3.2, 4.4; §0.5.

**Checks.**

- `H¹¹(M̄_{1,10}) = 0`, consistent with the polynomial count of §1.6.
- `dim H¹¹(M̄_{1,12}) = 2·11 = 22`; `dim H¹¹(M̄_{1,13}) = 2·66 = 132`.
- `H¹¹(M̄_{2,n}) = 0` for all `n`, so the STE generated by `H¹¹(M̄_{1,11})` meets genus two only through boundary pushforwards in higher degree.

### 4.8 Cusp-generated extensions are closed under all tautological operations

Let `S_ω` be the STE generated by `H¹¹(M̄_{1,11})`. Prove that `S_ω` contains `H¹¹(M̄_{g,n})` and `H¹³(M̄_{1,n})` for all stable pairs, and that `S_ω` is closed under forgetful pushforward and clutching pullback; the same holds for the STE generated by any rational sub-Hodge structures of the genus-one cusp realizations `S_{k+1} ⊂ H^k(M̄_{1,k})`, with their Galois span in the ℓ-adic realization (CLP, Corollary 2.3, Proposition 2.4 and Remark 2.5, p. 7). Route: `S₁₄ = 0` forces `H¹³(M̄_{1,n})` onto the boundary (§§0.4, 3.2); every class of `S_ω` is a decorated graph whose non-tautological decorations are `H¹¹`-classes on genus-one vertices (products of two odd classes in genus one are boundary combinations by §3.3); forgetful pushforward of `H¹¹(M̄_{1,n})` vanishes; §4.7 and the excess intersection formula give closure under clutching pullback.

*Needs:* §§0.4, 3.2, 3.3, 4.4, 4.7.

**Checks.**

- An arbitrary complex vector of `S₁₂ ⊗ ℂ` does not generate a motivic STE: the generated STE is that of its rational sub-Hodge structure, here all of `S₁₂`, since `S₁₂` is simple of rank two.
- The pushforward of a class of `H¹¹(M̄_{1,12})` along `M̄_{1,12} → M̄_{1,11}` lands in `H⁹(M̄_{1,11}) = 0`.

### 4.9 Finite generation in each cohomological degree

For every `k ≥ 0` prove that the STE generated by the groups `H^{k′}(M̄_{g′,n′})` with `k′ ≤ k`, `g′ < 3k′/2 + 1`, `n′ ≤ k′` and `4g′ − 4 + n′ ≥ k′` contains `H^k(M̄_{g,n})` for every stable pair; the set of such triples is finite (CLP, Theorem 1.3 and its proof, pp. 2 and 10). Route: apply §4.5; outside the region the quotient of §4.5 vanishes, by §4.2 when `n′ > k′` (for genus one by §3.2), by §3.5 when `k′ > 4g′ − 4 + n′` or `k′ ≤ (2g′ − 2)/3`, and by §2.6 in genus zero; inside the region the generators supply surjectivity.

*Needs:* §§2.6, 3.2, 3.5, 4.2, 4.5.

**Checks.**

- The genus bound is strict and the other two are weak: for `k′ = 4` the region allows `g′ ≤ 6`, not `g′ = 7`.
- There is no seed at `(k′, g′, n′) = (0, 0, 0)`, which is unstable; degree zero is the unit of `RH^*`.
- For `k = 11` the seed `H¹¹(M̄_{1,11})` lies in the region (`1 < 17.5`, `11 ≤ 11`, `4 − 4 + 11 ≥ 11`).

### 4.10 Finite generation for homology

For fixed `k ≥ 0` prove that there is a finitely generated STE `S` with `S^{2d−j}(M̄_{g,n}) = H^{2d−j}(M̄_{g,n})` for every stable pair and every `0 ≤ j ≤ k`; one generating family consists of the groups `H^{2d−j}(M̄_{g,n})`, `0 ≤ j ≤ min(k, 2d)`, at the pairs with `g ≥ 1` and either `n ≤ 1, 2g ≤ k` or `n ≥ 2, 2g − 2 + n ≤ k` (CLP, Remark 3.3, p. 10, which asserts the existence; the explicit region is derived here from §2.3). Route: induct on `j` and then on the stable-pair order; outside the region `H_j^{BM}(M_{g,n}) = 0` by §2.3, so by §0.4 `H_j(M̄_{g,n})` is pushed forward from the boundary, whose Künneth factors have homological degree at most `j`; genus zero is tautological.

*Needs:* §§0.4, 2.3, 2.6, 4.4.

**Checks.**

- Each group is translated with its own `d`: a fixed homological degree `j` is cohomological degree `2d_{g,n} − j`, which varies with the pair.
- For `k = 13` the region in genus one is `n ≤ 13`, in genus two `n ≤ 11`.

### 4.11 Finitely many simple realizations in each degree

For each `k` prove that only finitely many isomorphism classes of simple rational Hodge structures, respectively of simple `ℚ_ℓ[G_ℚ]`-modules, occur in `⊕_{g,n} H^k(M̄_{g,n})^ss` (CLP, Corollary 1.4, p. 2). Route: by §4.9 every class is a decorated graph built from finitely many seed groups, finitely many tensor lengths and edge Tate twists; graph operations give subquotients of finitely many tensor products.

*Needs:* §§4.4, 4.9.

**Checks.**

- The list depends on `k`; no finiteness across all degrees is asserted, and `S₁₂ L^m` occurs in degree `11 + 2m` for every `m ≥ 0`.
- In degree eleven the list is `{S₁₂}`; in degree twelve it is `{L⁶}`.

### 4.12 Top-heaviness

Let `S` be an STE, `(g, n)` stable, `d = d_{g,n}`, and `a` a tautological divisor class that is the pullback of an ample class on the coarse space. Prove that cup product with `a^{d−k}` is injective `S^k(M̄_{g,n}) → S^{2d−k}(M̄_{g,n})` for `0 ≤ k ≤ d`, and that cup product with `a` is injective `S^k → S^{k+2}` for `k < d`; hence `dim S^k ≤ dim S^{2d−k}` and the dimensions are nondecreasing up to the middle in each parity (CLP, Introduction, p. 3). Route: hard Lefschetz on `H^*` restricted to `S`, which `a` preserves because it is tautological; if `ax = 0` with `k < d` then `a^{d−k}x = 0`, so `x = 0`. CLP also asserts that dimensions decrease above the middle; that requires a separate argument and is not part of this target.

*Needs:* §4.4; StableReductionPartII, gap:pointed-coarse-projectivity; DeligneWeightsAndPurity, gap:dm-stack-hard-lefschetz; MixedHodgeModulesAndIrregularHodgeTheory, gap:dm-stack-mixed-hodge.

**Checks.**

- No perfect pairing on `S` is claimed: top-heaviness allows `dim S^k < dim S^{2d−k}`, and for `S = RH^*` with `k ≥ 22` the pairing can fail to be perfect (Petersen–Tommasi, recalled in CLP, p. 3).
- For `M̄_{0,4}`, `d = 1`: `a` maps `S⁰ = ℚ` isomorphically onto `S² = ℚ`.

### Examples

For `k = 13` the seeds of §4.9 in degree `k′ = 13` are the groups `H¹³(M̄_{g′,n′})` with `g′ ≤ 20`, `n′ ≤ 13` and `4g′ − 4 + n′ ≥ 13`; in genus one this is `n′ = 13` only, and in genus two `9 ≤ n′ ≤ 13`. The STE `S_ω` contains every `H¹¹` and every genus-one `H¹³`, and by Layer 12 every `H¹³`.

### Dependencies

Layers 0–3; StableReductionPartII, MC.2, MC.3, with gap:pointed-coarse-projectivity; DeligneWeightsAndPurity §8.24 with gap:dm-stack-hard-lefschetz; MixedHodgeModulesAndIrregularHodgeTheory with gap:dm-stack-mixed-hodge; SchurWeyl, Layers 7 and 9.

## Layer 5: Chow–Künneth generation and pure algebraicity

The Chow–Künneth generation property quantifies over every test stack. On an open stack it controls exactly the lowest-weight cohomology.

### 5.1 The Chow–Künneth generation property

A smooth finite-type algebraic stack `X` over a field `k`, stratified by quotient stacks, has the Chow–Künneth generation property (CKgP) if for every finite-type algebraic stack `Y` over `k` stratified by quotient stacks the exterior product `A_*(X) ⊗_ℚ A_*(Y) → A_*(X × Y)` is surjective (CLP, Definition 4.1, p. 11). `Y` need be neither smooth nor proper; the universal quantifier is part of the definition.

*Needs:* SchemeAndStackFoundations, gap:stack-rational-chow (Chow groups and exterior products of stacks stratified by quotient stacks).

API:

- For every admissible `Y`, each class on `X × Y` is a finite sum of exterior products.
- A class of dimension `r` on `X × Y` is a sum of exterior products of total dimension `r`, by projecting to the graded piece.
- An isomorphism of stacks preserves the CKgP.
- `Spec k` has the CKgP, its exterior product being the identity.

**Checks.**

- A point has the CKgP; the exterior product with any `Y` is the identity of `A_*(Y)`.
- `ℙ¹` has the CKgP, by localization from a point and `𝔸¹`, with `A_*(ℙ¹ × Y)` generated by the point and the fundamental class.
- A complex elliptic curve `E` does not have the CKgP: the diagonal of `E × E` has a component in `H¹ ⊗ H¹`, which no sum of exterior products of algebraic classes reaches. Testing only `Y = Spec k` would not detect this.

### 5.2 Permanence of the Chow–Künneth generation property

Within smooth finite-type stacks stratified by quotient stacks prove: the CKgP passes to open substacks; it descends along proper surjective morphisms representable by DM stacks; it holds for a stack with a finite stratification whose strata have it; it is equivalent for the total space and base of an affine bundle; it passes to Grassmann bundles; and `BGL_r`, `BSL_r`, `BPGL_r` have it (CLP, Proposition 4.2, p. 11, citing Canning–Larson §3.1). Route: localization after product with every `Y`; rational surjectivity of proper pushforward via a generically finite multisection; homotopy invariance; the Grassmann bundle formula; finite-dimensional approximations of classifying stacks.

*Needs:* §5.1; SchemeAndStackFoundations, gap:stack-rational-chow.

**Checks.**

- `BC₂` over a field of characteristic not two has the CKgP: `Spec k → BC₂` is proper, surjective and representable (a `C₂`-torsor), and `A^*(BC₂ × Y) = A^*(Y)` rationally. With integral coefficients `A¹(BC₂) = ℤ/2` would not be reached; the rational convention is essential.
- A Grassmann bundle of a vector bundle has the CKgP if its base does; a Brauer–Severi scheme without a vector-bundle presentation is not covered by this clause and is treated through a lift (§7.4).

### 5.3 The Chow–Künneth generation property makes pure cohomology algebraic

Let `X` be an open substack of a smooth proper Deligne–Mumford stack `X̄` over `ℂ` of pure dimension `d`, and suppose `X` has the CKgP. Prove that the cycle class map `⊕_i A^i(X) → ⊕_k W_k H^k(X)` is surjective; in particular `W_k H^k(X) = 0` for odd `k` and `W_k H^k(X)` is pure Hodge–Tate for even `k`. The same holds with `ℚ_ℓ`-coefficients over any field of characteristic zero, the closure `X̄` being smooth and proper (CLP, Lemma 4.3, pp. 11–12, and Remark 4.4, p. 12). Route: write the diagonal of `X̄ × X̄` as `Γ + Σ_ℓ Δ_ℓ` with `Γ` supported on `X̄ × (X̄ − X)` and `Δ_ℓ` sums of products `V × W` of codimensions `ℓ, d − ℓ`; for `α` lifting a class of `W_k H^k(X)`, `Δ_ℓ*α` is a cycle class and vanishes unless `k = 2d − 2ℓ`, and `Γ*α` restricts to zero on `X`.

*Needs:* §5.1; §0.4; MotivesAndAlgebraicCycles §0.6 (diagonal decompositions) and gap:dm-stack-cycle-class; SchemeAndStackFoundations, gap:stack-rational-chow.

**Checks.**

- Surjectivity is asserted, not injectivity: on `𝔾_m`, `A¹ = 0` and `W₂H² = 0`, while `A⁰ → W₀H⁰ = ℚ` is an isomorphism; on `ℙ¹` minus three points the statement concerns only `W₀H⁰`, not `H¹`, which has weight two.
- The proper case recovers the classical statement that the cycle class map is an isomorphism for smooth proper stacks with the CKgP (CLP, p. 11).

### 5.4 Known ranges of the Chow–Künneth generation property

For `g = 0` and every `n`, and for `g = 1, 2, 3, 4, 5, 6` with `n ≤ 10, 10, 11, 11, 7, 5` respectively, prove that `M_{g,n}` has the CKgP and `A^*(M_{g,n}) = R^*(M_{g,n})`; consequently `W_k H^k(M_{g,n}) = RH^k(M_{g,n})` and, for odd `k`, `W_k H^k(M_{g,n}) = Gr^W_k H_c^k(M_{g,n}) = 0` (Canning–Larson, Theorem 1.4; CLP, Table 1 and Proposition 4.5, pp. 12–13). The genus-seven range `n ≤ 3` is §7.7. Route: Hurwitz-space and low-gonality descriptions of `M_{g,n}` stratum by stratum, with §5.2, and §5.3 for the cohomological consequence; the compact-support statement is the dual of the pure one.

*Needs:* §§5.2, 5.3; §0.1; WeilConjectures, gap:open-dm-stack-duality.

**Checks.**

- The bounds are not uniform in the genus: `c(3) = 11`, `c(5) = 7`, `c(6) = 5`; and `c(1) = 10` because `H¹¹(M̄_{1,11}) ≠ 0` forces `W₁₁H¹¹(M_{1,11}) ≠ 0`.
- At `(1, 11)` the conclusion fails, consistent with the bound: `W₁₁H¹¹(M_{1,11}) = S₁₂` is not algebraic.

### Examples

`M_{0,n}` has the CKgP for every `n`, being an open subset of affine space minus hyperplanes. The diagonal of `ℙ¹` is `[pt × ℙ¹] + [ℙ¹ × pt]`, a sum of exterior products; that of an elliptic curve is not.

### Dependencies

Layer 0; SchemeAndStackFoundations, gap:stack-rational-chow; MotivesAndAlgebraicCycles §0.6 with gap:dm-stack-cycle-class; WeilConjectures, gap:open-dm-stack-duality.

## Layer 6: The tetragonal and low-gonality loci in genus seven

The complement of the pentagonal locus in `M_{7,n}` is covered by the low-gonality and tetragonal strata. A class being algebraic is not enough: its pushforward to `M_{7,n}` must be tautological.

### 6.1 Gonality strata in genus seven

Inside `M_{7,n}` let `M^{≤a}_{7,n}` be the locus of curves admitting a finite morphism of degree at most `a` to `ℙ¹` over the algebraic closure, and `M^{a}_{7,n} = M^{≤a}_{7,n} − M^{≤a−1}_{7,n}`. Prove that the loci `M^{≤a}` are closed and nested, that every genus-seven curve has gonality at most five, and that `M_{7,n} = M^{≤3}_{7,n} ⊔ M^{4}_{7,n} ⊔ M^{5}_{7,n}`; write `M°_{7,n} = M^5_{7,n}` for the open pentagonal locus (CLP, §5, opening, pp. 13–14). Route: relative linear series and properness of the relevant Hurwitz morphisms; the Brill–Noether bound `⌊(g + 3)/2⌋ = 5`.

*Needs:* StableReductionPartII, MC.0 and MC.2; SchemeAndStackFoundations §3.2 (Riemann–Hurwitz and degree bounds on curves).

API:

- The loci `M^{≤a}_{7,n}` are closed and nested for `a = 2, 3, 4, 5`, with `M^{≤5}_{7,n} = M_{7,n}`.
- Gonality strata with markings are the inverse images of the unmarked strata under the forgetful map.
- `M^{≤3}`, `M^4` and `M^5` partition `M_{7,n}`.
- A geometric pointed curve lies in `M^a_{7,n}` if and only if it admits a degree-`a` map to `ℙ¹` and no map of smaller positive degree.

**Checks.**

- A hyperelliptic genus-seven curve lies in `M^{≤3}` and not in `M^4`.
- A curve of gonality exactly four lies outside the pentagonal open `M°_{7,n}`.
- Adding a marking does not change gonality: the strata of `M_{7,n}` are pulled back from `M_7`.

### 6.2 The low-gonality locus

For `n ≤ 14` prove that `M^{≤3}_{7,n}` has the CKgP and that every Chow class on `M_{7,n}` supported on `M^{≤3}_{7,n}` is tautological (Canning–Larson, Lemma 9.9; CLP, §5.1, p. 13). The intrinsic Chow ring of the stratum and the classes it supports on `M_{7,n}` are distinguished.

*Needs:* §6.1; §§5.1–5.2; §0.1.

**Checks.**

- The support statement is the one that extends tautological generation from the complement: a class of `A^*(M^{≤3}_{7,n})` must push forward to a tautological class, which algebraicity alone does not give.
- The hyperelliptic locus is included in `M^{≤3}_{7,n}`.

### 6.3 The tetragonal Hurwitz stack and its Casnati–Ekedahl bundles

For `g ≥ 2` let `H_{4,g,n}` be the stack of degree-four covers `C → ℙ¹` by smooth curves of genus `g` with `n` distinct ordered marked points, modulo automorphisms of the target, and `H^◇_{4,g,n}` the open substack where `C` has gonality exactly four. The Casnati–Ekedahl structure theorem attaches to a cover a rank-three bundle `E` and a rank-two bundle `F` on `ℙ¹`, each of degree `g + 3`, with a canonical isomorphism `det E ≅ det F`, such that `C ⊂ ℙE^∨` is the zero locus of a section of `F^∨ ⊗ O(2)`. Construct these data over the `SL₂`-lift of the target on which `O(1)`, `E` and `F` exist, and the forgetful morphism `β′_n : H^◇_{4,g,n} → M_{g,n} − M^{≤3}_{g,n}`, proper with image `M^4_{g,n}` (CLP, §5.2, pp. 13–14; Canning–Larson genus seven, §§3–4).

*Needs:* §6.1; SchemeAndStackFoundations §§1.18–1.23 (stacks and quotients); AlgebraicModuliForArithmeticGeometry §1.3 (Grassmannians and projective bundles of quotients); StableReductionPartII, MC.2.

API:

- Forgetting the cover is proper from `H^◇_{4,7,n}` onto `M^4_{7,n}`.
- `E`, `F` and the isomorphism `det E ≅ det F` pull back along base change of families.
- The universal curve embeds in `ℙE^∨` as the zero locus of the universal section of `F^∨ ⊗ O(2)`.
- Base change of finite flat covers and marked sections induces pullback of Hurwitz families and of their Casnati–Ekedahl data on the `SL₂`-lift.
- An isomorphism of cover data is a commuting square of source and target isomorphisms preserving ordered sections; automorphisms of the target are retained.

**Checks.**

- At `g = 7` the ranks are `3` and `2` and both degrees are `10`.
- A degree-four map factoring through a hyperelliptic double cover is excluded from `H^◇` by the gonality condition.
- Exchanging the two defining quadrics changes a frame of `F` and not the cover.

### 6.4 Splitting strata of the tetragonal Hurwitz stack

In `H_{4,7,0}` let `X` be the locus with `E = O(3) ⊕ O(3) ⊕ O(4)` and `F` of type `(5, 5)` or `(4, 6)`; `Σ₂` the locus with `E = (2, 4, 4)`, `F = (4, 6)`; `Σ₃` the locus with `E = (2, 3, 5)`, `F = (4, 6)`; and `Z` the maximally unbalanced locus, which parametrizes hyperelliptic curves. Subscript `n` denotes the inverse image in `H_{4,7,n}`. Prove that `H^◇_{4,7,n} = X_n ⊔ Σ_{2,n} ⊔ Σ_{3,n}`, with `Y_n = Σ_{2,n} ⊔ Σ_{3,n}` closed (CLP, §5.2, pp. 14–15; Canning–Larson genus seven, §4.4). Route: semicontinuity of splitting types and the constraints from smoothness and irreducibility of `C`; each stratum is a quotient of an open set of equations by `SL₂ ⋉ (Aut O(e⃗) × Aut O(f⃗))`.

*Needs:* §6.3.

API:

- `X`, `Σ₂`, `Σ₃` form a finite stratification of `H^◇_{4,7,0}`; `Y = Σ₂ ⊔ Σ₃` is closed in it.
- The marked strata are the inverse images of the unmarked strata.
- Each listed splitting type has the rank and degree of `E` and `F`.
- Within `H^◇`, a geometric cover lies in `X`, `Σ₂` or `Σ₃` exactly when its pair of splitting types is the displayed one.

**Checks.**

- `3 + 3 + 4 = 10`, `5 + 5 = 4 + 6 = 10`, `2 + 4 + 4 = 2 + 3 + 5 = 10`.
- `Σ₂` and `Σ₃` are distinct although their `F` splitting types agree.
- The hyperelliptic stratum `Z` is not part of `H^◇`; its image lies in `M^{≤3}_{7,n}`.

### 6.5 Casnati–Ekedahl pushforwards are tautological

For `g ≥ 2`, suppose `x ∈ A^*(H^◇_{4,g,n})` lies in the image of `A^*((ℙE^∨)^n) → A^*(H^◇_{4,g,n})` along the map recording the images of the markings. Prove that `β′_{n*}x` is tautological on `M_{g,n} − M^{≤3}_{g,n}` (CLP, Lemma 5.1, p. 14, quoting Canning–Larson, Lemma 10.5). Route: expand in the hyperplane classes `z_i`, `ζ_i` and the Chern classes of `E`, `F`; push forward along the cover incidence and use Grothendieck–Riemann–Roch to express the results in kappa, psi and lambda classes.

*Needs:* §§6.3, 0.1, 0.2; SchemeAndStackFoundations, gap:stack-rational-chow (proper pushforward and Grothendieck–Riemann–Roch on stacks).

**Checks.**

- The conclusion is tautological, not merely algebraic: a statement that every pushed-forward class is algebraic would not feed §7.7.
- For `n = 0` and `x = 1` the pushforward is the fundamental class of the tetragonal locus, which is tautological.

### 6.6 The open tetragonal stratum with at most three markings

For `0 ≤ n ≤ 3` prove that `X_n` has the CKgP and that `A^*((ℙE^∨)^n) → A^*(X_n)` is surjective (CLP, Lemma 5.2, p. 14, through Canning–Larson, Lemmas 10.11 and 10.12). Route: present the equations vanishing at `n` points as a vector-bundle kernel over the `n`-fold incidence; evaluation at `n ≤ 3` distinct points of a smooth curve is surjective onto a rank-`2n` target; restrict to the smooth open and apply §5.2.

*Needs:* §§6.4, 5.2.

**Checks.**

- The evaluation target has rank `2n`, not `n`: `W = γ^*F^∨ ⊗ O(2)` has rank two.
- No conclusion is drawn for `n = 4`, where evaluation can fail to be surjective.

### 6.7 Chow generation on the special tetragonal strata

For `ℓ = 2, 3` and `n ≤ 3` prove that `A^*(Σ_{ℓ,n})` is generated over `A^*(Σ_ℓ)` by the classes `z_i, ζ_i`, that `Σ_{ℓ,n}` has the CKgP, and that `A^*((ℙE^∨)^n) → A^*(Σ_{ℓ,n})` is surjective (CLP, Lemmas 5.3 and 5.5, pp. 15–16; Canning–Larson genus seven, Lemmas 3.10, 4.2 and 4.3). Route: `Σ_ℓ` is an open substack of a vector bundle over the stack of pairs of bundles of fixed splitting type; with markings it is open in the restriction of the kernel of the evaluation map to the locus where evaluation is surjective; the base generators are restrictions of Chern classes of `E`, `F`.

*Needs:* §§6.4, 5.2.

**Checks.**

- `Σ₃` (with `E = (2,3,5)`) lies in the closure of `Σ₂` (with `E = (2,4,4)`), being more unbalanced; so `Σ_{3,n}` is closed in `Y_n` and the localization of §6.8 removes it first. Treating `Y_n` as a single stratum with one splitting type would be wrong.
- `A^*(B) → A^*(Σ_ℓ)` is surjective for both values of `ℓ`, from different lemmas (4.2 and 4.3(1) of Canning–Larson genus seven).

### 6.8 The tetragonal locus

For `n ≤ 3` prove that `H^◇_{4,7,n}` and `M^4_{7,n}` have the CKgP, that `A^*((ℙE^∨)^n) → A^*(H^◇_{4,7,n})` is surjective, and that every class on `M_{7,n}` supported on `M^4_{7,n}` is tautological modulo classes supported on `M^{≤3}_{7,n}` (CLP, Corollary 5.4 and Lemmas 5.6–5.7, pp. 16–17). Route: localization along `Y_n ⊂ H^◇` and `Σ_{3,n} ⊂ Y_n`; the fundamental classes `[Y_n]`, `[Σ_{3,n}]` are pulled back from the stack of bundle pairs (Canning–Larson genus seven, Lemma 4.8), so the projection formula lifts the supported terms; descend the CKgP along the proper map `β′_n` and push classes forward with §6.5.

*Needs:* §§6.5–6.7; §5.2; §6.2.

**Checks.**

- Surjectivity on each stratum is not enough: the fundamental classes of the closed strata must themselves be restrictions from `(ℙE^∨)^n`.
- Proper surjective maps are surjective on rational Chow groups, which is how classes supported on `M^4_{7,n}` come from `H^◇_{4,7,n}`.

### Examples

For `n = 0`, `X_0` is an open subset of a vector bundle over the stack of bundle pairs of types `(3,3,4)` and `(5,5)` or `(4,6)`; its Chow ring is generated by Chern classes of `E` and `F`.

### Dependencies

Layers 0 and 5; StableReductionPartII, MC.0, MC.2; SchemeAndStackFoundations §§1.18–1.23, §3.2, with gap:stack-rational-chow; AlgebraicModuliForArithmeticGeometry §1.3.

## Layer 7: The orthogonal Grassmannian and the Mukai model of pentagonal genus-seven curves

The half-spin representation is available algebraically (`TauCeti.spinPlus`). The work here is the family-level comparison with curve moduli and the descent of bundles and Chow classes through central quotients. The half-spin representation `S⁺` of `Spin₁₀` is not a representation of `SO₁₀`: the central element `−1` of `Spin₁₀` acts on `S⁺` by `−1`. Only the projective action descends, to `PSO₁₀ = Spin₁₀/μ₄`. The printed model (CLP, §5.3, equations (5.10)–(5.13), pp. 20–21) treats `S⁺` as a vector bundle on `BSO₁₀`; the targets below replace it by the `Spin₁₀`-lift and a rigidification, with the transfer of rational Chow groups proved separately.

### 7.1 Chow ring of the universal maximal isotropic Grassmannian

For a split nondegenerate quadratic space of dimension ten over a field of characteristic zero, fix one connected component `OG⁺(5, 10)` of its maximal isotropic Grassmannian. Prove that `[OG⁺(5, 10)/SO₁₀] ≅ BP` for the stabilizer `P` of a maximal isotropic subspace, a parabolic subgroup with Levi factor `GL₅` and unipotent radical isomorphic to `𝔾_a^{10}` (skew-symmetric `5 × 5` matrices); that `BGL₅ → BP` is an affine bundle; and hence that `BP` has the CKgP with `A^*(BP) = ℚ[c₁(U), …, c₅(U)]` for the tautological rank-five subbundle `U` (CLP, Lemma 5.8 and Remark 5.9, pp. 18–19). On `BP` the quadratic bundle `V` sits in the exact sequence `0 → U → V → U^∨ → 0`; the printed description `V = U ⊕ U^∨` holds after pullback to `BGL₅` but not `P`-equivariantly, and only the exact sequence is used.

*Needs:* §5.2; `TauCeti.spinPlus` and `TauCeti.finrank_spinPlus` (for the identification of the component with the spinor variety); SchemeAndStackFoundations, gap:stack-rational-chow.

**Checks.**

- The unipotent radical has dimension `10 = 5·4/2`, so `BGL₅ → BP` is an affine bundle of rank ten, and `dim OG⁺(5, 10) = dim SO₁₀ − dim P = 45 − (25 + 10) = 10`.
- The union of both components of the maximal isotropic Grassmannian is disconnected and is not the image of the spinor embedding, which sees one component.

### 7.2 Fibre powers of the orthogonal Grassmann bundle

For `n ≥ 1` let `OG(5, V)^n` be the `n`-fold fibre power over `BSO₁₀` of the fixed component of the orthogonal Grassmann bundle of the universal oriented quadratic bundle `V`. Prove that `OG(5, V)^n` has the CKgP and that its rational Chow ring is generated by the Chern classes of the tautological subbundles `U₁, …, U_n` (CLP, Lemma 5.10 and Corollary 5.11, p. 19). Route: for `n > 1`, `OG(5, V)^n` is the orthogonal Grassmann bundle of the pulled-back quadratic bundle over `OG(5, V)^{n−1}`, which carries the tautological isotropic subbundle `U_{n−1}`; reduce the structure group to the parabolic `P` and argue with the type-D Giambelli formula on the split fibre and a splitting-principle descent through the parabolic reduction. The printed proof of CLP Lemma 5.10 trivializes the underlying vector bundle on strata and treats the quadratic form as split there; a trivialization of the vector bundle does not trivialize a quadratic form, and the argument must use the parabolic reduction instead.

*Needs:* §§7.1, 5.2; SchemeAndStackFoundations, gap:stack-rational-chow.

**Checks.**

- `n = 0` is excluded: the empty fibre product is `BSO₁₀`, whose rational Chow ring is not generated by an empty list of Chern classes (it contains the Pontryagin and Euler classes).
- A nondegenerate quadratic form over a field need not be split even when its vector space is trivial: `x² + y²` over `ℝ` is anisotropic. The stratification argument must therefore keep track of the form, not only the bundle.
- For `n = 1` the statement is §7.1.

### 7.3 The Mukai model and its comparison with curve moduli

For `0 ≤ n ≤ 4` let `Z_n ⊂ OG(5, V)^n` be the open locus where the tautological lines `L₁, …, L_n ⊂ S⁺` are independent, constructed on the `Spin₁₀`-lift where `S⁺` is a vector bundle, and let `I_n` be the locally closed locus of `(p₁, …, p_n, Λ)` with `Λ` a seven-dimensional subspace of `S⁺` containing the lines `L_i` and with `ℙΛ ∩ OG⁺(5, 10)` a smooth curve. Prove that the rigidification of `[I_n/Spin₁₀]` by the central `μ₄`, which acts trivially on `I_n`, is equivalent to `M°_{7,n}`, compatibly with forgetting markings and preserving automorphism groups of pointed curves (CLP, §5.3, equations (5.10)–(5.13) and Lemma 5.13, pp. 20–21; Mukai, the genus-seven linear-section theorem, recalled in CLP §5.3.2). Route: Mukai's canonical construction `W = ker(Sym² H⁰(ω) → H⁰(ω²))`, a ten-dimensional space with a canonical nondegenerate quadratic form on `W^∨`, and the five-dimensional isotropic subspaces `W_p^⊥` from `ω(−p)`, give the map from pointed curves; the inverse sends `(p_i, Λ)` to `ℙΛ ∩ OG⁺(5, 10)`; independence of the marked points for `n ≤ 4` follows because a pentagonal curve has no `g¹_n` with `n ≤ 4`. The rigidification is AlgebraicModuliForArithmeticGeometry §5.1.

*Needs:* §6.1; §7.2; `TauCeti.spinPlus`, `TauCeti.spinPlusAction`, `TauCeti.spinPlusSubrep`, `TauCeti.finrank_spinPlus`; AlgebraicModuliForArithmeticGeometry §1.3 (Grassmannians of quotients) and §5.1 (rigidification); StableReductionPartII, MC.0.

**Checks.**

- The central `−1 ∈ Spin₁₀` acts on `S⁺` by `−1`, so there is no rank-sixteen half-spin vector bundle on `BSO₁₀`; the element `−1 ∈ SO₁₀` lifts to elements of order four acting by `±i`, which act trivially on `ℙS⁺` and on Grassmannians of `S⁺`.
- A generic pentagonal genus-seven curve has trivial automorphism group, while `[I_0/SO₁₀]` would give every point a `μ₂` stabilizer from the centre of `SO₁₀`; the comparison is with the effective quotient by `PSO₁₀`.
- At `n = 0` the model consists of seven-dimensional subspaces of the sixteen-dimensional half-spin space.

### 7.4 Descent of bundles and rational Chow transfer for the Mukai model

On the `Spin₁₀`-lift of §7.3 compute the characters of the central `μ₄` on `S⁺`, on each tautological line `L_i`, on the quotient `Q_n = S⁺/⊕L_i` and on the tautological subbundle `E_n ⊂ Q_n` of rank `7 − n`. Prove that pullback along the gerbe `[I_n/Spin₁₀] → M°_{7,n}` is an isomorphism on rational Chow groups, naturally after product with every finite-type stack stratified by quotient stacks, and that the CKgP descends along it; identify which character-zero combinations of these bundles descend, and compare their Chern classes with classes on `M°_{7,n}` compatibly with forgetting markings (CLP, Lemmas 5.13–5.17, pp. 20–22, read on the lift). Route: a gerbe banded by a finite group induces an isomorphism on rational Chow groups after every product; the map is proper, surjective and representable by DM stacks, so §5.2 applies; Chern classes of bundles of nonzero central character are compared through their tensor products with a line of the opposite character or through the projective bundles they define.

*Needs:* §§7.3, 5.2; SchemeAndStackFoundations, gap:stack-rational-chow; AlgebraicModuliForArithmeticGeometry §5.1.

**Checks.**

- A line bundle of nonzero `μ₄`-character, such as `O_{ℙS⁺}(−1)` restricted to a marked point, does not descend untwisted; the identity `c₁(L_i) = −ψ_i` of §7.6 is a statement about the descended twist determined by the canonical embedding.
- A rational Chow isomorphism for the base alone does not give the CKgP; the isomorphism must hold after product with every test stack.
- The projective bundle `ℙS⁺` descends to `BPSO₁₀` although `S⁺` does not.

### 7.5 The marked spinor incidence

On `Z_n`, for `0 ≤ n ≤ 4`, put `Q_n = coker(⊕_i L_i → S⁺)`, of rank `16 − n`, and let `G_n` be the Grassmann bundle of rank-nine locally free quotients of `Q_n` (AlgebraicModuliForArithmeticGeometry §1.3), equivalently of rank-`(7 − n)` subbundles `E_n ⊂ Q_n` with locally free quotient; the inverse image of `E_n` in `S⁺` is a rank-seven subbundle containing the lines `L_i`. The incidence `I_n` of §7.3 is the open locus of `G_n` where the corresponding linear section is a smooth curve (CLP, equations (5.11)–(5.13) and Lemma 5.13, pp. 20–21).

*Needs:* §§7.2–7.3; AlgebraicModuliForArithmeticGeometry §1.3 (`coherentGrassmannian` and `grassmannSmoothness`).

API:

- `rank Q_n = 16 − n`, `rank E_n = 7 − n`, and the universal quotient `Q_n/E_n` has rank nine.
- Forgetting a marked line compares the incidences for `n` and `n − 1` by the evident flag correspondence.
- After §7.3 the smooth-section locus is the pentagonal curve family with its markings.
- For a test scheme `T` over `Z_n`, points of `G_n` over `T` are locally free rank-nine quotients of `Q_n|_T` up to isomorphism of quotients, equivalently rank-`(7 − n)` subbundles with locally free quotient.
- The universal sequence `0 → E_n → Q_n → B_n → 0` and the inverse-image rank-seven subbundle of `S⁺` commute with base change.
- `G_n → Z_n` is smooth of relative dimension `9(7 − n)`.

**Checks.**

- `n = 0`: `G_0` is the Grassmannian of rank-nine quotients of a rank-sixteen space, equivalently of seven-dimensional subspaces, of dimension `63`.
- `n = 4`: `rank Q_4 = 12`, `rank E_4 = 3`, quotient rank nine.
- Two equal marked points give dependent lines and lie outside `Z_n`.
- The Grassmannian of rank-three quotients of `Q_4` has kernels of rank nine and cannot supply `E_4` of rank three; the rank-nine quotient convention is the right one.

### 7.6 Tautological Chern classes on the Mukai model

For `n ≤ 4`, after §7.4, prove: the bundle `U_i` restricted to `M°_{7,n}` sits in `0 → U_i → Sym² f_*(ω(−σ_i)) → f_*(ω(−σ_i)^{⊗2}) → 0`; `c₁(L_i) = −ψ_i` for the descended line of §7.4; and `0 → ⊕_i L_i → E^∨ → E_n → 0` with `E` the Hodge bundle, of ranks `n, 7, 7 − n`. Hence all Chern classes of `U_i`, `L_i` and `E_n` restrict to tautological classes on `M°_{7,n}` (CLP, Lemmas 5.14–5.16, pp. 21–22). Route: the relative quadric-multiplication sequence; the canonical embedding identifies the point line with `σ_i^*ω^∨`; the rank-seven span is the dual Hodge bundle; Grothendieck–Riemann–Roch for the universal curve.

*Needs:* §§7.4–7.5; §0.2; StableReductionPartII, MC.5 (Hodge bundle); SchemeAndStackFoundations, gap:stack-rational-chow (Grothendieck–Riemann–Roch on stacks).

**Checks.**

- The sign of the point line is negative: `L_i = σ_i^*ω^∨` has `c₁ = −ψ_i`.
- `rank E_n = 7 − n`, not `16 − n`: for `n = 0` it is the dual Hodge bundle of rank seven.
- Ranks in the last sequence add up: `n + (7 − n) = 7`.

### 7.7 The Chow–Künneth generation property in genus seven

For `0 ≤ n ≤ 4` prove that `M°_{7,n}` has the CKgP and `A^*(M°_{7,n}) = R^*(M°_{7,n})`; for `0 ≤ n ≤ 3` prove that `M_{7,n}` has the CKgP and `A^*(M_{7,n}) = R^*(M_{7,n})` (CLP, Theorem 1.10, p. 4; Lemmas 5.13 and 5.17, pp. 20–22). Route: for `1 ≤ n ≤ 4`, §§7.4–7.6 and §7.2 give generation by tautological classes on the open subset `I_n` of `G_n`. For `n = 0`, the forgetful map `π : M°_{7,1} → M°_7` is proper and surjective, so §5.2 gives the CKgP, and `π_*(ψ₁ · π^*α) = κ₀ α = 12α` (§0.2) shows that every class is tautological; this avoids the empty fibre product excluded in §7.2. For `n ≤ 3` combine with §§6.2 and 6.8 by localization along the gonality strata.

*Needs:* §§7.2, 7.4–7.6; §§6.2, 6.8; §§5.2, 0.2.

**Checks.**

- `n = 4` is a statement about the pentagonal locus only; `M_{7,4}` is not claimed, since §6.8 is limited to `n ≤ 3`.
- For `n = 0` the division by `κ₀ = 2·7 − 2 = 12` is legitimate over `ℚ`.
- The conclusion feeds §5.4's table at `c(7) = 3`.

## Layer 8: Hyperelliptic counts and the genus-four quadric sieve

The sieve keeps singular and nonreduced complete intersections until their inverse-zeta contributions cancel. The coefficient tables below are sieve terms, not counts of smooth fibres; the count of smooth fibres is their sum together with the tail of §8.13. Throughout, `p_r = 1 + q + ⋯ + q^r = #ℙ^r(𝔽_q)` and `q¹⁵` denotes `q^{15}`.

### 8.1 The hyperelliptic stack as a quotient of binary forms

For `g ≥ 2`, over `ℤ[1/2]`, prove that the stack `H_g` of hyperelliptic curves of genus `g` is the quotient of the open subscheme of binary forms of degree `2g + 2` with nonvanishing discriminant by a connected linear algebraic group `G` with `#G(𝔽_q) = (q² − 1)(q² − q)`, and that `#H_g(𝔽_q) = q^{2g−1}` for odd `q` (Arsie–Vistoli, Corollary 4.7; BFP, §5, pp. 1332–1333). Route: Lang's theorem for the connected group `G` gives `#H_g(𝔽_q) = #P_g/#G(𝔽_q)`, where `P_g` is the set of squarefree polynomials of degree `2g + 2` or `2g + 1`; unique factorization as a squarefree part times the square of a monic polynomial gives `#P_g = (q − 1)(q^{2g+2} − q^{2g})`.

*Needs:* StableReductionPartII, MC.0; WeilConjectures, WC.1 (stack mass, quotients by connected groups via Lang's theorem).

**Checks.**

- `(q − 1)(q^{2g+2} − q^{2g}) / ((q² − 1)(q² − q)) = q^{2g−1}`: the factor `(q − 1)q^{2g}(q² − 1)` cancels against `(q² − 1)q(q − 1)`.
- The count is a groupoid mass, not the number of isomorphism classes: every hyperelliptic curve has the hyperelliptic involution, so a curve with no further automorphisms has weight one half, and it and its quadratic twist are distinct classes.
- The binary-form model requires `q` odd; characteristic two is §8.3.

### 8.2 The first hyperelliptic Frobenius moments

For odd `q` and `g ≥ 2` let `a_C = q + 1 − #C(𝔽_q)`. Prove `Σ_{C ∈ [H_g(𝔽_q)]} a_C^k / #Aut(C)` equals `q^{2g−1}` for `k = 0`, `0` for odd `k`, and `q^{2g} − 1` for `k = 2` (BFP, Lemma 5.2, pp. 1332–1334). Route: twisting by a non-square exchanges `a_C` and `−a_C`, which kills odd moments; for `k = 2` write `a_C = −Σ_{x ∈ ℙ¹(𝔽_q)} χ(f(x))` with `χ` the quadratic character, separate the diagonal terms, which count forms not vanishing at `x`, from the off-diagonal terms, which vanish when summed over all forms of bounded degree by interpolation at two points and give `−#G(𝔽_q)` over squarefree forms by the squarefree–square factorization.

*Needs:* §8.1; `quadraticChar`, `quadraticChar_sum_zero`.

**Checks.**

- The second moment is `q^{2g} − 1`, not `q^{2g}`: the off-diagonal correlation over squarefree forms is `−#G(𝔽_q)`, not zero.
- Odd moments vanish as weighted sums; individual `a_C` need not be zero (a curve with `#C(𝔽_q) = q + 2` has `a_C = −1`).
- Summing `χ(f(x))χ(f(y))` over all binary forms of degree `d ≥ 1` with `x ≠ y` gives zero, because evaluation at two distinct points is surjective onto `𝔽_q²` and `Σ_a χ(a) = 0`.

### 8.3 Hyperelliptic counts with at most three markings

For every finite field and `g ≥ 2` prove `#H_g = q^{2g−1}`, `#H_{g,1} = (q + 1)q^{2g−1}`, `#H_{g,2} = (q + 2)q^{2g} − 1` and `#H_{g,3} = (q² + 3q − 1)q^{2g} − 3q`, the markings ordered and distinct (BFP, Proposition 5.1, p. 1333; Bergström, Theorem 10.3, for all characteristics). Route for odd `q`: `#H_{g,n} = Σ_C #C(#C − 1)⋯(#C − n + 1)/#Aut(C)` expanded in the moments of §8.2 with `#C = q + 1 − a_C`; for characteristic two use Bergström's computation.

*Needs:* §§8.1–8.2; WeilConjectures, WC.1.

**Checks.**

- At `g = 4, n = 0` the count is `q⁷`.
- For `n = 2` the factor is `#C(#C − 1)`, not `#C²`: `(q + 1)q·q^{2g−1} + (q^{2g} − 1) = (q + 2)q^{2g} − 1`.
- For `n = 3` the coefficient of `a_C²` in `N(N − 1)(N − 2)`, `N = q + 1 − a_C`, is `3q`, giving `(q³ − q)q^{2g−1} + 3q(q^{2g} − 1)`.

### 8.4 Quadric surfaces over finite fields

For every finite field `𝔽_q`, including characteristic two, prove that the geometrically reduced and geometrically irreducible quadric surfaces in `ℙ³` form three `PGL₄(𝔽_q)`-orbits: the cone `Q_con` over a smooth conic, isomorphic to `ℙ(1,1,2)`; the split smooth quadric `Q_spl ≅ ℙ¹ × ℙ¹`; and the non-split smooth quadric `Q_nsp`, which becomes `ℙ¹ × ℙ¹` over `𝔽_{q²}` with Frobenius exchanging the rulings. Prove that their stabilizers in `PGL₄(𝔽_q)` have orders `#Aut(Q_con) = q⁷ − q⁶ − q⁵ + q⁴`, `#Aut(Q_nsp) = 2(q⁶ − q²)` and `#Aut(Q_spl) = 2(q³ − q)²`, and that every automorphism of each surface is induced by `PGL₄` (BFP, §6 and equation (6), pp. 1334–1336). Route: classification of quadratic forms of rank three and four over finite fields, through the discriminant in odd characteristic and the Arf invariant in characteristic two; the stabilizers are `PGL₂(𝔽_q) × 𝔾_m ⋉ 𝔾_a³` for the cone, `(PGL₂ × PGL₂) ⋊ C₂` for the split quadric and `PGL₂(𝔽_{q²}) ⋊ C₂` for the non-split one. No roadmap states this classification; it is owned here.

*Needs:* Mathlib quadratic forms over fields (`QuadraticForm`); SchemeAndStackFoundations Layer 0 (projective space over a field).

**Checks.**

- `2(q³ − q)² = 2·#PGL₂(𝔽_q)²` and `2(q⁶ − q²) = 2·#PGL₂(𝔽_{q²})`; the factor two is the exchange of rulings, so dividing by `#PGL₂(𝔽_q)²` alone would double the split contribution.
- `q⁷ − q⁶ − q⁵ + q⁴ = q³ · q(q² − 1) · (q − 1)`: translations along the vertex direction, `PGL₂` on the conic, and scaling.
- A rank-two quadric `x₀² − εx₁²` with `ε` a non-square is irreducible over `𝔽_q` but geometrically a pair of conjugate planes; it is excluded, since the quadric of a canonical genus-four curve is geometrically irreducible.

### 8.5 Canonical genus-four quadric incidences

For a non-hyperelliptic curve `C` of genus four, the canonical image in `ℙ³` is the complete intersection of a unique geometrically irreducible quadric with a cubic; conversely every smooth `(2, 3)`-complete intersection in `ℙ³` is a canonical genus-four curve (BFP, §6, p. 1334). For each type of §8.4 define the equation space `V^con ≅ 𝔸¹⁵` of complete intersections of `Q_con` with a cubic not passing through the vertex, equivalently degree-six curves in `ℙ(1,1,2)` with nonzero `w³` coefficient normalized to one; `V^nsp ≅ V^spl ≅ ℙ¹⁵` the linear system of cubic sections; for `n ≥ 0` the incidence `V^•_n ⊂ V^• × (Q^•)^n` of an equation together with `n` distinct ordered points on its zero curve; and `C^•_n → V^•_n` the universal curve, singular and nonreduced fibres included (BFP, §§7–8, pp. 1336–1340).

*Needs:* §8.4; StableReductionPartII, MC.0 (genus-four curves); SchemeAndStackFoundations Layer 3 (canonical embeddings of curves).

API:

- `V^•_n` maps to `V^•` by forgetting the ordered points.
- The incidence points are pairwise distinct; the fibre curve may be singular at them.
- The type is cone, non-split smooth or split smooth over `𝔽_q`.
- A marked point imposes one linear condition on the cubic section; a singularity at a point imposes three, and a singularity at a marked point two more.
- A geometric point of `V^•_n` is an allowed equation together with an ordered tuple of distinct points of its zero curve; singular or nonreduced zero curves are permitted.

**Checks.**

- `#V^con(𝔽_q) = q¹⁵` and `#V^nsp(𝔽_q) = #V^spl(𝔽_q) = p₁₅`.
- A cubic through the vertex of the cone is excluded from `V^con`: such a complete intersection is singular at the vertex.
- A curve with a double component is a point of `V^•`; excluding nonreduced fibres before sieving loses the cancellations of §8.7.

### 8.6 Counting genus-four curves by their quadric

For every `q` and `n ≤ 3` prove `#(M_{4,n} − H_{4,n})(𝔽_q) = Σ_• N^•_n(q)/#Aut(Q^•)`, the sum over the three types, where `N^•_n(q)` is the number of `𝔽_q`-points of `V^•_n` with smooth fibre (BFP, Propositions 6.1–6.2 and equation (6), pp. 1334–1336). Route: `M₄ − H₄` is the quotient of the space of smooth `(2,3)`-complete intersections by the connected group `PGL₄`, so its mass is a count divided by `#PGL₄(𝔽_q)`; the orbit–stabilizer formula for the `PGL₄(𝔽_q)`-orbit of the quadric, with the disconnected stabilizers of §8.4, replaces the global division.

*Needs:* §§8.4–8.5; WeilConjectures, WC.1 (stack mass and quotients by connected groups).

**Checks.**

- All denominators are nonzero for every prime power `q ≥ 2`; at `q = 2`, `#Aut(Q_spl) = 2·36 = 72`.
- Connected-group division is applied only to `PGL₄`; applying it directly to the disconnected stabilizers would lose the factor two.

### 8.7 Nonreduced fibres and termination of the sieve

Prove: the reduced singular locus of a nonreduced fibre of `C^con_n` is isomorphic to `ℙ¹`, with even Betti sum two; on `Q_nsp` it is a `(1,1)` curve, possibly with an isolated point, with even Betti sum at most four; on `Q_spl` it is a line of a ruling or a `(1,1)` curve, possibly with points, with even Betti sum at most five. Hence the signed configuration coefficients of these singular loci vanish in degrees above the Betti sum, and the Hasse–Weil sieve of WeilConjectures, WC.1 applies to each family (BFP, Examples 7.7–7.9, pp. 1338–1340). Nilpotent structure does not change the point counts or `ℓ`-adic cohomology used.

*Needs:* §8.5; WeilConjectures, WC.1 (signed Frobenius configurations, termination for proper even cohomology, the Hasse–Weil sieve).

**Checks.**

- For `ℙ¹` the inverse zeta function is `(1 − t)(1 − qt) = 1 − (q + 1)t + qt²`, of degree two: its coefficient of `t²` is `q ≠ 0`, while the coefficients of `t³, t⁴, …` vanish and the coefficients sum to zero at `t = 1`.
- Coefficient vanishing above the Betti sum is different from the cancellation of the total at `t = 1`; both are used.

### 8.8 Inverse zeta functions of the three quadric types

## Layer 9: Genus-four point counts and odd vanishing through degree nine

The count recovers the Betti numbers of `M̄_{4,n}` only through the polynomial-count theorems for smooth proper stacks over all of `Spec ℤ`. Coefficients of the open counts are virtual and can be negative.

### 9.1 The equivariant Euler characteristic of `M_{4,3}`

Prove that the rational Euler characteristic of `M_{4,3}` is `−10` and that its `S₃`-equivariant Euler characteristic is `2s_{(3)} − 6s_{(2,1)}`, with sign coefficient zero (BFP, §10.1, p. 1355, and §11.3, p. 1361, citing Gorsky). These are Euler characteristics of rational cohomology of the stack (equal to those of its coarse space), not orbifold Euler characteristics. Route: Gorsky's formula sums, over finite cyclic automorphism types of genus-four curves, contributions computed from the Harer–Zagier orbifold Euler characteristics of the quotient moduli spaces (Gorsky, Proposition 3.1, Definition 3.5, Lemma 3.6 and Theorem 3.8). Two misprints of the preprint text are corrected in its use here, following Kannan, Remark 3.8: the genus of the quotient is `h = 1 − (Σ_{j=1}^{r} k_j)/2`, including the negative exponent `k_r`, as in Gorsky's equation (3); and in the local congruence count of modulus `r` the symbol `k` in `gcd(x_i, k)` must be `r`. With these corrections the genus-four, degree-three coefficient is `−(5/3)p₁³ + p₁p₂ + (8/3)p₃` in power sums, which is `2s_{(3)} − 6s_{(2,1)} + 0·s_{(1,1,1)}`.

*Needs:* §1.2; StableReductionPartII, MC.0; SchurWeyl, Layer 7 (power-sum and Schur bases).

**Checks.**

- At the identity, `2·1 − 6·2 = −10`: the ordinary value alone does not determine both Schur coefficients.
- For the identity automorphism of a genus-four curve the only exponent is `k₁ = −6`, and the corrected formula gives quotient genus `1 − (−6)/2 = 4`; the misprinted formula `(1 − Σk_j)/2` gives `7/2`.
- Converting: `p₁³ = s_{(3)} + 2s_{(2,1)} + s_{(1,1,1)}`, `p₁p₂ = s_{(3)} − s_{(1,1,1)}`, `p₃ = s_{(3)} − s_{(2,1)} + s_{(1,1,1)}`; the combination `−(5/3)p₁³ + p₁p₂ + (8/3)p₃` has coefficients `2`, `−6`, `0`.

### 9.3 The genus-four cutoff

*Needs:* WeilConjectures, WC.5 (the local cutoff) and WC.6 (smooth proper DM purity); DeligneWeightsAndPurity §8.24 (smooth proper DM duality); StableReductionPartII, MC.2.

## Layer 10: Tautological even cohomology and homology

Cohomological and homological statements now diverge: low even homology controls high complementary cohomology, and it does not make every low even cohomology group tautological.

### 10.1 Tautological cohomology through degree three

For every stable pair prove `H^k(M̄_{g,n}) = RH^k(M̄_{g,n})` for `0 ≤ k ≤ 3`: `H⁰ = ℚ`, `H¹ = H³ = 0`, and `H²` is spanned by the boundary divisor classes, the psi classes and `κ₁` (AC, Theorems (2.1) and (2.2); CLP, §6.1, p. 22). Route: §2.7 for odd degrees; the Arbarello–Cornalba induction for `H²`, with base cases from §1.6 and the stable range §3.4.

*Needs:* §§0.1, 2.7, 1.6, 3.4; §0.4.

**Checks.**

- `H²(M̄_{0,4}) = ℚ`, spanned by any of the three boundary points.
- An even Tate realization alone does not identify classes as tautological; the theorem identifies explicit generators.
- `dim H²(M̄_{1,1}) = 1`, spanned by `ψ₁ = δ_irr/12`.

### 10.2 All degree-four cohomology is tautological

For every stable pair prove `H⁴(M̄_{g,n}) = RH⁴(M̄_{g,n})` (CLP, Theorem 1.5(1), p. 2; §6.1, p. 22). Route: §4.9 with `k = 4` reduces to `g < 7` and `n ≤ 4`, where §5.4 makes the pure open quotient tautological; apply §4.5 to `RH^*`, including the same-degree triples with fewer markings, with §10.1 for lower degrees.

*Needs:* §§4.5, 4.9, 5.3, 5.4, 10.1.

**Checks.**

- Genus seven does not occur in the seed region: the genus bound `g′ < 3·4/2 + 1 = 7` is strict.
- For `(g, n) = (6, 4)`, `n = 4 ≤ c(6) = 5`, so §5.4 applies.

### 10.3 Degree-six cohomology in genus at least ten

For `g ≥ 10` and every `n` prove `H⁶(M̄_{g,n}) = RH⁶(M̄_{g,n})` (CLP, Theorem 1.5(2), p. 2; §6.1, p. 22). Route: `W₆H⁶(M_{g,n})` is tautological by §3.5; boundary classes are pushforwards of degree-four classes and of products of lower degrees, tautological by §§10.1–10.2; apply §0.4.

*Needs:* §§0.4, 3.5, 10.1, 10.2.

**Checks.**

- No statement is made for `g ≤ 9`; §10.6 gives a criterion there.
- `6 ≤ (2g − 2)/3` holds exactly when `g ≥ 10`.

### 10.4 Tautological even homology through degree fourteen

For every stable pair and every even `0 ≤ k ≤ 14` prove that `H^{2d−k}(M̄_{g,n})` is tautological, equivalently that `H_k(M̄_{g,n})` is spanned by tautological cycles; groups of negative cohomological degree are zero (CLP, Theorem 1.5(3), p. 2; §6.2, p. 23). Route: induct on `k` and on the stable-pair order with §0.4. By §2.3, `H_k^{BM}(M_{g,n}) = 0` unless `n ≤ 1, 2g ≤ k` or `n ≥ 2, 2g − 2 + n ≤ k`; for `g ≥ 3` and `k ≤ 14` all those pairs lie in the ranges of §§5.4 and 7.7, so the pure Borel–Moore classes are tautological by §5.3; genus one is §3.3 and genus two §4.3; boundary Künneth factors are even, because by §9.8 odd cohomology vanishes below degree eleven and two odd degrees at least eleven cannot sum to at most fourteen. The first sentence of the proof in CLP §6.2 writes the range as `k ≥ 14`; the theorem and the argument use `k ≤ 14`.

*Needs:* §§0.4, 2.3, 2.6, 3.3, 4.3, 5.3, 5.4, 7.7, 9.8.

**Checks.**

- The statement is not `H^k = RH^k` for even `k ≤ 14`; that is the conjecture of CLP, Conjecture 1.8, for `k ≥ 6`.
- At `k = 14` the genus-seven pairs `(7, 0)`, `(7, 1)` (with `2g = 14`) and `(7, 2)` (with `2g − 2 + n = 14`) are needed, which is why §7.7 is a prerequisite; genus eight does not occur, since `2g = 16 > 14`.
- For `g = 3` the pairs `n ≤ 11` with `2g − 2 + n ≤ 14` are within `c(3) = 11`.

### 10.5 The complementary degree-fourteen realization

For every stable pair prove `H^{2d−14}(M̄_{g,n})^ss ≅ L^{d−7 ⊕ m}` for some multiplicity `m` depending on the pair, as rational Hodge structures and as `ℚ_ℓ[G_ℚ]`-modules, with `m = 0` when `2d − 14` lies outside `[0, 2d]` (CLP, Corollary 6.2, p. 23). *Needs:* §10.4; MotivesAndAlgebraicCycles, gap:dm-stack-cycle-class.

**Checks.**

- For `M̄_{0,10}`, `d = 7`, so `H⁰ = L⁰ = ℚ`.

### 10.6 A finite criterion for degree-six cohomology

Prove: if the intersection pairing `RH⁶(M̄_{g,n}) × RH^{2d−6}(M̄_{g,n}) → ℚ(−d)` is perfect for every stable pair with `g ≤ 9` and `n ≤ 6`, then `H⁶(M̄_{g,n}) = RH⁶(M̄_{g,n})` for every stable pair (CLP, Remark 6.1, p. 22). *Needs:* §§4.2, 4.5, 10.3, 10.4.

**Checks.**

- The paired degrees are `6` and `2d − 6`; pairing `RH⁶` with itself is meaningful only when `d = 6`.
- For `k ≥ 22` the analogous pairing is not perfect in general (Petersen–Tommasi, recalled in CLP, p. 3), so the criterion is specific to degree six.

## Layer 11: Cusp-decorated graphs and the exceptional genus-two bases

Graphical bases count independent morphisms from the rank-two seed `S₁₂`. For `H¹⁵(M̄_{2,12})` the boundary restrictions detect `825` of the `836` copies; the remaining eleven are found through a nonzero pairing and an independent upper bound.

### 11.1 Galois realizations in degrees thirteen and fifteen in genus two

For every `n ≥ 0` prove that, as `ℚ_ℓ[G_ℚ]`-modules, `W₁₃H¹³(M_{2,n})^ss` and `H¹³(M̄_{2,n})^ss` are sums of copies of `L S₁₂`, and `W₁₅H¹⁵(M_{2,n})^ss` and `H¹⁵(M̄_{2,n})^ss` are sums of copies of `L² S₁₂`, multiplicities zero allowed (CLP, Proposition 2.8 and Corollary 2.9, p. 8). Route: the surjection `H^k(M̄_{2,n}) → W_kH^k(M_{2,n})` factors through the compact-type moduli space, whose cohomology decomposes into cohomology of symplectic local systems on `M₂^{ct}` and on `Sym² M_{1,1}` (Petersen, genus two, Theorem 2.1), the latter restricting to zero; local systems of odd weight have no cohomology because of the hyperelliptic involution, and for even weight Petersen's classification of the cohomology of local systems on the moduli of abelian surfaces (Petersen, abelian surfaces, Theorem 2.1) leaves only `L S₁₂` in pure weight thirteen and `L² S₁₂` in pure weight fifteen; boundary terms are controlled by §4.7, §9.8 and §10.1. The Hodge version of the degree-fifteen statement is §12.5.

*Needs:* §§3.1, 0.5, 0.4, 2.9, 4.7, 9.8, 10.1.

**Checks.**

- The statement concerns semisimplifications of Galois representations; it gives no splitting and does not deduce Hodge types from Frobenius polynomials.
- `S₁₆` does not occur in genus two: the only weight-fifteen pure piece allowed by the abelian-surface classification is `L² S₁₂`.

### 11.2 Cusp-decorated stable graphs

An `ω`-decorated stable graph of type `(g, n)` is a stable graph `Γ` with `n` legs and `e` edges, a designated genus-one vertex `v₀` with an ordered set of eleven of its flags, and unit decorations on the other vertices, possibly multiplied by tautological decorations. Pushforward along the clutching morphism of `Γ` of the pullback of `ω ∈ H¹¹(M̄_{1,11})` along the forgetful map retaining the eleven chosen flags of `v₀`, extended linearly over `S₁₂`, defines a morphism `L^e S₁₂ → H^{11+2e}(M̄_{g,n})`. A family of such graphs is a graphical basis of `H^k(M̄_{g,n})` when the corresponding morphisms form a basis of `Hom(L^e S₁₂, H^k(M̄_{g,n}))` and the evaluation map `Hom(L^e S₁₂, H^k) ⊗ L^e S₁₂ → H^k` is an isomorphism (CLP, §7.2, conventions 1–5, pp. 24–25).

*Needs:* §§4.8, 0.5; StableReductionPartII, MC.3.

API:

- A graph with its ordered eleven-flag set determines the morphism `L^e S₁₂ → H^{11+2e}(M̄_{g,n})`.
- Each edge adds two to the cohomological degree and one Tate twist `L`.
- The decorations `ω_i`, `ω_{ij}` are pullbacks along the maps forgetting the flags `i`, respectively `i, j`, the remaining flags of the genus-one vertex being retained in a fixed order.
- A basis of `m` morphisms gives `H^k ≅ (L^e S₁₂)^{⊕m}` when evaluation is an isomorphism.
- Permuting the eleven retained flags multiplies the morphism by the sign of the permutation, since `S₁₂` is the sign-isotypic part of `H¹¹(M̄_{1,11})`; relabelling the legs carries retained and forgotten flags along.

**Checks.**

- A two-edge graph with unit decorations contributes in degree `15` with source `L² S₁₂`.
- Eleven independent one-edge morphisms give a subspace of rational dimension `22`, not `11`.
- A genus-one vertex with fewer than eleven flags carries no `H¹¹` decoration, since `H¹¹(M̄_{1,m}) = 0` for `m ≤ 10`.

Route: pair against the basis `ω₂, …, ω₁₂` of `H¹¹(M̄_{1,12})` (§4.7); the pairing matrix is block diagonal with nonzero blocks; the multiplicity `11` is Getzler's (§1.6).

*Needs:* §§11.2, 3.2, 4.7, 1.6.

**Checks.**

- Rational dimension `22`.
- `S₁₄ = 0`, so `W₁₃H¹³(M_{1,12}) = 0` and the whole group is pushed forward from the boundary.

## Layer 12: Cusp homology and the realization classification in degrees thirteen to fifteen

The final induction uses the finite cusp bases and the oriented weight-fifteen complex. The passage from Hodge to Galois realizations uses the same geometric maps, not a comparison of Hodge numbers.

### 12.1 Cusp generation of degree-thirteen homology

- For `g = 2`, `2g − 2 + n ≤ 15` gives `n ≤ 13`, so `(2, 13)` is the last genus-two base case.

### 12.3 Realizations in the complementary odd degrees

For every stable pair prove that `H^{2d−13}(M̄_{g,n})^ss` is a sum of copies of `L^{d−12} S₁₂`, and `H^{2d−15}(M̄_{g,n})^ss` a sum of copies of `L^{d−13} S₁₂` and `L^{d−15} S₁₆`, in both realizations, multiplicities depending on the pair (CLP, Corollary 7.2, p. 24, and Corollary 7.11, p. 28). Route: each clutching pushforward adds a twist `L` and each forgetful pullback preserves weight; follow the inductions of §§12.1–12.2, with `H¹⁵(M̄_{1,15})^ss ≅ S₁₆ ⊕ (L² S₁₂)^{⊕186263}` from Getzler's genus-one calculation (CLP, Corollary 7.11, citing Getzler, p. 491).

*Needs:* §§12.1, 12.2, 0.5, 1.6.

**Checks.**

- The weights are `2d − 13` and `2d − 15`: `L^{d−12} S₁₂` has weight `2(d − 12) + 11 = 2d − 13`.
- These are statements about semisimplifications; equal-weight Galois extensions are not split.

### 12.4 A geometric splitting of `H¹⁵(M̄_{1,15})`

Let `V ⊂ H¹⁵(M̄_{1,15})` be the image of the boundary pushforwards and `W` the image of `H_c^{15}(M_{1,15}) → H¹⁵(M̄_{1,15})`. Prove `H¹⁵(M̄_{1,15}) = V ⊕ W` as Hodge structures and as Galois representations, with `W ≅ S₁₆` and `V^ss ≅ (L² S₁₂)^{⊕186263}`, and that `V` lies in the STE generated by `H¹¹(M̄_{1,11})` (CLP, Lemma 8.3, p. 29). Route: `0 → V → H¹⁵ → W₁₅H¹⁵(M_{1,15}) ≅ S₁₆ → 0` by §0.4; under Poincaré duality the inclusion of `W` is dual to this quotient; `V` has Hodge types `(13, 2), (2, 13)` and `W` types `(15, 0), (0, 15)`, so `V ⊥ W` and the pairing on `W` is nondegenerate; the splitting is defined by maps of varieties over `ℚ` and is therefore Galois-equivariant; `V ⊂ S_ω` by §4.8.

*Needs:* §§0.4, 0.5, 3.2, 4.8, 12.3; MixedHodgeModulesAndIrregularHodgeTheory, gap:dm-stack-mixed-hodge; WeilConjectures, WC.1 (stack Artin comparison).

**Checks.**

- The orthogonality uses Hodge types of actual geometric subspaces; an identity of virtual classes would not split the extension.
- `dim W = 2`, and `dim V = 2·186263 = 372526`.

### 12.5 Degree fifteen in genus two

For every `n`, with `d = 3 + n`, prove that the STE generated by `H¹¹(M̄_{1,11})` contains `H^{2d−15}(M̄_{2,n})`, and that `H¹⁵(M̄_{2,n})^ss` is a sum of copies of `L² S₁₂` as Hodge structures and as Galois representations (CLP, Lemma 8.1, p. 28). Route: by §12.2 the group `H^{2d−15}(M̄_{2,n})` is a quotient of a sum of images of geometric maps from twists of `H¹¹(M̄_{1,11})` and `H¹⁵(M̄_{1,15})`; restricted to `W ≅ S₁₆` (§12.4) each such map is zero, since by §11.1 and duality the target has no Galois constituent `L^{d−15}S₁₆` and a nonzero image of the simple object would be one; restricted to `V` it lands in the STE of `H¹¹(M̄_{1,11})`. Duality gives the statement for `H¹⁵`, and the geometric maps give it as Hodge structures. This does not assert STE generation of `H¹⁵(M̄_{2,n})` for all `n`.

*Needs:* §§11.1, 12.2, 12.3, 12.4, 4.8; WeilConjectures, WC.1 (stack Artin comparison); DeligneWeightsAndPurity §8.24 (smooth proper DM duality).

**Checks.**

- Excluding a constituent after semisimplification does not by itself produce STE maps: the splitting of §12.4 is what removes the `W`-part of the exhibited maps.
- The statement for `H¹⁵(M̄_{2,n})` is CLP Conjecture 7.12 in genus two only after duality and only for `H_{15}`; `H¹⁵(M̄_{2,12})` itself is §11.9.

### 12.6 The oriented weight-fifteen boundary complex

For a stable pair `(g, n)` let `C^j_{15}(g, n) = ⊕_{|E(Γ)|=j} (H¹⁵(M̄_Γ) ⊗ det ℚ^{E(Γ)})^{Aut Γ}`, the sum over stable graphs with `j` edges, `M̄_Γ = ∏_v M̄_{g_v,n_v}`; so `C⁰_{15} = H¹⁵(M̄_{g,n})`. The differential is the signed sum of restrictions adding one edge, with the exterior orientation; write `α = d₀` and `β = d₁` (CLP, equations (8.1)–(8.2), pp. 28–29). This is the weight-fifteen row of the weight spectral sequence of the normal crossings boundary: DeligneWeightsAndPurity §8.24 in the `ℓ`-adic realization, and its Hodge analogue (gap:dm-stack-mixed-hodge). The vertex factors are compact moduli, not open strata.

*Needs:* DeligneWeightsAndPurity §8.24; MixedHodgeModulesAndIrregularHodgeTheory, gap:dm-stack-mixed-hodge; StableReductionPartII, MC.3; §1.3.

API:

- `d_{j+1} d_j = 0`: two orders of adding two edges contribute with opposite signs.
- `α` is pullback to the normalized one-edge boundary strata.
- `β` preserves Hodge types and carries the action of graph automorphisms on `det ℚ^{E}`.
- The complex is `S_n`-equivariant and independent of the auxiliary ordering of edges up to canonical isomorphism.
- Reordering the edges of a summand by a permutation `τ` multiplies its orientation by `sign(τ)`; the signed restriction maps are independent of the chosen presentation.

**Checks.**

- A transposition of two edges acts by `−1` on `det ℚ^E`.
- A two-edge stratum with an edge-exchanging automorphism acting trivially on an `H¹⁵` summand contributes nothing to `C²` after the orientation twist.
- The ordinary graph transform of §1.3 has no such determinant.

### 12.7 Injectivity in the weight-fifteen row

For `g ≥ 3` and every `n` prove that `α : H¹⁵(M̄_{g,n}) → C¹_{15}(g, n)` is injective (CLP, Lemma 8.2, p. 28). Route: `α` is injective when `Gr^W_{15} H_c^{15}(M_{g,n}) = 0`; by §2.3 this holds if `n ≤ 1` and `2g > 15`, or `n ≥ 2` and `2g − 2 + n > 15`; the remaining pairs with `g ≥ 3` have the CKgP (§§5.4, 7.7), and §5.3 gives the vanishing. The printed proof uses only the bound `2g − 2 + n > 15`, which would leave `(8, 0)` and `(8, 1)` needing a CKgP that is not available; the bound `2g > 15` for `n ≤ 1` covers them.

*Needs:* §§12.6, 2.3, 5.3, 5.4, 7.7.

**Checks.**

- At `g = 8`, `n ≤ 1`: `2g = 16 > 15`, so `H_c^{15}(M_{8,n}) = 0` directly; no genus-eight CKgP is used.
- At `g = 7`, the pairs `(7, 0), (7, 1), (7, 2), (7, 3)` satisfy `2g − 2 + n ≤ 15` or `2g ≤ 15` and are covered by §7.7.

### 12.8 No Hodge type `(15, 0)` in genus at least two

For all `g ≥ 2` and `n` prove `H^{15,0}(M̄_{g,n}) = H^{0,15}(M̄_{g,n}) = 0` (CLP, proof of Theorem 1.1, pp. 28–29, Figure 3). Route: genus two is §12.5. For `g ≥ 3` induct on `g` and show that `β` is injective on the `(15, 0)` part of `C¹_{15}`, which with `βα = 0` and §12.7 gives the claim. A nonseparating one-edge summand has a vertex of genus `g − 1 ≥ 2`, so its `(15, 0)` part vanishes by induction. A separating summand can carry `(15, 0)` only on a genus-one side with at least fifteen flags, the other side having genus `g − 1 ≥ 2`; adding a loop at that other vertex lowers its genus to `g − 2` and gives a two-edge graph `Γ′` whose restriction map is injective on the genus-one cusp summand, while the other term of `β` on that summand comes from a source whose `(15, 0)` part vanishes.

*Needs:* §§12.5, 12.6, 12.7, 12.3.

**Checks.**

- Adding the loop preserves the total genus and lowers the vertex genus from `g − 1` to `g − 2`.
- The new vertex is stable even when `g − 2 = 1`, since it carries the separating flag and both loop flags.
- At `g = 1` the statement fails: `H^{15,0}(M̄_{1,15}) ≅ S₁₆^{15,0} ≠ 0`.

### 12.9 The realization classification

For every stable pair prove `H¹³(M̄_{g,n})^ss ≅ ⊕ L S₁₂` and `H¹⁴(M̄_{g,n})^ss ≅ ⊕ L⁷`, and for `g ≥ 2` prove `H¹⁵(M̄_{g,n})^ss ≅ ⊕ L² S₁₂`, as rational Hodge structures and as `ℚ_ℓ[G_ℚ]`-modules (CLP, Theorem 1.1, p. 1; §8, pp. 28–30). Route: duality applied to §§10.5 and 12.3 gives degrees thirteen and fourteen and leaves `S₁₆` as the only possible additional constituent in degree fifteen; §12.8 excludes it as a Hodge structure for `g ≥ 2`. For the Galois statement, the inductive maps of §12.2 exhibit `H^{2d−15}(M̄_{g,n})` as a quotient of twists of `H¹¹(M̄_{1,11})` and `H¹⁵(M̄_{1,15})`; each map restricted to `W ≅ S₁₆` (§12.4) is zero as a morphism of Hodge structures, hence of rational vector spaces, hence of Galois representations through the comparison; so no Galois constituent `S₁₆` remains. A comparison of Hodge numbers alone would not give this.

*Needs:* §§10.5, 12.3, 12.4, 12.8; MotivesAndAlgebraicCycles, gap:dm-stack-cycle-class; WeilConjectures, WC.1 (stack Artin comparison); DeligneWeightsAndPurity §8.24 (smooth proper DM duality).

**Checks.**

- At `(1, 15)` there is a summand `S₁₆`; the hypothesis `g ≥ 2` is essential.
- Each `L S₁₂` has rational dimension two and weight thirteen.
- `H¹⁴` has exponent `7`, while §10.5 has exponent `d − 7`; the two agree under duality.

### 12.10 Cohomology of genus-three moduli through eleven markings

For `n ≤ 11` prove that every `H^k(M̄_{3,n})^ss` is a finite direct sum of tensor products of powers of `L` and `S₁₂`, in both realizations; equivalently its class lies in the semiring generated by `L` and `S₁₂` (CLP, Theorem 1.9, p. 3, and its proof, p. 13). Route: by hard Lefschetz it suffices to treat `k ≤ d = 6 + n ≤ 17`; the boundary terms are products of groups `H^{k′}(M̄_{g′,n′})` with `k′ ≤ 15`, which are polynomials in `L` and `S₁₂` by §12.9, CLP-H11 and §§9.8, 10.1, the genus-one factors having at most twelve flags because `n ≤ 11`; the pure part `W_kH^k(M_{3,n})` is tautological by §§5.3–5.4.

*Needs:* §§0.4, 5.3, 5.4, 9.8, 10.1, 10.4, 12.9, 4.7; DeligneWeightsAndPurity, gap:dm-stack-hard-lefschetz; StableReductionPartII, gap:pointed-coarse-projectivity.

**Checks.**

- Products are retained: `S₁₂ ⊗ S₁₂` occurs through boundary strata with two genus-one vertices carrying cusp classes, and is not a sum of copies of `S₁₂`.
- The statement is about semisimplified realizations in a Grothendieck semiring, not a decomposition of Chow motives.
- For `n ≤ 11` the genus-one factors of the boundary divisors of `M̄_{3,n}` have at most `n + 1 ≤ 12` flags, inside the range `n′ ≤ 13` in which CLP's inputs describe all odd cohomology of degree at most fifteen; genus-two factors `M̄_{2,n+2}` are covered for every number of markings by §§11.1 and 12.9.

### Examples

`H¹³(M̄_{1,12}) ≅ (L S₁₂)^{⊕11}`; `H¹⁵(M̄_{2,12}) ≅ (L² S₁₂)^{⊕836}`; `H¹⁵(M̄_{1,15}) ≅ S₁₆ ⊕ V` with `V^ss ≅ (L² S₁₂)^{⊕186263}`.

## Corrections to the sources

The statements of this roadmap correct the following printed passages. Each correction is used where indicated; none changes a main theorem of the source.

- CLP, Lemma 2.1, hypothesis (2.2), pp. 4–5: only smaller triples with `k′ ≤ k − 2` are required, although the proof uses the same-degree triple with one marking fewer. The corrected criterion is §4.5, with the counterexample `S = RH^*`, `(g, n, k) = (1, 12, 11)` to the printed form. CLP Theorem 1.3 and its other applications remain valid, their seed regions containing the same-degree triples.
- CLP, §5.3.1, setup before Lemma 5.8, p. 18: the pulled-back quadratic bundle is written `U ⊕ U^∨`; `P`-equivariantly it is an extension `0 → U → V → U^∨ → 0` (§7.1). The Chern identities used are unaffected.
- CLP, proof of Lemma 5.10, p. 19: a trivialization of the underlying vector bundle is used as a trivialization of the quadratic form; §7.2 routes the argument through the parabolic reduction.
- CLP, §5.3, equations (5.10)–(5.13), pp. 20–22: the half-spin representation is treated as a representation of `SO₁₀` and the quotient by `SO₁₀` is identified with `M°_7`. The half-spin representation is a representation of `Spin₁₀`, on which `−1` acts by `−1`, and the effective quotient is by `PSO₁₀`; §§7.3–7.4 use the `Spin₁₀`-lift, the rigidification by `μ₄` and a separate rational Chow transfer. For `n = 0` the fibre-power argument of Lemma 5.13 is replaced by the forgetful route of §7.7.
- CLP, §6.2, first sentence of the proof of Theorem 1.5(3), p. 23: the range is written `k ≥ 14`; the theorem and the argument use `k ≤ 14` (§10.4).
- CLP, proof of Lemma 7.3, p. 27: the representation spanned by the symbols `M_{ij}` is written as induced from `S₁₀`; it is induced from `S₁₀ × S₂`, of dimension `66`, with the printed decomposition `T_{(2,1^{10})} ⊕ T_{(3,1^9)}` (§11.8).
- CLP, proof of Lemma 8.2, p. 28: only the bound `2g − 2 + n > 15` is used; for `n ≤ 1` the bound `2g > 15` is needed to cover `(8, 0)` and `(8, 1)` (§12.7).
- CLP, Introduction, p. 3: unimodality of the dimensions of an STE in each parity is asserted; hard Lefschetz gives injectivity up to the middle (§4.12), and decrease above the middle is not part of this roadmap's statement.
- BFP, Corollary 3.2, p. 1330: the conclusion names an arbitrary `M̄_{g,n}`; it concerns `M̄_{4,n}`, and all genera follow through the induction of §2.5 (§§9.3, 9.8).
- BFP, proof of Proposition 3.1, p. 1330: the error term in the trace comparison is written with exponent `d/2`; the hypothesis gives exponent `s/2` (WeilConjectures, WC.5, local cutoff, uses `s`).
- BFP, §10.1, p. 1355: in the displayed count of `M_{4,3}` the coefficient `−11` is attached to `q⁴`; it belongs to `q³` (§9.6).
- BFP, Remark 2.2, p. 1328: Proposition 2.1 is cited as Proposition 2.
- BFP, opening of §9, p. 1351: the Getzler–Kapranov formula is cited as Theorem 8.3; it is Theorem 8.13 (§1.4).
- BFP, equation (3), p. 1331: the `E₁` terms of the weight spectral sequence of normalized closed strata omit the orientation character `det ℚ^{E(G)}`; with it the graph invariants change, as the two-edge stratum of `M̄_{1,2}` shows (§1.5; DeligneWeightsAndPurity §8.24 retains the character).
- BFP, §9.1, p. 1351, last sentence on twisted counts: for a nonproper variety the trace formula uses compactly supported cohomology; for `𝔸¹` ordinary cohomology gives trace `1` while the count is `q`.
- BFP, Definition 9.1, p. 1351: an arbitrary twisted count is assigned an element of the representation ring; in general it is a class function, and lattice membership needs the hypotheses of WeilConjectures, WC.1 (§1.5, second Check).
- Gorsky, arXiv version 3, Theorem 3.8, p. 6, and Definition 3.5 with Lemma 3.6, p. 5: the genus of the quotient and the modulus in the local congruence count carry misprints, corrected as in §9.1 following Kannan, Remark 3.8, pp. 11–12.
