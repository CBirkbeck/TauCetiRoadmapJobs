## RT-GlobalGaloisDeformationsPartII~2b/F01 (P2) — applied

The witness holds. The 𝔽_p[Γ_F]-span of the inertia tuple is cyclic and, as a submodule of a product of copies of the semisimple module M*, semisimple, hence a quotient of the semisimple quotient of the group algebra of the finite image; a simple S occurs in it at most dim S / dim End(S) times (dimensions over 𝔽_p, End the 𝔽_p[Γ_F]-endomorphism ring; Wedderburn). The product ∏_b `M*_{e_b*}` has S at least as often as there are basis vectors with nonzero S-isotypic component, and the isotypic component is k-stable since k acts equivariantly. For k=𝔽_p the e_b* form a prime-field basis, so that count is at least m·dim S ≥ 2 dim S > dim S / dim End(S) for multiplicity m≥2. For k=𝔽₉, M*=k(χ), χ valued in 𝔽₃×: prime-field multiplicity 2, basis {1}, `M*_1`=𝔽₃·1, product 𝔽₃(χ), generated by (1); the criterion reads 1>1. Old (§2.9 prose): "for a semisimple M* with a simple constituent of multiplicity at least two, the joint inertia tuple cannot generate the entire product for any basis". New: restricted to k=𝔽_p, with the general-k sufficient criterion Σ_b mult_S(`M*_{e_b*}`) > dim S / dim End(S) and the remark that prime-field multiplicity alone does not suffice when [k:𝔽_p]>1. The bound on the span is stated for cyclic semisimple modules because it fails without semisimplicity (the regular module of C_p over 𝔽_p is cyclic with the trivial constituent p times), and the criterion is stated as sufficient, not as an equivalence: a tuple can generate a proper submodule of a product that is itself cyclic. The existing obstruction Check now carries both bounds; the 𝔽₉ negative control and the 𝔽₃ positive control are a new Check; the last Check's "repeated simple constituents" says over k=𝔽_p or when the criterion holds. The F₄ examples cited in the Check have k=𝔽_p (FKP19 line 2653: ρ̄ᵢ:Γ_ℚ→F₄(𝔽_p)), so they still witness the obstruction. Pattern search for "multiplicity", "repeated simple": the §0.3 and §8.6 Checks already separate k-multiplicity from prime-field multiplicity; the Scope and §3.2 sentences speak of the selection hypothesis covering repeated constituents and are unchanged; the Layer 2 Examples paragraph (two equal constituents, tuple (1,1)) satisfies the criterion with Σ=2>1.
