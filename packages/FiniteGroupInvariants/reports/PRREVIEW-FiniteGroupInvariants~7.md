**The 6.8 equivalence, checked.** Let `G` be finite, `T = T(G)` the product of the abelian minimal normal subgroups and `S` the product of the nonabelian ones `M₁, …, M_k`. Distinct minimal normal subgroups commute; a nonabelian minimal normal subgroup is a product of nonabelian simple groups, so it is centreless and meets the product of the others trivially. Hence `S = M₁ × ⋯ × M_k`, `T ∩ S = 1` (a minimal normal subgroup of `G` inside `S` is one of the `Mᵢ`), and `Soc(G) = T × S`, as README 1123 says. (⇒) If `Soc(G) = ⟨x⟩^G`, the `G`-map `Soc(G) → Soc(G)/S ≅ T` shows that `T` is the normal closure of one element, that is, a cyclic `ℤ[G]`-module (in an abelian normal subgroup the normal closure of `t` is `ℤ[G]·t`). (⇐) If `T = ℤ[G]·t`, put `x = t·m₁⋯m_k` with `1 ≠ mᵢ ∈ Mᵢ`. Since `Mᵢ` is centreless, `⁅x, g⁆ = ⁅mᵢ, g⁆ ≠ 1` for some `g ∈ Mᵢ`. That element lies in `⟨x⟩^G ∩ Mᵢ`, so `Mᵢ ≤ ⟨x⟩^G` by minimality. Then `S ≤ ⟨x⟩^G`, so `t ∈ ⟨x⟩^G` and `T ≤ ⟨x⟩^G`. The README's "since" clause names the two structural facts. The centre-freeness it relies on is what "nonabelian minimal normal" gives.
