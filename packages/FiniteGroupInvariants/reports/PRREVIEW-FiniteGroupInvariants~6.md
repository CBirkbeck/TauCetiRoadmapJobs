## R01 (P3) — 6.8 misdescribes Szechtman's restatement of Gaschütz's criterion

**Location:** `TauCetiRoadmap/FiniteGroupInvariants/README.md:1119–1120`: "the criterion is restated in szechtman-2015, p. 4, as the socle being a cyclic `ℤ[G]`-module"; References line 1755–1756 says the same.

**Claim:** szechtman-2015, p. 4, phrases Gaschütz's criterion on the socle.

**Witness:** szechtman-2015 lines 182–184 (p. 4): a group has a faithful irreducible representation iff `T(G)` is a cyclic `ℤG`-module, where `T(G)` (defined at lines 18–20, p. 1) is the subgroup generated by the torsion *abelian* minimal normal subgroups, not the socle. For `A₅` the socle is `A₅` and `T(A₅) = 1`; for `S₄` both are `V₄`.

The roadmap's own statement (`irrDegreeC_lt_top_iff`, `Suggested.lean:470–471`: the socle is the normal closure of one element) is right and is equivalent for finite `G`: the socle is `S(G) × T(G)` with `S(G)` the product of the nonabelian minimal normal subgroups, each of which is the normal closure of any of its nontrivial elements and commutes with the rest, so `Soc(G) = ⟨x⟩^G` for some `x` iff `T(G) ≅ Soc(G)/S(G)` is a cyclic `ℤ[G]`-module. Only the description of the source is wrong.

**Repair:** in 1119–1120 write "restated in szechtman-2015, p. 4, as `T(G)`, the product of the abelian minimal normal subgroups, being a cyclic `ℤ[G]`-module; for finite `G` this is equivalent to the socle being the normal closure of one element, since each nonabelian minimal normal subgroup is the normal closure of any of its nontrivial elements", and the same clause in the References entry. Tested: `S₄` (socle `V₄ = T(G)`, generated by `(12)(34)`: faithful irreducible of degree 3), `A₅` (`T = 1`, socle `A₅ = ⟨(123)⟩^G`: degree 3), `C₄ × C₄` (`T = C₂²` with trivial action, two generators: no faithful irreducible, as an abelian non-cyclic group).

## R02 (P3) — Guralnick's order-96 theorem is cited to the paper the open restatements do not attribute it to

**Location:** `TauCetiRoadmap/FiniteGroupInvariants/README.md:804–806` (4.1: "(guralnick-1982, pp. 319–330; the threshold `96` and the two groups of that order are restated in kunyavskii-2020, §3, p. 6)") and References 1716–1717 ("guralnick-1982 …: the order-`96` threshold (restated in kunyavskii-2020)").

**Claim:** the theorem that every group of order less than `96` has every element of its derived subgroup a commutator, with exactly two exceptions of order `96`, is in Guralnick, *Commutators and commutator subgroups*, Adv. Math. 45 (1982), 319–330.

**Witness:** the restatement the README itself cites attributes it elsewhere: kunyavskii-2020 lines 87–89 (p. 6) credit Guralnick's 1977 thesis "(see [39])", and its reference [39] (lines 866–867) is R. M. Guralnick, *Expressing group elements as commutators*, Rocky Mountain J. Math. 10 (1980), 651–654. Two further open texts agree: K. Conrad's note *SL(2,R) and a non-commutator* (kconrad.math.uconn.edu, lines 122–124 and 135 of its text: "due to Guralnick [1]", [1] = the 1980 Rocky Mountain paper) and G. Malle's Bourbaki talk 1069 (bourbaki.fr, lines 13–15: order `96`, two groups, "[Gu80]" = the same paper). None of the three cites the 1982 Advances paper for it. Neither Guralnick paper can be read from here (Project Euclid and ScienceDirect refuse the connection; the sources service finds no PDF at the 1980 DOI), so I cannot exclude that the 1982 paper repeats the result, but the primary locator the roadmap gives for its hardest Layer 4 input is, on all the evidence available, the wrong paper.

**Repair:** cite R. M. Guralnick, *Expressing group elements as commutators*, Rocky Mountain J. Math. 10 (1980), 651–654 (DOI `10.1216/RMJ-1980-10-3-651`) as the primary for `commutatorLength_le_one_of_card_lt` in 4.1 and in the References, with a new manifest key `guralnick-1980` (`uncertified` if the service cannot fetch it, as it could not here), and keep `guralnick-1982` only if 4.1 states something it is known to prove (the README currently cites it for nothing else). Tested on the instances of the statement: `S₃`, `Q₈`, `C₂`, `A₅` (orders `6, 8, 2, 60 < 96`: commutator lengths `1, 1, 0, 1`) and the two groups of order `96` (length `2`; Conrad records `29` commutators in each).
