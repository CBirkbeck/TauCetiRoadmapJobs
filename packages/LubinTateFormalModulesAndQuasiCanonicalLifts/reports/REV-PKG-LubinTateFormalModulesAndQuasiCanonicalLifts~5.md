1. **F01** (P1) The formal torsion spectrum is not the finite group scheme

   **Location:** `packages/LubinTateFormalModulesAndQuasiCanonicalLifts/README.md:738`: “the formal law makes `G[π^m] = Spf R[[X]]/([π^m]_G)` a finite locally free commutative group scheme”.

   **Claim:** The displayed formal spectrum itself is a finite locally free group scheme over `R`, usable for the geometric torsion and generic fibres of §§2.2 and 5.1.

   **Witness:** For `R=ℤ₃`, `G=Ĝ_m`, `m=1`, put `A=ℤ₃[T]/(T³−1)`. `Spf A` has one point; `Spec A` has three primes and three geometric generic points. See `TauCeti/AlgebraicGeometry/FormalScheme/Spf/Carrier.lean:92`.

   Here `A ≅ ℤ₃[[X]]/(3X+3X²+X³)` by `T=1+X`. Its adic topology can be defined by `(3)`; the induced `(3,X)` topology is equivalent. The formal spectrum contains just the maximal prime `(3,T−1)`. The ordinary spectrum also contains the two generic primes `(T−1)` and `(T²+T+1)`. The pinned declaration `TauCeti.FormalScheme.Spf.carrier_eq_zeroLocus` identifies the formal carrier with `V(3)`, rather than all of `Spec A`. Thus this is a category error, not a defect in the rank calculation. An Artinian test base conceals it because its ideal of definition is nilpotent.

   **Exact repair:** Set `A_m = R[[X]]/([π^m]_G)` and define the finite group scheme as `G[π^m] = Spec A_m`. In §2.1 explicitly construct its Hopf structure from the formal law: `A_m` and its finite tensor powers are finite free over the complete base, so evaluation of the formal addition, inverse and action gives the ordinary algebra maps. Identify its formal completion with the formal kernel `Spf A_m`. Use this ordinary finite group scheme for the exact sequences, Barsotti–Tate levels and subsequent geometric points. Add the preceding mixed-characteristic example as a negative-control Check distinguishing `Spec` and `Spf`; keep the generality of `R` and all rank statements.

   **Repair test:** The repaired construction gives `μ₃ = Spec ℤ₃[T]/(T³−1)`, with `Δ(T)=T⊗T`, counit `T↦1` and inverse `T↦T²`. Its rank is 3, its special fibre is `𝔽₃[X]/(X³)`, and its geometric generic fibre has the three distinct cube roots of unity. At `m=0`, `Spec R` gives the identity group; its completion is `Spf R`. Over `𝔽₃[ε]/ε²`, the nilpotent base topology makes the formal and ordinary spectra agree, retaining the nonreduced test. The displayed occurrence is the only `Spf` construction in this README. No preceding repair report settled this issue.
