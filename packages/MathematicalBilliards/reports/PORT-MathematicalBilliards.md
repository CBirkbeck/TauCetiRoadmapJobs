## Draft pull request body

This roadmap builds the two classical planar billiard systems as dynamical systems, from Mathlib's translation numbers, measure-preserving and ergodic actions and Tau Ceti's plane topology (Jordan, Schoenflies, crosscuts), Bochner theorem, strongly continuous unitary groups and structure groupoids of group actions. For strictly convex tables of every regularity `C^k`, `k ≥ 1`, with curvature allowed to vanish, it constructs the billiard ball map in the coordinates `(s, δ)`, proves that it is a homeomorphism of the phase cylinder whose closed-cylinder extension is a topological twist and that it preserves `sin δ dδ ds`, and for `k ≥ 2` that it is an exact monotone twist map generated by the chord length. For smooth tables it is identified with the next-collision map of SymplecticGeometry/CotangentLagrangian C3.1, which it extends to finite regularity. On a general theory of twist maps of the annulus and the non-wandering set, it proves Birkhoff's theorem that rotational invariant curves are graphs (Lipschitz graphs for `k ≥ 2`, by Fathi's topological argument), builds rotation numbers on Mathlib's `CircleDeg1Lift.translationNumber`, and proves Birkhoff's theorem on two geometrically distinct periodic orbits of every rotation type `(p, q)`, variationally and at regularity `C¹`. For polygonal tables, holes allowed, it constructs the phase space, the singular set and the almost-everywhere billiard flow as an honest measure-preserving action of `ℝ`, the reflection group and non-ergodicity of rational tables, translation surfaces on Tau Ceti's groupoid of local translations, and the Katok–Zemlyakov surface with its genus and the unfolding conjugacy in every direction class. It closes with weak mixing of measure-preserving `ℝ`-actions: strong continuity of the Koopman group of a jointly measurable flow (no topology on the space), Wiener's lemma on `ℝ`, the continuous-time mean ergodic projection with its spectral atomic mass, the eigenfunction criterion and the multiplier property. These are the inputs that the Birkhoff–Poritsky theorem, weakly mixing polygonal billiards and translation-surface dynamics are built on, and ClassicalErgodicTheory already cites §§12.1–12.5 and Layers 7–8 of this roadmap as their owner.

## Fixes from the upstream read of the README

1. **§4.4, recurrence to non-wandering** (maintainer note 3). The bullet assumed a second-countable space and attributed the conclusion to C3.1. C3.1's neighbourhood-return form has no such hypothesis, and the step from it to non-wandering is this roadmap's own. The bullet now states it for a measurable self-map of a topological space with its Borel σ-algebra preserving a finite measure of full support, gives the step (a neighbourhood contains a nonempty open `V`; return to `V` gives `f^n(U) ∩ U ≠ ∅`), and cites Herman1983 lines 434–438 for what that passage actually says. I checked it in the cached text: Poincaré recurrence for an invariant probability of full support.
2. **C3.1 supplier contract** (maintainer note 2): "periodicity exactly when `arccos(v)/π` is rational" became "its rational-rotation periodicity criterion", C3.1's own wording. C3.1 states no formula.
3. **§6.3, orientation** (maintainer note 5): added the comparison. C3.1's curve has no orientation hypothesis. A closed unit-speed curve that is injective on `[0, ℓ)` and meets each tangent line only at its point of tangency lies on one side of each tangent line, the same side for every point, so it or its reversal is a table with the same inscribed polygons; the `(1, N)` case gives C3.1's conclusion with least period exactly `N`.
4. **Library claims outside Scope**: §10.1's "which the pins do not provide" and the contract's "is found at the pins … while … is not" moved to one sentence in Scope ("Mathlib has no `ContMDiffConstSMul` instance for that translation action; §10.1 proves it"), verified at the pin as above.
5. **Scope, C3.1's primitive identity**: written as "`β̃*(v ds) − v ds` is the differential of the chord length", not C3.1's `dℓ`, because `ℓ` is the perimeter here.
6. **§3.3, second proof**: `AddCircle.measurePreserving_mk` uses the fundamental interval `(t, t + T]`, so the change of variables is on `(0, ℓ] × (0, π)`, which differs from `[0, ℓ) × (0, π)` by a null set; the text said `[0, ℓ)`.
