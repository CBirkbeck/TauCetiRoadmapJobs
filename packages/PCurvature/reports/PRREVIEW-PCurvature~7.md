## R01 (P2) — the effective Honda statement after 4.3 is still false with `c ∈ ℚ` free and `σ` computed from `A/B`

Location: `TauCetiRoadmap/PCurvature/README.md` lines 631–636: "For `u = c·A/B` with `A, B ∈ ℤ[x]` coprime and primitive, `deg A < deg B`, `B` squarefree … and `c ∈ ℚ`, the equation `y′ = u y` has a nonzero algebraic solution iff its `p`-curvature vanishes at every prime `p ∤ res(B, B′)` below a bound `σ` explicit in the coefficients of `R(w) = res_x(B, A − wB′)`".

Claim: `R(w)`, `res(B, B′)` and hence `σ` are computed from `A, B` alone, but the `p`-curvatures tested are those of `u = c·A/B`; at a prime dividing the numerator of `c` the reduction of `u` is `0` and its `p`-curvature vanishes whatever `R` does modulo `p`. So the "if" direction fails as soon as `c` is divisible by every tested prime at which `R` does not split. The source has the same defect: Theorem 1.3 (furnsinn-pannier, printed p. 3, read with pdftotext since the OCR at lines 113–119 drops its first line) is stated for `u = c·a/b`, `c ∈ ℚ`, with `R` and `Δ_b` from `a, b`, and its proof (lines 260–262) "reduces to `c = 1`" by "`y` algebraic iff `y^c` algebraic", which preserves algebraicity but not the set of primes at which the `p`-curvature vanishes. The `c ∈ ℚ` clause was already in the text the previous review read and was not caught then.

Witness: `A = 1`, `B = x² + 1`, `c` the product of the primes `p ≡ 3 (mod 4)` below `σ`. All stated hypotheses hold (`A, B` coprime and primitive, `0 < 2`, `B` squarefree, `res(B, B′) = res(x²+1, 2x) = 4`). `R(w) = res_x(x²+1, 1 − 2wx) = 4w² + 1` (`r_2 = 4 = Δ_b`), `R_0 = R`, `Δ_R = res_w(4w²+1, 8w) = 64`, `δ(Δ_R) = 2`, `M = ⌈2.826·64·8⌉ = 1447`, root bound `1/2`, `N = ⌈6.076·½·1447⌉ = 4396`, `σ = 2895·4396 + 2894 = 12 729 314` (any admissible root bound gives a finite `σ`; the argument needs only that `σ` is finite and independent of `c`). Tested primes: odd `p < σ`. For `p ≡ 1 (mod 4)`: `p ∤ c`, `i ∈ 𝔽_p`, `ū = c̄/(x²+1)` has simple poles `±i` with residues `∓c̄ i/2 ∈ 𝔽_p` and no polynomial part, so `ψ_p = 0` (2.4, or Fürnsinn–Pannier Proposition 2.3). For `p ≡ 3 (mod 4)`, `p < σ`: `p | c`, `ū = 0`, `ψ_p = 0^p + D^{p−1}(0) = 0`. So every tested `p`-curvature vanishes; but `u = c/(x²+1)` has residues `∓ci/2 ∉ ℚ` (`c ≠ 0`), so by 4.3 over `ℚ` (or Fürnsinn–Pannier Proposition 2.2 (2)) `y′ = u y` has no nonzero algebraic solution (the Check `1/(x²+1)` of 4.3, scaled by `c`). No modification of the tested set rescues `c`: excluding the primes dividing `c` leaves exactly the primes `≡ 1 (mod 4)`, where `R` splits although it does not split over `ℚ`, so Theorem 1.2 no longer applies. A second, smaller defect of the same clause: for `c = 1/3`, `A = 1`, `B = x` (`σ = 139`, `res(B, B′) = 1`), `y = x^{1/3}` is algebraic while the reduction of `u` at `p = 3` is not defined (the README's Convention 6 makes it junk `0`), so the "only if" direction holds only by the junk convention.

Repair: drop `c`. Every `u ∈ ℚ(x)^×` is `A/B` with `A, B ∈ ℤ[x]` coprime, and the proof needs nothing more: for `p ∤ res(B, B′)` one has `p ∤ lc(B)` (`res(B, B′)` is divisible by `lc(B)` when `deg B ≥ 1`), so `deg B̄ = deg B > deg Ā`, `B̄` is squarefree, and `ψ_p = 0` iff `R̄` splits in `𝔽_p[w]` (Proposition 2.3 (1) ⇔ (4)); Theorem 1.2 applied to `±R ∈ ℤ[w]` (leading coefficient `±res(B, B′)`) finishes. For example: "For `u = A/B` with `A, B ∈ ℤ[x]` coprime, `deg A < deg B` and `B` squarefree (so `res(B, B′) ≠ 0`), the equation `y′ = u y` has a nonzero algebraic solution iff its `p`-curvature vanishes at every prime `p ∤ res(B, B′)` below a bound `σ` explicit in the coefficients of `R(w) = res_x(B, A − wB′)` (Fürnsinn–Pannier, Theorem 1.3 with `c = 1` and under the hypotheses of their Proposition 2.2 (3), …)". "Primitive" may go with `c` (it is not needed; `u = 3/x` and `u = 1/(3x)` both behave: `R = w − 3`, root `3`, `y = x³`; `R = 3w − 1`, root `1/3`, `y = x^{1/3}`, `p = 3 | res(3x, 3) = 3` excluded). On the witness: `c/(x²+1)` is no longer an instance; `1/(x²+1)` itself (`c = 1`) has `ψ_3 ≠ 0` with `3 ∤ 4`, `3 < σ`, so the test fails, as it must. The two existing witnesses (`B = (x+1)²`, and `A = Nx² + Nx + 1`, `B = −(x+1)`) are unaffected (`c = 1` in both). The closing sentence may then record that the printed Theorem 1.3 omits the two hypotheses and lets `c` vary.
