## R01 (P2) — `G_sc(t)` can be stated at the pins, but `Suggested.lean` leaves out its smoothness and generation and calls it not statable

As it stands, the acceptance test checks only the adjoint Geck carrier. That carrier is `G_sc(t)` only for `E₈`, `F₄` and `G₂`, so for `Aₙ`, `Bₙ`, `Cₙ`, `Dₙ`, `E₆` and `E₇` nothing about the roadmap's main object is tested. The reason given, in the module docstring of `TauCetiRoadmap/ChevalleyGroups/Suggested.lean` (lines 33–36, repeated for `B₂` at lines 51–52), is that the pins lack "the group scheme they generate, nor the nilpotency of the numbered generators". That reason does not hold.

How I know:

- Every other argument is pinned in exactly the form it consumes: `t.kostantForm_apply_mem_scLattice ht`, `t.scWeightBasis ht` and `t.scWeight ht` (`…/LieAlgebra/SimplyConnectedModule/Lattice.lean`, lines 149, 166 and 186).

The same holds, for every field `k`, for `Subgroup.closure (⋃ i, Set.range (kostantToralRootSubgroupPoints … hnil (t.scWeightBasis ht) (t.scWeight ht) i k)) = ⊤`. These are the `G_sc(t)` cases of §4.3 and §4.4 (the second headline of the Summary), and both are true:

- For `A₁` the scheme is `SL₂`, of relative dimension `1 + 2 = 3`.
- `SL₂(𝔽₂) ≅ S₃` is generated by its two numbered root elements, which are two transpositions.

The hypothesis `hnil` is the proposition §1.1 proves. It is not a stand-in, and it asks the implementer for nothing beyond that target.

## C01 — §4.2 cites Theorem 5.1.13 for the cover by Weyl translates, which that theorem does not state

Lines 641–643 attach "Conrad, *Reductive group schemes*, Theorem 5.1.13, p. 143" to the open immersion and to "the cover of the group by the Weyl translates of its image". The theorem (Conrad2014 lines 6011–6019) states two things: the product decomposition of `U_Φ⁺`, and that `U⁻ × T × U → G` is an open immersion. It says nothing about a cover.

The cover is Corollary 1.4.14, p. 39 (Conrad2014 line 1648), over an algebraically closed field. Conrad applies it fibre by fibre over a base on pp. 169–170 (Conrad2014 lines 7086–7088). Cite Theorem 5.1.13 for the open cell and Corollary 1.4.14 for the cover.

## C02 — In §4.2 the step from the Bruhat decomposition is a left translation, not a conjugation

Line 631 says that the Bruhat decomposition, "conjugated by a representative of the longest element", gives `g ∈ U⁻(k) T_D(k) n_w U(k)`. Conjugating by `n₀` turns `B n_w B` into `U⁻ T n_{w₀ww₀} U⁻`, with `U⁻` on both sides. The block-minor argument that follows needs an upper unitriangular right factor.

Left translation gives the stated form: `E(k) = n₀ E(k) = ⊔_w U⁻(k) T_D(k) n₀ n_w U(k)`, because `n₀ U n₀⁻¹ = U⁻` and `n₀` normalizes `T`. In `SL₂` conjugation by `n₀` sends the upper triangular group to the lower one, so the conjugated decomposition has lower triangular factors on both sides. Write "translated on the left by".
