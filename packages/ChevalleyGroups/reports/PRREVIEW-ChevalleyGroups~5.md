## R01 (P2) — The comparison `G_sc(t) → G_ad(t)` can be stated at the pins, but `Suggested.lean` leaves it out and calls Layer 6 not statable

As it stands, the fourth headline of the Summary, Chevalley's comparison theorem, has no statement in the acceptance test. Yet its main case, the map from `G_sc(t)` to the adjoint form (`README.md` §6.3, lines 1063–1065), is the comparison that CFSGStatement consumes. The module docstring (`Suggested.lean`, lines 69–71) gives the reason: the theorem "is stated for Chevalley data … neither is at the pins". That is true of a general datum `D`. It is not true of the two carriers on which the file already states smoothness, generation, the centre and the exceptional isogenies.

Both carriers are pinned objects of `Grp (Over (Spec ℤ))`:

- `kostantToralGroupScheme` (`ToralClosure/Basic.lean`, line 161), with scheme-level root subgroups `kostantRootSubgroupToToral` (line 250);
- `TauCeti.DynkinType.geckGroupScheme` (`GeckLattice/GroupScheme.lean`, line 148), with `geckRootSubgroup` (line 222).

With `open CategoryTheory`, the following elaborates against the pins with only the `sorry` warning:

It is true: it is the central isogeny for `Q ⊆ P` (ReductiveGroups §9B.2, transported along §2.9; Conrad, Theorem 6.1.16(1)). For `A₁` it is `SL₂ → PGL₂`, with kernel `μ₂`. It is not padding either. It asks for the existence of a group-scheme morphism over `ℤ`, which the pins do not supply. The status matches smoothness, which is also taken from ReductiveGroups §9B.1 and is stated in the file.

The smallest change: state this theorem, with a docstring that points to §6.3. For `E₈`, `F₄` and `G₂`, where both weight lattices are `P`, you could add that `f` is an isomorphism. Then narrow the Layer 6 bullet of the docstring to general Chevalley data.

## R02 (P2) — Two sentences on the adjoint torus say for every field what holds only for some fields

A reader takes these as facts about every field, and the roadmap itself contradicts them.

- Convention 5 (lines 104–107) says that, over a field that is not algebraically closed, the image of the full-weight parametrization "is smaller than `T_D(k)`".
- The Layer 5 introduction (lines 772–774) says the adjoint items do not extend to an arbitrary field, where the torus `Hom(Q, kˣ)` "is larger than the image of that parametrization and is not generated by root subgroups".

Three counterexamples:

- For `E₈`, `F₄` and `G₂`, `P = Q` (§2.3, lines 364–366, `span_range_geckWeight_E8_eq_top`). The restriction `Hom(P, kˣ) → Hom(Q, kˣ)` is then the identity over every field. For `F₄` and `G₂` the exceptional isogeny exists on the adjoint points over every field of characteristic `p`, because they are `G_sc(t)` and `exists_scSpecialIsogeny` covers them.
- Over `𝔽₂`, `T_D(𝔽₂)` is trivial for every datum (the Check of §3.5, line 578).
- For adjoint `A₁` over `𝔽₄`, `s ↦ s²` is bijective on the group `𝔽₄ˣ` of order 3. Its image is the whole torus, and `PGL₂(𝔽₄) = PSL₂(𝔽₄)` is generated by its root subgroups.

The scope sentence at lines 1224–1225, "holds over an arbitrary field or ring", has the same slip: §4.5 fails over `ℚ` by its own Check.

The smallest change:

- Convention 5: write "can be smaller (for adjoint `A₁` over `ℚ` it misses `[diag(2, 1)]`)".
- Layer 5 introduction: "where, unless `t` is `E₈`, `F₄` or `G₂`, the torus can be larger than that image and need not be generated by root subgroups".
- Scope: "holds over the fields and rings its statements name".
