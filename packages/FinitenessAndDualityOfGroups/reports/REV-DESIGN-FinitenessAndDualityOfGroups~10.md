1. **F01** (P1) Tensor-line stabilizers need not be finite over the zero ring

   **Location:** `packages/FinitenessAndDualityOfGroups/design/README.md:993`, §3.1(f): “the stabilizer of a line spanned by a tensor of basis elements”; the following clause asserts that its intersection with the normal core is trivial. Target 3.1 of `design/plan.json` also promises finite stabilizers.

   **Claim:** over every commutative ring, stabilizers of the indicated submodules have trivial intersection with the core, hence are finite.

   **Witness:** Take the zero ring, `H = G = ℤ`, and `F₀ = R[H]`, with its regular formal basis indexed by `H`. Every spanned submodule is zero. Its stabilizer and its intersection with the core are `ℤ`.

   A basis over the zero ring can have an arbitrary index type: the coordinate module and the free module are both zero. Thus the regular action on the basis indices is free although the action on the images of the basis vectors, and on their spans, is trivial. If “line” requires a nonzero vector, there are instead no such lines; that interpretation also fails to specify the promised basis-orbit construction. The zero ring is explicitly admitted elsewhere in this design.

   **Exact repair:** replace the submodule stabilizer by the stabilizer of a formal homogeneous basis tuple, before its Koszul sign is applied. State the input as an `H`-equivariant `R`-basis indexed by a free `H`-set, equivalently the regular `R`-basis obtained from an `RH`-basis. Define `K` on those indices and define its sign character from the graded permutation. Use these `K` in the induced-module decomposition. Synchronize target 3.1 and add the zero-ring Check; do not exclude the zero ring.

   **Retest:** for the same regular index set and index-one induction, the tuple stabilizer is `1`, its intersection with the core is `1`, and its orbit contributes `Ind₁^ℤ(0) = 0`, as required. For `H = 1`, `G = C₂`, and a degree-one singleton basis, the tuple stabilizer is still `C₂` and its character sends the swap to `−1`; the existing sign Check survives, including in characteristic two.

3. **F03** (P2) The centralizer-trace theorem already has an earlier owner

   **Location:** `packages/FinitenessAndDualityOfGroups/design/README.md:1582`, §7.2: the projective trace “is `|C_G(g)| · r_P([g⁻¹])`”; lines 1586–1593 give a fresh proof. Plan target 7.2 owns the same scalar identity.

   **Claim:** this roadmap owns the finite-group centralizer-factor trace identity, beyond importing the generic Hattori–Stallings trace in §7.1.

   **Witness:** main's `TauCetiRoadmap/EtaleCohomologyFoundations/README.md:1330`–1340 already owns identity-coefficient traces and their centralizer-factor formula for arbitrary coefficient rings. Open #196, `TauCetiRoadmap/CohomologicalPointCounting/TraceFormula/README.md:189`–195, also plans this formula.

   Specialize the main roadmap's extension to `G × ℤ`, let the extra factor act trivially on `P`, and choose `(g, 1)` over the positive generator. Its centralizer is `C_G(g)` and its scalar trace is exactly the trace in §7.2. The genuinely additional interface here is the identification of that normalized centralizer trace with the conjugacy-coordinate rank `r_P([g⁻¹])`. The main theorem applies to finite projectives, including nonfree modules, and does not divide by group orders. This is duplication by object, not merely similar terminology.

   **Exact repair:** expand Scope and the EtaleCohomologyFoundations §F.10.3 supplier contract to import its centralizer trace and scalar identity. Narrow §7.2 and plan target 7.2 to the comparison between that supplied normalized trace and the conjugacy-coordinate rank from §7.1, explicitly saying how this interface differs. Obtain the displayed scalar identity by the cited supplier theorem; remove its independent reproving route. Keep the general conjugacy-coordinate construction of §7.1. The supplier is main's EtaleCohomologyFoundations, never the prohibited CohomologicalPointCounting roadmap.

   **Retest:** for `C₃` over `ℂ` and the idempotent for the character `g ↦ ω`, the normalized centralizer trace and `r_P([g⁻¹])` are both `ω/3`; the imported scalar formula gives `3(ω/3) = ω`. Using `[g]` would give `ω⁻¹`. For `G = 1`, `R = ℚ × ℚ`, `P = R(1,0)`, both normalized traces are `(1,0)`, and the factor is one. Thus the repair retains the nonfree-projective and inverse-convention tests.
