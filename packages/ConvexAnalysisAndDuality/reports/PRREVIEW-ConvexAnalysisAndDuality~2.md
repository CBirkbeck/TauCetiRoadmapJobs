## R01 (P2) — The homogenization in the proof route of 1.3 is not a closed cone for an unbounded polyhedron, so the cone form of Minkowski–Weyl does not apply to it

An implementer who follows the route is stuck at its first step for every unbounded polyhedron, which is the only case where the cone `PointedCone.hull ℝ D` of the statement is not `{0}`. The route (README, lines 333–336) reads: "Polyhedra reduce to cones by homogenization `s ↦ {(t • x, t) | t ≥ 0, x ∈ s}` in `V × ℝ`, to which the cone form of ScatteringDiagrams §1.1 applies".

Take `s = [0, ∞) ⊆ ℝ`. The set is `{(a, t) | a ≥ 0, t > 0} ∪ {(0, 0)}`: it contains `(1, 1/n)` for every `n` but not the limit `(1, 0)`. A finitely generated cone and a cone that is the dual of a finite set are both closed, so this set is neither, and ScatteringDiagrams §1.1 says nothing about it. (The same formula is `coneOver` in 6.2, where `K` is compact and the cone is closed; here it is not.)

The smallest repair is to homogenize the inequalities instead of the points. For `s = {x | ∀ i, gᵢ x ≤ 0}` with `gᵢ x = ℓᵢ x − bᵢ`, take `{(x, t) | t ≥ 0, ∀ i, ℓᵢ x ≤ t bᵢ}`, which is the dual of a finite set by construction; the cone form gives finitely many generators, those with `t > 0` rescaled to `t = 1` give `P`, those with `t = 0` give `D`, and the slice at `t = 1` is `s`. For the converse, apply the cone form to the cone generated by `(p, 1)`, `p ∈ P`, and `(d, 0)`, `d ∈ D`. Alternatively, route through the unbounded form of 1.2 (line-free closed convex sets), which the second bullet of 1.2 (lines 307–309) says 1.3 uses although neither the route nor the *Needs:* line of 1.3 mentions it; then add 1.2 to that *Needs:* line.

## C01 — The sentence on polar cones in 2.2 does not say which cone is `-ProperCone.innerDual` "of the dual", and read literally it has the wrong sign

The third bullet of 2.2 (lines 429–431) says of a cone `K` that the bipolar is the closed convex cone generated by `K` and "for a Hilbert space this is `-ProperCone.innerDual` of the dual". For `K = [0, ∞) ⊆ ℝ`: `innerDual K = [0, ∞)`, the polar cone is `-innerDual K = (-∞, 0]`, and the bipolar is `innerDual (innerDual K) = [0, ∞)`, whereas `-innerDual (innerDual K) = (-∞, 0]`. Say: the polar cone is `-ProperCone.innerDual K`, and the bipolar is `ProperCone.innerDual (ProperCone.innerDual K)`, which is Mathlib's `ProperCone.innerDual_innerDual` for a closed convex cone (`Mathlib/Analysis/Convex/Cone/InnerDual.lean`, line 103).

## C02 — A quadratic program is not an instance of 5.4 with `K` the orthant

The Layer 5 examples (lines 1120–1121) say the "Lagrangian dual of a quadratic program" is an instance of 5.4 "with `K` the orthant". The program of 5.4 has a linear objective, so with the orthant it is a linear program, the case of 5.3. A convex quadratic program needs the second-order cone to be put in the form of 5.4, or it is an instance of 5.1 directly. Write "linear program" for the orthant, or name the second-order cone for the quadratic program.

## C03 — The scope section assigns to the first-order-methods roadmap material that roadmap does not own

"Not owned here" (lines 110–114) lists Moreau envelopes, the Moreau decomposition for proximal maps, the proximal point method, alternating projections and Douglas–Rachford as belonging to the first-order-methods roadmap. At the head of the open FirstOrderOptimization roadmap, the scope section owns the nearest-point and proximal maps, the subgradient method, proximal gradient descent, FISTA and stochastic methods, and places "primal–dual and splitting methods other than forward–backward" out of scope; it has no target on Moreau envelopes, the Moreau decomposition, the proximal point method or alternating projections. A reader is sent to a roadmap that does not plan these. List only what that roadmap owns (proximal maps, the Baillon–Haddad theorem of its 1.2, projected and proximal gradient, the subgradient method, acceleration), and move the rest to the boundary list.

## C04 — Three bold targets are called headlines of the same standing but are stated only in prose, and the module docstring names none as not statable

The summary (lines 48–51) and the docstring of `Suggested.lean` (lines 26–30) say that the other headlines, "bold or not", are targets of the same standing stated in prose. The bold ones are the conjugate of a sum (5.1), the relative interior of a linear image (3.6) and the steepness form of essential smoothness (4.3). Either state them (the conjugate of a sum and the steepness equivalence are the two other roadmaps will cite), or call them supporting targets rather than headlines.

## N02 — Two Checks need the standing `⊥` or finiteness hypothesis written in

`infConv (convexIndicator {0}) g = g` (line 516) fails for a `g` that takes `⊥`: with `g 0 = ⊥` and `g = 0` elsewhere, the term `y = 1` at `x = 1` is `⊤ + ⊥ = ⊥ ≠ g 1`; say "for `g` never `⊥`". In the negative control of 5.1 (line 1103), `g` is constantly `⊤` near each `x < 0` and so is continuous there as an `EReal` function; say "finite and continuous at no point of `dom f`".
