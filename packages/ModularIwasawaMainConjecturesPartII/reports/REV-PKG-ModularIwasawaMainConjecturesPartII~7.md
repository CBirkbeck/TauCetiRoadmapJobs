   **Location:** `packages/ModularIwasawaMainConjecturesPartII/README.md:499`, §4.5: “Here `ζ(χ) = (N(a) − σ_a)⁻¹·a ζ(χ)` and `J_Λ` is the kernel of the cyclotomic character.” Also the uses in §§4.6 and 6.1.

   **Witness:** README:41 gives `φ(gx)=χ(g)⁻¹gφ(x)`. For `K=ℚ(i)`, `p=3`, the quadratic ray character modulo `(3)`, and `a=(2+i), b=(7)`, the augmented correct factors are `6,48`; the printed factors are `4,48`.

   The semilinearity sends the untwisted auxiliary relation of §2.4 to the factors
   `s_a(χ)=N(a)−χ(σ_a)⁻¹[σ_a|Γ]`.
   Here `K(3)=ℚ(i,√3)`, `χ(σ_a)=−1` and `χ(σ_b)=1`. Both ideals satisfy the coprimality requirements. At augmentation, write the common correctly normalized value as `u≠0`; nonvanishing follows from §2.5 for this nontrivial character. Then `aζ(χ)=6u` and `bζ(χ)=48u`. The definition in §4.5 gives `3u/2` and `u`, respectively. It therefore does not define the claimed auxiliary-independent object. Do not identify it with the untwisted cyclotomic kernel without proving the relevant equality. Propagate that definition through §§4.5–4.6 and their use in §6.1, including the determinant calculation and twisting argument. Add this nontrivial-character Check: `6u/6=48u/48=u`, whereas the untwisted denominators give unequal values. This preserves the all-prime scope.

2. **F02** (P1) The corrected complex norm is not propagated to the Tamagawa zeta element

   **Location:** `packages/ModularIwasawaMainConjecturesPartII/README.md:472`, §4.3: “it equals `(N(a) − η(a))·z_p(η)`”; compare the normalization added at README:348 and the zeta element imported in §4.2.

   **Claim:** the inverse theta unit, with the corrected squared complex norm in §2.5, equals the indicated multiple of JLK's Tamagawa zeta element without a factor of two.

   **Witness:** JLK lines 394–398 define the ordinary complex absolute value for the Beilinson regulator. In README:354's quadratic example, its regulator is `4 log(2+√3)`, while `(25−1)L′(η,0)=8 log(2+√3)`.

   Take `K=ℚ(i)`, `p=3`, `η` the quadratic character of conductor `(3)`, and `a=(5)`. The character is ramified at the unique prime above 3, as §4.3 requires, so `z_p(η)=z(η)`. Put `R=log(2+√3)`. The ordinary logarithms of the two theta values, recomputed from Kato's product, are `0.6619210721546844` and `5.929752659853957`. Thus the η-weighted logarithm of the inverse theta unit is `4R=5.26783158769927`. On the other hand `L′(η,0)=R/3`, and JLK's normalization of `z(η)` (JLK lines 480–483 and 773–775) makes the alleged right side have regulator `24·R/3=8R=10.5356631753985`. The injective regulator separates the two elements.

   The new §2.5 correctly doubles the logarithm to obtain the Dirichlet normalization. That change cannot silently double the Beilinson regulator used to define the zeta element and its determinant trivialization. The repair report's assertion that §§2.7 and 4.3 are consistent is correct for the nonvanishing needed by §2.7, but false for §4.3's equality.

   **Exact repair:** distinguish `r_D=2r_B` and the corresponding zeta elements `z_D=z_B/2`. With the JLK element `z_B`, write the comparison as `ζ_K(η⁻¹)=(N(a)−η(a))z_B,p(η)/2`. Alternatively use `z_D` throughout, explicitly transporting the determinant trivialization and lattice statement of §4.2. The displayed tensor of divided elliptic units then differs from the tensor of JLK zeta generators by `2^(−m)` for `m` character factors; account for this factor in §4.3 and re-establish the dyadic integral step in §4.5 in the chosen convention. Do not treat 2 as an integral unit or restrict the roadmap to odd primes. Add the Check `12·R/3=4R`; a rank-one dyadic generator scaled by 2 has valuation increased by 1. Rational comparisons after inverting `p` are not disproved by this scalar discrepancy.

3. **F03** (P2) The Euler-factor lattice reformulation needs a nonvanishing hypothesis

   **Location:** `packages/ModularIwasawaMainConjecturesPartII/README.md:460`, §4.2: “for abelian `L ⊃ F ⊃ K`, the `O_p`-module `⊗_η r_p(z_p(η))O_p` … equals” the tensor of determinant lattices. README:466 explicitly allows a zero Euler factor.

   **Claim:** the reformulation by vectors `z_p(η)=∏(1−η(𝔭))z(η)` is a lattice identity for arbitrary such extensions and primes.

   **Witness:** take `K=ℚ(i)`, `F=K`, `L=K(3)=ℚ(i,√3)`, `p=13`, and its quadratic character η. Since `4²≡3 mod 13`, both primes over 13 split in `L/K`: `z_13(η)=0`, whereas the determinant line has rank one.

   There is just one character in `Ĝ_L∖Ĝ_F`. Both its Euler factors are `1−1=0`. The left side is the zero module. The determinant of the perfect cohomology complex is an invertible module and remains one-dimensional over the coefficient field. Thus it cannot equal zero. Saying “uses `z_p`, not `z`” in the Check does not repair the missing determinant identification when the local cohomology has invariants.

   **Exact repair:** retain the general scalar Tamagawa-number conjecture, but restrict this particular vector-based reformulation to characters for which every removed Euler factor is nonzero. Ramification at every prime above `p`, already imposed in §4.3, is a sufficient condition. State the exceptional case using the full local determinant/leading-term construction, rather than identifying a zero vector with a lattice generator. Replace README:466 by the negative control above. At `p=3` the same η is ramified, the removed factors are 1, and the reformulation needed by §4.3 survives; at `p=13` the abbreviated formula is correctly inapplicable. JLK Corollary 2.11, lines 512–522, does not state the needed restriction either.

Then the supplier's `α′_AGR=ψ`, and its cohomological Galois realization is the one used in §7.2. State the CM-predicate comparison through this twist, and update Scope and the supplier contract accordingly. Add a weight-three Check: at the prime above 5, `α_AGR=ψ/√5` and `α′_AGR=ψ`, so both sides have absolute value 5. For `k=2` this reduces to the existing Check. This completes the ownership repair without re-planning the supplier's induction theorem.
