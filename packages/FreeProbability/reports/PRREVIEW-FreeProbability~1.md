## R01 (P2) — RandomMatrices Layer 6 and this roadmap both own the scalar free product, ⊞ and ⊠ with their transforms, the free Poisson limit and the Weingarten formulas, and each says the other consumes them

An implementer of μ ⊞ ν, the reduced-word free product with positive state or the Weingarten formula cannot tell which roadmap's statement is the target or where the declaration will live. The single-owner rule exists to prevent exactly this.

How I know: in Scope and ownership, the paragraph "Shared with RandomMatrices" (lines 1549–1561) lists these four overlaps and says "The two roadmaps describe one implementation of each of these constructions, in the generality and conventions specified here". The supplier contracts (lines 1800–1802) consume RandomMatrices "Layer 6 as the shared bounded scalar core". Yet §§3.3–3.4, 5.1, 5.6–5.7, 6.5 and 8.5–8.6 state the same objects as targets here. On main, RandomMatrices plans the same targets: §6.2 builds free products with positive state from reduced words, §6.3 defines ⊞ and ⊠ and proves S-multiplicativity, and §6.5 builds the Weingarten formulas with their large-dimension estimates. Its scope (RandomMatrices README, lines 81–85) says that this roadmap "extends Layer 6 rather than supplying it" and consumes it. CStarAlgebras (line 80) says the opposite: this roadmap owns free products with prescribed states.

The smallest change is to give each shared object one owner in Scope and ownership. There are two ways to do it:

- Cite RandomMatrices §§6.2–6.5 for the scalar reduced-word free product, ⊞ and ⊠ with their R- and S-transforms, the jump-one free Poisson limit and the Weingarten formulas, and keep here only what goes beyond them: the C*- and von Neumann completions, faithfulness, general jumps and the statements without norm bounds. RandomMatrices on main already reads this way.
- Keep the objects here, and record that RandomMatrices Layer 6 is to cite these sections instead. The description would then name that revision of RandomMatrices.

## R02 (P2) — Dykema's theorem and the free Lévy–Khintchine theorem can be stated at the pins, but `Suggested.lean` omits them as "not statable"

These are two of the four headlines of the overview. Without them, the acceptance test checks nothing in Layers 3 and 6.

How I know: the module docstring (lines 25–28 and 36–39) gives one reason for both: the pins have no reduced free product, and no free product in which to realize ⊞. That shows the construction is missing, not that the theorems cannot be stated.

- **Dykema's theorem** has an equivalent intrinsic form. Take a unital C*-algebra A with a state φ whose GNS representation (Mathlib `PositiveLinearMap.gnsStarAlgHom`) is injective, generated by closed star subalgebras that are free for φ and on each of which φ is faithful. Such (A, φ) is the reduced free product of the restrictions, so the theorem says that φ is then faithful.
- **The free Lévy–Khintchine theorem** needs only the README's own definition of free infinite divisibility, read as an existence of free realizations. Its statement uses Tau Ceti's `PositiveLinearMap.law` and `TauCeti.freeCumulant`.

I elaborated both statements below against the pinned Mathlib and Tau Ceti. The file imported `Mathlib` and four Tau Ceti modules, used `open scoped ComplexOrder` and no `set_option`, and produced only the two `sorry` warnings:

## C02 — The hypotheses "faithful" and "tracial" in §1.2 are not used, and the non-tracial case is the one §3.7 needs

§1.2 (line 178) assumes a faithful normal tracial state. The route it gives uses only normality. Kaplansky density supplies bounded nets converging strongly. Multiplication is jointly strongly continuous on bounded sets. A normal state is weak-operator continuous on bounded sets, so recentring the approximants keeps them centred in the limit. §3.7 builds von Neumann free products from faithful normal states that need not be tracial, so it cannot use §1.2 as stated. The fix is to state §1.2 for a normal state.

## C03 — The Layer 7 example "a circular element and a Haar unitary are R-diagonal with equal determining sequences" can be read as a false claim

On line 1237 the sentence reads naturally as saying that the two elements share their sequences. They do not: α₂ = 0 for a circular element and α₂ = −1 for a Haar unitary (§7.4). The intended claim is presumably α = β for each element, which holds because both are tracial. The fix is to write "each R-diagonal with α = β".
