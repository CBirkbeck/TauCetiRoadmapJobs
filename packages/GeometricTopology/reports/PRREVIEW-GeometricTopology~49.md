## R02 (P3) — The tie-break Check of the reading lemma does not say which strand sits where, and in its natural reading puts the front strand of `σ_0` on the side that makes the closure crossing left-handed

**Location:** `README.md:2685–2690` ("the standard models of `σ_0` and `σ_0⁻¹` on two strands … at the crossing height their strands sit at `(0, 1)`, `(0, −1)` and at `(0, −1)`, `(0, 1)` respectively … their closures have writhes `+1` and `−1`").

**Claim:** this Check is the only place in the roadmap that gives the front/back convention of Artin.P a coordinate value, so it is the witness that pins the side (`:2463–2467` defines "front" only as the side that makes the closure of `σ_i` positive). It does not say which of the two listed points belongs to the strand leaving position `0`. In the natural reading (position order) the strand leaving position `0` passes in front at second coordinate `+1` for `σ_0`. With the roadmap's own orientation convention (`:189–191`: `ℝ³` with its standard orientation, a right-handed crossing counts `+1`) and the coordinates (first disc coordinate, second disc coordinate, height), that model's crossing is left-handed: a crossing's sign is the sign of `det[t_over, t_under, v]` with `v` pointing from the under-strand to the over-strand (the standard positive picture, over-strand `(1,1,0)`, under-strand `(−1,1,0)`, `v = (0,0,1)`, gives `+2`). For the Check's model, `t_A = (1, 0, 1)` (first coordinate increasing, over), `t_B = (−1, 0, 1)`, `v = (0, 2, 0)`, and `det = −4`. So the model listed first is `σ_0⁻¹`'s under the closure convention, and the Check as read contradicts `:2467` ("the closure of `σ_i` has a positive crossing"). With the points swapped (`v = (0, −2, 0)`) the determinant is `+4` and the Check is right. The Check's actual point, that both models have the same first-coordinate order at every height and that the straight-line homotopy between them collides at `(0, 0)`, holds either way.

**Witness:** `README.md:189–191` and `:2463–2467`; the two determinants above.

**Repair:** write the Check with the strands named and the side that the closure convention forces: "for `σ_0` the strand leaving position `0` passes at `(0, −1/2)` and the strand leaving position `1` at `(0, 1/2)` at the crossing height; for `σ_0⁻¹` the two second coordinates are exchanged; with `ℝ³` standard-oriented the first is right-handed (the determinant of the over-tangent `(1, 0, 1)`, the under-tangent `(−1, 0, 1)` and the vector `(0, −1, 0)` from the under-strand to the over-strand is `+2`), as the closure convention requires". Retest: the sign of `σ_0` agrees with `crossingSign_closure` (`+1` for the letter `(0, 1)`), and the mirror model gives `−2`.

## R03 (P3) — Two new Checks of Artin.P describe braids that are not in the carrier: strands on the side wall, and endpoints off the base configuration without straight collars

**Location:** `README.md:2687–2688` ("their strands sit at `(0, 1)`, `(0, −1)`"), and `:2696–2702` ("the two-strand braid whose strands have first coordinates `(t − 1/2)²` and `0` and distinct constant second coordinates").

**Claim:** the carrier of Artin.P is a disjoint union of smooth arcs in `int(D²)×[0,1]` with endpoint sets `P×{0}`, `P×{1}` for `P` on the horizontal diameter, straight in collars of the two faces (`:2446–2452`), and `D²` is the closed unit disc (`:441`, the homeomorphism of ReductiveGroups §11A onto the open unit disc). The points `(0, ±1)` lie on the boundary circle, not in `int(D²)`, so the "standard models" of the tie-break Check are not geometric braids of the carrier. The transversality example has strands at first coordinates `(t − 1/2)²` and `0` with distinct constant second coordinates: at `t = 0` and `t = 1` the two points are `(1/4, y₀)` and `(0, y₁)` with `y₀ ≠ y₁`, which is not a set `P` on the horizontal diameter, and `(t − 1/2)²` is not constant near either face, so the example is not a geometric braid either. Its mathematical point (a non-transverse double point of the projection that exchanges nothing) is right, and the Check's conclusion that the braid is the straight one with trivial permutation holds for a corrected example.

**Witness:** `README.md:2446–2452`, `:441`; `(1/2 − 1/2)² = 0 = ` the other strand's first coordinate at `t = 1/2` with derivative `2(t − 1/2) = 0` there (not transverse); `(0 − 1/2)² = 1/4 ≠ 0` at the ends.

**Repair:** scale the tie-break models into the disc (`(0, ±1/2)`, as in R02). For the transversality example take `P = {(0, 0), (1/4, 0)}`, strand `B` constant at `(0, 0)`, and strand `A` at `(1/4 − φ(t), ψ(t))` with `φ, ψ` smooth bumps vanishing on the collars, `φ(1/2) = 1/4`, `φ′(1/2) = 0`, `ψ(1/2) = 1/2`, `0 ≤ φ ≤ 1/4`, `0 ≤ ψ ≤ 1/2`: the projection has one double point at height `1/2`, non-transverse, the second coordinates differ there, and the braid is straight in the collars with endpoints `P`. Retest: `A` and `B` never coincide (`ψ(1/2) ≠ 0` where `φ = 1/4`), the first-coordinate order is the same at every height, and the straight-line homotopy to the straight braid is collision-free.
