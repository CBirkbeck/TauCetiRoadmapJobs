1. **F01** (P1) Noncompactness does not imply failure of reflexivity

   **Location:** README:285, §0.7: “which for non-compact G is neither of compact type nor reflexive.”

   **Claim:** The function space C^an(G,E) is never reflexive when G is noncompact.

   **Witness:** G=ℤ with its discrete zero-dimensional analytic structure: C^an(G,E)=E^ℤ, its strong dual is E^(ℤ), and its strong bidual is E^ℤ. Thus the claimed failure of reflexivity is false.

   **Exact repair and test:** Remove the blanket nonreflexivity assertion. The coordinatewise continuity argument needs reflexivity only for C^an(H,E) with H compact, followed by the universal property of the product; it needs no assertion about reflexivity of the whole product. Add the discrete-ℤ negative control. Explicitly, continuous forms on E^ℤ have finite support; bounded subsets of its strong dual have uniformly finite support and bounded coefficients. The bidual therefore has precisely the original product topology. Finite-coordinate seminorms `max_(|j|≤N) |x_j|` recover it. The repaired continuity proof gives the same continuous coordinate maps on this example.

2. **F02** (P2) A unipotent orbit does not reach every highest-weight line

   **Location:** README:431, §1.6: “any nonzero vector can be moved by a small unipotent subgroup into E_λ ⊗ π^U”.

   **Claim:** Acting by the subgroup moves a nonzero vector into the indicated highest-weight subspace.

   **Witness:** Take W=Sym²(E²), π=1 and v=e₁e₂. Every upper unipotent u(t) gives u(t)v=e₁e₂+t e₁²; the e₁e₂ coefficient remains 1, so no such translate is highest weight.

   **Exact repair and test:** Say that the E[U]-submodule generated by v contains a nonzero vector of E_λ⊗π^U, after shrinking U to fix the finitely many smooth coefficients. Cite the successive-difference argument in ST01 lines 519–558, Proposition 3.4, p. 13. This is a linear combination of translates, not a single translate. Add the negative control above: `(u(t)−1)v=t e₁²≠0` for t≠0, including t in an arbitrarily small subgroup and at p=2. The replacement proof step succeeds on the instance that refutes the original wording.

3. **F03** (P2) The endpoint category O needs split Cartan weights

   **Location:** README:807, §4.2(ii): “O^g consists of the finite-dimensional semisimple 𝔤-modules”.

   **Claim:** Every finite-dimensional semisimple module of the split reductive Lie algebra is an object of O^g over the stated arbitrary characteristic-zero field k.

   **Witness:** Let k=ℚ₃ and 𝔤=𝔥=Lie(𝔾_m). Let its generator act on k² by `[[0,3],[1,0]]`. This is the simple k[t]-module k[t]/(t²−3), but it has no k-valued weight.

   **Exact repair and test:** Replace the endpoint description by “the finite-dimensional semisimple 𝔤-modules whose restriction to 𝔥 splits into k-valued weight spaces.” Keep O^p as a subcategory of O. Add this example to the Checks, and distinguish semisimplicity from split semisimplicity in the source correction. A root of t²−3 would have valuation 1/2, impossible in ℚ₃; the sum of k-weight spaces has dimension 0 instead of 2. The corrected endpoint excludes it and includes the two one-dimensional summands after extending to ℚ₃(√3). For 𝔤=𝔥, the corrected category consists exactly of finite sums of one-dimensional weight modules. OS15 lines 315–325 has the same coefficient-field omission; its finite-extension convention is explicit at OS15 lines 146–154.

4. **F04** (P2) The solid comparison uses functions with the wrong group action

   **Location:** README:734, §3.8: “O_(𝔾_r) restricted to M_0 is the finite induction from M_r”. The same paragraph asserts flatness over E_■[M_0].

   **Claim:** Functions on a shrinking polydisc subgroup carry the translation action of the larger compact group and equal the induced function space; its distribution algebra can then be used over that larger group's Iwasawa algebra.

   **Witness:** M₀=ℤ₂ and M_r=2ℤ₂. Translation by 1 sends 0 outside the smaller disc. Functions on one disc have no nontrivial idempotent; induction has two disc components and the idempotent (1,0).

   **Exact repair and test:** For (ii), work with M_r throughout: compare solid M_r-cohomology with continuous M_r-cohomology of the fixed-radius Banach analytic functions tensored with V, then take the indicated colimit. Do not apply Shapiro to the functions of just one smaller disc. Alternatively explicitly induce that space, obtaining the disjoint union of its M₀-coset translates. For (iii), use §2.5 after the comparison, or introduce the actual full-point-group analytic neighborhoods and h+ distribution stages of RJRC22 Proposition 4.48. RJRC22 lines 138–156 requires a finite union of polydiscs with the full fixed rational-point group; lines 2065–2079 use D^(h+)(G,E), not the distribution algebra of a smaller rational-point subgroup. Add the ℤ₂/2ℤ₂ domain and idempotent Check. The induced repair gives E⟨T⟩×E⟨T⟩ and a valid ℤ₂ action; the single-disc repair uses only 2ℤ₂ and also has a valid action. No new acyclicity theorem is required.
