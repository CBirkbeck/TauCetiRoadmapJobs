# Roadmap: inert anticyclotomic CM main conjecture

**Summary.** This roadmap derives the global arithmetic of a CM elliptic curve at a prime p≥5 inert in its CM field from Rubin's integral signed decomposition of anticyclotomic local units, which AnticyclotomicLocalUnits proves. Starting from a Gross Q-curve E over the Hilbert class field H of an imaginary quadratic field K, with p∤h_K and coefficients in the ring of integers R of a finite extension of K_p, it transports the signed formal-point Kummer images to the anticyclotomic tower and cuts out signed, strict and relaxed Selmer groups. It then defines Rubin's integral anticyclotomic p-adic L-function as the coordinate of the primitive elliptic-unit class in a signed basis, and compares the compact and discrete Selmer modules through global duality. The end theorems are BKO21 Theorem 6.1, that the Pontryagin dual of the signed Selmer group whose sign is opposite to the root number is a finitely generated torsion Λ_R-module with characteristic ideal generated by Rubin's p-adic L-function, integrally over Λ_R, and BKO21 Theorem 6.2, the finite-character bound for the χ-part of the Mordell–Weil and Selmer ranks by the order of vanishing of that L-function at χ, with one extra dimension in the opposite parity, when E is defined over K.

## Conventions

Fix a prime p≥5 and an imaginary quadratic field K in which p is inert and p∤h_K; H is the Hilbert class field of K. Fix embeddings ι_∞:ℚ̄→ℂ and ι_p:ℚ̄→ℂ_p. Since (p) is principal its Artin symbol in Gal(H/K) is trivial, so p splits completely in H; let 𝔭 be the place of H above p selected by ι_p, so that H_𝔭=K_p.

A *Gross datum* consists of an algebraic Hecke character φ of K of infinity type (1,0), with conductor f prime to p, φ((α))=α for α≡1 modulo f, and φ(ā)=conjugate(φ(a)); a CM elliptic curve E over H with End_H(E)=O_K whose Hecke character is φ∘N_(H/K) (a Q-curve in the sense of Gross), with good reduction at every place above p; a Weierstrass model of E over the integers of H smooth at 𝔭 with invariant differential ω; and Ω∈ℂ× whose multiple ΩO_K is the period lattice of (E,ω) (NonordinaryCMSpecialValues NCM.0/cm-period; ComplexMultiplicationAndExplicitReciprocity §4.3). Put ε=W(φ)∈{+1,−1}, the root number of L(φ,s), identified with the sign ε∈{+,−}.

Let Φ=K_p, the unramified quadratic extension of ℚ_p, O its ring of integers, π=−p, and F the Lubin–Tate formal O-module for π, with normalised logarithm λ, as in AnticyclotomicLocalUnits (Conventions). Let L/Φ be a finite extension inside ℂ_p containing the values of the p-adic avatar φ_p:G_K→L×, R its ring of integers, T=R(φ_p) the free rank-one R-module on which G_K acts by φ_p, V_T=T⊗_R L and W=V_T/T=T⊗_O Φ/O. Write T′=Hom_(ℤ_p)(W,μ_(p^∞)) for the Cartier-dual lattice; under the coefficient trace it is the inverse-different twist of Hom_R(T,R)(1), and it is never identified with T without a stated comparison (SelmerIwasawaCohomology §2.13).

Let K∞/K be the anticyclotomic ℤ_p-extension, K_n its layer of degree pⁿ, Γ⁻=Gal(K∞/K) with topological generator γ, Γ_n=Γ⁻^(pⁿ), and K̃∞/K the ℤ_p²-extension with Γ=Gal(K̃∞/K)=Γ⁺×Γ⁻, the eigenspaces of complex conjugation. Put Λ_R=R⟦Γ⁻⟧, identified with R⟦T⟧ by γ↦1+T (PadicMeasuresIwasawaAlgebras §4.5), and Λ_(2)=R⟦Γ⟧. Λ_R is a two-dimensional regular local ring, hence a noetherian normal unique factorisation domain, and characteristic ideals over it are those of PadicMeasuresIwasawaAlgebras §4.6; every characteristic-ideal identity in this roadmap is integral and retains the prime (π).

For a finite continuous character χ:Γ⁻→ℚ̄_p×, its conductor exponent is c(χ)=0 if χ=1 and c(χ)=r+1 if χ has exact order p^r, r≥1; Ξ⁺ and Ξ⁻ are the characters of even and odd conductor exponent (AnticyclotomicLocalUnits §0.1). A character of exact order p^r factors through Gal(K_n/K) for every n≥r; the source's indexing by the conductor uses the deeper layer n=c(χ). Unit signs kill the opposite parity and point signs kill the same parity:

| Character at p=5 | c(χ) | Sign | Signed unit module killing χ | Signed point module killing χ |
|---|---:|---|---|---|
| trivial | 0 | + | minus | plus |
| exact order 5 | 2 | + | minus | plus |
| exact order 25 | 3 | − | plus | minus |

Agboola–Howard index characters by the parity of their order and place the trivial character in their minus family (AHII05 §2, p.613, and Remark 3.2, p.614): their set of sign s is Ξ^(−s) here. Their point spaces, signed local conditions and signed Selmer groups of label s kill their character set of sign −s, which is Ξ^s here, so these objects carry the same label in both papers. Only character-set labels are translated; the label of X_(−ε) is not.

The derivatives δ and δ_χ on V∞* are those of AnticyclotomicLocalUnits §§1.2–1.4, with δ_χ(r·x)=r(χ⁻¹)δ_χ(x) for r∈Λ_O and δ_1=(1+p⁻¹)δ. They extend R-linearly to V∞*⊗_O R with values in ℂ_p through ι_p. The Pontryagin dual of a discrete module carries the contragredient action (γf)(x)=f(γ⁻¹x) and (rf)(x)=f(rx) (SelmerIwasawaCohomology §2.10), so the χ-part of a discrete module corresponds to the χ⁻¹-part of its dual. For f∈Λ_R and a finite character ρ with values in a finite extension L_ρ/L, ord_ρ f is the order of vanishing of f(T) at T=ρ(γ)−1 in the open unit disc of ℂ_p, equivalently the multiplicity of the height-one prime P_ρ=ker(ρ:Λ_R→L_ρ) in (f).

The finite-level rank and control statements of Layers 5–6 make one further assumption, displayed at each target: E is defined over K, and V_T is the CM component of V_pE in the fixed embedding. Then End_K(E)=O_K forces K(j(E))=H to equal K, so h_K=1, H=K, φ is the Hecke character of E/K and R=O may be taken. Character values lie in a finite extension L_χ/L and every dimension is over L_χ. The main-conjecture statements of Layers 0–4 do not assume this.

Native carriers use their library universes; fields, towers and character fields are taken inside the fixed closures, and every inverse or direct limit is over the countable cofinal system of layers. Natural-number subtraction is never evaluated below zero: the least layer of the trivial character is K_0 by definition, not c−1.

## How to read the build

Layer 0 fixes the arithmetic objects: the Gross datum, the identification of global and local towers, the conductor and sign dictionary, the local discrete module with its dual lattice, and the R-extended signed unit modules. Layer 1 transports the signed formal-point Kummer images to the global tower and cuts out the strict, signed, ordinary and relaxed Selmer groups with their compact and discrete limits. Layer 2 specialises the early CM class at trivial auxiliary character, places it in its signed line and defines Rubin's integral p-adic L-function as its coordinate. Layer 3 builds the all-auxiliary global elliptic-unit module, identifies compact local cohomology with the signed local-unit module and proves the global ranks and the strict comparison from Rubin's two-variable theorem. Layer 4 runs global duality to opposite signed torsion and the signed characteristic formula, and compares the localised global lattice with the primitive coordinate, proving the main conjecture. Layers 5–6 prove signed and strict control at finite level and the finite-character rank bounds. Each *Needs:* line lists what its statement and its indicated route use, so an implementer who proves a target another way is not blocked behind an unrelated layer; in particular the main conjecture does not depend on §4.3.

## Layer 0: The inert CM datum and the tower dictionary

All targets use the standing hypotheses of the Conventions: p≥5 inert in K, p∤h_K, a Gross datum over H and the coefficient ring R. Nothing in this layer assumes that E descends to K or that 2 splits in K.

### 0.1 The inert Gross CM datum

Define an inert CM datum as a Gross datum (φ,E,model,ω,Ω) together with the place 𝔭 of H, the coefficient ring R containing the values of φ_p, the avatar lattice T=R(φ_p) with a fixed G_H-equivariant identification of T with T_πE⊗_O R in the chosen CM embedding, W=T⊗_O Φ/O, Λ_R and ε=W(φ). It is a record of data on the supplied carriers, not a new theory of CM curves or Hecke characters. Coefficient extension along a finite local map R→R′ replaces T by T⊗_R R′, W by T⊗_R R′⊗_O Φ/O and Λ_R by Λ_(R′)=Λ_R⊗̂_R R′ (BKO21 §3.0.2, pp.951–952, and §6, pp.960–961).

A route is as follows. Take the character, curve, model, differential and period from NonordinaryCMSpecialValues NCM.0/cm-period and ComplexMultiplicationAndExplicitReciprocity §§4.1, 4.3, recording them rather than inferring a descent of E to K. Form R(φ_p) on the supplied rank-one Tate component of §4.1 and compare its quotient with T⊗_O Φ/O by flatness of R over O.

Its API consists of the following facts on these carriers and with these hypotheses.

- An inert CM datum is assembled from a supplied Gross realization together with the explicit smooth model, differential and period comparison; no splitting hypothesis on p or on 2 is an argument.
- Restricted to G_H, T is T_πE⊗_O R in the chosen CM embedding.
- Coefficient extension along R→R′ preserves the CM representation; the identity extension is the identity and the extension along a composite R→R′→R″ is the composite of the two extensions.
- Replacing ω by aω with a∈H× replaces Ω by aΩ; an integral comparison treats a as a unit only when both differentials are bases of the same smooth integral model at 𝔭.

**Checks.**

- For K=ℚ(√−23), the reduced forms of discriminant −23 are (1,1,6),(2,1,3),(2,−1,3), so h_K=3, and −23≡2 modulo 5 is not a square modulo 5, so p=5 is inert and 5∤3. A datum over this K, with E defined over the cubic field H and not over K, is admitted; an extra class-number-one condition would wrongly reject it.
- An ordinary-reduction hypothesis (p split in K) cannot be an argument: for p inert the CM curve has supersingular reduction at 𝔭 and Frobenius φ((p))=−p (NonordinaryCMSpecialValues NCM.2/inert-frobenius), whose trace over ℚ is 0.
- After R→R′ the discrete module is T⊗_R R′⊗_O Φ/O, of R′-corank one. The quotient (T⊗_R L′)/T of the larger vector space by the old lattice is not torsion when L′≠L, since it surjects onto the nonzero L-vector space (T⊗_R L′)/(T⊗_R L), so it cannot be the discrete module.

*Needs:* NonordinaryCMSpecialValues NCM.0/cm-period, NCM.2/inert-frobenius; ComplexMultiplicationAndExplicitReciprocity §§4.1, 4.3; TauCetiRoadmap.EllipticCurves Layer 1.

### 0.2 Global and local anticyclotomic towers

The completion of K_n at the unique place above 𝔭 is identified with Ψ_n over Φ, compatibly with the inclusions K_n⊂K_(n+1) and Ψ_n⊂Ψ_(n+1), with the Galois actions, with norms and traces, and hence with restriction and corestriction of cohomology. A route is as follows. Because (p) is principal, p splits completely in H, so H_𝔭=Φ. Identify the degree-pⁿ quotients and transport the transition maps by functoriality of the Artin map.

**Checks.**

- At n=0 both sides are Φ: K_0=K and Ψ_0=Φ.
- [Ψ_1:Φ]=p, which is 5 at p=5, while the first tame degree [Φ(F[π]):Φ]=p²−1 is 24; an identification of K_1 with the π-division field would compare fields of different degree.

### 0.3 Conductor and sign dictionary

A character χ of Γ⁻ of exact order p^r, r≥1, factors exactly through Gal(K_r/K) and has conductor exponent c(χ)=r+1; the trivial character has c=0 and factors through Gal(K_0/K). For every n≥r the character factors through Gal(K_n/K). Agboola–Howard's sign-s set is Ξ^(−s), and their signed point spaces, signed local conditions and signed Selmer groups of label s coincide with the objects of label s defined here (AHII05 §2, p.613, and Remark 3.2, p.614; BKO21 §2, p.948, and §6.0.2, p.962).

A route is as follows. Use the conductor filtration of AnticyclotomicLocalUnits §0.1 transported by §0.2. Agboola–Howard's minus set consists of the trivial character and the characters of exact order p^k with k odd, that is c=k+1 even or c=0; their plus set consists of exact orders p^k with k≥2 even, that is c odd. Their point space of label s kills their set of sign −s, which is Ξ^s, as does A^s. Inversion preserves the order and the conductor.

**Checks.**

- The trivial character, a character of exact order p and one of exact order p² have conductor exponents 0, 2 and 3 and signs +, + and −. Using the parity of the order instead gives +, −, + for the order exponents 0, 1, 2 and the wrong sign at order p.
- The trivial character lies in Agboola–Howard's minus set and in Ξ⁺ here; their nontrivial root-number formula (3.1) is not applied at r=0.
- A character of exact order p² factors through Gal(K_2/K) and through Gal(K_3/K), the source's indexing by the conductor; it does not factor through Gal(K_1/K). Requiring the degree-p^c layer to be the least field of definition would be false.

### 0.4 The local discrete CM module and its dual lattice

The chosen CM comparison gives an R[G_Φ]-isomorphism W|_(G_Φ)≃F[π^∞]⊗_O R, natural under finite coefficient extension. The Cartier-dual lattice T′=Hom_(ℤ_p)(W,μ_(p^∞)) is identified, through the coefficient trace Tr_(L/ℚ_p), with the twist of Hom_R(T,R)(1) by the inverse different of R over ℤ_p; changing the trivialisation of the inverse different changes this identification by a unit of R. The local Tate pairing H¹(Ψ_n,T′)×H¹(Ψ_n,W)→ℚ_p/ℤ_p is perfect (BKO21 §6.0.2, pp.961–962; AHII05 §2, p.613, and Proposition 3.4 with (3.2), p.615).

A route is as follows. Restrict T to G_H, use H_𝔭=Φ and NonordinaryCMSpecialValues NCM.2/cm-lubin-tate to identify the formal group of the good model with F over O, then pass from T to the union of T/π^kT. Its API consists of the following facts on these carriers and with these hypotheses.

- W restricted to G_Φ is F[π^∞]⊗_O R, compatibly with coefficient extension.
- T′ is the Cartier dual of W; under the trace it is the inverse-different twist of Hom_R(T,R)(1).
- A change of trace-dual basis changes the pairing maps by a unit of R.

**Checks.**

- For R=O unramified over ℤ_p the inverse different is O itself and T′≃Hom_O(T,O)(1). For R=O[√π], ramified of index 2 over O, the different over ℤ_p is generated by 2√π, the inverse different is (√π)⁻¹R, and (√π)⁻¹R/R≃R/√πR has p² elements; identifying T′ with Hom_R(T,R)(1) without the twist is off by this module, the obstruction recorded in SelmerIwasawaCohomology §2.13.

### 0.5 Signed decomposition over the CM coefficient ring

### 1.2 Independence of the local CM comparison

### 1.3 Rational local vanishing away from p

For every n≥0 and every finite place v of K_n not above p, H¹((K_n)_v,V_T)=0. Consequently the finite condition H¹_f((K_n)_v,W), the image of H¹_f((K_n)_v,V_T)=0, is zero. The group H¹((K_n)_v,W) itself is finite and need not vanish (AHI06 Lemma 1.1.4 and the sentence after it, p.1006; BKO21 §6.0.2, p.962).

A route is as follows. E has integral j-invariant and so potentially good reduction at v; pass to a finite extension of (K_n)_v over which it has good reduction, where restriction is injective on rational cohomology because corestriction after restriction is multiplication by the degree (ArithmeticGaloisDuality §R02.2.3). There inertia acts trivially on V_T and the arithmetic Frobenius acts by an eigenvalue of complex absolute value √q (ComplexMultiplicationAndExplicitReciprocity §4.1, `cmFrobeniusIdentities`), so neither V_T nor its Tate dual V_T^∨(1) has invariants. Local duality gives H²=0 and the local Euler characteristic formula for residue characteristic different from p gives H¹=0.

**Checks.**

- At a good place v with q=Nv, the invariants of V_T are zero because the Frobenius eigenvalue α has |α|=√q≠1, and those of V_T^∨(1) are zero because q/α has absolute value √q≠1; at q=4, for instance, √q=2.
- The integral group need not vanish. At a good place v, W is unramified and divisible, so H¹ of the unramified quotient vanishes and H¹((K_n)_v,W) is the group of Frobenius invariants of W(−1); it is nonzero exactly when α≡q modulo the maximal ideal of R, where α is the Frobenius eigenvalue on T and q=Nv. The statement asserts vanishing only rationally.
- At a bad place where E acquires good reduction over an extension of degree e, the argument uses injectivity of restriction on V_T-coefficients, valid because e is invertible in ℚ_p; on W-coefficients restriction can have kernel of exponent dividing e, so no integral vanishing is claimed.

### 1.4 The four global Selmer conditions

- For χ of conductor exponent 2 the local L-factor of φ̄χ at p is 1, so a formula retaining the factor 1+p⁻¹ at nontrivial χ is wrong by 6/5 at p=5.

### 2.3 Sign and nonvanishing of the primitive class

ξ∈V_R^ε and ξ≠0, so Λ_Rξ is free of rank one. More precisely δ_χ(ξ)=0 for every χ∈Ξ^(−ε), and δ_χ(ξ)≠0 for all but finitely many χ∈Ξ^ε. ξ need not be a basis of V_R^ε (BKO21 §6.0.1, p.961; AHII05 Proposition 3.1 and Remark 3.2, p.614).

A route is as follows. For χ∈Ξ^ε the root number is +1 and NonordinaryCMSpecialValues NCM.5/central-nonvanishing gives L(φ̄χ,1)≠0 for all but finitely many χ, so ξ≠0 by §2.2; Λ_R is a domain and V_R^ε is free of rank one.

**Checks.**

- For ε=− the trivial derivative δ(ξ)=L(φ̄,1)/Ω vanishes by the sign of the functional equation; the nontrivial formula (−1)^c ε would give (−1)^0·(−1)=−1 as well, but it is not used at c=0.

### 2.4 Rubin's integral anticyclotomic p-adic L-function

Choose a Λ_R-basis v_ε of V_R^ε. Define C_∞=Λ_Rξ⊂V_R^ε and Rubin's p-adic L-function L_p=L_p(φ,Ω,v_ε)∈Λ_R by the unique equality ξ=L_p·v_ε. Then C_∞ is free of rank one and V_R^ε/C_∞≃Λ_R/(L_p). For u∈Λ_R× the basis uv_ε gives the coordinate u⁻¹L_p; the principal ideal (L_p) does not depend on the basis (BKO21 §6.0.1, p.961).

A route is as follows. Take the coordinate of ξ in the basis v_ε (`Module.Basis`). The quotient isomorphism is the basis isomorphism V_R^ε≃Λ_R followed by the quotient by L_p. Unit changes of basis and of differential preserve (L_p) by `Ideal.span_singleton_mul_left_unit`. Integrality comes from ξ∈V_R and v_ε a basis of V_R^ε, not from interpolation.

Its API consists of the following facts on these carriers and with these hypotheses.

- L_p is the coordinate of ξ in the basis v_ε, and ξ=L_p·v_ε is the unique expression of ξ in that basis.
- For u∈Λ_R×, the coordinate of ξ in the basis uv_ε is u⁻¹L_p, and both generate the same principal ideal.
- The basis induces an isomorphism V_R^ε/C_∞≃Λ_R/(L_p) of Λ_R-modules.

**Checks.**

- For ξ=a·v and the basis v′=u·v, the coordinate is u⁻¹a; with a=T and u=1+T this is T/(1+T), which generates (T).
- If a∈R⟦T⟧ does not have coefficients in O, the coordinate lies in Λ_R and is not replaced by an element of Λ_O; for R ramified over O of degree e, the coordinate π_R of π_R·v has (π_R)^e=(π) up to a unit.

### 2.5 Interpolation

For every nontrivial χ∈Ξ^ε, δ_(χ⁻¹)(v_ε)≠0 and

    L_p(χ) = L(conjugate(φχ),1) / (Ω·δ_(χ⁻¹)(v_ε)),

where L_p(χ) is the specialisation of L_p at γ↦χ(γ). Hence L_p(χ)≠0 exactly when L(conjugate(φχ),1)≠0. At χ=1 and ε=+, δ(ξ)=L_p(1)δ(v_+) with δ(v_+) a unit of R. At χ=1 and ε=−, δ(ξ)=δ(v_−)=0 and this relation does not compute L_p(1) (BKO21 §6.0.1, p.961, and Corollary 2.2, p.949).

A route is as follows. Apply δ_(χ⁻¹) to ξ=L_p·v_ε, using δ_(χ⁻¹)(a·v)=a(χ)δ_(χ⁻¹)(v) from the inverse covariance of the derivatives. Use χ⁻¹=χ̄ for finite characters to rewrite φ̄χ⁻¹ as conjugate(φχ) in §2.2. The character χ⁻¹ lies in Ξ^ε because it has the same conductor, so §0.5 gives δ_(χ⁻¹)(v_ε)≠0.

**Checks.**

- With a=γ, δ_(χ⁻¹)(γ·v)=χ(γ)δ_(χ⁻¹)(v)=ζδ_(χ⁻¹)(v) for χ(γ)=ζ; locating the specialisation at χ⁻¹ instead gives ζ⁻¹, which differs from ζ for χ nontrivial (ζ⁴≠ζ for ζ of order 5).
- At ε=− no quotient by δ(v_−)=0 is formed, and nothing follows about L_p(1): in the lattice model ξ=πv_− of the worked examples, L_p=π has L_p(1)=π≠0 although δ(ξ)=0.
- For χ∈Ξ^(−ε) the formula is not asserted: both δ_(χ⁻¹)(ξ) and δ_(χ⁻¹)(v_ε) vanish.

*Needs:* §2.4; §2.2; §0.5; AnticyclotomicLocalUnits §§1.3, 8.4.

### 4.2 Opposite signed torsion

S_ε has rank one, S_(−ε)=S_str=0, X_ε has rank one and X_(−ε) is a finitely generated torsion Λ_R-module. Moreover S_ε=S_rel. The vanishing assertions are equalities of compact modules (AHII05 Theorem 3.6, p.616, and the proof of Theorem 4.2, p.617; BKO21 Theorem 2.1, p.949).

A route is as follows. C⊂S_ε is nonzero (§§2.3, 3.1), so S_ε has rank one inside the rank-one S_rel. So S_(−ε) and S_str have rank zero and vanish in the torsion-free S_rel. The ranks of X_s follow from §4.1. Finally S_rel/S_ε is torsion and injects into H/H^ε, free of rank one by §3.3, so it vanishes.

**Checks.**

- The nonzero localised elliptic-unit class excludes a strict submodule of rank one: if S_str had rank one, S_rel/S_str would be a torsion module injecting into H, hence zero, and every class of S_rel would localise to zero.
- The sign is that of the primitive class: for ε=+ the torsion dual is X_−, and for ε=− it is X_+; interchanging them contradicts the rank one of X_ε given by §4.1.
- Without the integral splitting of §3.3 one only knows that S_rel/S_ε injects into H/H^ε, which need not be torsion-free, so S_ε=S_rel would not follow.

### 4.4 The opposite signed characteristic ideal

The following two identities hold over Λ_R.

1. char((X_ε)_tors)·char(H^ε/S_ε)=char(S_rel/C).
2. char(X_(−ε))=char(H^ε/loc(C)), where loc is localisation at 𝔭, injective on S_ε because S_str=0.

(AHII05 Theorem 4.2 with (4.1)–(4.4), pp.616–617.)

A route is as follows. For the second identity, which is the one the main conjecture uses, §4.1 with s=−ε, S_(−ε)=0, S_ε=S_rel and H/H^(−ε)≃H^ε (§§3.3, 4.2) gives 0→H^ε/S_ε→X_(−ε)→X_str→0, and there is an exact sequence 0→S_ε/C→H^ε/loc(C)→H^ε/S_ε→0. Multiplicativity of characteristic ideals and §3.5 give char(X_(−ε))=char(H^ε/S_ε)·char(S_rel/C)=char(H^ε/loc(C)); every module here is torsion. For the first identity, §4.1 with s=ε and S_str=0 gives 0→S_ε→H^ε→X_rel→X_ε→0; since H^ε/S_ε is torsion, it yields 0→H^ε/S_ε→(X_rel)_tors→(X_ε)_tors→0, and §§4.3 and 3.5 conclude.

**Checks.**

- In the model H^ε=Λ_R, S_ε=S_rel=(T)⊂Λ_R, C=(T²) and X_str with char (T): the second identity gives char(X_(−ε))=(T)·(T)=(T²)=char(Λ_R/(T²)), consistent with the two exact sequences; replacing loc(C) by S_ε would give (T) instead.
- Every height-one prime is counted, including (π): with C=πS_ε the factor char(S_ε/C)=(π) appears in char(X_(−ε)) and is lost by any identity proved after inverting p.
- The second identity uses §3.5 and not §4.3; the first uses both. A route through §4.3 is not needed for the main conjecture.

*Needs:* §4.2; §3.5; §3.3; PadicMeasuresIwasawaAlgebras §4.6 (`charIdeal_mul_of_exact`); for identity 1 also §4.3.

### 4.5 The localised global lattice and the primitive class

Under H^ε≃V_R^ε of §3.3, char(H^ε/loc(C))=(L_p(φ,Ω,v_ε)); equivalently, the coordinates of loc(C) in the basis v_ε generate the ideal (L_p). The comparison accounts for every auxiliary ideal, for the primitive and imprimitive normalisations, for the CM period and the trace-dual bases, and for the inversion of characters; an equality after tensoring with L is not sufficient (AHII05 Theorem 4.3 and the paragraph after it, p.617; BKO21 §6.0.1, p.961, and the proof of Theorem 6.1, p.962).

A route is as follows. Transport the localised classes loc(c_a) through §3.3 and compute their coordinates by explicit reciprocity (AnticyclotomicLocalUnits §4.3; NonordinaryCMSpecialValues NCM.1/elliptic-reciprocity): each is L_p times the image of the distribution factor of a, up to the period and trace units of §§0.1, 0.4. Show that these distribution factors generate the unit ideal of Λ_R after the φ-projection, or retain the precise correction divisor they generate. Compare the primitive ν=1 normalisation of §2.1 without dividing by a nonunit. Translate Agboola–Howard's direct-character formula to the inverse-character convention of §2.5. Only after this integral lattice comparison may infinitely many matching characters be used, since an equality in the fraction field could hide a power of π.

**Checks.**

- Restriction of scalars from Λ_R to Λ_O multiplies degrees: for R/O of degree d and f with coefficients in O, the Λ_O-characteristic ideal of Λ_R/(f) is (f^d), not (f).
- Agboola–Howard's interpolation pairs χ with δ_χ(v_ε), the one used here pairs χ with δ_(χ⁻¹)(v_ε); for a character of order 5 these evaluate the generator at ζ and at ζ⁴, which differ unless the generator is invariant under the involution.

### 4.6 The inert anticyclotomic CM main conjecture

For an inert CM datum, X_(−ε) is a finitely generated torsion Λ_R-module and char_(Λ_R)(X_(−ε))=(L_p(φ,Ω,v_ε)) as an integral ideal of Λ_R. The theorem allows E to be defined only over H, with p≥5 inert in K, p∤h_K and the standing CM and good-reduction conditions; it does not assume that E descends to K or that 2 splits in K. The ideal is unchanged by unit changes of v_ε and of the integral differential, and compatible finite coefficient extension R→R′ carries the identity over Λ_R to the identity over Λ_(R′). Restriction of scalars to Λ_O replaces (L_p) by its norm (BKO21 Theorem 6.1 and its proof, p.962).

A route is as follows. Combine §4.4(2) with §4.5. Keep Λ_R fixed through both identifications, including at the prime (π). Base change uses PadicMeasuresIwasawaAlgebras §4.6's base change along finite free O→O′, transported to R→R′, and restriction of scalars uses the supplier contract below.

**Checks.**

- The torsion module is X_− when ε=+ and X_+ when ε=−; the module X_ε has rank one by §4.2 and has no characteristic ideal in this sense.
- For R/O of degree d and L_p with coefficients in O, restriction of scalars gives the ideal (L_p^d), which at d=2 and L_p=T is (T²); an unchanged ideal (T) would be wrong.
- The ideal (L_p) contains the factor (π) whenever ξ∈πV_R^ε, as in the lattice model ξ=πv_ε of the worked examples, where X_(−ε) then has characteristic ideal divisible by (π) and positive μ-invariant.

*Needs:* §4.4; §4.5; §4.2; PadicMeasuresIwasawaAlgebras §4.6 (gap:restriction-of-scalars-finite-extension).

## Layer 5: Finite-level signed and strict control

The targets of §§5.2–5.4 and 5.6 assume in addition that E is defined over K and that V_T is the CM component of V_pE in the fixed embedding (Conventions); then h_K=1 and R=O may be taken. Character values lie in a finite extension L_χ/L and every dimension is over L_χ. Sections 5.1 and 5.5 are algebraic and need no such hypothesis.

### 5.1 Signed control elements

For n≥0 define

    ω_n⁺ = ∏_(1≤k≤n, k even) Φ_(p^k)(γ),    ω_n⁻ = (γ−1)·∏_(1≤k≤n, k odd) Φ_(p^k)(γ)

in Λ_R, where Φ_m is the m-th cyclotomic polynomial (`Polynomial.cyclotomic`). Then ω_n⁺ω_n⁻=γ^(pⁿ)−1. For a character χ of Γ⁻ factoring through Γ⁻/Γ_n, χ(ω_n^s)=0 exactly when χ∈Ξ^(−s). Consequently ω_n^s annihilates A_n^s, and ω_n^(−s) carries the ordinary formal points F(Ψ_n) into A_n^s and the ordinary Selmer group Sel(K_n,W) into Sel_s(K_n,W) (AHII05 §5, p.618, definitions and (5.1)).

A route is as follows. The product formula is the factorisation X^(pⁿ)−1=∏_(0≤k≤n)Φ_(p^k)(X) (`Polynomial.cyclotomic_prime_pow_mul_X_pow_sub_one` and induction; PadicMeasuresIwasawaAlgebras §4.3), with Φ_1(X)=X−1. A character of exact order p^k, k≥1, sends γ to a root of Φ_(p^k) and has c=k+1, so the roots of ω_n⁺ are the characters with c odd, Ξ⁻, and those of ω_n⁻ are the trivial character and c even, Ξ⁺. On points use λ_χ(f·y)=f(χ)λ_χ(y) and injectivity of λ on the torsion-free F(Ψ_n); transfer to Selmer groups by equivariance of localisation.

Its API consists of the following facts on these carriers and with these hypotheses.

- ω_n^s is the displayed product in Λ_R for sign s and level n.
- ω_n⁺ω_n⁻=γ^(pⁿ)−1 for every n≥0.
- For χ factoring through Γ⁻/Γ_n, χ(ω_n^s)=0 if and only if χ∈Ξ^(−s).
- ω_n^s·A_n^s=0, ω_n^(−s)·F(Ψ_n)⊆A_n^s and ω_n^(−s)·Sel(K_n,W)⊆Sel_s(K_n,W).

**Checks.**

- At n=0 the plus product is empty, so ω_0⁺=1, and ω_0⁻=γ−1; their product is γ−1=γ^(p⁰)−1. Exchanging them would make ω_0⁺ vanish at the trivial character, which lies in Ξ⁺, contradicting the zero-set law.
- At p=5 and n=2, ω_2⁺=Φ_25(γ) and ω_2⁻=(γ−1)Φ_5(γ), with degrees 20 and 5 adding to 25; a character of order 5 kills ω_2⁻ only, and one of order 25 kills ω_2⁺ only.
- The two elements have no common zero in characteristic zero but need not generate the unit ideal of Λ_R: at n=1, ω_1⁺=1 and the ideal is the unit ideal, while at n=2 the ideal (Φ_25(γ),(γ−1)Φ_5(γ)) contains a power of p, through the resultants Φ_25(1)=5 and Res(Φ_25,Φ_5), and is contained in (p,T), since all three polynomials reduce to powers of T modulo p.

*Needs:* §0.3; §1.1; §1.4; AnticyclotomicLocalUnits §3.1; PadicMeasuresIwasawaAlgebras §4.3; `Polynomial.cyclotomic`; `Polynomial.cyclotomic_prime_pow_mul_X_pow_sub_one`.

### 5.2 Signed local control

Assume E is defined over K. For s∈{+,−} and n≥0 the restriction map L_n^s→L_∞^s[ω_n^s] is injective with finite cokernel. All statements use the Kummer images of §1.1 and the coefficient ring R (AHII05 Lemma 5.1 and its proof, pp.618–619).

A route is as follows. H⁰(Ψ∞,W)=0 by the tame argument of §3.3; coefficient extension creates no invariants. Inflation–restriction gives injectivity. The R-corank of L_n^s is the R-rank of Λ_R/ω_n^s, by the logarithm and the normal basis theorem (`IsGalois.normalBasis`) applied to F(Ψ_n)⊗Φ≃Φ[Gal(Ψ_n/Φ)]. The R-corank of L_∞^s[ω_n^s] is also the R-rank of Λ_R/ω_n^s, because the dual of L_∞^s is H/H^s, free of rank one by §3.3. Equal coranks and injectivity give a finite cokernel.

**Checks.**

- The proof needs the tame field: the pro-p property of Gal(Ψ∞/Φ) alone says nothing about W[π], whereas G_Φ acts on W[π]≃F[π]⊗R through the cyclic group Gal(Φ(F[π])/Φ) of order p²−1, which is 24 at p=5, without nonzero fixed vectors, and that group meets Gal(Ψ∞/Φ) trivially.
- At n=1 and s=+, ω_1⁺=1, so L_∞⁺[1]=0 and L_1⁺=0: the plus condition at level 1 is zero because every character through level 1 has even conductor.
- The cokernel is finite, not zero in general; surjectivity over R is not asserted.

*Needs:* §5.1; §1.1; §3.3; SelmerIwasawaCohomology §3.11; ArithmeticGaloisDuality §R02.2.2; `IsGalois.normalBasis`.

### 5.3 Signed Selmer control

Assume E is defined over K. For s∈{+,−} and n≥0, let X_(n,s) be the Pontryagin dual of Sel_s(K_n,W) with the trace convention of §0.4. The restriction map induces a surjection X_s/ω_n^sX_s→X_(n,s)/ω_n^sX_(n,s) with finite kernel; equivalently, restriction Sel_s(K_n,W)[ω_n^s]→Sel_s(K∞,W)[ω_n^s] is injective with finite cokernel. After tensoring with L_χ and projecting to a character χ with χ(ω_n^s)=0, it identifies the χ⁻¹-specialisation of X_s with the χ-part of the rational signed Selmer group over K_n (AHII05 Theorem 5.2 and its proof, pp.619–620).

A route is as follows. Compare global localisation kernels by inflation–restriction and the snake lemma (SelmerIwasawaCohomology §3.11), using the image of localisation rather than assuming it surjective. At 𝔭 use §5.2. Away from p only places above f matter, since the comparison is in cohomology unramified outside fp; at w|v|f the kernel of restriction is a quotient of W^(G_(K∞,w)), which lies in W^(I_v), a proper R-submodule of W≃L/R because φ is ramified at v, hence finite. Global injectivity uses H⁰(K∞,W)=0 from §5.2. Dualise with the involution to obtain the stated direction.

**Checks.**

- The kernel on the dual side corresponds to the cokernel of the injective discrete map, so the dual map is surjective; the reverse direction would claim injectivity of a map with a possibly nonzero finite kernel.
- At a place v|f the local error W^(I_v) is finite because every proper R-submodule of L/R is π^(−k)R/R for some k; rational vanishing of H¹ away from p (§1.3) is a different fact and does not bound these integral errors.
- At n=0 and s=+, ω_0⁺=1, so both sides are zero; at n=0 and s=−, ω_0⁻=γ−1 and the statement compares the Γ⁻-coinvariants of X_− with the dual of Sel_−(K,W)=Sel(K,W).

*Needs:* §5.2; §1.4; §1.5; SelmerIwasawaCohomology §§3.3, 3.11.

### 5.4 Finite-level decomposition into the two signed pieces

Assume E is defined over K. Let X_n be the dual of Sel(K_n,W). The map X_n→X_(n,+)/ω_n⁺X_(n,+)⊕X_(n,−)/ω_n⁻X_(n,−), dual to the sum of the inclusions Sel_+(K_n,W)[ω_n⁺]⊕Sel_−(K_n,W)[ω_n⁻]→Sel(K_n,W), has finite kernel and cokernel. After tensoring with L it is an isomorphism compatible with every character projector after extension to L_χ. For χ∈Ξ^ε factoring through Gal(K_n/K), Sel_(−ε)(K_n,V_pE)^χ=Sel(K_n,V_pE)^χ (AHII05 Proposition 5.3 and its proof, p.620, with the misprint recorded in the References; BKO21 proof of Theorem 6.2, p.963).

A route is as follows. Use ω_n⁺ω_n⁻=γ^(pⁿ)−1, which kills Sel(K_n,W) and X_n, and the inclusions ω_n^(−s)Sel(K_n,W)⊆Sel_s(K_n,W) of §5.1 to build the comparison maps. Their kernels and cokernels are modules over the finite ring Λ_R/(ω_n⁺,ω_n⁻), finite but not zero in general. After inverting p the character projectors split the disjoint root sets of ω_n⁺ and ω_n⁻. A character χ∈Ξ^ε survives the point condition of sign −ε, which kills Ξ^(−ε), so the χ-parts of the local conditions of sign −ε and of the ordinary condition agree.

**Checks.**

- At χ=1 and ε=+, the trivial character lies in Ξ⁺, and the minus point condition A_n⁻ retains it, so Sel_−(K_n,V_pE)^1=Sel(K_n,V_pE)^1; the plus condition kills it.
- Integrally only finite kernel and cokernel are asserted: Λ_R/(ω_2⁺,ω_2⁻) is a nonzero finite ring, so the integral map need not be an isomorphism.
- For χ of order 25 at p=5 and ε=+, χ∈Ξ⁻=Ξ^(−ε), and the equality of χ-parts is not asserted for the minus sign; there the plus condition is the one that retains χ.

*Needs:* §5.1; §5.3; §1.4; PadicMeasuresIwasawaAlgebras §4.3.

### 5.5 Specialisation dimension and local length

Let A be a commutative local ring with residue field κ and M an A-module. Then dim_κ(κ⊗_A M)≤length_A(M) in ℕ∞. In particular, for a finitely generated torsion Λ_R-module X and a finite character ρ of Γ⁻ with values in L_ρ, applied to the discrete valuation ring A=(Λ_R)_(P_ρ), whose residue field is the field L(ρ) generated by the values of ρ, dim_(L(ρ))(X⊗_(Λ_R,ρ)L(ρ))≤length_A(X_(P_ρ))=ord_ρ char(X). If a generator f of char(X) is a unit multiple of an element of Λ_O, then ord_ρ f=ord_(ρ⁻¹) f (BKO21 proof of Theorem 6.2, pp.962–963).

A route is as follows. κ⊗_A M is an A-module quotient of M, so its A-length is at most that of M (`Module.length_le_of_surjective`). Its A-length equals its κ-length (`IsLocalRing.length_restrictScalars` with B=κ), which is its κ-dimension (`Module.length_eq_finrank`, or ∞ when infinite). Λ_R is regular local of dimension two, so its localisation at the height-one prime P_ρ is a discrete valuation ring with residue field Frac(Λ_R/P_ρ)=L(ρ), and X⊗κ(P_ρ) is the specialisation at ρ. The length of X_(P_ρ) is the coefficient of P_ρ in charDivisor(X), which is ord_ρ char(X) by the supplier contract with PadicMeasuresIwasawaAlgebras (gap:character-prime-multiplicity). For the last assertion, an automorphism of Φ(μ_(p^r)) over Φ sends ρ(γ) to ρ(γ)⁻¹, because Φ/ℚ_p is unramified and so Gal(Φ(μ_(p^r))/Φ)≃(ℤ/p^r)×, and it fixes the coefficients of f.

**Checks.**

- For A a discrete valuation ring and M=A/𝔪^k, κ⊗M=κ has dimension 1 and M has length k, so the inequality is strict for k≥2.
- For M=κ², dimension and length are both 2.
- For X=Λ_R/(g²) with g the minimal polynomial of ρ(γ)−1, the specialisation has dimension 1 and ord_ρ char(X)=2. For R ramified with μ_p⊂L, the coefficient automorphism need not invert ρ(γ), so ord_ρ and ord_(ρ⁻¹) are kept apart for a general R-coefficient generator.

*Needs:* `Module.length`; `Module.length_le_of_surjective`; `IsLocalRing.length_restrictScalars`; `Module.length_eq_finrank`; PadicMeasuresIwasawaAlgebras §4.6 (gap:character-prime-multiplicity).

### 5.6 Strict specialisation inequality

Assume E is defined over K. For every finite character χ of Γ⁻ factoring through Gal(K_n/K),

    dim_(L_χ) Sel_str(K_n,V_pE)^χ ≤ dim_(L_χ)(X_str ⊗_(Λ_R,χ⁻¹) L_χ) ≤ ord_(χ⁻¹) char(X_str).

The middle term is the specialisation of X_str at χ⁻¹, because the dual action inverts characters. Only injectivity of restriction is used; no finite-cokernel control is asserted (BKO21 proof of Theorem 6.2, pp.962–963; AHII05 Lemma 5.1 and its proof, p.618).

A route is as follows. Restriction Sel_str(K_n,W)→Sel_str(K∞,W)^(Γ_n) is injective: its kernel lies in H¹(Γ_n,W^(G_(K∞))) (SelmerIwasawaCohomology §3.11), and W^(G_(K∞)) lies in H⁰(Ψ∞,W)=0 (§5.2); restriction preserves the zero local conditions. After tensoring with L_χ the corank of the χ-part of Sel_str(K_n,W) is dim Sel_str(K_n,V_pE)^χ, the strict conditions on V_pE and W being propagated from each other (SelmerIwasawaCohomology §§2.5–2.6). The dual of Sel_str(K∞,W)^(Γ_n) is X_str/(γ^(pⁿ)−1)X_str, whose χ⁻¹-part after tensoring with L_χ is X_str⊗_(Λ_R,χ⁻¹)L_χ. Apply §5.5 at the prime of χ⁻¹ to the finitely generated torsion module X_str (§3.4).

**Checks.**

- Repeated factors raise the order without raising the dimension: X_str=Λ_R/(g²) gives dimension 1 at χ⁻¹ and order 2.
- The argument covers χ=1: injectivity uses only H⁰(K∞,W)=0, and the trivial character equals its inverse.
- No surjectivity of restriction is claimed: a finite cokernel would not affect the inequality, and an infinite one would only make the left side smaller.

*Needs:* §1.4; §1.5; §0.4; §3.4; §5.2; §5.5; SelmerIwasawaCohomology §§2.5–2.6, 3.11.

## Layer 6: Finite-character CM rank bounds

Sections 6.2–6.4 assume in addition that E is defined over K and that V_T is the CM component of V_pE (Conventions); then h_K=1 and R=O. Section 6.1 holds for every inert CM datum.

### 6.1 The strict characteristic divisor contains L_p

The surjection X_(−ε)→X_str of §4.1 has torsion kernel H^ε/S_ε, so char(X_(−ε))=char(H^ε/S_ε)·char(X_str); hence (L_p)⊆char(X_str) and ord_ρ char(X_str)≤ord_ρ L_p at every finite character ρ. When R=O, ξ and a generator of V∞*,ε are O-integral, so (L_p) is extended from Λ_O and ord_(χ⁻¹)L_p=ord_χ L_p; for a general inert CM datum the two orders are kept apart (BKO21 proof of Theorem 6.2, pp.962–963).

A route is as follows. Use §4.1 with s=−ε and the torsion of X_(−ε) (§4.2), multiplicativity of characteristic ideals and §4.6. Translate the product of integral ideals into containment and then into orders at height-one primes; apply §5.5 for the comparison of ρ and ρ⁻¹.

**Checks.**

- In the model char(X_(−ε))=(fg) and char(X_str)=(f), the containment is (fg)⊆(f); the reverse containment (f)⊆(fg) fails for g a nonunit such as g=T.
- At a character ρ with ord_ρ f=1 and ord_ρ g=2, the orders are 1≤3.
- For R ramified with μ_p⊂L and a generator not extended from Λ_O, ord_ρ and ord_(ρ⁻¹) can differ, so the equality is asserted only when R=O.

*Needs:* §4.1; §4.2; §4.6; §5.5; PadicMeasuresIwasawaAlgebras §4.6 (`charIdeal_mul_of_exact`).

### 6.2 The one-dimensional CM local correction

Assume E is defined over K. For every finite character χ of Γ⁻ factoring through Gal(K_n/K), dim Sel(K_n,V_pE)^χ−dim Sel_str(K_n,V_pE)^χ≤1. Indeed the quotient embeds into the χ-part of the ordinary local Kummer image at 𝔭, which is one-dimensional over L_χ for the CM component (BKO21 proof of Theorem 6.2, p.963).

A route is as follows. Localisation at 𝔭 on the ordinary Selmer group has kernel the strict Selmer group, the conditions away from p being equal. The formal logarithm identifies E((K_n)_𝔭)⊗Φ with Ψ_n≃Φ[Gal(Ψ_n/Φ)] by the normal basis theorem, in which each character occurs once. Extend to L_χ before applying the character idempotent and conclude from the injection.

**Checks.**

- The local image can have dimension 0 or 1; the statement is an inequality and does not assert that global localisation is onto the local line.
- Over ℚ_p rather than over the CM coefficient field, E((K_n)_𝔭)⊗ℚ_p has dimension 2pⁿ, and its χ-part over L_χ⊗ℚ_p would count both CM embeddings, giving 2 instead of 1.
- At n=0 and χ=1, E(Φ)⊗Φ≃Φ is one-dimensional, so the ordinary and strict rational Selmer groups over K differ by at most one dimension.

*Needs:* §1.4; §0.3; §5.4; `IsGalois.normalBasis`.

### 6.3 The matching-parity bound

Assume E is defined over K. If χ∈Ξ^ε factors through Gal(K_n/K), then dim Sel(K_n,V_pE)^χ=dim Sel_(−ε)(K_n,V_pE)^χ≤ord_χ L_p. The trivial character is covered exactly when ε=+ (BKO21 Theorem 6.2 and the last paragraph of its proof, pp.962–963; AHII05 Theorem 5.2 and Proposition 5.3, pp.619–620).

A route is as follows. Since χ∈Ξ^ε, χ(ω_n^(−ε))=0 by §5.1. Signed control for the sign −ε at χ (§5.3) identifies Sel_(−ε)(K_n,V_pE)^χ with the χ⁻¹-specialisation of X_(−ε), whose dimension is at most ord_(χ⁻¹)char(X_(−ε))=ord_(χ⁻¹)L_p by §§5.5 and 4.6, and this equals ord_χ L_p because R=O (§6.1). The equality with the ordinary χ-part is §5.4.

**Checks.**

- A character of exact order p has conductor exponent 2 and tests the Ξ⁺ branch; one of exact order p² has exponent 3 and tests Ξ⁻.
- At χ=1 and ε=+, the ordinary component equals the minus signed one by §5.4, and the bound reads dim Sel(K,V_pE)≤ord_1 L_p, the order of vanishing of L_p at T=0.
- If ord_χ L_p=0, the χ-part of the rational Selmer group vanishes.

*Needs:* §4.6; §5.1; §5.3; §5.4; §5.5; §6.1.

### 6.4 Finite-character CM rank bounds

Assume E is defined over K. Let χ be a finite character of Γ⁻ of exact order p^r, r≥0, and let n≥r, so that χ factors through Gal(K_n/K); this includes the source's layer n=c(χ). With rank_CM E(K_n)^χ=dim_(L_χ)(E(K_n)⊗_(O_K)L_χ)^χ, through the fixed embedding O_K→Φ⊂L_χ,

    rank_CM E(K_n)^χ ≤ dim Sel(K_n,V_pE)^χ ≤ ord_χ L_p        if χ ∈ Ξ^ε,
    rank_CM E(K_n)^χ ≤ dim Sel(K_n,V_pE)^χ ≤ ord_χ L_p + 1    if χ ∈ Ξ^(−ε).

Neither equality nor a leading-term formula is asserted (BKO21 Theorem 6.2 and its proof, pp.962–963).

A route is as follows. The Kummer map gives an injection of E(K_n)⊗_(O_K)L_χ into Sel(K_n,V_pE)⊗L_χ, equivariant for Gal(K_n/K), with torsion killed by the tensor product. For χ∈Ξ^ε use §6.3. For χ∈Ξ^(−ε) combine §§6.2, 5.6 and 6.1: dim Sel^χ≤dim Sel_str^χ+1≤ord_(χ⁻¹)char(X_str)+1≤ord_(χ⁻¹)L_p+1=ord_χ L_p+1.

**Checks.**

- For ε=+, χ=1 lies in Ξ^ε and receives no extra one; for ε=−, χ=1 lies in Ξ^(−ε) and receives the bound ord_1 L_p+1.
- If ord_χ L_p=0, the matching χ-part of the Mordell–Weil group has rank 0, and the opposite one has rank at most 1.
- The main conjecture of §4.6 for E over H is not turned into this assertion: for K=ℚ(√−23), h_K=3 and no maximal-CM curve with Hecke character over K is defined over K, so §6.4 says nothing there while §4.6 applies.

*Needs:* §6.1; §5.6; §6.2; §6.3; TauCetiRoadmap.EllipticCurves Layer 7.

### Examples

Let ε=+ and χ have exact order 5 at p=5. Then χ∈Ξ⁺ and the Mordell–Weil and Selmer χ-parts over K_1 are bounded by ord_χ L_p with no extra dimension. For χ of exact order 25, χ∈Ξ⁻ and the bound over K_2 is ord_χ L_p+1, obtained through the strict Selmer group.

## Worked examples

Take a character χ of order p. Its conductor exponent is 2, so χ∈Ξ⁺. If ε=+, the primitive class lies in V_R⁺ and the torsion dual is X_−; the minus point condition kills Ξ⁻ and so retains χ, and the finite Selmer bound at χ has no extra dimension. If ε=−, the same χ lies in the opposite parity and only the strict route of §§5.6, 6.1–6.2 bounds it, with the extra one. For a character of order p² the conductor exponent is 3 and both branches reverse. Translating Agboola–Howard's order-indexed sets by a uniform sign reversal would put the order-p character in the wrong branch (BKO21 §6.0.2, p.962, footnote 3, p.960; AHII05 Remark 3.2, p.614).

At χ=1, ω_n⁻ has a zero and ω_n⁺ has none. The minus formal-point condition retains the ordinary component at the base layer, so ε=+ again uses X_− with no extra one, while for ε=− the bound allows the extra one. In the latter case δ vanishes on V_R⁻, and no interpolation formula divides by δ(v_−); this does not force L_p(1)=0.

For a lattice test let ξ=πv_ε in the free line V_R^ε with basis v_ε. Then L_p=π, V_R^ε/C_∞=Λ_R/(π), and the characteristic ideal retains (π). Replacing v_ε by uv_ε for a unit u replaces the coordinate by u⁻¹π, with the same ideal. After inverting p the quotient disappears, so an equality proved only after inverting p loses exactly the information the integral main conjecture records.

For a coefficient test let R/O have degree d and let f have coefficients in O. The Λ_R-module Λ_R/(f) has characteristic ideal (f) over Λ_R, while as a Λ_O-module it is (Λ_O/(f))^d with characteristic ideal (f^d). For general f use its norm. This rejects an identity that keeps the same element over both rings.

For a specialisation test, at a characteristic-zero character prime with local parameter t, the localised module A/(t^k) has specialisation dimension 1 and characteristic multiplicity k. The rank inequality is therefore dimension at most order, with strict inequality for k>1. A finite R-module can be a nonzero integral control error while contributing nothing to a rational character dimension; the control arguments therefore track finite errors and characteristic lengths separately.
