## Mathematical corrections and limits of support

1. **Glued-lattice Hodge-profile support.** The accepted design repeats the rectangular profile set printed in BHHMS23 Theorem 8.3.9 and Corollary 8.3.10. That index set conflicts with the defined lattices. Equation (76) gives L_j[1/p]=P̃[1/p]⊕⊕_(i≤j)(V(α_i)⊗P̃)[1/p]. Equations (71)–(72) identify the disjoint characteristic-zero type supports, so at most one embedding can have the raised Hodge pair. For f=2, j=1, the profiles are 00,10,01, where 0=(1,0) and 1=(2,−1). Profile 11 is absent: the representation has rank (1+3+3) dim P=7 dim P; adding the double-adjoint tensor would require another 9 dim P and give 16 dim P. At the generic point of a nonzero 11-type component the module is zero, while a positive-rank free module over the rectangular intersection would be nonzero. The proof itself computes the smaller annihilator: parallel-profile primes intersected with the single-raised-profile primes. The README §§4.8–4.9 therefore state that support, retaining freeness and all fibre/GK consequences. Evidence: BHHMS23 lines 4675–4695, 4953–4963, 5070–5073, 5119–5148, pp. 92–93, 99, 101, 103. The cached PDF page 101 was also rendered and visually read to verify that the rectangular index set is in the typeset source rather than an extraction error. This finding is about arXiv v7; no claim is made about an inaccessible publisher PDF or about whether an erratum exists.
2. **Integral versus residual comparison in the two-weight route.** The comparison involving a finite mod p interval uses the reduction of a type lattice, and freeness is reduced from the integral regular type ring. A p-torsion module cannot be isomorphic to a nonzero p-torsion-free integral value. Example: for A=O[[x,y]]/(xy−p), A/p is p-torsion and A is p-torsion-free. The support ring is R∞/I_𝒥; an annihilator ideal, rather than a quotient ring, equals I_𝒥. Evidence: BHHMS23 lines 4628–4639, 4657–4660. This is a correction/clarification of the design's notation, not a claim that an overbar lost by extraction is a source error.
3. **Residual Hecke ideal.** The ideal over O is the preimage of the residual evaluation ideal and includes p. Residual trace and determinant values are not unspecified elements of O. With q=5 and roots 2,3 in 𝔽₂₉ one gets S=25,T=9 and polynomial X²−5X+6. Evidence: BHHMS23 lines 4449–4451; BDJ10 §4.
4. **Coefficient dual and square-root twist.** The coefficient σ* means the Pontryagin dual even for an O-lattice; it is not Hom_O(σ,O) on torsion coefficients. The twist is fixed by θ_w²=ψ_w⁻¹c_σ,w⁻¹ and a chosen unramified value; on scalar z, the determinant twist contributes θ_w(z)². This makes the claimed central character a defining equation. Evidence: EGS15 lines 385–387, 1702–1716 with the central-character correction in BHHMS23 lines 4442–4448.
5. **Completed-cohomology adapters.** Reduction/invariants are qualified by the imported finite-level control, not asserted for arbitrary inverse-limit cohomology. O --p→ O has integral cohomology in one degree but nonzero residual groups in two. The definite function space has no extraneous G_F-action. An H¹ Hecke image does not acquire Eisenstein factors merely because H⁰ or H² exists: the unchecked assertion was replaced by the two-factor algebra 𝔽×𝔽 as a possible semilocality control. Evidence: BHHMS23 §8.5, lines 5409–5444; CompletedCohomologyPartII §§2.4, 3.7, 8.8–8.15.
6. **Zero and logarithm conventions.** Ordinary projective-dimension and dimension equalities exclude zero; grade zero is not assigned to the zero module. The growth logarithm is evaluated only for nonzero finite modules and n≥2, avoiding log base 1 and log 0. Noncommutative CM excludes zero; zero patching-functor values are allowed separately. The general Iwasawa layer uses a DVR uniformizer ϖ, including ramified coefficients and p=2, while the global application has O=W(𝔽), ϖ=p.
7. **Inherited accepted corrections retained.** Projective/injective finite deck-group modules are free only for p-groups. At w₁ the discriminant excludes the Frobenius ratios q and q⁻¹. The lower-bound level argument enlarges the other levels to K₁ and then includes the invariant subspace into the original space. An integral lattice's constituents mean those of its reduction. Augmentation initially gives a surjection to the locally constrained global ring. The unrestricted determinant has f+1 parameters, whereas releasing a fixed Hodge/inertial determinant adds one unramified parameter. Banach admissibility uses the open residual kernel, not the characteristic-zero specialization kernel. The reverse D₀-embedding is proved from localized exactness and essential socle, before the glued-lattice and GK arguments.

The source-issue notes were applied with their accepted scope: the Hom dimension in Proposition 8.3.7 is r, not f (BHHMS23 line 5037 versus lines 4722–4727); the type-to-prime norm-determinant twist stays negative (BHHMS23 lines 5075, 5097, 5107); the centre uses ψ⁻¹ and the finite central quotient curve (BHHMS23 lines 4442–4461). Miracle flatness is Proposition A.30 in GN22 v5, not a verified journal misprint (GN22 line 2242). EGS's use of 2012 Gee–Kisin numbering is an edition map, not a source error; the rejected allegation is not repeated as an erratum.
