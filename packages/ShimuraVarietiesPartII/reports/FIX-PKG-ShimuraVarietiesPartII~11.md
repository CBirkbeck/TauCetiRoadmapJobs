**The widened statement is true.** I proved it myself, without simplicity, following the steps of KPZ line 3435–3442:

1. *The weight lies in G.* For a Hodge-type datum, w_h is the scalar cocharacter of GSp(V), so it is defined over Q and lies in G.
2. *Z_G(R) is compact modulo the weight.* Z_G,R centralizes h. If g centralizes h(i) and has multiplier c, then g scales the positive form Ψ(x,h(i)y) by c, so c>0 and g/√c lies in a compact orthogonal group. Hence Z_G(R)/w_h(R^×) is compact.
3. *The weight splits off.* The weight space Q·w_h ⊂ X_*(Z_G°)⊗Q is Galois-stable. By Maschke it has a Galois-stable complement U, on which every complex conjugation acts by −1. So Z_G° splits over a CM field, Z_(G,0) (with X_*=U) is defined over Q, and Z_G°=Z_(G,0)·w_h(G_m).
4. *G is generated by G^c and the weight.* G=G^der·Z_G°=G^c·w_h(G_m).
5. *The kernel of c is G^c.* The quotient G/G^c is a quotient of G_m. The image of the compact connected h(U_1) in it is a compact connected subgroup of R^×, hence trivial. So w_h(−1)=h(−1)∈G^c. Since c(w_h(t))=t^(∓2), ker c=G^c·w_h(μ₂)=G^c.
6. *The sequence for the centre.* Write z∈Z_G∩G^c as z=d·s with d∈G^der and s∈Z_(G,0). Then d∈Z_G∩G^der=Z_(G^der). So Z_G∩G^c=Z^c_G, and ker(c|Z_G)=Z^c_G. Also Z_G=Z_(G^der)·Z_G°=Z^c_G·w_h(G_m), so c|Z_G is onto.
7. *The product clause.* The common-multiplier subgroup of a finite product of Hodge-type data is connected (it is an extension of G_m by ∏G_i^c), and it is of Hodge type on ⊕V_i. Its compact central torus is ∏Z_(G_i,0): ranks Σr_i+1−1=Σr_i. So its Z^c is ∏Z^c_(G_i).

None of these steps uses simplicity.
