## R02 (P2) — The Šilov idempotent theorem is claimed as library reuse, but nothing supplies it

**Location:** `README.md:802–804`: "For commutative unital algebras reuse the character description of the spectrum, invertibility iff the transform has no zeros, and the Šilov idempotent theorem: clopen subsets of the character space correspond bijectively to idempotents."

**Claim:** The Šilov idempotent theorem is available for reuse (from Mathlib, the supplier contracts name nothing else for this sentence).

**Witness:** The pinned Mathlib (`358314d4`) and Tau Ceti contain no declaration about it: `grep -rli "shilov\|Šilov\|silov"` over both libraries returns nothing, and no section of this roadmap or of a cited supplier states it. It is not a corollary of the one-variable calculus of §2.1: that calculus produces the idempotent of a clopen subset of `σ(a)` for one element `a`, while a clopen subset of the character space of a non-singly-generated algebra need not be the preimage of a clopen subset of any single `σ(a)`; the standard proof (Dales §2.4, Šilov's idempotent theorem) uses the Arens–Calderón several-variable holomorphic calculus, which no layer here builds. Nothing downstream (`grep -n "Šilov\|idempotent theorem"` finds only line 803) consumes it, so it is a target with no route rather than a used prerequisite.

**Repair:** Either delete the clause "and the Šilov idempotent theorem: clopen subsets of the character space correspond bijectively to idempotents" (the two reused facts before it are in Mathlib: `mem_spectrum_iff_exists`, `spectrum.gelfandTransform_eq`), or own it: add to §2.1 a target for the several-variable holomorphic calculus on finitely many commuting elements with its joint-spectrum and Oka–Weil inputs, state Šilov's theorem in §3.1 as a target with a `*Needs:*` line naming that calculus, and cite Dales §2.4 at the claim (uncertified by locator alone). Instance that refutes the shortcut: in `A = C(X, ℂ)` with `X = {0,1} × [0,1]` the clopen set `{0} × [0,1]` is the preimage of a clopen subset of `σ(a)` for `a = ` the first coordinate, but for the algebra generated by `a` and a second element with connected spectrum, the single-element calculi see only the components of each `σ(a)`; the theorem is about the joint character space.
