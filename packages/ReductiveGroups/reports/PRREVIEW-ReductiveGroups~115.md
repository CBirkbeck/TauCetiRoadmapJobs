# Review of ReductiveGroups (pull request #2530 at c90e370)

**Verdict:** needs changes

**Summary.** The verdict is needs changes, for three findings, all in the new §§9B.5–9B.8. First, §9B.7's field-points target says that GPin, GSpin and Spin over any field are Mathlib's Lipschitz and spin groups. That is false in rank 0 and over 𝔽₂ in rank 4, and the README's own GSpin₀ Check and its §9B.8 GSpin₄ both contradict it. Second, two clauses of §9B.7's structure statement fail: the centre of even-rank GSpin is connected in characteristic two, and Spin is the non-smooth μ₂ in rank at most one. Third, four other roadmaps plan the same orthogonal, spin and similitude group schemes, and the scope section does not name them. There are also four small corrections and three format notes; nothing is left for a human to decide.

## R01 (P1) — The field-points target of §9B.7 is false in rank 0 and over 𝔽₂ in rank 4, and the roadmap's own statements refute it

The "Field points" bullet (lines 4987–4991) makes three identifications over every field K. GPin(q)(K) is the homogeneous part of Mathlib's `lipschitzGroup`, GSpin(q)(K) is its even part, and Spin(q)(K) is `spinGroup`. The bullet also says the target "extends" the characteristic-not-two Tau Ceti statement. An implementer is asked to prove something false, and a reader computing GSpin points from it gets wrong orders.

Mathlib's `lipschitzGroup` (Mathlib/LinearAlgebra/CliffordAlgebra/SpinGroup.lean:62) is the subgroup generated by the invertible vectors. Two cases break the identification:

- **Rank 0.** No vector is invertible, so `lipschitzGroup` and `spinGroup` are trivial. But GSpin(q₀)(ℚ) = C₀(q₀)^× = ℚ^×, and Spin(q₀)(ℚ) = {±1}. The Check at line 5032 itself says GSpin₀ = 𝔾_m.
- **𝔽₂, rank 4.** Take q₄ = x₁x₄ + x₂x₃. I enumerated O(q₄)(𝔽₂) and found 72 elements. The six reflections in the vectors with q(v) = 1 generate a subgroup of only 36 elements (Dieudonné's exception to Cartan–Dieudonné), and products of an even number of them form a subgroup of 18. Since 𝔽₂^× = 1, the even part of the Lipschitz group embeds in SO(q₄)(𝔽₂) with image of order 18. On the other side, §9B.8's GSpin₄ ≅ {(g₁, g₂) : det g₁ = det g₂} (line 5043) gives GSpin₄(𝔽₂) = GL₂(𝔽₂)², of order 36. §9B.7's own exact sequence with Hilbert 90 also lifts all 36 elements of SO(q₄)(𝔽₂). The same count refutes Spin(q₄)(𝔽₂) = `spinGroup`.

The Tau Ceti theorem cited for this bullet (TauCeti/LinearAlgebra/CliffordAlgebra/Lipschitz/CliffordGroup.lean:140) assumes `[Invertible (2 : K)]`, nondegeneracy and a unit value of Q. Its module docstring notes that the rank-0 case fails.

Smallest change: state the three identifications for l ≥ 1 and K of characteristic not two, and drop "which this target extends". In characteristic two, describe GPin(q)(K) by its defining condition only. The 𝔽₂ count (18 against 36) would make a useful negative Check.

## R02 (P2) — Two clauses of §9B.7's Structure bullet fail in small rank or in characteristic two

The Structure bullet (lines 5001–5004) makes two claims: that GSpin(q) and Spin(q) are smooth with connected reductive fibres, and that the identity component of the centre of GSpin(q) is the central 𝔾_m for l ≥ 3. The Check at lines 5032–5033 adds that the centre of GSpin_l has two connected components for l = 2n ≥ 4. GSpin_l is a group scheme over ℤ, so these claims cover every fibre, including characteristic two.

- **The centre for even l = 2n ≥ 4.** The roots ±e_i ± e_j force a₁ = … = a_n = ε with ε² = 1, so the centre is {(c, ε)} ≅ 𝔾_m × μ₂. For l = 4 these are the scalar pairs (a, b) with a² = b² in the README's own model of GSpin₄. Over a field of characteristic two, μ₂ = Spec k[x]/(x − 1)² is connected. So the centre is connected, and its identity component is 𝔾_m × μ₂, not 𝔾_m. Over ℤ, μ₂ is connected too. Conrad describes this centre as an extension of μ₂ by 𝔾_m (conrad-rgs line 13822, p. 336). The two-component statements in Cai–Friedberg–Kaplan §1 (arXiv v5, p. 7) and Asgari–Shahidi §2 are made over fields of characteristic zero.
- **Spin in rank at most one.** The README's own conventions give GSpin = 𝔾_m for l ≤ 1 (line 5032) with ν(c) = c². So Spin(q) = μ₂, which is not smooth over ℤ and has two points over ℚ.

Smallest change:
- In the Structure bullet, say that the maximal central torus (the radical) of GSpin(q) is the central 𝔾_m for l ≥ 3.
- Restrict the smoothness and connectedness of Spin(q) to l ≥ 2.
- In the Check, say that the centre of GSpin_l for l = 2n ≥ 4 is 𝔾_m × μ₂. It has two components over fields of characteristic not two, and is connected and nonreduced in characteristic two.

## R03 (P2) — Other roadmaps also plan the orthogonal, spin and similitude group schemes of §§9B.5–9B.7, and the scope section does not name them

Two plans of one object get built twice, and nothing says which one the other cites. The plans already disagree on a convention (Spin in rank 0, below). The scope paragraph settles only AutomorphicLFunctionsAndLocalFactorsPartII §0.0.

- **LefschetzPencilsAndVanishingCycles (on main).** Its §2.1 (README line 1491) covers ordinarity of a quadratic form over any base, with the fibre criterion. Its §2.2 (README lines 1551–1595, `evenClifford_center_etale_azumaya`) covers the finite étale rank-two centre of the even Clifford algebra in positive even rank. §9B.6 restates both: the nondegeneracy convention at line 4819, and the centre at lines 4899–4901, citing Knus. No *Needs* entry names a supplier for either. The restated centre sentence is also false at l = 0, where Z(q) = R; §2.2's positive-rank hypothesis excludes that case.
- **QuadraticFormsOverABase (open).** Its Layer 3 (README lines 631–711 at its head; ownership list line 49) builds O(Q) over an arbitrary base as the closed subgroup scheme of GLₙ cut out by the coefficients of Q(gx) − Q(x). It compares it with `TauCeti.ConstantForm` at the polar matrix when 2 is invertible, and proves μ₂ × SO(Q) ≅ O(Q) in odd rank and the smoothness of O and SO. Its Layers 1–2 cover the half-discriminant and the unit criteria. §9B.6 plans the same objects (lines 4808–4819, 4884–4893 and 4908–4912).
- **OrthogonalTamagawaAndLatticeMass (open).** Its §0.1 (README lines 129–141) constructs O, SO and Spin of a nondegenerate quadratic space over a field of characteristic not two, with Spin → SO a central isogeny. Its §0.1a (README line 153) builds O(q) over perfect fields of characteristic two. It also defines Spin of rank 0 as trivial, whereas §9B.7 makes it μ₂.
- **ClassicalGroupsPartII (open).** Its CG.0.2 (README lines 237–299) constructs Sp₂g × 𝔾_m → GSp₂g with kernel μ₂ and the fppf-quotient identification. Its CG.0.4 (README lines 353–394) builds the diagonal GSp torus with simple roots e_i − e_{i+1} and 2e_g − e_t. §9B.5's Similitudes bullet (lines 4858–4866) plans the same over ℤ.

Smallest change. ReductiveGroups is on main and may not consume an open roadmap, so it is the owner of the integral objects.
- Cite LefschetzPencilsAndVanishingCycles §§2.1–2.2, with their positive-rank hypothesis, instead of restating them. Do this in §9B.6's conventions, the Dickson paragraph, the *Needs* line and the supplier contracts.
- Add one sentence to the scope paragraph, as was done for AutomorphicLFunctionsAndLocalFactorsPartII §0.0. It would name QuadraticFormsOverABase Layers 1–3, OrthogonalTamagawaAndLatticeMass §§0.1–0.1a, and ClassicalGroupsPartII CG.0.2 and CG.0.4 as special cases of §§9B.5–9B.7.
- Agree with OrthogonalTamagawaAndLatticeMass on one rank-0 convention for Spin.

## C01 — The similitude bullet calls one map both an isomorphism and not surjective on ℚ-points

Lines 4860–4862 read: "`(𝔾_m × Sp₂ₘ)/μ₂ → GSp₂ₘ` … is an isomorphism of group schemes over `ℤ` … and is not surjective on `ℚ`-points". An isomorphism of group schemes is bijective on the ℚ-points of the fppf quotient. What fails is the map on points before sheafifying, 𝔾_m(ℚ) × Sp₂ₘ(ℚ) → GSp₂ₘ(ℚ), as the Check with diag(2, 2, 1, 1) shows. Write "while 𝔾_m(ℚ) × Sp₂ₘ(ℚ) → GSp₂ₘ(ℚ) is not surjective".

## C02 — The GSp torus and its Check use the antidiagonal model, not the stated carrier

The carrier is `SimilitudeGroup.GSp` with J = −Matrix.J = [[0, I], [−I, 0]] (line 1197). In that carrier:
- the torus diag(t₁, t₂, c t₂⁻¹, c t₁⁻¹) of line 4863 lies in GSp₄ only when t₁ = t₂;
- diag(2, 1, 1, 2), which the Check at line 4878 says is not in GSp₄, lies in it with ν = 2.

Both statements are right in the J_{Sp₄} model. The Sp part reaches that model by the signed permutation P of "Other Gram matrices"; the similitude bullet has no such step. Either say that the GSp torus and Check are taken after the same conjugation by P, or write the torus in the carrier as diag(t₁, …, t_m, c t₁⁻¹, …, c t_m⁻¹).
