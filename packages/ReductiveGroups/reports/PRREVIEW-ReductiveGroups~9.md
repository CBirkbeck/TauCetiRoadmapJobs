## R01 (P1) — The Frobenius Check identifies two different kernels

**Location:** `TauCetiRoadmap/ReductiveGroups/README.md:3545`: “its kernel is the distribution algebra of α_p, of dimension p”.

**Claim:** This is false for the induced map on distributions, and contradicts the augmentation-ideal formulation at lines 3490–3492. The group-scheme kernel and the kernel of the distribution algebra homomorphism are different objects.

**Witness:** At p=2, Dist(F)(γ₀)=γ₀≠0 although γ₀ belongs to Dist(α₂). Conversely γ₃ lies in the map's kernel but not in span{γ₀,γ₁}=Dist(α₂). All odd γ indices lie in the kernel.

**Repair:** Say that the *group-scheme* kernel is αₚ, whose distributions have basis γ₀,…,γₚ₋₁. The distribution-map kernel is the ideal generated by their augmentation ideal, equivalently the span of γₙ with p not dividing n. Keep γ₀ and γₚ₊₁ as negative controls. For p=2 this gives an infinite-dimensional kernel spanned by γ₁,γ₃,…, while Dist(α₂) has dimension 2 and contains the unit. The same correction gives γ₃↦γ₁ and γ₄↦0 at p=3.

## R02 (P1) — Swapping free generators does not preserve their product

**Location:** `TauCetiRoadmap/ReductiveGroups/README.md:4023`: “The swap (a,b)↦(b,a) also preserves the product”.

**Claim:** The asserted Check is false in precisely the noncommutative fundamental group being used to fix the half-twist convention.

**Witness:** In the free group, ab and ba are distinct reduced words. The quotient a=(12), b=(23) in S₃ gives ab=(123) and ba=(132). The Hurwitz rule instead gives (aba⁻¹)a=ab.

**Repair:** Replace that clause by “the swap sends ab to ba and is an involution”. Retain the valid Hurwitz square and inverse calculations. This repairs the Check on the free generators and on the displayed S₃ quotient; no commuting-generator assumption is appropriate here.

## R03 (P1) — Every displayed conjugate is unipotent

**Location:** `TauCetiRoadmap/ReductiveGroups/README.md:1894`: “are diagonal or unipotent only for s=0”.

**Claim:** The displayed matrices belong to a conjugate of U⁻, so all are unipotent. The intersection calculation needs membership in the *fixed upper-unitriangular subgroup* U⁺, not unipotence.

**Witness:** Over ℚ, s=1 gives M=[[2,−1],[1,0]]≠I and (M−I)²=0. For general s, M−I=s[[1,−1],[1,−1]], whose square is zero. Its lower-left entry is s.

**Repair:** Replace “unipotent” by “upper unitriangular in the chosen basis”. Then membership in T or U⁺ forces s=0, while the nonidentity s=1 matrix remains unipotent. The claimed intersection dimensions 1,0,0,0 and failure of the common-torus formula survive this correction.
