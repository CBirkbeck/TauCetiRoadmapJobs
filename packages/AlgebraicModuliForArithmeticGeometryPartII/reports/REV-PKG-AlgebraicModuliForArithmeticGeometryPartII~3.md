1. **F01** (P2) The exceptional self-intersection omits the residue degree

   **Location:** README:821, §4.5: “For the blow-up of a point, ((E·E)) = (−1).”

   **Claim:** Blowing up any closed point of a regular surface over the arbitrary ground field of §4.5 gives exceptional self-intersection −1 in the package's ground-field normalization.

   **Witness:** Blow up P²_ℝ at x=(u²+1,v). Then E=P¹_ℂ and O(E)|E=O(−1), so E²=χ_ℝ(O(−1))−χ_ℝ(O)=0−2=−2.

   **Exact repair:** State E²=−[κ(x):k] for a closed point x of a regular k-surface, and −1 when x is k-rational. Add the displayed degree-two real example as a negative-control Check. Keep the arbitrary-field negative-definiteness theorem. README:23 already requires this restriction-of-scalars factor, and §4.3 uses k-degrees. The repaired formula gives −2 in the counterexample and −1 at a rational point. Over ℂ the former exceptional divisor becomes two disjoint curves of square −1, so its full pullback has square −2, as required by field-extension invariance. The rational-point example at README:794 is sound. The value 1 at README:1010 is a relative degree over κ(x), and should remain 1.

2. **F02** (P2) The étale–Galois dictionary needs commutativity

   **Location:** README:447, §2.6: “Étale group schemes over k ≃ discrete Gal(k^sep/k)-modules”.

   **Claim:** The entire category of étale group schemes corresponds to Galois modules. The preceding bullet correctly calls the particular numerical quotient commutative, but does not restrict this categorical assertion.

   **Witness:** Over an algebraically closed field the constant S₃ scheme is finite étale. Its products (12)(23)=(123) and (23)(12)=(132) differ, so its group cannot underlie an abelian Galois module.

   **Exact repair:** Say “Under the supplied equivalence between commutative étale group schemes and discrete abelian groups with continuous Galois action, Num corresponds to its geometric numerical lattice.” Cite SchemeAndStackFoundations §2.15 `etale_galois_comparison` in §2.6's Needs and the supplier contract for this application. Add a Check separating the constant cyclic C₃ scheme, which gives the trivial-action module ℤ/3, from constant S₃, which is outside this equivalence. The existing P¹ example still gives ℤ with trivial action, and the conjugate pair still gives ℤ² with its swap. This correction does not require the acting Galois group to be abelian.

For the positive-power proof, finitely many geometric numerical basis vectors each acquire a positive norm exponent and a positive Brauer exponent. Their product works for every class; for rank zero the empty product is 1. Conversely d(σv−v)=0 with d>0 and a torsion-free lattice forces σv=v. For the pure-curve proof, Cartier divisors away from the other components and associated points give positive multiples of the coordinate multidegrees. Thus a fixed lattice fixes each component.
