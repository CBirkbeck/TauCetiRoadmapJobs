## F01 (P2) — applied differently

Old: the docstring said alternating containment (§7.4a) could not be stated. New: the theorem `parabolic_alternating_containment` (MP18 Theorem 1.3; MP18 line 90, proof at line 460). It assumes p prime, p%4=1, and Γ-transitivity up to sign. It concludes that every π in `alternatingGroup` of the `signStep` quotient of nonzero coefficient-one triples comes from some f in `Submonoid.closure` of the Vieta moves and coordinate permutations. Here "comes from" means: f sends every nonzero triple x to a nonzero triple y with π⟦x⟧=⟦y⟧. The docstring and README §7.4a are updated.

I rejected the reviewer's suggested carrier, "the subgroup of `Equiv.Perm Y` generated by induced moves". Inducing the moves on the quotient needs proofs that they preserve the surface and respect signs. That would mean either sorry'd data or proofs inside definitions. The submonoid of self-maps of `Fin 3 → ZMod p` needs neither. Because every generator is an involution, the monoid and the group have the same image. Requiring y to exist forces f to preserve X*.

## F02 (P2) — applied differently

Old: "explicit cage range", "explicitly proved cage/endgame range" and "the cage/endgame and sign-invariance hypotheses" (§§5.3, 5.4, 5.13, 5.14) were never defined. New: §5.3 defines the cage–endgame condition, with `IsCagePoint` (§3.1) and `endgameBound` (§5.4). The condition asks for a point c whose Γ-orbit C contains every cage point, and requires that outside C each companion order dividing p∓1 is at most K_p^∓. §§5.4, 5.13 and 5.14 now use this condition. §5.13 becomes the headline `uniform_connectivity_interval`.

This is applied differently in two ways:

- **Sign invariance is now proved, not assumed.** §5.3 proves it from cage containment, following EFLMT24 Lemma 2.2 (line 191). A cage point whose four sign translates are all in the cage exists for every p>3: for p≡1 mod 4, negating a trace of order p−1 keeps order p−1; for p≡3 mod 4 the same holds with order p+1. Sign changes commute with the Vieta moves. I computed this for every prime 5≤p≤257.
- **"Cage nonempty" was dropped as a hypothesis.** It holds for every p>3: the slice of r+r⁻¹ for a primitive root r is nonempty. It is now an API bullet in §3.1.

The condition stays a hypothesis because BGS16 Proposition 6 holds only "for p large" (BGS16 line 320). So no numerical range can be asserted.
