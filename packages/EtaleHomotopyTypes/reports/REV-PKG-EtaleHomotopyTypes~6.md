1. **F01** (P2) The 0-acyclicity criterion requested from EtaleCohomologyFoundations is false in the direction §§3.8 and 3.8a use

   **Location.** `packages/EtaleHomotopyTypes/README.md:135` (gap `nonabelian-local-acyclicity`, identical in `design/plan.json`): "a coherent morphism is 0-acyclic exactly when its geometric fibres are nonempty and connected (SGA 4 XV 1.11, 1.16, 2.1 and 4.1)"; line 634: "for coherent morphisms 0-acyclicity is equivalent to nonempty connected geometric fibres"; line 615: "0-acyclic, its geometric fibres being nonempty and connected".

   **Claim.** For a coherent morphism, nonempty connected geometric fibres imply 0-acyclicity; §3.8a's projections `X ×_K Y → Y` and §3.8's elementary fibrations obtain their 0-acyclicity from this.

   **Witness.** Let `R` be a discrete valuation ring with residue field `k` and fraction field `K`, and `f : X = Spec k ⊔ Spec K → S = Spec R`. `f` is affine, hence coherent, and both geometric fibres are single points. For a two-element constant sheaf `C`, `Γ(S,C) = C` while `Γ(X,C) = C × C`, so `C → f_*f^*C` is not bijective and `f` is not 0-acyclic: the very consequence the route at line 636 draws from 0-acyclicity ("every connected finite étale cover of `S` pulls back to a connected cover of `X`", SGA1 line 12134) fails for the trivial cover. The equivalence restates the parenthetical of SGA1 XIII Proposition 4.1 (SGA1 line 12121–12123), which is wrong as printed (Source issues). SGA 4 XV 1.16 is uncertified, so the criterion cannot be checked at its source; the counterexample stands on the README's own use. Both uses here are of flat, finitely presented morphisms (the projection at 634; a smooth `f` at 615), for which the direction holds: such an `f` is universally open, so a clopen piece of `X ×_S S̃` (`S̃` a strict localization of `S`) missing the closed fibre and the piece containing it would both map onto `S̃` and disconnect a geometric fibre.

   **Repair.** In the `needs` sentence (README 135 and `design/plan.json`), replace "a coherent morphism is 0-acyclic exactly when its geometric fibres are nonempty and connected" by "a coherent morphism that is universally open (flat of finite presentation suffices) is 0-acyclic exactly when its geometric fibres are nonempty and connected", keeping the SGA 4 XV 1.16 locator; at 634 add "`f` is flat of finite presentation, hence universally open" before the fibre argument; at 615 write "being smooth, hence universally open"; and add to §3.8a's Checks the negative control "`Spec k ⊔ Spec K → Spec R` has one-point geometric fibres and is not 0-acyclic, since a two-element constant sheaf has twice as many sections upstairs; it is not open at the closed point, so the universally-open hypothesis excludes it". Tested on the refuting instance: the repaired statement makes no claim about it (the image of `Spec k` is the closed point, not open); on `X ×_K Y → Y` and on smooth `f` the hypothesis holds and the conclusions of §§3.8, 3.8a and 3.9a are unchanged, as is SGA1's own use at line 12298, where the morphism is the flat projection.
