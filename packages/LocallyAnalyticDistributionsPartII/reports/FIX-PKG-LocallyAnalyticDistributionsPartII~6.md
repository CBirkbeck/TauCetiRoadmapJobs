## F01 (P1) — applied

Old (§4.1): `λ : Z(g) → K`, `D(G, K)_λ := D(G, K) ⊗_{Z(g),λ} K`, with ker λ in the rational centre. New: Z_K = Z(U(g_K)) = K ⊗_{ℚ_p} Z(g); λ is a K-algebra character Z_K → K; the tensor product and every kernel are over Z_K. Changed the same way in Conventions 6, §1.3, §4.2, §4.3 (antipode extended K-linearly) and the §4.14 descent. A negative-control Check was added.

**Verification.** In §4.1, g is the ℚ_p-Lie algebra (Layer 1: g = ℚ_p ⊗ L_U), so Z(g) is a ℚ_p-algebra. For 𝔾 = G = 1, p = 3 and K = ℚ₃(√3), Z(g) = ℚ₃. The README's tensor product was then K ⊗_{ℚ₃} K ≅ K × K, which has K-dimension 2. The quotient on the same line and the claimed strong dual C^la(1, K)′ = K have K-dimension 1. The finding is correct.

The sources agree with the repair:
- FU24 line 792 (Theorem 5.2) uses ℚ_p as the coefficient field on both sides.
- ST02 line 636 (Proposition 3.7) extends the centre to Z(g) ⊗_L K.
- LieHighestWeight Layer 7 (on main) defines central characters as K-algebra maps `Z(U(L)) →ₐ[K] K` for a K-Lie algebra L. That is exactly the repaired notion with L = g_K.
