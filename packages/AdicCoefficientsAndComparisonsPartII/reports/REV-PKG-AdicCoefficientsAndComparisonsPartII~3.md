2. **F02** (P2) A projective modification needs a nonempty log ideal

   **Location:** packages/AdicCoefficientsAndComparisonsPartII/README.md:976, 983 and 985. Quotation: “If X is quasi-excellent and noetherian, the normalized blow-up is a projective modification”.

   **Claim:** Coherence of the log ideal and quasi-excellence of the log regular base suffice for this modification conclusion.

   **Witness:** On X = Spec F₂ with trivial log structure, take I empty. It is coherently generated by the empty set. Its ordinary ideal is zero; the Rees algebra has no positive-degree part, so its Proj and normalization are empty.

   **Exact repair:** Retain the general blow-up comparison, but require the log ideal to have a nonempty stalk at every geometric point for the **modification** conclusion and the corresponding proof sentence. Equivalently in this log regular coherent setting, its ordinary ideal must be the unit ideal at every generic point. Say this explicitly rather than merely requiring global nonemptiness, which would still allow deletion of a connected component. Add the empty-ideal negative control. The supplier's universal-property contract can still allow the empty ideal with empty blow-up; it does not by itself promise a modification. Compare ilo14 line 4451, VIII §3.1.19, p. 101, which explicitly restricts its monoidal blow-up assertion to nonempty ideals; coherence alone in ilo14 lines 4444 and 4761 supplies no such restriction.

   **Repair tested:** For I empty on Spec F₂, the repaired modification hypothesis is false, and the actual map empty → Spec F₂ is not dominant and is not an isomorphism on any dense open. For the unit ideal the blow-up is the identity. For the existing A₁ toric example, the ideal is nonempty at every stalk and the normalization has the same fraction field, so generic degree remains 1. On two disjoint field points, an ideal empty on one component deletes exactly that component and is correctly excluded.

3. **F03** (P3) Monoidally smooth maps lack their own API and discriminating Checks

   **Location:** packages/AdicCoefficientsAndComparisonsPartII/README.md:1004–1012. Quotation: “A map is monoidally smooth when its maps of characteristic monoid stalks are locally of the form P → P ⊕ ℕ^r”.

   **Claim:** This introduces a definition used to specify the desingularization functor's generality, but its only labelled Check concerns freeness on a singular cusp, not a morphism.

   **Witness:** The strict map Spec k → A¹ with trivial logs has characteristic map 0 → 0; a free-factor map has ℕ → ℕ²; the Kummer square map has ℕ → ℕ, 1 ↦ 2. The predicate gives true, true, false.

   **Exact repair:** Give this definition an API in prose, including identity/composition, strict maps and the stalkwise criterion, and at least three map Checks. Use: the strict closed immersion with trivial logs (monoidally smooth although not smooth); the axes projection to the logged affine line (adds one free characteristic generator); and the square map on the logged affine line in characteristic 3 (Kummer étale but not monoidally smooth). Keep these in the README, with no Lean padding.

   **Repair tested:** The respective group cokernels are 0, ℤ and ℤ/2. The last cannot be a free monoid-factor inclusion; the first separates this notion from ordinary smoothness, and the second separates it from strictness. These agree with ilo14 line 4847, VIII §3.3.14, p. 109.
