## Mathematical corrections and limits

The accepted source corrections E1–E13 are carried into the package where relevant. Their locators were checked in the fetched texts. These are the mathematical consequences, stated without source passages:

- E1: The relative-curve route with unrestricted base distillation uses char(S) ⊆ P. The char(X) form is retained by working over active reduced components, shrinking there, and extending the finite extensions and modifications; unused components and the empty source are handled separately. An arbitrary maximally dominating map need not be dominant on every target component.
- E2–E4: In Galois-closure tameness the easy implication is from closure to subextension; the compositum of conjugates is the closure. The height-one citation has no part (ii). The residual exponent in the high-rank argument is the final residue exponent, not the characteristic of an intermediate residue field.
- E5 and E12: Resolving alterations are projective by convention. Blow up the prescribed closed subset first, so its inverse image is a Cartier support and then a union of SNC components. A point contained in V(xy) is not itself a divisor.
- E6: The separable geometric route uses a maximal separable P-extension, with the separate valued-field tame export; it cannot silently use an unrestricted maximal extension containing purely inseparable roots.
- E7 and E9: Replace the standard étale polynomial by the actual minimal polynomial before claiming the localized algebra embeds in the field; the unramified-locus argument is the first part of that lemma.
- E8: Separatedness gives injectivity of forgetting a valuative centre. No converse for an arbitrary fixed pointed scheme is asserted.
- E10: The curve construction defines its total boundary and the composite map is log smooth; it is the source which is log regular.
- E11: Cossart–Piltant Theorem 1.1 gives projectivity over a specified affine cover. The full universal projective and non-separated theorem is the PA.0.12 proof obligation. The char(X), char(S) and refinement statements preserve that requirement, rather than attributing it to the source.
- E13: Unit chart coordinates have group rank ν and branch coordinates monoid rank μ; the local model uses these distinct factors.

Additional corrections from the package pass are recorded with their computed counterexamples in the tables below: degree-one maps to generically nonreduced schemes are not modifications; P-tameness needs a characteristic-compatible point before comparison with a localization of Z; a trivial log structure with free characteristic on a singular scheme is not a resolution; finite affine quotients retain finite-type hypotheses; total and horizontal boundaries differ; unit exponents enter the log-chart torsion condition; markings require an étale splitting before they are ordered; and a stable modification need not be a stable pointed curve. Reducible endpoint arguments keep all required target components, rather than selecting an arbitrary component. The underlying morphism of a saturated normalized blow-up is declared projective only under finite-normalization hypotheses. Non-quasi-compact monoidal towers are locally finite, rather than given an unproved uniform finite length.

The README cannot support an unconditional globally projective resolution of arbitrary quasi-excellent threefold alterations from the cited Cossart–Piltant statement alone. It records this mathematical proof obligation and keeps affected endpoint statements conditional on its resolution. No assertion that a small example proves this missing global theorem is made. The field, excellent one-dimensional and excellent-surface cases do not depend on that obligation. The full targets are retained; none is replaced by a good-prime or separated-only shortcut.
