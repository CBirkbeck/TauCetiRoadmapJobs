## R01 (P1) — The enlarged dyadic cutoffs do not reconstruct the input

**Location:** `TauCetiRoadmap/PseudodifferentialOperators/README.md:1117–1118`: “with a second dyadic partition equal to 1 on the supports of the first, write the transition T on a block as Σ_k Δ_j T Δ̃_k”.

**Claim:** The new proof conflates the partition of unity used to analyze u with the enlarged cutoffs used to act on each analyzed block. Enlarged cutoffs equal to one on overlapping supports are not themselves a nonnegative partition of unity. Without the original Δ_k on the input, the displayed sum need not equal Δ_j T. This fails already for the identity transition, which satisfies all the new hypotheses. The Besov boundedness theorem itself is not refuted; its proposed reconstruction identity is. Bergh–Löfström’s analysis/synthesis construction also keeps the analyzed coefficients (bl line 4387–4395, printed p. 150).

**Witness:** At one frequency let ψ₁=ψ₂=1/2 and all other analysis multipliers vanish. Enlarged cutoffs have ψ̃₁=ψ̃₂=1. For T=Id, the claimed j=1 sum has multiplier 1, whereas Δ₁T has multiplier 1/2.

This is an ordinary smooth dyadic example: choose a smooth even low cutoff χ, nonincreasing on [0,∞), equal to 1 on [−1,1], zero outside (−2,2), and with χ(3/2)=1/2. Set ψ₀=χ and ψ_k(ξ)=χ(2^(−k)ξ)−χ(2^(1−k)ξ). At ξ=3 the displayed values hold. Choose enlarged cutoffs supported sufficiently close to the respective supports; at ξ=3 only the first two enlarged annular cutoffs are nonzero. The plane wave exp(6πix) belongs to B⁰_(∞,∞), so this is also a test within the promised endpoint range.

**Exact repair:** Call ψ̃_k an enlarged cutoff family, not a second partition of unity. Require ψ̃_k ψ_k=ψ_k, uniform rescaled derivative bounds and finite overlap. Write

`Δ_j(Tu) = Σ_k (Δ_j T Δ̃_k)(Δ_k u)`.

Retain the stated kernel estimate for Δ_j T Δ̃_k, and apply it to Δ_k u before weighted sequence summation. Include the overlap example as a reconstruction Check. On that example the repaired j=1 multiplier is (1/2)(1·1/2+1·1/2)=1/2, exactly Δ₁T. No narrower p, q or s range is needed.
