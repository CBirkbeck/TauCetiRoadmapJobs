   **Claim.** For a fixed finite extension F/ℚ_p and a Macintyre-definable bijection f:X→Y of n-dimensional definable sets, a definable open Z⊆X with lower-dimensional complements exists on which f is strictly differentiable with invertible derivative, and this follows from CGH Theorem 3.3.2 and Proposition 3.3.5 by the argument outlined in CL's proof of Theorem 3.2.1.

   **Witness.** `cluckers_loeser_2008` lines 678–696 (p.17): the proof of Theorem 3.2.1 is an outline that takes its three steps — piecewise continuity, the partition of the graph into manifolds, the constant-rank refinement — from "Lemma 3.12", "Lemma 3.18" and "Proposition 3.29" of its reference [13], Denef–van den Dries, *p-adic and real subanalytic sets* (line 3796), and says "we refer to [13] for the details". The README's "dimension theory of §2.2" does not exist: §2.2 (lines 357–376) states cell decomposition and preparation and no notion of dimension for Macintyre-definable sets, yet line 429 uses "dimension less than n", "a definable bijection preserves dimension" and the Haar-nullity of lower-dimensional sets in regime (i).

   **Why it is P2 and not P1.** The statement is true: over a finite extension K of ℚ_p, in the Macintyre language with coefficients from K (`cluckers_comte_loeser_2012` lines 159–164), every definable set admits a finite partition into definable K-analytic manifolds with a given definable function analytic of constant rank on each piece (Theorem 4.2.5, p.23, lines 935–941, and the analytic-centre cell decomposition Definition 1.4.1/Theorem 1.4.2, p.6, lines 206–227).    **Exact repair.** In the regime (i) paragraph of line 429 replace the sentence from "run the argument of the proof of CL Theorem 3.2.1" through "asserts no analyticity" by: apply the finite definable analytic stratification of Cluckers–Comte–Loeser (Theorem 4.2.5, p.23, with the first sentence of its proof: strata are definable F-analytic manifolds on which the given definable function is analytic of constant rank; cells with analytic centres, Definition 1.4.1 and Theorem 1.4.2, p.6) to the coordinate functions of f on X and of f⁻¹ on Y; let Z be the union of the n-dimensional strata of X on which f is analytic, intersected with f⁻¹ of the corresponding union for f⁻¹.
